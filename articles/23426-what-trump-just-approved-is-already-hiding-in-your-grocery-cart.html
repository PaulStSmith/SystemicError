What Trump just approved is already hiding in your grocery cart

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20264 min read

The Price of a Cheap Burger Is Regulatory Surrender

This Was a Political Choice

Donald Trump used presidential authority to admit roughly 660 million pounds of foreign beef without the usual out-of-quota tariff. The reporting places that decision alongside weakened food-safety enforcement, opaque sourcing, and a private Oval Office meeting with JBS co-owner Joesley Batista. Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins says she does not know where the imported meat will originate. Consumers will not know either, because federal law no longer requires country-of-origin labels for beef and pork.

That is the essential power structure: the president controls the proclamation, executive agencies control enforcement, and Congress controls funding and disclosure requirements. Consumers control none of those things. Neither do American ranchers expected to compete with the newly favored imports.

The Mystery Was Manufactured

The absence of origin labels is treated as an information gap, but it is the result of legislation. Congress repealed mandatory labeling for beef and pork in 2015 by placing the measure inside a massive spending bill after meatpacking interests lobbied Washington.

Calling the resulting product “mystery meat” risks making deliberate opacity sound accidental. There is no technical obstacle preventing a package from identifying where its beef came from. The obstacle is political: disclosure would give buyers information that large processors prefer not to provide.

That arrangement transfers power from consumers and producers to the corporations controlling slaughter, processing, blending, and distribution. The public assumes the government cannot tell it more. In reality, the government decided not to require more.

Access Produces Results

The sequence involving JBS deserves direct scrutiny. Batista reportedly met privately with Trump and argued for lower tariffs on Brazilian beef. Trump announced his import plan the next day. The expanded quota was placed in a category dominated by Brazil.

That sequence does not, by itself, prove a corrupt bargain. It does establish extraordinary access followed immediately by a policy benefiting the visitor’s industry.

The surrounding record sharpens the concern. Batista and his brother previously admitted bribing Brazilian officials. Trump’s first administration fined their companies under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act. Pilgrim’s Pride later contributed $5 million to Trump’s 2025 inauguration; Trump suspended enforcement of that anti-bribery law, and his Securities and Exchange Commission approved JBS’s long-blocked New York Stock Exchange listing.

The political meaning is not hidden in a hamburger. A corporation with money, connections, and Oval Office access received decisions it wanted from institutions capable of delivering them. Consumers received less information about what they would be eating.

Safety Capacity Is Being Dismantled

Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s Department of Health and Human Services announced thousands of layoffs across federal health agencies, including 3,500 at the FDA. According to the source, those dismissed included laboratory scientists who test food and communications staff responsible for public recall warnings.

The administration also seeks to transfer routine inspections to states while cutting federal laboratory capacity. Republican lawmakers, meanwhile, are considering deep reductions to the grants supporting those state inspections. This is not decentralization backed by adequate resources. It is responsibility pushed downward while funding is pulled away.

The same pattern appears in food traceability. A rule designed to locate contaminated products rapidly was delayed until 2028 after industry objections, and Congress barred the FDA from funding earlier enforcement. When an outbreak spreads, officials can then describe the source as difficult to identify—as though lawmakers and administrators had not deliberately disabled the mechanism built to identify it.

Failure Is the Policy’s Output

Food regulation becomes visible mainly when it breaks. A delayed warning, an untraceable shipment, or an understaffed laboratory sounds bureaucratic until the consequence is hospitalization. The source reports more than 18,000 confirmed cases, 990 hospitalizations, and two deaths in a Cyclospora outbreak spanning 49 states. It also reports that the FDA’s success in identifying outbreak sources fell from 77 percent in 2024 to 36 percent in 2025.

Those figures do not establish that every illness resulted from a specific cut. They do show a system losing investigative capacity while facing simultaneous outbreaks. Weakening that system and then pleading uncertainty is institutional cowardice: officials claim authority when granting favors and discover helplessness when asked to account for the consequences.

The misdirection is to blame faceless “government failure,” careless consumers, foreign producers, or overwhelmed states. The actors with power are in Washington. They cut staff, postponed safeguards, restricted enforcement, concealed origins, and altered import rules.

Privatized Profit, Socialized Risk

The larger pattern is regulatory capture with a populist sales pitch. Corporate processors gain cheaper inputs and fewer disclosure obligations. Political leaders gain a temporary talking point about grocery prices. The risks—contamination, delayed recalls, hospital bills, lost confidence, and pressure on domestic ranchers—are distributed across everyone else.

This is how deregulation protects power while pretending to liberate the public. The corporation keeps the information. The executive grants the exemption. Congress removes the guardrail. The agency loses the personnel needed to detect the damage. Then the person standing in the supermarket is told that the origin of the beef is simply unknowable.

It is not unknowable. It has been made undisclosed, untraceable, and weakly inspected because powerful institutions chose those conditions—and because the people benefiting from them had access to the officials empowered to choose.
