All 4 living former presidents deny Trump's bizarre Iran bombing claim

Aides to all four living presidents confirmed to CNN on Monday that none of them have spoken with President Donald Trump about his bombing strikes in Iran, as Trump claimed during a press conference.

Trump claimed in the Oval Office that he had spoken to a former president about bombing Iran, and that the former president had approved of the operation. Trump added that the former president claimed he “wished” he had made the decision to bomb Iran. The president also declined to identify which president he was talking about.

The four living former presidents are: Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama, and Joe Biden.

CNN’s Jeff Zeleny and Samantha Waldenberg reached out to aides for each of the four living former presidents, all of whom told them that the former presidents did not speak to Trump about the strikes.

“A spokesman for Clinton told CNN that no recent conversations have taken place between Clinton and Trump — about Iran or anything else,” they reported.

“Aides to Bush, Obama and Biden offered similar sentiments on Monday, saying there is no record of any communications with Trump,” the report added.

Our Analysis:

The Phantom Approval: Dissecting Trump’s Claims on Bombing Iran

In a recent press conference, President Donald Trump made a bold, if not bewildering, claim: he asserted that he had consulted with a former president about his decision to conduct bombing strikes in Iran, and that this unnamed ex-president had expressed support for the operation, even regretting not having taken similar action themselves. This statement, on its face, packs the dual punch of attempting to legitimize a controversial military decision while also tying it to a bipartisan seal of approval. But a closer examination, grounded in the facts reported by CNN, reveals a narrative far more tangled — and perhaps more deceptive — than initially meets the eye.

Who Holds the Power?

First, it’s crucial to underline that the decision to carry out bombing strikes in a foreign nation squarely falls within the purview of the sitting president, in consultation with their advisors and relevant governmental departments. The institutional power to authorize military action rests with the current administration, not with former presidents, regardless of their experiences or opinions.

The Decision-Maker

The decision to bomb Iran, as reported, was made by President Trump. This action, and any diplomatic or military strategy surrounding it, is a direct outcome of the current administration’s policies and worldview. Trump’s attempt to bring unnamed predecessors into the conversation does nothing to shift the locus of responsibility for this decision; it remains firmly with him.

Misdirection and Responsibility

The article’s framing, based on Trump’s claims, initially seems to suggest a broader consensus or continuity in U.S. foreign policy toward Iran, spanning multiple administrations. However, the swift and unanimous denials from aides to all four living former presidents — Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama, and Joe Biden — not only refute Trump’s assertion but also spotlight a deliberate attempt to misdirect responsibility and fabricate a sense of bipartisan support where none exists.

False Equivalences and Lazy Narratives

Trump’s claim, if left unchallenged, propagates several dangerous narratives. It falsely equates non-action by previous presidents with tacit approval of his administration’s policies. Furthermore, it lazily suggests that the complex geopolitical decision-making surrounding Iran can be boiled down to a simple matter of willingness or courage to act, ignoring the nuanced, strategic considerations that inform such decisions.

Rejecting Convenient Scapegoats

By asserting that a former president not only approved of but wished for the bombing strikes, Trump attempts to create a convenient scapegoat, should the fallout from the action prove negative. This sidesteps a fundamental accountability principle: the current administration must own its foreign policy decisions, successes, and failures alike.

Conclusion

The CNN report uncovers a troubling attempt by President Trump to rewrite the narrative of a unilateral military action into a bipartisan endeavor supported by the wisdom of his predecessors. This move, however, is transparently disingenuous. None of the living former presidents were consulted about the Iran strikes, much less offered their approval.

The real story here is not about who supposedly supported what, but about who actually holds the power and responsibility for the decision to bomb Iran. That person is President Donald Trump. Attempts to shift blame or manufacture a legacy of cross-administration support not only fail but also highlight a concerning propensity to distort reality for political gain.

In “Systemic Error,” we stand committed to dissecting these narratives, refusing to let false equivalence or convenient scapegoats cloud the truth. The responsibility for the bombing strikes in Iran lies solely with the Trump administration, no matter how they spin it.
