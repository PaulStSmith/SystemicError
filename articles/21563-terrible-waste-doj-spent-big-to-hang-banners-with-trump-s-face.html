'Terrible waste': DOJ spent big to hang banners with Trump's face

When Justice Becomes a Prop Department

The Real Story

Politico reports that the Department of Justice spent nearly $1 million on a so-called “beautification” project at Main Justice, including celebratory banners featuring Donald Trump’s face and the slogan “Make America Safe Again.” That is the surface fact. The deeper fact is simpler: a federal law-enforcement department used public money and administrative secrecy to stage loyalty imagery for the president.

Who Held Power

This was not some rogue clerk’s prank. The institutional actors here were the DOJ leadership, the contractors executing the work, and the administration that set the direction. The department itself said the banners were part of a broader project and boasted that it was carrying out work “at President Trump’s direction.” That is the power structure in plain view: the Justice Department aligning its physical space, spending, and messaging with the president’s branding.

The Costume of Administration

The contract language does the usual bureaucratic trick: it turns political spectacle into maintenance. “RFK Beautification Project,” facade lighting, exterior walls, celebratory banners. That is how partisanship gets laundered through procurement language until it looks like infrastructure. The anonymous official’s claim that the project was “urgent” and had to be done before the 250th anniversary only makes the choreography more obvious. The deadline is the excuse; the image is the point.

Misdirection by Design

This story is already trying to hide behind process. The line item for creating and hanging the banners was blacked out. The DOJ spokesperson talks about history, country, and safety instead of answering the obvious question: why is the Justice Department spending public money to decorate its headquarters with the president’s face? Sen. Adam Schiff is right to call it wasteful and potentially illegal, but the larger issue is not merely cost. It is the conversion of an enforcement agency into a stage set for executive self-glorification.

What This Reveals

This is how authoritarian politics normalizes itself inside institutions: not always with a purge, but with signage, contracts, euphemisms, and budget lines. The danger is not only that Trump gets his face on a banner. It is that the Department of Justice is made to look like an accessory to the man it is supposed to constrain. Once an institution built around law starts celebrating a ruler, the corruption is no longer symbolic. It is administrative.

The Pattern

The relevant pattern is not confusion. It is submission. Public power is being spent to broadcast political obedience while bureaucratic language is used to make that obedience seem routine. That is the real scandal here: a federal department helping manufacture personal authority and then asking the public to mistake it for government work.
