Speaking to reporters Tuesday from Air Force One on the return from Scotland, Trump was asked about his White House saying the reason that Epstein and his former friend Trump split up was because Epstein was a "creep." Yet, when Trump spoke to the press on Monday, he said it was a result of Epstein poaching staff from Mar-a-Lago.

"Mr. President, you said yesterday your falling out with Jeffrey Epstein was over him taking some of the workers from your business. But your administration in the past said you threw him out because he was a 'creep.' Can you explain that discrepancy?" the reporter asked.

"They're the same thing, you know? A little bit of the same thing. But he took people that worked for me and I told him don't do it anymore and he did it. And I said stay the hell out of here," he replied.

The reporter followed up, "So is that what was meant by being a 'creep?'"

When Trump asked the reporter who she was, the reporter answered that she was with NBC News.

"NBC? NBC Fake News. Ok. NBC's one of the worst. What else do you have?" Trump snapped.

Speaking to the press in Scotland Monday, Trump said, "Very easy to explain. But I don't want to waste your time by explaining it. But for years, I wouldn't talk to Jeffrey Epstein. I wouldn't talk because he did something that was inappropriate. He hired help, and I said, don't ever do that again. He stole people that worked for me. I said, don't ever do that again. He did it again. And I threw him out of the place, persona non grata. I threw him out, and that was it."

The first Epstein accuser to come forward publicly was Virginia Giuffre, a former Mar-a-Lago locker room attendant who was under 18 when she testified that Ghislaine Maxwell offered her an interview for the chance to train as a massage therapist. Ultimately, she told the BBC that she was "passed around like a platter of fruit" to Epstein and his friends.

She accused Prince Andrew of sexual abuse three times, which he continues to deny.

Virginia Giuffre died by suicide in April.

So, it's possible Trump is referring to Giuffre, who was poached from Mar-a-Lago and also sexually abused by Epstein.

In another conversation with a reporter aboard Air Force One, Trump described staff who were taken from his Mar-a-Lago spa.

"Well, I don't want to say, but everyone knows the people that were taken, and it was the concept of taking people that work, for me, is bad, but that story has been pretty well out there, and the answer is yes, they were in the spa," Trump said, according to TNND. "People that work in this five-star great spa, one of the best spas in the world, at Mar-a-Lago, and people were taken out of the spa, hired by him."

"I didn't know that, and then when I heard about it, I told him, I said, listen, we don't want you taking our people, whether it was spa or not spa," Trump continued. "I don't want to take your people. And he was fine. And then not too long after that, he did it again. And I said, out of here."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Trump-Epstein Saga: A Masterclass in Evasion and Deflection

In a recent interaction aboard Air Force One, President Donald Trump found himself ensnared once more in questions about his past association with Jeffrey Epstein, a convicted sex offender. The queries from reporters centered not just on the nature of their fallout but on the muddled explanations Trump has offered over time. His responses, or lack thereof, serve as a glaring exhibit of his characteristic evasion and deflection tactics, especially when confronted with uncomfortable truths.

The Conflicting Accounts

On one day, Trump tells the press that his disassociation from Epstein was due to the latter poaching staff from Mar-a-Lago, his opulent private club. Yet, previous statements from his administration painted Epstein as a "creep," insinuating moral and ethical reasons for their split. When pressed by a reporter to reconcile these discrepancies, Trump's answer was a nebulous blend of both reasons, topped with a dash of his signature disdain for the media, particularly NBC News, whom he labeled as "one of the worst" and purveyors of "Fake News."

This inconsistency is not just a minor oversight; it's a glaring spotlight on Trump's often contradictory narrative. The ease with which he shifts his stance, coupled with his attempt to undermine the reporter's credibility by attacking their affiliation, is a classic Trump maneuver to divert attention from the substance of the inquiry.

The Epstein Connection

The article mentions Virginia Giuffre, the first Epstein accuser to come forward publicly, who was a former Mar-a-Lago locker room attendant and underage at the time of her accusations. Her tragic death by suicide adds a somber note to the saga, highlighting the very real human cost of Epstein's crimes. Trump's vague references to staff poaching, potentially including Giuffre, do little to clarify his stance or his moral compass regarding the exploitation that occurred under Epstein's watch.

Trump’s attempt to frame the issue as a simple dispute over employee poaching from his "five-star great spa" at Mar-a-Lago grotesquely underplays the severity and criminality of Epstein's actions. The mention of Giuffre and the implications of her being "passed around like a platter of fruit" to Epstein and his associates, including the accused Prince Andrew, underscores the egregiousness of Trump's obfuscation.

The Art of Deflection

Trump’s responses are emblematic of his broader strategy when dealing with the press: when confronted, deflect, and attack. His refusal to provide a straightforward explanation, coupled with his ad hominem attacks on the reporter's credibility, serves as a smokescreen, designed to obfuscate the truth and redirect the narrative away from his association with Epstein.

Moreover, Trump’s labeling of NBC News as "Fake News" is a tired tactic meant to undermine the legitimacy of critical journalism. It's a defense mechanism against accountability, where the focus is shifted from the validity of the questions to the supposed bias of the questioner.

Conclusion

The discourse surrounding Trump's explanations of his relationship with Epstein reveals much about his approach to governance, media relations, and accountability. It's a dance of evasion, where clarity is sacrificed on the altar of political expediency. The tragic undertones of the Epstein saga, marred by abuse and the loss of lives like Giuffre's, deserve far more than to be relegated to fodder for Trump's deflection tactics.

In this masterclass of evasion, Trump demonstrates yet again that when it comes to facing uncomfortable truths, his preferred route is diversion rather than confrontation. The implications of such an approach, both morally and politically, are profound and deeply troubling.
