Republicans 'losing faith' as Trump is plagued by 'historically low' approval ratings

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Tumultuous Second Term: A Critical ExaminationIn Conclusion: A Reflection of Systemic Failure

When MSNBC's Rachel Maddow offered a scathing critique of President Donald Trump's first 100 days back in the White House, she told colleague Katie Phang that she was struck not only by how badly he has "botched" his second presidency, but also, by how quickly his poll numbers have plummeted.

Trump's hardcore MAGA base remains devoted to him, and right-wing media outlets — from Fox News to Newsmax to Steve Bannon's "War Room" podcast — continue giving him lavish praise. But many polls are showing independents and swing voters giving him low marks on everything from immigration to the economy.

The Guardian's Adam Gabbatt examines Trump's plummeting poll numbers in an article published on Saturday, April 26.

READ MORE: Maddow: Trump has 'botched the first 100 days of his presidency' more than any other US president

"Americans, including some Republicans, are losing faith in Donald Trump across a range of key issues, according to polling released this week," Gabbatt explains. "One survey found a majority describing the president’s second stint in the White House so far as 'scary.' Along with poor ratings on the economy and Trump's immigration policy, a survey released on Saturday found that only 24 percent of Americans believe Trump has focused on the right priorities as president."

Gabbatt adds, "That poll comes as Trump's popularity is historically low for a leader this early in a term. More than half of voters disapprove of Trump's performance as president, and majorities oppose his tariff policies and slashing of the federal workforce."

An Associated Press/NORC Center poll released on April 26 found that 54 percent of Republicans believe Trump is focused on the "right priorities." But the number falls to only 9 percent among independents.

A New York Times/Siena College poll also released in late April found Trump's overall approval is at 42 percent overall but only 29 percent among independents. That poll also found that only 43 percent of voters approve of his handling of the economy.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tumultuous Second Term: A Critical Examination

When MSNBC's Rachel Maddow illuminated the chaotic beginnings of President Donald Trump's second term, she wasn't just critiquing a presidency; she was spotlighting a systemic failure that goes beyond one individual. Maddow's conversation with Katie Phang was more than a rundown of presidential mishaps; it was a reflection of a deeper malaise within the American political system.

Trump's "Botched" Presidency and Plummeting Poll Numbers

It's no secret that Trump's presidency has been polarizing from the start. However, the speed at which his approval ratings have dipped in his second term is not just a testament to his leadership (or lack thereof) but also to the fickleness of political loyalty and the volatile nature of American politics. The Guardian's Adam Gabbatt provides a sobering overview of Trump's plummeting popularity, citing key issues such as immigration and the economy that have led to widespread dissatisfaction.

The Illusion of Unwavering Support

Despite the dwindling support from independents and swing voters, Trump's MAGA base remains "devoted" to him. This unwavering support, amplified by right-wing media giants like Fox News, Newsmax, and Steve Bannon's "War Room" podcast, paints a picture of a divided country where facts often succumb to fervor. The danger of such blind loyalty is its ability to obscure reality, turning political discourse into a battleground of us vs. them.

Polls: The Unforgiving Mirror to Trump's Presidency

Polling data serves as an unforgiving mirror to Trump's presidency, revealing a growing discontent among the American populace. With descriptions of his presidency as "scary" and only 24 percent of Americans believing he has focused on the right priorities, the numbers paint a grim picture of a leader out of touch with the needs of his people. The disparity between Republican support and the dismal 9 percent approval among independents underscores a fractured nation, torn between party loyalty and the harsh realities of Trump's policy impacts.

The Economic Disconnect

One of the most telling aspects of Trump's approval ratings is the disconnect between his handling of the economy and public perception. Despite what Trump and his allies may proclaim, a 43 percent voter approval on economic matters speaks volumes. It's indicative of a broader issue where economic policies favor the wealthy, leaving the average American feeling disconnected and disenfranchised.

In Conclusion: A Reflection of Systemic Failure

Maddow's critique and the subsequent analysis by Gabbatt are not just about Trump's failings but are reflective of a larger systemic issue. A political system that allows for such divisiveness, misinformation, and polarized media coverage is inherently flawed. Trump's presidency, both first and second terms, serves as a stark example of these systemic failures. As we move forward, it's crucial to address these underlying issues, fostering a political environment where truth, integrity, and accountability are not just expected but demanded by the electorate. The tale of Trump's tumultuous second term should serve as a cautionary tale, a prompt for introspection and systemic reform.
