Trump's no-bid contract spree is worse than anyone thought —and it's just the beginning

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Trump’s No-Bid State

Power, Not Confusion

The story here is not “ethical concerns” or “watchdog outrage.” It is raw institutional capture. Donald Trump is using federal contracting power to channel public money toward loyalists, donors, friends, and family-adjacent operators, while the public gets inflated bills and fake justification in return. That is not bureaucratic drift. It is the conversion of government procurement into a rewards system.

Who Decides

The decision-makers are not abstract. The Interior Department handed out a $1.7 million no-bid contract for the Reflecting Pool and a second $14.7 million contract for waterproofing it. Another no-bid award went to Barnard Construction for 112.5 miles of “secondary” border wall in New Mexico. CoreCivic got the Leavenworth prison deal to house immigrants the administration wants deported. Kristi Noem’s department paid $220 million for a publicity machine that showcased her in costume, and the company was run by her husband. These are not accidents. They are executive choices, made through agencies that are supposed to serve the public and are instead serving a political network.

The Misdirection

The favored lie in these stories is that no-bid contracts are a technical exception, reserved for rare expertise or urgent need. Sometimes that is true. Here, it is a cover story. The source makes clear that most federal contracts are supposed to be open bid, yet this administration is treating the exception as the rule. When the same circle of loyalists keeps showing up around the money, the issue is not process. It is patronage with paperwork.

Pay, Loyalty, Repeat

The real pattern is moral hazard with a presidential seal on it. Trump once boasted that competition sharpens pricing and quality in his private business. In office, he is doing the opposite: spending other people’s money without the discipline that would apply if he were footing the bill himself. That is the central corruption of this kind of politics. Public money becomes politically fungible, and then the administration selects winners based on allegiance rather than competence.

The Democratic State as Spoils Machine

This is also why “oversight” is not enough as a slogan. Oversight matters, but it is a brake after the vehicle has already been hijacked. What is on display is a governing style that treats the federal state as a private construction site and a loyalty-distribution system. The Wall Street version is not capitalism; the Trump version is cartel politics with public funding.

What This Reveals

The deeper lesson is that authoritarian politics does not always arrive through grand constitutional crisis. Sometimes it arrives through contracting forms, procurement loopholes, and a flood of quietly overpriced deals that reward friends and punish everyone else. The administration is not merely wasting money. It is teaching government to behave like a racket, then asking taxpayers to applaud the renovation.
