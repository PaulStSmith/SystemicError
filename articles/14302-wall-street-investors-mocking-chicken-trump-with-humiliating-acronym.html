Wall Street investors mocking 'chicken' Trump with humiliating acronym

Wall Street investors have spotted one of president Donald Trump's tendencies and have started betting against him.

They've even come up with a derogatory term – "TACO," and acronym for "Trump Always Chickens Out" – for the president's erratic handling of his on-again, off-again tariffs, because they've noticed his tendency to announce punishing duties that send markets into the tank, only to back off a short time later and goose the market, reported HuffPost.

"The recent rally has a lot to do with markets realizing that the U.S. administration does not have a very high tolerance for market and economic pressure, and will be quick to back off when tariffs cause pain," wrote Financial Times commentator Robert Armstrong, who is credited with coining the term earlier this month. "This is the Taco theory: Trump Always Chickens Out."

This dynamic played out in recent days, when markets drooped at the end of last week with Trump's announcement of 50 percent tariffs on the European Union, only to back off Sunday with a delay for a new round of trade talks – which drove major gains when markets reopened after the Memorial Day holiday.

“Once he delivers bad news, investors are buying those stocks when they are beaten down waiting for him to chicken out and watching those stocks rebound in value,” said Ted Jenkin, president of Exit Stage Left Advisors.

Analysts said this dynamic is entirely unique to the current president.

“Under no previous presidency did we have active markets betting on the president’s resolve,” said University of Michigan economist Justin Wolfers. “There was no BACO trade, no CACO trade – nothing. It was always taken as a given that when the president spoke on Monday, he would likely still mean it on Tuesday. That’s no longer true. But what’s really hard is that it’s not even obvious when it’ll be true, and when it won’t be. Madness.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Deal or The Farce of the TACO? A Dive into Trump's Tariff Tango

In the swirling vortex that is President Donald Trump's economic policy, Wall Street has seemingly cracked the code, distilling the chaos into four simple letters: TACO. This acronym, standing for "Trump Always Chickens Out," serves not just as a pithy summation of the president's approach to tariffs but as a searing indictment of his erratic policy maneuvers. Let's dissect this revelation, as reported by HuffPost, with the precision of a scalpel and the skepticism it duly warrants.

The TACO Phenomenon: Betting Against Predictable Unpredictability

It appears that Trump's strategy, if one can call it that, involves the dramatic announcement of punishing tariffs, which inevitably sends markets into a nosedive, only to then backtrack, allowing the markets to recover. This pattern has become so predictable that investors are now gaming it, essentially betting against Trump's resolve. According to Financial Times commentator Robert Armstrong, who is credited with coining the term "TACO," this represents a realization that the U.S. administration has a low tolerance for market and economic pressure.

A Pattern of Capitulation

This so-called "Taco theory" was exemplified recently with Trump's announcement of a 50 percent tariff on the European Union, a move that initially sent markets tumbling. True to form, Trump then delayed these tariffs in favor of new trade talks, resulting in a market rebound. This cycle of threat and retreat has become a hallmark of his administration, prompting Ted Jenkin of Exit Stage Left Advisors to observe that investors now actively wait for Trump to "chicken out" before capitalizing on market recoveries.

Unprecedented Market Manipulation

What sets this presidency apart, according to University of Michigan economist Justin Wolfers, is the active betting against a president's resolve, a situation unheard of in previous administrations. The predictability of presidential policy, once a given, has been replaced by a maddening inconsistency where even the president's word from one day may not hold to the next.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

The implications of the TACO phenomenon are profound and deeply troubling. It suggests a governance style that is not only unpredictable but one that actively undermines market stability and investor confidence. The fact that investors are now betting on the president's lack of resolve is a damning reflection of the state of economic policy under Trump's administration. It underscores a volatile approach to international trade that prioritizes bluster and brinkmanship over coherent strategy.

This is not just a quirky anecdote about Wall Street witticisms; it's a glaring red flag about the erratic nature of Trump's economic leadership. The "Taco theory" might provide a moment of levity, but the underlying reality it represents is anything but amusing. It's a stark reminder of the precariousness of basing economic policy on caprice rather than consistency, and the inherent risks of a presidency that operates more on impulse than on informed strategy.
