'Powerful': Expert reveals how GOP-appointed judge aims to pit Supreme Court against Trump

An order recently striking down an effort by President Donald Trump's administration to keep a man incarcerated in a foreign country without due process is piquing the interest of one legal expert, who argues it may have been written for a specific audience.

During a Thursday segment with CNN host John Berman, legal analyst Elie Honig — a former federal prosecutor — broke down the seven-page opinion issued by U.S. Circuit Court Judge J. Harvie Wilkinson III (an appointee of former President Ronald Reagan), which was joined by two other judges on a panel from the 4th Circuit Court of Appeals. That opinion denied the Trump administration Department of Justice's request for a stay on an order issued by U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis in the case of mistakenly deported Maryland resident Kilmar Abrego Garcia, along with his request for a writ of mandamus (an order instructing a government official to perform a specific duty).

Honig argued Wilkinson — who he noted was a "widely respected conservative — was also writing his opinion "for the U.S. Supreme Court," as he is "one of the very few appellate court judges in the country who has real sway with the Justice Scalias, Alitos, Gorsuches, et cetera," referencing three of the most conservative members of the High Court. He characterized Wilkinson's seven-page opinion as "a plea to rationality."

"I think he's anticipating, probably correctly, that this case will, one way or another, wind up at the Supreme Court," Honig said. "And I think he's inviting, exhorting the Supreme Court, 'we need you to get more firm and more specific here. The stakes are too high.'"

Last week, the Supreme Court handed down a 9-0 decision telling the administration it had to "facilitate" the return of Abrego Garcia, though Trump has yet to do so. Honig posited that Wilkinson is aiming to convince the six-member conservative supermajority on the Court by way of acknowledging their actions as the lodestar by which lower court judges like him base their decisions. And in doing so, Honig argued that SCOTUS could be pigeonholed into holding fast and adding teeth to their prior order.

"He acknowledges in the short term, the executive branch will do what it will do. And there's not a whole heck of a lot we, the courts, can do about it. We rely on the power of words, the power of suasion. but ultimately, the courts and the law will rule. when history looks back at this, it will not look fondly on you. executive branch, if you continue to defy the law," Honig said. "I think that's a very powerful way that he says it."

Our Analysis:

A Critical Analysis of Judicial Pushback Against Executive Overreach

In a recent legal skirmish that sheds light on the ongoing battle between the judiciary and the executive branch, a notable order emerged from the desk of U.S. Circuit Court Judge J. Harvie Wilkinson III, striking down an attempt by the Trump administration to indefinitely detain a man in a foreign country without due process. This decision, and the subsequent analysis by legal analyst Elie Honig, offers a compelling glimpse into the intricate dance of power, persuasion, and principle that defines our constitutional system.

The Heart of the Matter

At the core of this legal drama is the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident mistakenly deported and whose plight became a symbol of the executive branch’s cavalier approach to individual rights and due process. The denial by Wilkinson, a widely respected conservative judge, of the Trump administration's Department of Justice request for a stay, signals a critical stance against executive overreach.

Wilkinson's Strategic Play

The analysis by Honig brings to light Judge Wilkinson’s calculated move in crafting his opinion not just for the present audience but with an eye towards influencing the highest court in the land. Wilkinson’s seven-page opinion is described as a "plea to rationality" in the face of what he perceives as an impending review by the U.S. Supreme Court. By invoking the names of the Court's conservative stalwarts—Scalias, Alitos, Gorsuches—Wilkinson is not merely name-dropping but strategically positioning his argument in alignment with conservative judicial philosophy, in hopes of swaying the Court’s six-member conservative supermajority.

The Supreme Court's Role and Responsibility

What's striking here is the overt acknowledgment by Wilkinson of the Supreme Court's critical role as the ultimate arbiter of justice and the rule of law. His opinion serves as a clarion call for the Court to "get more firm and more specific" in curtailing executive overreach, especially in matters as grave as depriving an individual of their liberty without due process. The Supreme Court’s unanimous decision to facilitate Abrego Garcia's return, paradoxically, stands in stark contrast to the Trump administration's reluctance to comply, underscoring the tension between the judiciary's rulings and the executive's willingness to abide by them.

A Powerful Condemnation and a Warning

Honig’s interpretation of Wilkinson’s words as a powerful condemnation of the executive branch’s defiance of the law is particularly poignant. Wilkinson’s assertion that history will not look fondly upon those who defy the rule of law is both a warning and a reminder of the judiciary's long memory and moral compass. It encapsulates a broader concern about the erosion of democratic norms and the sanctity of individual rights in the face of political and administrative convenience.

Conclusion

This case, and Wilkinson’s opinion in particular, serves as a stark reminder of the delicate balance of power enshrined in our Constitution. It's a testament to the judiciary's role as a check on the potential excesses of the executive branch. As this saga unfolds, it will undoubtedly serve as a crucial case study in the enduring struggle between maintaining security and upholding the bedrock principles of justice and liberty.

In essence, the unfolding narrative around Kilmar Abrego Garcia’s case is more than a legal battle; it's a litmus test for the resilience of our constitutional democracy in the face of challenges that seek to undermine its very foundation. And as observers, analysts, and participants in this democracy, it behooves us to pay close attention to these battles, for they will determine the shape of our rights and freedoms for generations to come.
