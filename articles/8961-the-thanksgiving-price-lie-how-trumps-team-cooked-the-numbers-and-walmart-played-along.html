The Thanksgiving Price Lie: How Trump’s Team Cooked the Numbers and Walmart Played Along

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Cooking the BooksThe Real Walmart Special: Corporate PRThe Inflation MirageThe Audacity of HypocrisyManufactured NostalgiaWhat Accountability Looks LikeFinal Slice

Donald Trump is at it again — this time claiming that Thanksgiving 2025 will be 25% cheaper than under Joe Biden. According to him, Walmart says so. Cue the applause from MAGA-land, the nodding pundits, and the Facebook uncles who think a Butterball is proof of economic genius. The problem? It’s a lie — a cynical bit of retail stagecraft repackaged as a political miracle.

The video that exposed this farce, from the YouTube channel GodlessGardener, cuts straight through the bullshit. The creator, audibly exasperated, lays out two Thanksgiving shopping lists: Walmart’s 2024 “average meal” under Biden, and Walmart’s 2025 “cheaper” one under Trump. The difference isn’t 25% off — it’s 25% missing.

Cooking the Books

Let’s start with the obvious: you can’t claim prices dropped if you’re not buying the same thing. The 2024 list included a full spread — a 10-to-16-pound turkey, rolls, corn, cranberry sauce, green beans, fried onions, mushroom soup, gravy, pie ingredients, broth, and potatoes. The whole traditional layout.

The 2025 “Trump special,” by contrast, swaps in a smaller turkey, downgrades items, and — get this — cuts dessert entirely. The cranberry sauce is gone, replaced by fresh cranberries you have to prepare yourself. There’s no pie, no gravy, and fewer side dishes overall. The kind of “meal” you’d serve if your oven broke and your family stopped loving you.

It’s like comparing a three-course dinner to a gas station sandwich and declaring the latter proof of economic progress.

The Real Walmart Special: Corporate PR

What’s truly galling here isn’t just Trump’s predictable deceit — it’s Walmart’s willingness to play along. Corporations like Walmart know exactly what they’re doing when they release these “holiday basket” press kits. They’re designed for headlines, not accuracy. Every year, they tweak the contents, swap brands, and choose sizes that make the total price look appealing.

Then politicians weaponize that marketing fluff to declare victory over inflation. Trump’s camp didn’t discover a cheaper Thanksgiving — they found a cheaper list. It’s the same statistical sleight of hand used to declare victory in a war that’s still going.

Walmart benefits twice: first, from the publicity, and second, from the free advertising in right-wing echo chambers. The company’s logo gets plastered next to Trump’s latest economic lie, and no one at corporate seems too concerned. After all, outrage is engagement, and engagement sells turkeys.

The Inflation Mirage

Inflation is an easy villain — and an even easier distraction. Prices have indeed risen in the past few years, largely due to global supply disruptions, pandemic hangovers, and corporate profiteering disguised as “cost adjustments.” The data shows that corporate markups accounted for nearly 53% of price increases during the pandemic years — a windfall that hasn’t exactly trickled down to workers.

But for the Trump camp, none of that matters. Their economic argument runs on vibes, not data. They point to a Walmart ad, twist it into scripture, and ignore the reality that food insecurity, corporate consolidation, and stagnant wages didn’t start in 2021 — they’ve been festering for decades under both parties. Trump’s self-branded “cheaper Thanksgiving” is just the latest in a long line of political photo ops masquerading as fiscal salvation.

The Audacity of Hypocrisy

Let’s not forget that under Trump’s own administration, food prices rose nearly every year. The U.S. Department of Agriculture’s own data shows that grocery costs climbed steadily through 2017–2020. Fuel prices were erratic, not because of policy genius but because of OPEC negotiations and demand shocks. Yet here we are in 2025, being told that the guy who gave billionaires a tax cut and farmers a trade war is now the savior of the American dinner table.

It’s economic cosplay — populism dressed in a supermarket apron.

Manufactured Nostalgia

The emotional bait in all this is nostalgia. “Remember when groceries were cheap?” Sure. But remember why? Wages were lower, the global economy was different, and most of us weren’t paying attention to the real cost — environmental, social, and human — behind those prices. The illusion of affordability came from squeezing workers, outsourcing production, and hollowing out local economies. That’s not prosperity; it’s a pyramid scheme with a dinner roll on top.

Trump’s narrative banks on the collective amnesia of consumers who feel squeezed, and rightfully so, but are too exhausted to audit the scam. When people are paying $6 for eggs, they don’t have time to fact-check whether the turkey list matches last year’s. And that’s exactly what political marketers count on: fatigue.

What Accountability Looks Like

The video ends with a call to action: email Walmart. Demand transparency. Not because Walmart will suddenly develop a conscience, but because every corporate stunt should meet resistance. The idea that an election narrative can be shaped by grocery store PR should horrify anyone who still believes democracy requires truth.

Holding this administration — or any administration — accountable means cutting through the noise, calling out manipulation when we see it, and refusing to swallow the reheated lies of populist branding. Trump’s campaign doesn’t care about your grocery bill. They care that you believe it’s shrinking — even as corporate profits bloat.

Final Slice

This entire episode is a microcosm of the modern propaganda economy: a feedback loop between corporate press releases, political opportunists, and a public drowning in disinformation. It’s not about turkeys; it’s about trust. And every time a lie like this goes unchecked, that trust rots a little further.

So no, Thanksgiving isn’t cheaper under Trump. The only thing that’s been discounted is the truth.
