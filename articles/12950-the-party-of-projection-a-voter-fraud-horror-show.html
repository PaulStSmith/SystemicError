The Party of Projection: A Voter Fraud Horror Show

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

For a political movement so obsessed with voter fraud, you'd think the Republican Party would be better at avoiding committing it. But like clockwork, the loudest voices screeching about "stolen elections" and "rigged votes" seem to have their hands caught in the ballot box. Not metaphorically. Literally.

Let’s start with the case of Vicki Stuart, a 64-year-old postal worker from Mesa County, Colorado. Stuart wasn’t content to just spread conspiracy theories—she tried her hand at manufacturing them. She stole over a dozen mail-in ballots and filled them out fraudulently under the guise of “testing the election system.” Because nothing screams civic integrity like felony identity theft and elections forgery. Stuart was sentenced to five years in prison. Her accomplice, also a registered Republican, awaits sentencing.

Then there's James Saunders, a Trump supporter and former IRS attorney, who decided the democratic process was optional. He voted in both Florida and Ohio—not once, but in two elections: the 2020 presidential and the 2022 midterms. This wasn't a slip-up. It was a deliberate, calculated violation of electoral law. Ohio courts sentenced him to three years in prison. His lawyer tried to frame it as an act of confusion, but the judge wasn't buying it. And neither should we.

And it only gets worse.

A Republican official who had the gall to accuse others of election fraud has now been convicted of voting illegally nine times. NINE. The very people sowing doubt in the minds of voters are the ones actually defiling the process. It would be laughable if it weren’t so corrosive to democracy.

Let’s not forget Cheryl Hall, a Trump loyalist who submitted at least 10 falsified voter registration forms—switching Democrats and Independents into the Republican Party without their consent. Investigators suspect she may have done this to over a hundred voters. If that doesn’t qualify as a coordinated attempt to manipulate voter rolls, what does?

These cases are not isolated. According to NBC News and multiple state-level investigations, the list of Republicans convicted of voter fraud is steadily growing. It’s not a one-off. It’s a pattern.

Now here’s the kicker: these very same individuals and their political allies are the ones pushing for restrictive voting laws, cutting down early voting days, closing polling stations in urban centers, and making it harder for students and poor people to vote. Why? Because their argument is that voter fraud is rampant. They use the fraud they commit as the justification to disenfranchise the voters who don’t support them. That’s not just hypocrisy. That’s a long con.

In this twisted theater, Republicans have cast themselves as the defenders of democracy while actively undermining it. They torch the voting system, then parade around in firefighter helmets, demanding more gasoline.

Let’s be brutally honest: there is no widespread voter fraud problem being perpetrated by Democrats. Every election expert worth their salt has confirmed this, repeatedly. What we do have is a Republican voter fraud problem, weaponized as a talking point and executed as a tactic.

We are not dealing with political theater here. These aren’t memes. These are felony convictions. Prison sentences. Real ballots corrupted, real elections threatened. And real people—American voters—whose faith in the system is shaken every time a bad-faith actor in a red tie gets caught stuffing the democratic process into a shredder.

The irony would be delicious if it weren’t so dangerous. The same people chanting "Stop the Steal" have been quietly perfecting the art of the steal. And while they rage about imaginary mules and bamboo ballots, their own party is rotting from within.

This isn’t about partisanship. It’s about integrity. It’s about who is actually working to protect democracy, and who is exploiting it while pointing fingers at everyone else.

So the next time a MAGA hat bobs into your timeline with wild accusations of voter fraud, just remember: projection isn’t just a psychological defense mechanism. In Republican politics, it’s a strategy.

And it’s time we call it what it is: a systemic error.
