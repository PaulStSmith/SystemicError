'He was bragging about it': GOP insiders flag moment Trump lost key contest 'out of spite'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 6, 20254 min read

Donald Trump meets British Prime Minister Keir Starmer in Scotland. Christopher Furlong/Pool via REUTERS

President Donald Trump shot his party in the foot with a move to punish New Jersey, analyst Jonathan Lemire told a panel of MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” on Wednesday — mere weeks before an election in which his chosen candidate in the governor race, Jack Ciattarelli, took a severe drubbing to Democratic Congresswoman Mikie Sherrill.

Trump continues to lick his wounds and publicly proclaim the Democrats’ massive wins around the country were not his fault. But, Lemire said, there is one clear thing he did to make New Jersey voters angry, and GOP strategists were seeing it with their own eyes.

“Some of this was a response to Trump overreaction,” said Lemire. “On the New Jersey race. I heard from two different operatives last night, one Republican, one Democrat, who more or less said the same thing. One word: Gateway. The tunnel that Trump killed, the project that would have helped—”

“He was bragging about it,” cut in anchor Joe Scarborough. “Bragging about it two weeks before the election, bragging about having a Darth Vader hand. He basically said, I killed that project.”

“A tunnel that would have really helped New Jersey residents commuting into New York City,” said Lemire. “He killed it out of spite because he’s angry at Charles Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries because of the shutdown. And they, Democrats, were who were very nervous about this race and were frankly, surprised by the margin of victory last night, said the Gateway Tunnel was sort of the turning point.”

“And then, of course, on the other side of that tunnel ... we have New York City and we have Zohran Mamdani, with his stunning victory here, defeating Andrew Cuomo handily by nine or so points,” he added. “And look, it’s very clear that President Trump is going to use Mamdani as an excuse. I’ve been told and reported and wrote about it last night. They’ve kind of held off going after New York City tunnel aside because they wanted to win. They’re going to go after him now. We’ll see if that’s popular, though it has not been another when he’s gone after other cities.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Misstep: A Deep Dive into the New Jersey Election Fallout

In an act that can only be described as political self-sabotage, President Donald Trump managed to alienate voters in New Jersey, contributing to a significant loss for his endorsed candidate in the state’s gubernatorial race. This analysis unpacks the fateful decisions and the stark repercussions laid bare by Jonathan Lemire on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe.”

The Gateway Project Debacle

At the heart of Trump’s blunder is the Gateway Tunnel project, a critical infrastructure initiative intended to ease the daily commute for New Jersey residents traveling to New York City. Trump’s decision to halt this project wasn’t rooted in policy considerations or fiscal prudence. No, it was a move characterized by spite, aimed at Democratic leaders Charles Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries over political grievances. This wasn’t just a political chess move; it was a vindictive play, with Trump boasting about his decision as if he wielded the “Darth Vader hand” in the saga of American infrastructure.

The Electorate’s Response

The fallout from this decision was palpable. Lemire’s insights from both Republican and Democratic operatives highlight the same conclusion: the Gateway Tunnel was a pivotal issue for New Jersey voters. Trump’s actions directly contributed to the “severe drubbing” of his chosen candidate, Jack Ciattarelli, by Democratic Congresswoman Mikie Sherrill. This wasn’t merely a defeat; it was a resounding rejection of Trump’s influence and decisions.

Beyond New Jersey: The Wider Implications

Trump’s missteps in New Jersey are not an isolated incident but part of a broader pattern of behavior that sees cities and projects as pawns in his political vendettas. The article hints at Trump’s potential to use Zohran Mamdani’s victory in New York City as another scapegoat for his failures, despite the clear message from voters. It’s a predictable tactic, but as Lemire suggests, it’s one that has not historically paid dividends for Trump.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Political Pettiness

What this episode reveals is a glaring example of how Trump’s personal grievances and political tactics can backfire, with real consequences for his party and the constituents they serve. The Gateway Tunnel debacle is a testament to the fact that voters are increasingly unwilling to endorse candidates who prioritize political vendettas over tangible benefits for their communities.

In the end, Trump’s decision to punish New Jersey by killing the Gateway project did not just hurt his chosen candidate; it underscored his broader disregard for the practical needs of Americans. As the GOP looks ahead, one wonders if they will learn from these mistakes or continue to be hamstrung by the whims of an individual who places personal vendettas above the greater good.
