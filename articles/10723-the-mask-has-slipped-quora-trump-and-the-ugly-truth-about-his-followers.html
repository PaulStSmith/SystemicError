The Mask Has Slipped: Quora, Trump, and the Ugly Truth About His Followers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 8, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Every so often, a raw, unfiltered truth cuts through the endless smog of political spin. On Quora, buried among the usual handwringing questions about “bias” and “why is everyone so mean to conservatives,” one answer detonated with the precision of a well-aimed grenade. Asked why Quora seems “so blatantly left wing,” the respondent tore into the questioner with a merciless catalogue of Trump’s crimes, failures, and grotesqueries. And then, with surgical repetition, they drove home the core truth: supporting Trump is not a matter of political difference — it is a moral failure. Those who continue to stand by him are not simply misinformed. They are bad people.

That blunt conclusion has rattled around in my head because it punctures the most persistent myth in American politics: that Trump’s followers are just poor, misunderstood patriots who got swept up in something they didn’t quite understand. That’s a lie, and it’s a lie meant to absolve.

The First Excuse: “We Didn’t Know”

The Quora answer begins where most Trump defenses stumble — the 2016 election. Trump was already a walking billboard for corruption and cruelty. His “birther” crusade against Obama was not just a sideshow; it was a racist smear that launched his political persona. He was already known for bankrupt casinos, stiffing contractors, and marketing fraud dressed up as business genius. He had already bragged about grabbing women “by the pussy” and leered at teenage girls on camera. His friendship with Epstein and his creepy comments about his own daughter were public knowledge. None of it was hidden.

Yet his supporters excused it all. They saw the racism and winked. They saw the misogyny and shrugged. They heard the lies and told themselves, “He’s just being real.” They chose him precisely because he promised cruelty against the people they already despised. And they wrapped it all in a thin veneer of “he’s an outsider” and “he tells it like it is.”

This is the first rot: pretending they didn’t know who Trump was when they voted for him. They knew. They just didn’t care.

Riding the Obama Train — Until He Wrecked It

The economy Trump inherited was strong — stronger than he would ever admit. Job growth, GDP expansion, and stock market gains had been steady under Obama. Trump slapped his name on that train and rode it until the derailment of COVID exposed his incompetence. A president who spent years telling the world he was a genius businessman turned out to be a flailing, delusional disaster who watched over a million Americans die while insisting it was all fine.

His voters? They excused it. They blamed Fauci, China, Democrats, the media, anyone but the man at the helm. Because that’s what “good people” do.

The Impeachments, the Insurrection, the Felonies

Trump was impeached twice — once for shaking down Ukraine in pursuit of dirt on Biden, once for inciting an insurrection against his own government. No modern president has been impeached twice. He has since been found liable for sexual assault. His businesses were convicted of criminal fraud. He himself is now a convicted felon. He fomented an attempted coup. He tried to end democracy in America.

Every one of these facts is not just known — they are history. And still, tens of millions of Americans cheer for him. They invent conspiracies to explain it away, to pretend the courts, the juries, the witnesses, the tapes, the documents are all lies. They accuse Democrats of “weaponizing” the law while worshiping a man who breaks it as casually as most people check their phones. This is not ignorance. This is willful complicity.

The Refrain: That’s What “Good People” Do

The rhetorical brilliance of the Quora answer lies in its relentless repetition. Every time another Trump atrocity is listed, it lands with the refrain: because that’s what good people do. Supporters excused his sexual predation, because that’s what good people do. They ignored his frauds, because that’s what good people do. They dismissed the insurrection, because that’s what good people do.

The sarcasm is razor sharp. It drags the Trump base’s favorite self-defense — “we’re good people” — and shoves it face-first into the mud. The writer refuses to allow them the comfort of moral ambiguity. If you still support Trump, after everything, you are not a good person. You are a bad one.

The Rotten Core of Trumpism

The American political establishment, especially the mainstream press, has long coddled Trump voters under the fiction that they are the “real America,” the salt of the earth. This narrative has done more damage than we admit. It obscures the fact that Trumpism is not an accident — it is the logical conclusion of decades of racism, resentment, and authoritarian fantasy simmering in American life.

Trump didn’t create his base. He gave it permission. He told them their bigotry was fine, their cruelty was strength, their ignorance was wisdom. He didn’t brainwash them. He reflected them. He showed them who they already were, and they loved it.

That’s why the Quora answer matters. It refuses to sanitize. It refuses to say, “Oh, they’re just misled.” It says what must be said: Trump’s followers are not the victims of some cosmic misunderstanding. They are active participants in dismantling democracy.

The GOP’s Rot and America’s Excuses

Let’s be clear: the Republican Party has known all of this, too. They saw Trump’s racism, misogyny, corruption, and authoritarianism. And they embraced him because power mattered more than principle. Every Republican leader who still backs him is complicit. Every billionaire who funds him is complicit. Every media executive who soft-pedals him is complicit.

But none of that absolves the “average” voter. The people who stuck Trump signs on their lawns, who wore MAGA hats to their kids’ soccer games, who stormed school board meetings to scream about imaginary CRT and drag queens — they are not innocent pawns. They are willing foot soldiers for a movement that openly dreams of minority rule, theocracy, and dictatorship.

The End of Pretending

The Quora answer ends with a brutal kicker: Trump supporters defend it all because that’s what bad people do. No more hedging. No more pretending they are just misguided neighbors who’ll come around with the right facts. They are bad people making bad choices with full knowledge of what those choices mean.

That’s the part that polite society doesn’t want to admit. It is easier to believe millions of our fellow citizens are simply “confused” than to confront the truth that they are complicit in evil. But history doesn’t care about our comfort. The Germans who cheered Hitler weren’t “misunderstood.” The white Southerners who defended segregation weren’t “just misled.” They were people who chose cruelty because it suited them. The same is true now.

Why the Answer Resonates

In a landscape saturated with think pieces, handwringing, and moral relativism, this Quora answer cuts through because it is mercilessly honest. It doesn’t indulge the fantasy that Trumpism is about economic anxiety or cultural misunderstanding. It names it: racism, misogyny, corruption, authoritarianism, cruelty. And it names those who support it: bad people.

That may sound harsh. It is harsh. But sometimes truth requires harshness. To call a man who brags about sexual assault, foments insurrection, and commits fraud a “bad president” is understatement. To call those who still cheer for him “bad people” is simply accurate.

Conclusion: Stop Making Excuses

Trump is not the disease. He is the symptom. The disease is the willingness of millions to embrace cruelty as politics, to shrug off corruption as toughness, to defend insurrection as patriotism. That is not the mark of good people. That is the mark of bad ones.

And if that makes some Trump supporters uncomfortable, good. They should be uncomfortable. Because the mask has slipped, and the truth is staring us all in the face: supporting Trump is not a political choice. It is a moral one. And if you choose him, you have chosen wrong.
