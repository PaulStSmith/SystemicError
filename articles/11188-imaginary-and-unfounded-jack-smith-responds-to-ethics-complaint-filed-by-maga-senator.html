'Imaginary and unfounded': Jack Smith responds to ethics complaint filed by MAGA senator

Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) persuaded the Office of Special Counsel, which has no affiliation with Smith's role during Joe Biden's presidency, to investigate whether he violated the law by seeking to quickly bring Trump to trial, but Smith and his lawyers Lanny Breuer and Peter Koski challenged the allegations in a three-page letter to the acting special counsel Jamieson Greer, reported the New York Times.

“The predicate for this investigation is imaginary and unfounded,” they wrote. “Mr. Smith followed well-established legal principles in conducting the investigations into President Trump, and the courts presiding over the resulting prosecutions have already rejected the spurious allegations that the manner in which Mr. Smith prosecuted these cases was somehow improper.”

Cotton accused the former special counsel, who was appointed by former Attorney General Merrick Garland and resigned after Trump's election, of having "no rationale except for an attempt to affect the 2024 election results" with his investigations of the once and future president during Biden's presidency.

“This investigation is premised on a partisan complaint that suggests the ordinary operation of the criminal justice system should be disrupted by the whims of a political contest,” Smith's lawyers wrote. “But the notion that justice should yield to politics is antithetical to the rule of law.”

The letter noted that Greer's office has publicly confirmed the ethics investigation, but they wrote that neither Smith nor his lawyers have received any inquiries from his office, and they urged him not to reach a conclusion without engaging.

"[We] welcome the opportunity to engage with your office and are confident that as you become familiar with the facts and the record, you will conclude that there is no basis to find a violation of the Hatch Act and that these allegations are wholly without merit," the letter stated.

Ethics expert Richard Painter told the Times a Hatch Act investigation of Smith makes no sense.

“I just don’t see how this comes anywhere close to a Hatch Act violation,” Painter said. “If Smith had made public statements shortly before the election about the filings, we would have an issue that would need to be addressed. Or if he’d written Congress, it would need to be addressed. I see no evidence that Jack Smith did anything of the kind. He simply filed pleadings with the court, and the pleadings were accepted by the courts.”

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Political Theater: Unpacking the Ethics Complaints Against Jack Smith

In the latest installment of what can only be described as political theater par excellence, we find Jack Smith, a former special counsel, at the center of a storm brewed in the cauldrons of partisan politics. The allegations against him, stirred up by President Trump's allies and notably championed by Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR), are a fascinating glimpse into the mechanisms of power, the manipulation of legal systems, and the ever-present shadow of electoral politics over the scales of justice.

The Allegations: A Cloak of Partisanship

At the heart of this drama lies an ethics complaint filed against Smith, accusing him of rushing to bring Trump to trial, ostensibly to influence the 2024 election. Yet, in a detailed three-page rebuttal, Smith and his legal team, consisting of Lanny Breuer and Peter Koski, dismantle these accusations with a precision that leaves little room for doubt. They argue, quite convincingly, that the "predicate for this investigation is imaginary and unfounded."

The claim that Smith's actions were driven by a desire to meddle in the upcoming election is a narrative as thin as it is transparent. It reeks of an attempt to weaponize the Office of Special Counsel against a perceived political enemy under the guise of protecting the integrity of the electoral process. Yet, what it actually does is highlight a dangerous trend: the erosion of judicial independence and the politicization of legal accountability.

The Defense: A Fortress of Fact

Smith's defense is not merely a rebuttal but a clarion call for the preservation of the rule of law. His attorneys' response paints a picture of a man who operated within the well-established boundaries of legal principles, whose actions were vetted and validated by the courts themselves. This is not the behavior of someone looking to subvert justice for political gain; it is the conduct of a legal professional committed to the pursuit of justice, irrespective of the political winds that may howl outside the courtroom doors.

The allegations, as Smith's team points out, presuppose that the "ordinary operation of the criminal justice system" should bend to the will of political expediency. This notion is not only dangerous but fundamentally antithetical to everything the rule of law stands for. It is a stark reminder of the lengths to which some will go to shield themselves from accountability, even if it means undermining the very foundations of the justice system.

The Hatch Act: A Red Herring?

Central to the allegations against Smith is the suggestion that he may have violated the Hatch Act. Yet, as ethics expert Richard Painter points out, this line of attack is tenuous at best. The Hatch Act is designed to prevent pernicious political influence on federal employees' duties, not to serve as a cudgel against those who seek to hold the powerful to account. Smith's filings, accepted by the courts, were far removed from the kind of partisan politicking the Hatch Act is meant to curb.

Conclusion: The Real Violation

In the end, this investigation into Jack Smith appears less about ensuring ethical conduct and more about attempting to manipulate the legal system for political ends. It is a microcosm of a larger, more troubling trend: the weaponization of ethics investigations to undermine political adversaries.

The real violation here is not on the part of Jack Smith, but those who seek to use the power of their office to shield themselves from scrutiny. It is a stark reminder that in the current political climate, the pursuit of justice is not only about navigating the complexities of the law but also weathering the storms of political machinations and partisan witch hunts.

In the theater of American politics, truth often becomes the first casualty. It is incumbent upon us, the audience, to see through the spectacle and demand a performance grounded not in the pursuit of power, but in the principles of justice and the rule of law.
