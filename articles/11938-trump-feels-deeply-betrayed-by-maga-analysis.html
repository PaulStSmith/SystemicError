Trump feels 'deeply betrayed' by MAGA: analysis

President Donald Trump lied to the press on Wednesday when he dismissed reporting that White House officials would join a secret meeting to discuss how to deal with the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. As it turns out, the meeting did happen, MSNBC reported Thursday. It's likely because Trump is feeling "deeply betrayed" by his supporters who believed what he told them.

Writing on Thursday, The Atlantic's Jonathan Lemire explained that Trump spent so much time "intimating during the campaign that something was nefarious about the government’s handling of the [Epstein] case."

Now, MAGA doesn't believe him when he claims that it was all "fake news."

He's done everything he can to try and distract from the matter, from implying his government could indict former President Barack Obama to claiming he's taking over Washington, D.C., he attacked Rosie O'Donnell, cut the Department of Education by 40%, bragged he was "saved by God," got into a fight over windmills with Scotland and Republicans, announced he was suing Fox and Wall Street Journal owner Rupert Murdoch, and many many more. Nothing has worked.

"He—the president, their leader, the martyr who had endured scandals and prosecution and an assassin’s bullet on their behalf—had repeatedly told them it was time to move on, and that alone should suffice," wrote Lemire in July. "Why, he groused, would the White House add fuel to the fire, would it play into the media’s narrative?"

He recalled an interview with Steve Bannon "War Room" correspondent Natalie Winters, who told The New York Times that the Epstein case goes to the "very foundation of MAGA" because "it gets to the heart of who is in control of the country."

“I just think it’s frankly very grifty to have spent your entire career promoting, even if it weren’t the Epstein thing directly, but the idea that there is this deep state, the idea that there’s this unelected class of, you know, bankers, corporation, countries, intel agencies, blah, blah, blah,” she told the Times. "And then finally, you have the power to expose it, and either you’re not, because there’s nothing there, in which case it makes you a liar—and I don’t believe that—or you’re ineffective, or you’re compromised."

Lemire noted that Trump is even raging about MAGA influencers who he thinks used him to profit and get famous and now have abandoned him. Trump told one outside advisor that “disloyal” influencers “have forgotten whose name is above the door.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tangled Web of Lies and Distractions: The Epstein Scandal Unraveled

In a recent revelation that should surprise exactly no one, President Donald Trump was caught lying to the press about a secret meeting concerning how to navigate the choppy waters of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. This was reported by MSNBC, a day after Trump's denial, showcasing a pattern of deception that's become all too familiar.

Trump's response to the Epstein saga is a masterclass in deflection and misinformation. He has tried every trick in the book to divert attention from the issue: from ludicrous claims of taking over Washington, D.C., to suing media moguls, and even invoking divine intervention claiming he was "saved by God." Yet, none of his antics seem to have the desired effect. Why? Because the foundation of his support—the MAGA movement—feels "deeply betrayed."

Jonathan Lemire of The Atlantic provides a poignant analysis, suggesting that Trump's own rhetoric during his campaign—where he hinted at dark, nefarious dealings within the government concerning Epstein—has come back to haunt him. Now, when he cries "fake news," his base, conditioned to suspect a deep state conspiracy, doesn't buy it.

The irony is as thick as the walls of Mar-a-Lago. Trump, who has built his political identity on the notion of battling an unseen, corrupt elite, finds himself in a quagmire of his own making. Natalie Winters, speaking to The New York Times, cuts to the heart of the matter: Trump has either been ineffective in exposing this so-called deep state, or he was never honest about its existence to begin with. Either way, it's a losing situation for him.

Trump's frustration isn't just with the situation but extends to MAGA influencers, whom he accuses of using his name for their gain and then abandoning him. His lament that these "disloyal" influencers have forgotten "whose name is above the door" is a stark reminder of his transactional view of loyalty and politics.

What Does This Mean for MAGA and Trump's Legacy?

First and foremost, this episode is a glaring testament to Trump's unraveling credibility. If your political capital is built on the promise of draining the swamp, being caught in a lie about a meeting discussing a high-profile case linked to the very elite you vow to fight against is not a good look.

Moreover, it highlights a broader issue within the MAGA movement—a crisis of belief. If the foundation of MAGA is the fight against a supposed deep state, and the leader of this movement either fails to expose it or is found to be part of it, where does that leave its followers?

In the grand scheme of things, Trump's handling of the Epstein scandal—and his subsequent reaction to the fallout—may be seen as a microcosm of his presidency: full of sound and fury, signifying nothing. His legacy, rather than being one of a transformative political figure, may well be remembered for its chaos, lies, and ultimately, its failure to live up to its own mythos.

In conclusion, Trump's attempt to navigate the Epstein scandal has only served to entangle him further in a web of lies and mistrust. As the facts come to light, one can't help but wonder: how will Trump spin this tale? But more importantly, how much longer will his base follow him down this rabbit hole? Only time will tell, but as of now, the emperor's lack of clothes is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.
