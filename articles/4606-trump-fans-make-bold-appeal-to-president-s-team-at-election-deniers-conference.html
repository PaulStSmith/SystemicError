Trump fans make bold appeal to president's team at election deniers conference

President Donald Trump holds a Cabinet meeting, Wednesday, April 30, 2025, in the Cabinet Room. (Official White House Photo by Molly Riley)

A collection of Donald Trump supporters is putting pressure on members of the president’s team who appeared at an election deniers conference.

Staffers including Kurt Olsen, a White House lawyer charged with reinvestigating the 2020 election, and Heather Honey, the Department of Homeland Security official in charge of election integrity, were featured at the event organized by Michael Flynn, Trump’s former national security adviser. Photos and videos reviewed by ProPublica also confirmed the attendance of Cleta Mitchell, who directs the Election Integrity Network.

Mitchell’s group has, in the past, spread false claims about election fraud. Experts have warned that the meeting may have seen the president’s supporters put together a plan to ease possible losses in the upcoming midterm elections.

Current members of staff, including Office of the Director of National Intelligence Clay Parikh, and Mac Warner, who handled election litigation at the Justice Department, were present for a dinner that would, according to Salon, help shape the upcoming elections.

Brendan Fischer, a director at the Campaign Legal Center, a nonpartisan pro-democracy organization, warned that the meeting highlights a potential risk to the upcoming elections in November.

He said, “The meeting shows that the same people who tried to overturn the 2020 election have only grown better organized and are now embedded in the machinery of government.

“This creates substantial risk that the administration is laying the groundwork to improperly reshape elections ahead of the midterms or even go against the will of the voters.”

An unnamed White House official says federal officials’ attendance is not a sign of agreement but “common practice” for those wanting to communicate ideas with the president’s team.

Activists are believed to have left the convention convinced that Trump should take further control of the midterm elections by declaring a national emergency. Whether the president does this remains to be seen, but attendees like former Overstock.com CEO Patrick Byrne believe Trump is “playing nice” in not orchestrating a takeover.

He added, “But at some point, he’s got to do something, the muscular thing: declare a national emergency.”

Analysis: Trump Supporters’ Pressure on Election Integrity Officials

In a stark revelation that underscores the continuing saga of election denial within Trump’s orbit, a recent gathering has brought to light the concerted efforts by a faction of Donald Trump supporters aimed at influencing key members of his team. This event, attended by individuals who have previously been implicated in spreading unfounded claims about election fraud, is not just another meeting. The Cast of Characters

First, let’s dissect the attendees of this controversial rendezvous. From Kurt Olsen, a White House lawyer, to Heather Honey of the Department of Homeland Security, the lineup reads like a who’s who of Trump loyalists. These are not fringe characters; they are individuals who have been entrusted with significant responsibilities pertaining to the integrity of the nation’s elections. Their presence at an event organized by Michael Flynn, a figure synonymous with spreading conspiracy theories, is not just problematic; it’s alarming.

Cleta Mitchell, who leads the Election Integrity Network, also made her presence felt. Remember, this is the same Mitchell whose organization has been at the forefront of disseminating false claims about election fraud. The participation of such individuals in a conference that reportedly aimed at “easing possible losses in the upcoming midterm elections” is not a mere gathering. The Alarming Implications

Brendan Fischer’s warning cannot be overstated. The involvement of these figures in the machinery of government poses a “substantial risk” to the integrity of future elections. This meeting was not about ensuring that elections are free and fair. Instead, it was about consolidating power and potentially setting the stage for disputing unfavorable outcomes in the midterms.

The unnamed White House official’s attempt to downplay federal officials’ attendance as “common practice” is a weak deflection. When the attendees are linked to efforts to subvert democratic processes, their presence is not just about exchanging ideas. It’s about endorsing a dangerous agenda.

Perhaps the most chilling aspect of this entire episode is the push for Trump to declare a national emergency to take control of the midterm elections. The audacity of such a suggestion cannot be understated. It represents a blatant disregard for democratic principles and an outright flirtation with authoritarianism.
