Cosplaying as a cop: Former Trump lawyer's latest push is embarrassing

The Trump lawyer who is now New Jersey’s acting U.S. attorney ratcheted up the buffoonery this week with her claim that she will investigate our governor and attorney general.

Alina Habba appeared on Sean Hannity’s Fox News show Thursday to say she’s opening an investigation into Gov. Phil Murphy and Attorney General Matt Platkin over the Immigrant Trust Directive, a state order that bars state and local police officers from becoming de facto immigration agents.

“We are to take all criminal, violent criminals and criminals, out of this country and completely enforce federal law, and anybody who does get in that way, in the way of what we are doing, which is not political, it is simply against crime, will be charged in the state of New Jersey for obstruction, for concealment, and I will come after them hard. Those investigations will start immediately,” Habba told Hannity.

A normal prosecutor would probably keep this under wraps so her targets wouldn’t know from minute one that they’re being investigated. But for all we know, there is no actual investigation. Habba, clearly reveling in the attention this high-profile appointment has granted her, wanted to say something that would make headlines, so she did. I wouldn’t be surprised if her on-air threat is as far as this alleged investigation goes.

That Habba is targeting Murphy and Platkin for the Immigrant Trust Directive is not a surprise. Anti-immigration hard-liners like Habba have twisted efforts like the directive to claim that it does something it does not. It does not bar police from working with immigrant agents to arrest suspected criminals. What it does is prevent local cops from seeing a brown person on the street and asking for their papers. But the facts about the directive are not important for Habba; what’s important is she can use it to promise retribution for her — and President Trump’s — political foes.

Multiple judges have rejected legal challenges to the Immigrant Trust Directive, including one judge appointed by President Trump himself.

Murphy was asked about Habba’s claim on a podcast recorded Friday. His response was fairly muted considering Habba basically threatened to arrest him, but at least he defended his administration’s efforts to protect the countless immigrants living in New Jersey who have done nothing wrong.

“We go after criminals hard, period, full stop, regardless of their immigration status. What we don’t do is we don’t go after somebody for jaywalking or where there’s no probable cause of a crime. And we’re very clear about that,” Murphy said.

Platkin, speaking at an unrelated press conference in Newark, was a bit more knives-out when asked about Habba.

“I hope she starts doing her job,” Platkin said.

Seems unlikely. The U.S. attorney gig is a plum appointment, but it’s probably a bit of a slog overseeing nearly 200 attorneys and handling some complicated cases. That’s probably why we didn’t see a lot of Habba’s predecessors, Craig Carpenito and Philip Sellinger, yet Habba is out here cosplaying as a cop for the New York Post and targeting Democrats on Fox News. That sounds a lot more fun than doing work.

Amy Torres, executive director of the New Jersey Alliance for Immigrant Justice, told me Habba’s threat against Murphy and Platkin is in line with the Trump administration’s focus on TV ratings and viral social media posts rather than governing. It’s also part of a broader attempt by the administration to make sure their opponents know they could end up in jail or, if possible, deported.

“They’ll go after political opponents, whether those are individuals, whether those are student protestors, whether those are institutions like law firms and universities, or whether those are state governments,” Torres said

Our Analysis:

The Theatrical Crusade Against Immigration: A Closer Look at Alina Habba's Performance

In a recent spectacle that blurs the lines between legal action and political theater, Alina Habba, a Trump-affiliated lawyer turned New Jersey’s acting U.S. attorney, has thrust herself into the spotlight with declarations that veer on the edge of the absurd. Habba, in a performance worthy of prime-time news, announced on Sean Hannity’s Fox News show her intent to launch investigations against New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy and Attorney General Matt Platkin over the Immigrant Trust Directive. This directive, it's crucial to note, is a policy designed to prevent state and local police from being commandeered into federal immigration enforcement roles.

Habba’s claims, ostensibly draped in the guise of a crusade against crime, unravel under even the most cursory examination. Her assertion that she will “come after them hard” for obstructing federal law enforcement is as misguided as it is misinformed. Notably, her understanding (or lack thereof) of the Immigrant Trust Directive is emblematic of a broader trend among anti-immigration hard-liners: a willful distortion of policy to serve political ends. The directive does not, contrary to Habba’s implications, provide a shield for criminals; rather, it ensures local law enforcement is not diverted from its primary role by federal immigration concerns.

Legal Theater Over Substance

What stands out in Habba’s declaration is not just the misrepresentation of policy but the spectacle of the announcement itself. A seasoned prosecutor might opt for discretion, allowing investigations to unfold away from the public eye. Yet, Habba chose the route of public spectacle, sharing her plans with a national audience on Fox News. This raises questions about the sincerity of the investigation and whether it is merely a performative act designed to garner headlines and curry favor with political allies.

A Pattern of Political Retribution

This move by Habba is hardly an isolated incident but rather a continuation of a disturbing pattern within certain factions of the political landscape: the use of legal mechanisms as tools of political retribution. The targeting of Governor Murphy and Attorney General Platkin through a distorted interpretation of the Immigrant Trust Directive is a stark illustration of this tactic. It's an approach that seeks to vilify immigrants and cast policies aimed at protecting their rights as obstacles to law enforcement.

A Misguided Focus

The real tragedy here is the misallocation of resources and focus. New Jersey, like any jurisdiction, faces genuine challenges in crime prevention and law enforcement. Yet, Habba’s prioritization of high-profile, politically motivated investigations diverts attention and resources from the pressing issues at hand. It's a classic case of political theater overshadowing substantive policy-making and law enforcement work.

Conclusion

Alina Habba’s recent actions and statements represent a troubling confluence of politics, misinformation, and legal authority. By misrepresenting the Immigrant Trust Directive and announcing investigations against political opponents on national television, Habba has not only undermined the integrity of her office but has also diverted critical focus from the real issues facing New Jersey’s law enforcement community. In doing so, she contributes to a polarized and distorted discourse on immigration and law enforcement, one that prioritizes spectacle over substance and political retribution over genuine public safety concerns.

It’s imperative for the public and policymakers alike to see through this charade and demand a return to principled, effective governance that addresses the real needs of the community rather than engaging in political score-settling.
