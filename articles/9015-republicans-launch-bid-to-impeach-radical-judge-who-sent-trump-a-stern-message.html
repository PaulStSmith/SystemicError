A Republican lawmaker is pushing to impeach President Donald Trump‘s latest enemy — a “radical activist” judge the DOJ has previously targeted — but failed after a clerical error put a stop to the attack.

Rep. Brandon Gill (R-TX) announced Tuesday in a social post that he filed articles of impeachment against U.S. District Judge James Boasberg and his alleged role in the FBI’s “Arctic Frost” investigation under former special counsel Jack Smith. Boasberg reportedly issued nondisclosure orders in Smith’s investigation.

“Judge Boasberg abused his power by weaponizing the judiciary against critics of the Biden Administration,” Gill wrote. “As part of the Arctic Frost Investigation, Judge Boasberg signed off on frivolous nondisclosure orders to conceal the fact that sitting US Senators were being surveilled. Not only was this action egregiously improper; it was a gross violation of the separation of powers.
Judge Boasberg is guilty of high crimes and misdemeanors, is unfit for office, and should be impeached.”

Rep. Andy Biggs (R-AZ) announced that he is a co-sponsor on X, saying, “Boasberg must be brought to account for his actions.”

Boasberg “dramatically scolded” Department of Justice officials in March, sending a “message” to the entire administration that their continued challenges to the U.S. judicial system “is not acceptable.” The judge reportedly issued the takedown of deputy assistant attorney general Drew Ensign “over the government’s conduct in the case” as the DOJ continued to defend the administration’s failure to comply with a court order to return flights carrying alleged Venezuelan gang members to a prison in El Salvador.

At the time, Trump responded in a social post on his Truth Social platform and argued that Boasberg was trying to usurp his presidential authority.

“No District Court Judge, or any Judge, can assume the duties of the President of the United States. Only Crime and Chaos would result,” Trump wrote.

Our Analysis:

Impeachment Crusade: A Tale of Political Theater

In a bold move that reeks of political vendetta rather than a quest for justice, Rep. Brandon Gill (R-TX) has launched an impeachment tirade against U.S. District Judge James Boasberg, branding him a “radical activist.” This move, ostensibly in response to Boasberg’s actions in the FBI’s “Arctic Frost” investigation, underlines a dangerous trend of weaponizing impeachment for partisan gain.

The Allegations: A Thin Veil Over Political Motivations

Gill’s accusations hinge on Boasberg’s issuance of nondisclosure orders during the investigation led by former special counsel Jack Smith. According to Gill, these orders were a blatant abuse of power, aimed at shielding the surveillance of sitting US Senators from public scrutiny. Yet, this narrative conveniently omits a critical analysis of the nondisclosure orders’ legal grounding or necessity within the scope of the investigation.

The claim that Boasberg’s actions represent a “gross violation of the separation of powers” is, at best, a dramatic overstatement. It disregards the judiciary’s role in overseeing and, when necessary, authorizing surveillance activities, especially within the context of national security or criminal investigations.

The Response: Echoes of Partisan Echo Chambers

Rep. Andy Biggs (R-AZ)’s quick jump on the impeachment bandwagon, without providing substantial evidence or legal rationale, further illustrates the partisan underpinnings of this initiative. It’s a clear echo of the political echo chamber, where the soundness of legal arguments often gives way to the loudness of political rhetoric.

Boasberg’s “Crime”: Upholding Judicial Independence

Ironically, the actions for which Boasberg is now being vilified—specifically, his rebuke of the Department of Justice’s conduct—highlight his commitment to judicial independence rather than a usurpation of presidential authority. His scolding of DOJ officials for their defense of administrative failures to comply with court orders is not an overreach but a necessary check on executive power.

President Trump’s response to Boasberg’s actions, claiming an encroachment on presidential authority, is a classic example of conflating the enforcement of judicial orders with an attack on executive prerogatives. This misrepresentation is not just misleading; it’s a dangerous undermining of judicial authority and its critical role in our system of checks and balances.

Conclusion: The Real “High Crimes and Misdemeanors”

In this ongoing saga of political maneuvering, the real “high crimes and misdemeanors” may not lie with Judge Boasberg but with the attempts to impeach him based on flimsy, politically motivated arguments. This episode should alarm anyone concerned with the integrity of our political and judicial systems. It’s not just an attack on a single judge but a frontal assault on the very principles of judicial independence and the separation of powers.

In the end, this impeachment push may fade into the background noise of partisan politics, but its implications for the rule of law and democratic governance are far-reaching and deeply concerning. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant against such baseless attacks and stand in defense of the institutions that underpin our society.
