Trump's DHS chief and border czar not speaking to each other as angry rift grows: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Fractured Front of Trump’s Homeland Security: A Closer Look

Tom Homan, who was appointed “border czar” by U.S. President Donald Trump, speaks to the media outside the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 10, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

In yet another sign of breakdown within President Donald Trump’s Homeland Security operations, Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and White House “border czar” Tom Homan have fallen out, and are barely even speaking to each other, Axios reported on Thursday.

Homan, a former acting director of Immigration and Customs Enforcement, has been a public face of Trump’s immigration policies and caused a stir by repeatedly threatening the arrest of mayors and Democratic officials who don’t cooperate with federal agents — though even he tried to cool down the tension when Trump threatened to arrest California Gov. Gavin Newsom in the summer. He has also been investigated for taking a $50,000 bribe from undercover FBI agents, although nothing appears to have come of it.

This comes after initial reporting that the Trump administration was considering removing Noem as head of DHS, possibly at the direction of White House adviser Stephen Miller — which the administration has thoroughly denied, and which it turns out may have been a rumor planted by Homan’s allies as the feud between them grows.

While “Noem’s backers blamed the whisper campaign,” on Homan’s associates, they “deny any involvement,” said the report. “Neither Noem nor Homan are going anywhere, according to administration officials inside and outside the White House. Trump has shown no signs of wanting a change.”

Trump is aware of the enmity between his two key immigration officials, the report said, and it doesn’t bother him. One source told Axios, “Trump is like that. He kind of likes people competing against each other. He thinks it makes the product better.”

As all of this is going on, however, other Trump administration insiders are frustrated with Noem, both for using government resources to promote her own political brand, and farming out so many decisions to Trump campaign strategist Corey Lewandowski, with whom she has long been rumored to be having a romantic affair.

Our Analysis:

The Fractured Front of Trump’s Homeland Security: A Closer Look

In the latest circus act that is the Trump administration’s handling of Homeland Security, we find ourselves witnessing an almost Shakespearian drama unfold. Kristi Noem, the Secretary of the Department of Homeland Security, and Tom Homan, the so-called “border czar,” are at the heart of this tragicomedy, having apparently decided that speaking to one another is beneath them. This revelation, courtesy of Axios, is not just a petty squabble but a symptom of a deeper malaise within the administration.

Homan, a figure who has not shied away from controversy, has been the bulldog of Trump’s immigration policies. His threats to arrest Democratic mayors and officials who dare to defy federal agents’ whims have been both alarming and absurd. His recent attempt to dial back the animosity—after Trump’s outlandish threat to arrest California Governor Gavin Newsom—reads more like a bad attempt at damage control than a genuine change of heart. And let’s not gloss over the fact that Homan was investigated for allegedly taking a $50,000 bribe from undercover FBI agents. Though “nothing appears to have come of it,” this tidbit deliciously underscores the ethical quagmire that seems to be a prerequisite for Trump appointees.

The plot thickens with reports that Noem’s tenure as DHS head was under threat, supposedly at the behest of Stephen Miller, another contentious figure in Trump’s orbit. This rumor, potentially the handiwork of Homan’s allies, adds a layer of intrigue and backstabbing worthy of a political thriller. Noem’s camp has pointed fingers at Homan’s supporters for this smear campaign, though, predictably, they have denied any involvement. Amidst these accusations and denials, the administration insists that both Noem and Homan are here to stay, with Trump seemingly unbothered—or perhaps, amused—by the discord among his ranks.

Trump’s apparent endorsement of infighting, under the misguided belief that it “makes the product better,” is a bewildering management strategy that would be laughable if the stakes weren’t so high. The notion that internal competition is beneficial in a setting that demands cohesion and collaboration for national security is not just flawed; it’s dangerous.

Adding another layer of controversy is the criticism of Noem for her alleged misuse of government resources to further her political ambitions and her decision to delegate numerous responsibilities to Trump campaign strategist Corey Lewandowski. The rumors of a romantic involvement between Noem and Lewandowski only serve to complicate the already murky waters of her professional conduct and decision-making.

In sum, this episode is a glaring reminder of the chaotic and dysfunctional nature of the Trump administration’s approach to governance and Homeland Security. It’s a saga of personal vendettas, ethical ambiguities, and political maneuvering that undermines the very essence of public service and national safety. As these power plays continue to unfold with no concern for the collateral damage, one can’t help but wonder: at what point does the circus cease to be entertaining and start being a national security risk?
