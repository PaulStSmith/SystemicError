'I’m gonna say break it apart': Republicans struggling with Trump’s 'beautiful' megabill

By Paulo Santos

Sen. Lindsey Graham of South Carolina on January 16, 2025 (Maxim Elramsisy/Shutterstock.com)

President Donald Trump is expecting GOP lawmakers, who have small majorities in both houses of Congress, to pass a "big, beautiful bill" — which will combine his policy priorities — and get it onto his desk for signature.

But Republicans in Congress don't necessarily see eye to when it comes to specifics. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-Louisiana) is fighting with members of his caucus, and Senate Republicans have their own ideas on the form a megabill should take.

In an article published by Politico on April 14, reporters Jordain Carney and Meredith Lee Hill describe the struggles of putting a GOP megabill passed.

"They know it's going to be big," Carney and Hill explain. "They want it to be beautiful. Now, congressional Republicans need to decide what's going to be in it — and they're confronting the very real possibility they might not be able to figure it out. A Thursday, (April 10) House vote might have finalized a fiscal framework for the GOP's domestic policy megabill, but completing that intermediate step exposed huge fissures between the House and Senate over a range of issues crucial to finishing the sprawling legislation that's expected to span tax cuts, border security, energy and more."

Sen. John Kennedy (R-Louisiana) described the challenges of putting together a GOP megabill and getting it passed.

Kennedy told Politico, "I know the dialectic is supposed to produce a final result, but sometimes it doesn't — many times up here, it doesn't. We can spend an entire year getting nothing done."

Senate Budget Chairman Lindsey Graham (R-South Carolina) is urging fellow Republicans to get something passed sooner rather than later — even if it doesn't include everything Trump wants it to.

"If this thing drags out too long," Graham told Politico, "I’m gonna say break it apart."

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Legislative Labyrinth: Trump's "Big, Beautiful Bill" Faces an Uphill Battle

The latest spectacle from the GOP's camp, as detailed by Politico's Jordain Carney and Meredith Lee Hill, reads less like a coherent strategy to legislate and more like a script torn from a political theater of the absurd. President Donald Trump's dream of a "big, beautiful bill" that encapsulates all his policy priorities is facing not just an uphill battle but a veritable mountain range of GOP discord and legislative disarray.

The Disunited State of the GOP

At the heart of this saga is the glaring disunity within the Republican ranks. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-Louisiana) finds himself locked in a struggle not against the opposition, but with members of his own caucus. Meanwhile, Senate Republicans are dancing to a different tune altogether, each with their own vision of what this megabill should entail. This isn't just a rift; it's a chasm that threatens to swallow any hope of cohesive policy-making.

The Illusion of Progress

The Politico article paints a vivid picture of a party at war with itself. A vote on Thursday, April 10, supposedly moved the fiscal framework for this domestic policy megabill forward, but instead, it merely highlighted the "huge fissures" between the House and Senate. We're talking about a legislative effort that spans from tax cuts to border security, and energy, and beyond. Yet, the real story here is not about the substance of the bill but the inability of the GOP to unify behind a common agenda.

The Voices of Reason(?) in the Chaos

Senator John Kennedy (R-Louisiana) offers a moment of candid reflection amidst the cacophony, acknowledging the dialectic's failure to yield results. It's a rare admission of legislative impotence that's all too common in today's political landscape. On the other side, you have Senate Budget Chairman Lindsey Graham (R-South Carolina), who seems to be one of the few voices of pragmatism, urging his colleagues to pass something before the entire endeavor collapses under its own weight. Graham's willingness to compromise — even if it means abandoning some of Trump's wishlist — underscores the desperation at play.

The Bottom Line

What we're witnessing is not governance; it's a spectacle. The GOP's internal divisions are so pronounced that even the basic task of deciding what to include in a legislative package has become a Sisyphean task. Trump's call for a "big, beautiful bill" is more fantasy than feasible policy initiative, a point that's lost on nobody, perhaps, except the President himself.

As the GOP struggles to navigate its own ideological battlegrounds, the real casualties are the American people, left waiting for substantive policy that addresses their needs. The promise of a megabill that tackles everything from tax cuts to energy policy is a tantalizing one. Yet, as it stands, this promise is nothing more than a mirage, shimmering in the distance but ever out of reach.

In their report, Carney and Hill have not just chronicled the challenges of putting together a GOP megabill; they've laid bare the systemic failures of a party mired in conflict and contradiction. The question now is not whether the GOP can deliver on Trump's lofty ambitions, but whether it can overcome its divisions to legislate at all. As the saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the path forward is fraught with more questions than answers, and the clock is ticking.
