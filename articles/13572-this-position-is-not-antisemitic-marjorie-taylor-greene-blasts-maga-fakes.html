'This position is NOT antisemitic': Marjorie Taylor Greene blasts MAGA 'fakes'

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) on Sunday insisted a position she is taking is "NOT antisemitic."

Greene, who has been accused of antisemitism related to her prior comments, took to X over the weekend to declare that some people are showing they aren't "real" America First MAGA patriots.

"Everyone is finding out who are real America First/MAGA and who were fake and just said it because it was popular. Unfortunately the list of fakes are becoming quite long and exposed themselves quickly," she said. "Anyone slobbering for the U.S. to become fully involved in the Israel/Iran war is not America First/MAGA."

She continued, saying, "Wishing for murder of innocent people is disgusting. We are sick and tired of foreign wars. All of them. And this one will quickly engulf the Middle East, BRICS, and NATO as countries are required to take a side. Real America First/MAGA wants world peace for all people and doesn’t want our military killed and forever injured physically and mentally."

"We love our U.S. military and love them helping to secure our borders and our cities for the defense of OUR PEOPLE AND OUR COUNTRY. They are our friends, spouses, cousins, nieces, nephews, sisters, brothers, sons, and daughters. We have spent TRILLIONS in the Middle East and we have dealt with the aftermath of death, blown apart bodies, never ending suicides, and disabling PTSD," she added. "All because they told us propaganda as to why we must sacrifice our own to defend some other country’s borders and some other country’s borders. I don’t want to see Israel bombed or Iran bombed or Gaza bombed. I don’t want to see Ukraine bombed or Russia bombed. And we do NOT want to be involved or required to pay for ANY OF IT!!! We are $36+ TRILLION in debt and have mountains of our own problems. We have giant planks sticking out of our own eyes while we complain about splinters in other’s eyes."

She went on to say that, "Every country involved and all over the world can be happy, successful, and rich if we all work together and seek peace and prosperity."

"Taking this position is NOT antisemitic," the lawmaker insisted. "It’s rational, sane, and loving toward all people. Taking this position of peace and prosperity for all is not isolationism, it leads to GREAT trade deals and GREAT economies that help ALL PEOPLE. It’s what many Americans voted for in 2024. America First! America First!! America First!!! That is how we Make America Great Again!!!!!"

For his part, Donald Trump has left the door open to becoming more involved in Israel's assault on Iran, and said that "America First" means what he says it means.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Cloaking Prejudice: Marjorie Taylor Greene's Rhetorical Gymnastics

In what can only be described as a tour de force of rhetorical contortionism, U.S. Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) has once again ignited the public discourse with claims that her position on the Israel/Iran conflict is "NOT antisemitic." Greene, a figure no stranger to controversy and accusations of prejudice, has taken to the social media platform X to delineate her stance, intertwining her argument with the broader "America First/MAGA" ideology. Her assertions demand a critical examination, not only of their factual accuracy but of the underlying implications they carry.

"America First" or Isolationism in Disguise?

Greene's proclamation that wishing for the U.S. to become fully involved in the Israel/Iran war is antithetical to "America First/MAGA" principles is a curious conflation of isolationism with patriotism. While advocating for peace and expressing weariness of foreign wars can be legitimate positions, Greene's framing is peculiarly selective. Her emphasis on the U.S. avoiding involvement in specific international conflicts, notably those involving Israel and Iran, while simultaneously professing a love for military defense of "OUR PEOPLE AND OUR COUNTRY," raises questions about the consistency and sincerity of her "America First" advocacy.

The Economics of Prejudice

One of the more insidious aspects of Greene's argument lies in her manipulation of economic rhetoric to mask a potentially deeper prejudice. By highlighting the U.S.'s $36+ trillion debt and the financial burdens of military involvement abroad, Greene attempts to appeal to the fiscal sensibilities of her audience. However, this economic concern conveniently aligns with a broader narrative that seeks to minimize U.S. engagement with countries like Israel, Iran, Ukraine, and Russia—nations currently embroiled in geopolitical struggles with significant global implications.

The False Equivalence of Peace

Greene's assertion that her stance is "rational, sane, and loving toward all people" and not antisemitic is a masterclass in false equivalence. Advocating for peace and prosperity is a noble endeavor, but when such advocacy selectively targets involvement in conflicts that affect Jewish and Muslim-majority countries while cloaked in the rhetoric of "America First," it demands scrutiny. The simplification of complex international relations into a binary of peace-loving patriotism versus warmongering interventionism is not only intellectually dishonest; it's dangerously reductive.

The Trump Conundrum

Adding to the convoluted narrative is Donald Trump's ambiguous stance on the matter. His assertion that "America First" means whatever he decides it means adds a layer of unpredictability and personal whim to a policy position that should be grounded in consistent principles and informed by geopolitical realities. This capriciousness only serves to further muddle the waters of an already murky discourse.

Conclusion: A Cloak of Plausible Deniability

In conclusion, Marjorie Taylor Greene's statements represent a calculated attempt to exploit economic anxieties, isolationist sentiments, and the complex nature of international diplomacy to advance a specific ideological agenda. By wrapping her arguments in the flag of "America First" patriotism and economic rationality, Greene seeks to establish a veneer of plausible deniability for her positions. However, the selective application of these principles, coupled with a history of controversial remarks, suggests a deeper, more troubling undercurrent of prejudice. It's essential for the public discourse to critically examine and challenge such rhetoric, ensuring that the cloak of patriotism is not used to obscure underlying biases and divisive ideologies.
