Red flags fly after conservative harassment network targeting teachers exposed: 'Chilling'

Based on an article by Matthew Chapman

The Conservative Surveillance State Has Found Academia

A Deliberate Campaign

Columbia’s Tow Center describes a coordinated right-wing effort to pry into universities with hidden cameras and mass public-records requests, then use the resulting outrage to ruin careers. Accuracy in Media, Heritage’s Oversight Project, and Metric Media have flooded campuses with FOIA demands and undercover sting operations; the reported fallout includes firings, forced resignations, and a wrongful-termination lawsuit after a secretly filmed Texas A&M lecture on gender identity.

Power, Not Transparency

This is not a search for truth. It is a use of institutional force by actors who know exactly what they are doing. The relevant power sits with the network of conservative groups, their donors, and the public officials whose politics make this campaign possible. Metric Media’s 9,000-plus FOIA requests across all 50 states are not normal oversight. They are an industrialized harassment tactic dressed up as civic virtue.

The Real Audience Is Not Parents

Brian Timpone’s line about parents “knowing what they are paying professors to teach” gives the game away. The point is not to inform families. It is to build a permission structure for punishing faculty whose work touches race, gender, indigeneity, or any other subject that right-wing activists want disciplined out of public life. The hidden-camera clips are bait. The real product is fear.

Bureaucratic Cowardice Does the Rest

The story is also about how quickly universities fold. At Texas A&M, the fallout did not stop with one lecturer. McCoul, two staffers, and the university’s own president all lost their jobs. That is not accountability; it is institutional panic. Administrators repeatedly choose the shortest route to containing controversy, even when that means handing victory to a political operation built around intimidation.

Misdirection As Method

The framing of these campaigns as “transparency” is a lie designed to obscure the actual mechanism: selective surveillance aimed at a political enemy. The supposed concern is classroom content. The actual effect is to make professors calculate every word before they say it. Joan Scott’s point is the key one: the chill is not collateral damage. It is the objective.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics advances in institutions it does not formally control: by weaponizing paperwork, surveillance, and media mimicry until public-service institutions start policing themselves. The targets are higher education today because universities are one of the last places where anti-authoritarian, anti-racist, and gender-critical ideas can still be taught without asking permission from the right. The response cannot be technocratic cleanup. It has to be a refusal to treat organized political harassment as ordinary oversight.
