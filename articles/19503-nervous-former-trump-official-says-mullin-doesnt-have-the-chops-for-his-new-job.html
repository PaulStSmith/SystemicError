Kevin Carroll, the former Homeland Security senior counselor in President Donald Trump’s first term, says Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-Okla.) is not at all ready for his new role as head of Homeland Security.

On Thursday, Trump announced that Mullin will become the new Secretary of Homeland Security, replacing embattled Kristi Noem. But Carroll, who served under former Homeland Security Director John Kelly, said this is not the time to fill such as large security agency with a politician.

“I’m not sure that Senator Mullin is really qualified. I mean, most of the other secretaries of Homeland Security have had substantial experience in federal law enforcement or the military, or have held senior executive positions,” said Carroll. “He’s an impressive athlete. He was a successful, small businessman. But we’re in a severe threat environment right now [with the invasion of Iran]. It’s probably the highest threat environment since 9/11 … I really don’t think it’s time for him to be in his first national security position or his first executive position.”

“What about having been a member of congress and a senator?” asked CNN anchor Brianna Keilar.

But Carroll said Mullin’s lawmaker skills and his background in mixed martial arts hardly qualified him, particularly considering the last person thrown from the job — Noem — also served as a politician.

“If he’d been through a period of doing intensive oversight and investigations, work into DHS, that might help. But really, considering the counter-terrorism threat environment right now, I’m worried about somebody learning the ropes as the head of the third biggest department of government,” said Carroll.

“She was inexperienced and it didn’t go well for her,” Carroll added. “And I really think in that role, you need somebody who’s had significant national security experience, significant executive experience which you just don’t get as a legislator. … There are certainly Lebanese Hezbollah operatives in the country … And we really need to be at the top of our game right now. I mean, we’ve decapitated the [Iranian] regime. We’ve called for proxy forces to go in and carve up the country. The QUDS Force has absolutely no reason to hold back right now.”

Our Analysis:

Mullin’s Qualifications: A Misstep in Homeland Security Leadership?

In what appears to be a controversial decision by President Donald Trump, Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-Okla.) has been announced as the new Secretary of Homeland Security, stepping into a role vacated by Kristi Noem. This move has sparked immediate criticism, notably from Kevin Carroll, a former Homeland Security senior counselor during Trump’s first term. Carroll’s skepticism about Mullin’s qualifications underscores a broader concern: Is political loyalty trumping expertise in critical national security positions?

The Qualifications Debate

Carroll’s critique of Mullin’s appointment is scathing and straightforward. He points out that most predecessors in the role brought substantial experience in federal law enforcement, the military, or senior executive positions—areas where Mullin is notably lacking. Mullin, despite his accomplishments as a “successful, small businessman” and an “impressive athlete,” does not bring the crucial national security or executive leadership experience to the table. This gap is particularly concerning given the current “severe threat environment,” likened to the post-9/11 era due to the invasion of Iran.

The argument here isn’t just about experience for experience’s sake. It’s about the specific, nuanced understanding of federal law enforcement and national security operations required to navigate and mitigate the complex threats the United States currently faces. The mention of Lebanese Hezbollah operatives in the country and the decapitation of the Iranian regime only add layers to the already dense security landscape that Mullin is expected to manage.

The Politician in a Security Role: A Pattern of Concern

This isn’t the first time Trump has selected a politician for a role typically reserved for those with substantial security or military backgrounds. Kristi Noem’s tenure as Secretary of Homeland Security was evidently deemed unsuccessful by Carroll, who highlights the importance of “significant national security experience” and “significant executive experience” for the role—attributes that cannot be acquired through legislative work alone.

Carroll’s concerns raise essential questions about the criteria for selecting individuals for critical national security positions. Is the Trump administration privileging political loyalty or alignment over the necessary qualifications and experience? And at what cost to national security?

The Underlying Issue: National Security at Stake

The decision to appoint Mullin, especially amidst a heightened threat environment, is not just a matter of filling a vacancy. It’s a reflection of how the Trump administration prioritizes qualifications and expertise in national security roles. Carroll’s apprehensions suggest a potential underestimation of the complexities involved in leading the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), the third largest department in the U.S. government, tasked with nothing less than the safety and security of the country.

Mullin’s appointment could be seen as a risky gamble, one that places immense pressure on an individual without the traditional background or experience in national security or executive leadership. While Mullin’s past achievements are not to be dismissed lightly, the leap from business and athletics to the helm of the DHS in a time of unprecedented threats is a monumental one.

Conclusion: A Call for Reevaluation

The crux of Carroll’s argument—and indeed, the concern of many watching this unfold—is that national security is too critical an area to serve as a testing ground for political appointees lacking in relevant experience. As the United States faces significant internal and external threats, the leadership of the DHS should not be a position awarded on any basis other than the ability to effectively protect the nation.

In this light, Mullin’s appointment is not just a matter of personal qualification but a reflection of broader decision-making processes within the Trump administration. It underscores the need for a reevaluation of how national security roles are filled, emphasizing expertise, experience, and a deep understanding of the complex security landscape over political considerations. The safety of the nation depends on it.
