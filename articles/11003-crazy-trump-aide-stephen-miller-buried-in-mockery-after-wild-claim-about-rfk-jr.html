'Crazy': Trump aide Stephen Miller buried in mockery after wild claim about RFK Jr.

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Appointment of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.: A Mire of Incompetence and Conspiracy

White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller speaks to reporters on the West Wing driveway at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 29, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy Jr. — an environmental lawyer, former leader of a children’s anti-vaccine organization, and a promoter of conspiracy theories — is being praised by White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller as a “foremost” global health expert and a “crown jewel” of the Trump administration.

Kennedy has no medical degree or formal training, nor does he hold any degrees in public health.

Kennedy’s challenges this week include his attempt to fire the newly confirmed director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and announcing that most Americans will not be eligible to receive COVID vaccines without a doctor’s prescription and at least one underlying health condition. (Future CDC advisory panel regulations may alter that landscape.)

Kennedy was assailed by medical experts this week when he declared that, while walking through an airport, he could see the “mitochondrial” illness and inflammation of children, which he claimed he could detect “from their faces, from their body movements and from their lack of social connection.”

READ MORE: ‘Glass Jaws’: Democrats Cast Ernst Exit as Harbinger of Weakening GOP

Miller, who also holds no medical degree, told reporters on Friday (video below) that “the CDC’s credibility was shattered during the COVID era.”

“CDC used to be, of course, seen widely around the world as a premier health agency, and much of the world discovered in the last few years, that CDC was actually staffed by a lot of very partisan, and very political bureaucrats who weren’t at all concerned about public health and weren’t actually very knowledgeable about public health,” he baselessly alleged.

“And we are working hard, and more importantly, Secretary Kennedy — one of the world’s foremost voices, advocates, and experts on public health — is working hard to restore the credibility and the integrity of CDC as a scientific organization committed to the scientific method, and getting to the root causes of the public health epidemic in this country,” Miller continued.

READ MORE: Johnson Pins Gun Violence on ‘Mental Health’ After Trump Slashes $1B in School Counseling

Asked if there are any concerns about Kennedy’s leadership, and despite the resignations this week of top CDC scientists in response to the president’s firing of the CDC director, Miller declared, “Secretary Kennedy has been a crown jewel of this administration who’s working tirelessly to improve public health for all Americans.”

Critics blasted Miller.

“Calling RFK Jr. ‘one of the world’s foremost experts on public health’ with a straight face is crazy,” wrote The Lincoln Project.

“I’m a an MD, PhD, physician toxicologist and drug developer. This is the biggest pile of horse-s– I have seen in months of horses–,” declared Peter H Proctor MD, PhD.

Our Analysis:

The Appointment of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.: A Mire of Incompetence and Conspiracy

In a move that can only be described as a political farce, Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy Jr., known for his anti-vaccine rhetoric and lack of any formal medical or public health training, has been lauded by White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller as a “foremost global health expert” and the “crown jewel” of the Trump administration. This commendation is not just a stretch; it's a leap into the abyss of absurdity.

Kennedy, whose credentials are as thin as rice paper when it comes to public health, has recently attempted to fire the newly confirmed director of the CDC and announced restrictive measures on COVID vaccine eligibility that could only be described as antithetical to public health best practices. His claim of being able to diagnose “mitochondrial illness” in children by merely looking at them is not just scientifically baseless, but it borders on the fantastical.

Stephen Miller's attack on the CDC’s credibility is a masterclass in misinformation. His statement that the CDC was staffed by “partisan and political bureaucrats” is not just baseless but ironic, considering the political nature of Kennedy’s appointment. Miller's unfounded allegations serve only to undermine public trust in one of the most critical institutions in the fight against diseases.

The praise showered on Kennedy by Miller, particularly the description of him as a “foremost expert” on public health, is not just misleading; it's a dangerous precedent. It suggests that expertise and knowledge are of little value, and that political loyalty trumps the need for qualified individuals in critical roles, especially during a global health crisis.

The reactions from the medical community highlight the gravity of the situation. The statement from Peter H. Proctor, MD, PhD, branding Miller’s and Kennedy’s assertions as the “biggest pile of horse-s—” he has seen, underscores the frustration and disbelief among healthcare professionals. When those at the forefront of medicine and research are appalled, it’s a clear indicator that the administration has veered far from logic and reason.

In endorsing Kennedy, the administration is not just making a mockery of the position he holds; it's endangering public health. The resignations of top CDC scientists in response to these developments are a somber reminder that the repercussions of such appointments are far-reaching, impacting not just the credibility of the CDC but the overall health and well-being of the American public.

Stephen Miller's portrayal of Kennedy as a “crown jewel” is not just a gross misrepresentation, but it's a stark reflection of an administration that values loyalty over expertise, conspiracy over science, and personal gain over public good. The appointment of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is a systemic error, one that underscores the need for a rigorous reevaluation of the criteria for selecting individuals for critical public health roles.
