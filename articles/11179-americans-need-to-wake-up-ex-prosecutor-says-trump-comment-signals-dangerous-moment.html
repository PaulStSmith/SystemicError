'Americans need to wake up': Ex-prosecutor says Trump comment signals 'dangerous moment'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

"We are in a dangerous moment," and Donald Trump just signaled that, according to a former federal prosecutor.

Ex-prosecutor Joyce Vance, who also serves as a legal analyst for journalists, frequently discusses Trump's actions, especially when they crossover with the legal world. However, Trump's recent comment about how some Americans want a dictator caused the attorney to switch up her tone as she informed readers.

Declaring that "this administration is on the dictator track," Vance reported, "In an Oval Office ramble Monday, Trump, after calling Chicago a killing field, stumbled into saying 'a lot of people are saying maybe we’d like a dictator. I don’t like a dictator. I’m not a dictator. I’m a man with great common sense and a smart person.'"

This quote along with other recent Trump actions caused the lawyer to issue a call to action for all Americans.

"Armed National Guardsmen have been deployed in the District of Columbia to keep American citizens in line. Trump is trying to normalize the militarization of our country. This is where it starts, not where it will end. Trump is dropping the pretense of abiding by the rule of law. Sure, he still drapes himself in it enough that anyone who wants to can turn a blind eye and pretend that he’s complying with his oath of office, which requires him to support and defend the Constitution," she wrote. "But everyone who is paying attention and being honest with themselves understands that we are in a dangerous moment where Trump’s path toward authoritarianism is accelerating, and Americans need to wake up."

Going even further, the legal expert argues that "Trump trumps up excuses to justify doing as he pleases, which mostly involves accumulating more of the power of government in his own hands."

"A national emergency here, an invasion there. No matter what the truth is, he makes it up as he goes along, shamelessly lying about crime going up, when it’s in fact going down, and accusing agencies that release the statistics that contradict him of fraud when he’s called on the lies," she added. "This manner of operation makes it clear that it’s not about democracy and the American people; it’s about Trump and protecting Trump. It’s critical to make sure that the people around you understand this. It’s a week for serious conversations about what we need to do to protect the future of this country."

Additionally, Vance notes that Trump signed a new executive order in his ongoing efforts to takeover DC, calling the order "a thinly disguised effort to expand the use of the National Guard nationwide," which would be "a new leaf in the would-be autocrat’s playbook."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Dictatorial Drift: A Critical Analysis

In a recent commentary, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance sounded an alarm that echoes through the halls of democracy. Her analysis of Donald Trump's recent actions and statements paints a grim picture of a leader inching towards authoritarianism. Let's dissect her observations and the broader implications for American democracy.

The Dangerous Ramblings of a President

Vance highlights a particularly disturbing comment from Trump, where he mentions that "a lot of people are saying maybe we’d like a dictator." This off-the-cuff remark, dismissed by some as another example of Trump's infamous rambling, is anything but harmless. It's a chilling indicator of Trump's comfort with the language of autocracy—language that should never find a home in the lexicon of an American president. Trump's quick self-exoneration, claiming he is not a dictator but merely a "smart person" with "great common sense," does little to assuage concerns. Instead, it amplifies them, showcasing a profound disconnect from the foundational values of the republic.

Militarization of the American Streets

The deployment of Armed National Guardsmen in the District of Columbia, as Vance points out, is a stark manifestation of Trump's authoritarian inclinations. The normalization of military presence to quell civil unrest under the guise of maintaining order is a tactic ripped straight from the dictator's playbook. Trump's efforts to militarize the country are not just troubling; they are an affront to the principles of democracy and freedom.

The Erosion of the Rule of Law

Trump's disregard for the rule of law is not a new critique, but it's one that Vance argues is reaching a boiling point. Wrapping himself in the trappings of legal compliance, Trump has consistently demonstrated a preference for ruling by decree—through executive orders and actions that stretch, if not outright exceed, the limits of presidential power. This strategy, coupled with his penchant for lying about easily verifiable facts (such as crime statistics), reveals a leader who believes himself above the law. This behavior is not only undemocratic; it's dangerous.

The Playbook of an Autocrat

The executive order to expand the use of the National Guard nationwide, as Vance notes, is a "thinly disguised effort" indicative of Trump's autocratic ambitions. It's a move that should alarm any observer of history, as it suggests a desire not just for more control, but for total control. This is not the behavior of a president committed to democratic norms and the welfare of his country. It's the behavior of a would-be autocrat.

The Verdict

Joyce Vance's call to action is not just warranted; it's imperative. The signs of Trump's authoritarian drift are not subtle, and they demand a response not just from political leaders, but from all Americans. This is a moment of reckoning for the United States, a test of its commitment to democracy and the rule of law. The question now is not whether Trump will continue on this path, but whether the American people and their institutions will stand up to halt this descent into autocracy.

In summary, Joyce Vance's analysis is a sobering reminder of the fragility of democracy. It underscores the need for vigilance, accountability, and, most importantly, action to protect the future of the country. The time for serious conversations is indeed now; the future of America depends on it.
