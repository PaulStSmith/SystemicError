The Lawyer In The Chair

The Office Has Already Been Captured

Todd Blanche’s Senate hearing was not really about whether he and Donald Trump are “friends.” It was about something more serious and more corrosive: the nation’s top law-enforcement office is being run by Trump’s former personal lawyer. Blanche’s own stumble on the word “lawyer” was revealing only because it briefly stripped away the euphemism.

The source material gives the essentials. Blanche has been acting attorney general since April, after Trump ousted Pam Bondi. He also helped delay Trump’s New York sentencing until after the 2024 election. That is not distance from Trump. That is service to him.

Semantics as Cover

Sen. John Kennedy’s question about friendship was easy theater. It turned a structural corruption problem into a personality quiz. That is a useful trick for people who would rather not say the obvious: Blanche is not an independent attorney general in any meaningful sense. He is a loyalist occupying a post that is supposed to be insulated from the president’s private legal interests.

The correction from “I’m his lawyer” to “was his lawyer” was not a meaningful clarification. It was damage control. The relationship may have changed on paper; the political function has not.

The Real Questions

The real issue is not Blanche’s social tie to Trump. It is his demonstrated willingness to use legal power in Trump’s interest. The source points to three pressure points already hanging over his tenure: the Epstein files, the canceled $1.8 billion “anti-weaponization” fund, and prosecutions of Trump’s perceived enemies.

Those are not random controversies. They are clues. They suggest an administration that wants the Justice Department to operate as a shield for the president and a weapon against enemies, while blanketing the whole arrangement in procedural language and fake institutional gravity.

Power Moves Downward

Who holds actual power here? Trump does. Who enabled the outcome? Blanche did, and the Republican Senate is now providing the ritual of confirmation after the fact. The hearing is not a check on power if it merely stages questions while the underlying capture remains intact.

That is the misdirection in the story. The public is invited to focus on Blanche’s awkward wording, as if the problem were personal discomfort. The problem is institutional submission. When the attorney general is a former personal lawyer, the Department of Justice stops looking like an independent branch and starts looking like an extension of the president’s legal defense team.

The Pattern Is The Point

This is what authoritarian governance looks like in the American version: not uniforms and decrees first, but loyalists in the machinery, semantic fog over obvious conflicts, and prosecutions framed as “perceived enemies” instead of political targeting. Power is not hiding. It is being normalized.

The larger lesson is plain. Once a president can convert the Justice Department into a personnel office for personal loyalty, every hearing becomes a performance and every ethical concern becomes background noise. Blanche’s stumble mattered because it exposed the truth the proceeding was designed to bury: this is not law enforcement standing apart from Trump. It is Trump, translated into bureaucracy.
