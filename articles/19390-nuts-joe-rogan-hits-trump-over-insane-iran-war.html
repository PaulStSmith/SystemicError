Podcaster Joe Rogan slammed President Donald Trump for waging an “insane” series of strikes in Iran after running for election by promising not to start wars for regime change.

During a Tuesday conversation on Rogan’s podcast, author Michael Shellenberger said he had scrapped a column on the war in Iran because Trump’s reasoning was unclear.

“But it’s not clear that they’re really out for regime change or they’re just asserting power,” he explained. “I mean, some of it’s art of the deal, changing the person that we’re negotiating with. That’s Venezuela and Iran. Is it really going to change those regimes? I don’t think — most people don’t think so, but I’m not sure that that’s what they’re going for.”

“Well, neither thing made any sense to me,” Rogan replied. “The Venezuela thing, I mean, look, they wanted him out forever.”

“They go in, kidnap him, get him out,” he continued. “This one’s nuts. Like, and what’s happening in Tel Aviv. It’s hard to know what’s real and what’s not because there’s a lot of fake video going around and a lot of weird posts on X.”

“They might say that we want that or whatever, but that’s not ultimately; they’re not acting on the basis of achieving regime change,” Shellenberger insisted.

“But just seems so insane based on what he ran on,” Rogan remarked. “I mean, this is why a lot of people feel betrayed, right? He ran on no more wars and these stupid, senseless wars, and then we have one that we can’t even really clearly defined why we did it.”

Our Analysis:

The Rogan-Trump Conundrum: A Tale of Promises and Perplexities

In a recent episode that could only be described as a blend of bewilderment and criticism, Joe Rogan took a stark stance against President Donald Trump’s military actions in Iran, highlighting a glaring contradiction between Trump’s campaign promises and his presidential decisions. This episode, featuring author Michael Shellenberger, sheds light on the murky waters of Trump’s foreign policy, particularly his approach to Iran and Venezuela.

The Art of the Deal or the Art of War?

Shellenberger, initially poised to pen a column on the war in Iran, found himself at a crossroads, puzzled by Trump’s unclear motives. Is this a strategic power assertion or a clumsy stumble towards regime change? The ambiguity of Trump’s intentions leaves much to the imagination. The discussion ventures into the realm of speculation, with Shellenberger suggesting that Trump’s actions might be less about regime change and more about renegotiating power dynamics, albeit in a manner that raises more questions than answers.

Rogan’s Razor Sharp Critique

Rogan, known for his no-holds-barred commentary, cuts to the core of the issue, expressing confusion and disillusionment with Trump’s foreign policy antics. He points out the inherent contradiction in Trump’s actions in Iran and Venezuela vis-à-vis his campaign rhetoric that championed non-intervention and criticized senseless wars. “But just seems so insane based on what he ran on,” Rogan remarks, capturing the sentiment of betrayal felt by many of Trump’s supporters. This statement not only highlights the discrepancy between Trump’s promises and actions but also underscores the broader issue of political accountability and the ease with which campaign promises are discarded once in office.

The Fog of War... and Information

Rogan’s mention of the difficulty in discerning reality amidst the chaos of “fake video” and “weird posts on X” touches upon a critical aspect of modern conflict: the information war. The challenge of navigating through misinformation complicates public understanding of foreign policy decisions, further muddling Trump’s already opaque strategies. This confusion is emblematic of a larger crisis in political communication, where truth becomes a casualty of war and digital manipulation.

Conclusion: A Symphony of Dissonance

The conversation between Rogan and Shellenberger serves as a microcosm of the broader disillusionment and confusion surrounding Trump’s presidency, especially regarding foreign policy. The episode encapsulates a moment of reckoning, where the promises of non-intervention clash with the realities of military strikes and the complexities of international politics.

In essence, Rogan’s critique sheds light on the dissonance between Trump’s campaign assurances and his presidential actions, a pattern all too familiar in the political landscape. This episode not only questions the integrity of Trump’s decisions but also challenges the electorate to reflect on the perennial gap between political rhetoric and reality.
