'You know you're going to get crushed!' MSNBC panel laughs at Trump's new demand

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Donald Trump's Gerrymandering Gambit: A Laughable Justification

Joe Scarborough, Mika Brzezinski, Willie Geist (MSNBC screenshot)

Donald Trump's excited explanation about why he deserves extra seats in the House to justify the controversial gerrymandering attempt in Texas was laughed off on MSNBC on Wednesday morning.

On "Morning Joe," the hosts played a clip of Trump from a call-in to CNBC on Tuesday, where the president asserted, "California is gerrymandered. We should have many more seats in Congress. In California, it’s all gerrymandered. And we have an opportunity in Texas to pick up five seats. We have a really good governor, and we have good people in Texas. And I won Texas. I got the highest vote in the history of Texas, as you probably know, and we are entitled to five more seats."

That was followed by a clip of Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick (R) appearing with Fox News personality Sean Hannity saying of Democrats, "They've lost the minds of their voters who are moving towards the Republican party all over the country. We're a red state, we deserve more representation."

That led Joe Scarborough to burst out laughing, as co-host Mika Brzezinski looked skeptical and Willie Geist smirked.

"Wait, wait!" the laughing Scarborough exclaimed as Brzezinski added, "Okay...?"

"'The problem is that they've lost the vote," he continued. "No, no, you're the one who's rigging the maps because you know you're going to get crushed next year! That's what you're scared of."

"You're scared Democrats are going to crush Republicans," he continued while still laughing. "Who's lost the voters here? Because that is hilarious. Not only is it hilarious, it's so shortsighted because California and other states are just going to come back and do the same thing. So, again, I'm not exactly sure what they're trying to prove, but they're not going to prove it. "

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump's Gerrymandering Gambit: A Laughable Justification

In a recent spectacle that could only emerge from the circus of current American politics, Donald Trump provided an "excited explanation" on why he believes he deserves extra seats in the House, essentially justifying the controversial gerrymandering attempt in Texas. This episode was met with laughter and skepticism on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," highlighting not just the audacity of the claim, but the sheer farcical nature of the logic behind it.

Trump's assertion, made during a call-in to CNBC, that California is gerrymandered and thus, by some twisted logic, entitles him to "pick up five seats" in Texas, is not just flawed; it's laughably absurd. He claims, "We have a really good governor, and we have good people in Texas. And I won Texas. I got the highest vote in the history of Texas, as you probably know, and we are entitled to five more seats." This rationale seems to stem from a bizarre understanding of electoral entitlement, rather than any grounded legal or ethical principle.

Following Trump's logic, Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick (R) chimed in with a similarly ludicrous statement on Fox News, saying, "They've lost the minds of their voters who are moving towards the Republican party all over the country. We're a red state, we deserve more representation." This statement not only reeks of entitlement but also blatantly ignores the democratic principle that representation should be based on population, not political allegiance.

The reactions from the "Morning Joe" hosts encapsulate the situation perfectly. Joe Scarborough's laughter and Mika Brzezinski's skepticism are fitting responses to the absurdity of the situation. Scarborough's point, "No, no, you're the one who's rigging the maps because you know you're going to get crushed next year! That's what you're scared of," hits the nail on the head. The fear of losing power seems to be driving these gerrymandering attempts, rather than any legitimate concern for fair representation.

Furthermore, Scarborough points out the shortsightedness of this strategy, as it only invites retaliation in kind from other states like California, perpetuating a cycle of gerrymandering that undermines democratic principles.

In essence, the attempt to justify gerrymandering in Texas by pointing to alleged gerrymandering in California is a disturbingly flawed argument that highlights a broader issue in American politics: the willingness to undermine democratic processes for political gain. This episode serves as a stark reminder of the need for electoral reform and the dangers of allowing political parties to manipulate electoral boundaries for their advantage. The laughter it elicited on "Morning Joe" is a fitting response to the absurdity of the situation, but the underlying issues it exposes are no laughing matter.
