Kristi Noem has major incentive to leave DHS for Senate bid – and quickly

Kristi Noem has major incentive to leave her job overseeing the Department of Homeland Security and return to South Dakota for a possible Senate bid, but she has to make her decision quickly.

President Donald Trump has been deeply reluctant to fire cabinet members in his second term, but Noem has faced intense bipartisan criticism – and from within DHS – since the shootings of Renée Good and Alex Pretti by immigration agents in Minneapolis, and The Atlantic reported that Republicans back in her home state believe she’s plotting a return to challenge Sen. Mike Rounds (R-SD).

“It’s something people are talking about across the state,” said one Republican involved in South Dakota politics, “and based on everything I’ve been hearing, Mike Rounds would handily win that race.”

Republican strategists have warned Noem’s deportation operation is a political liability heading into the midterm, and one source familiar with the discussions told The Atlantic that her position in the cabinet is no longer secure, although Trump continues to praise her in public comments and the White House disputed that changes were being considered.

“President Trump has assembled the most talented and America First cabinet and staff in history,” said White House spokesperson Davis Ingle.

Tricia McLaughlin, who announced this week she’s leaving her positions as DHS spokesperson, declined to comment on Noem’s political future, but she has a major incentive to leave now and launch a bid to challenge Rounds – another former South Dakota governor – for a Senate seat.

“To get into the race, Noem would have to register for the primary and collect the 2,171 supportive signatures statewide by the end of next month,” wrote The Atlantic’s Michael Scherer. “A Noem adviser told me today that she has no plans to leave. But Noem could have an incentive to seek elective office if Democrats remain on track to win back control of the House and launch investigations into her tenure at the Department of Homeland Security. A Senate seat would give her both a professional staff and a fundraising platform to help defend herself.”

Rounds spoke out against Noem after she revealed her decision to kill her family dog because she believed it was untrainable, and a GOP strategist alluded to that infamous lore when assessing the political state of play in a potential matchup between the DHS secretary and Rounds, who’s been criticized by Trump but is an ally of Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) .

“Mike Rounds is so unpopular amongst Republican primary voters, he’d lose to a dead dog,” said that national Republican strategist.

A Deep Dive into Kristi Noem’s Predicament and Political Maneuvering

The political realm is ablaze with the latest development from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), where Secretary Kristi Noem finds herself in the eye of a storm, not just for her controversial actions but also for her potential political maneuvers. Let’s dissect the layers of this unfolding drama with the precision of a surgeon and the clarity this murky situation desperately needs.

The Controversy at DHS

Firstly, Noem’s position has been on shaky ground following the tragic shootings of Renée Good and Alex Pretti by immigration agents in Minneapolis. These incidents have not only drawn bipartisan criticism but have also stirred discontent within the DHS itself. It’s a classic case of leadership under fire, both figuratively and literally. The response, or lack thereof, from President Donald Trump, who has shown a marked reluctance to dismiss cabinet members in his second term, adds another layer of complexity to the issue.

Political Calculations and Speculations

The whispers of Noem’s potential Senate bid to unseat Sen. Mike Rounds (R-SD) are growing louder, with speculations rife in South Dakota’s political circles. The Atlantic’s report paints a picture of a political landscape where Noem’s possible pivot back to state politics is seen not just as ambition, but perhaps, as a strategic retreat from a tumultuous tenure at DHS. The notion that Rounds would “handily win” such a race, as per a Republican insider, speaks volumes about the perceived political clout and standing of both figures within the state.

The Deportation Operation: A Political Albatross?

Noem’s deportation operation has been flagged as a “political liability” heading into the midterm elections. In the brutal arena of politics, such operations can often be double-edged swords, potentially rallying a base while alienating moderate and undecided voters. This aspect of her tenure, coupled with the tragic incidents under her watch, has evidently made her position within the cabinet and her future political prospects subjects of intense speculation.

The Curious Case of the Family Dog

In an anecdote that seems more befitting a dark comedy than political reality, Rounds’ criticism of Noem over her decision to euthanize her family dog for being “untrainable” adds a surreal layer to their political rivalry. This bizarre episode, while seemingly trivial, underscores the personal animosities and the high stakes involved in this political chess game. The notion that Rounds is so unpopular that he’d “lose to a dead dog,” as per a GOP strategist, might be hyperbolic but it’s a telling commentary on the state of political discourse and perception.

Conclusion: Political Chess or a House of Cards?

As Kristi Noem navigates the treacherous waters of her current predicament, the political implications of her decisions loom large. Whether her contemplation of a Senate bid is a tactical retreat or a calculated advance remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the intersection of her controversial tenure at DHS, the tragic incidents under her watch, and the speculative political maneuvers ahead paint a picture of a political landscape fraught with intrigue, ambition, and desperation.

In the end, the question isn’t just about Noem’s political future but also about the broader implications of her actions and decisions on the fabric of American politics. As we watch this drama unfold, let’s not lose sight of the larger narrative at play here — the quest for power, the allure of ambition, and the inevitable collateral damage that comes with it.
