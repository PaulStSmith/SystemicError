'Patently insufficient': Justice Jackson hammers 'botched' pro-Trump ruling

Judge Ketanji Brown Jackson testifies during the Senate Judiciary Committee’s confirmation hearing on her nomination to the U.S. Supreme Court, on Capitol Hill in Washington, on March 22, 2022. Credit: REUTERS/Michael A McCoy

In a dissent included in an unsigned order released by the Supreme Court on Friday, Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson scolded her colleagues for not giving enough serious consideration to all parties involved in an emergency appeal from Donald Trump's Department of Justice.

As the New York Times reported, the majority ruled in favor of the president by revoking a policy put in place under former President Joe Biden that is allowing more than 500,000 immigrants to stay in the U.S. temporarily on humanitarian grounds.

The ruling means immigrants from Cuba, Nicaragua, Venezuela and Haiti now face the threat of being swiftly deported while their individual cases are still under review in the U.S.

The court wrote an "order entered by the United States District Court for the District of Massachusetts ... is stayed pending the disposition of the appeal in the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit and disposition of a petition for a writ of certiorari, if such a writ is timely sought. Should certiorari be denied, this stay shall terminate automatically."

That earned a scathing response from Justice Brown Jackson that the majority had screwed up.

"The Court has plainly botched this assessment today," she wrote. "It requires next to nothing from the Government with respect to irreparable harm. And it undervalues the devastating consequences of allowing the Government to precipitously upend the lives and livelihoods of nearly half a million noncitizens while their legal claims are pending."

She advised, "Even if the Government is likely to win on the merits, in our legal system, success takes time and the stay standards require more than anticipated victory. I would have denied the Government’s application because its harm-related showing is patently insufficient. The balance of the equities also weighs heavily in respondents’ favor."

"While it is apparent that the Government seeks a stay to enable it to inflict maximum predecision damage, court-ordered stays exist to minimize — not maximize — harm to litigating parties," she asserted.

Our Analysis:

Supreme Court's Decision: A Critical Analysis

In a dramatic turn of events that reeks of political machination and a profound disregard for humanitarian considerations, the Supreme Court has once again found itself at the center of controversy. This time, the court's majority has sided with a request from Donald Trump's Department of Justice, effectively dismantling a policy instituted under the Biden administration that allowed over 500,000 immigrants from countries like Cuba, Nicaragua, Venezuela, and Haiti to stay in the U.S. on humanitarian grounds temporarily. This decision, as stark as it is, lays bare the court's current ideological leanings and underscores a troubling trend of decisions that seem more aligned with political agendas than with the principles of justice and equity.

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson, in a fiery and scathing dissent, lambasted her colleagues for their failure to adequately weigh the consequences of their decision. Her dissent not only shines a light on the court's seemingly cavalier attitude towards the lives and livelihoods of nearly half a million noncitizens but also raises critical questions about the court's understanding of its role and responsibilities.

The Court's "Botched Assessment"

Justice Brown Jackson's criticism of the court's decision is both pointed and poignant. She accuses the majority of "plainly botch[ing] this assessment" by requiring "next to nothing from the Government with respect to irreparable harm." In essence, the court has decided to prioritize the administration's policy objectives over the immediate and devastating impact its decision will have on the lives of countless individuals. This, according to Justice Brown Jackson, is a clear misstep, showcasing a fundamental misunderstanding or, worse, a willful ignorance of the stay standards that are supposed to guide the court's decisions in such matters.

The majority's decision, as outlined, allows for the swift deportation of immigrants from the mentioned countries while their legal claims are still under review. This not only upends the lives of these individuals but also sets a dangerous precedent that could affect future immigration policy and the treatment of immigrants in the United States.

The Misuse of Court-Ordered Stays

Justice Brown Jackson's dissent also touches on a critical issue: the misuse of court-ordered stays. She argues that such stays are meant to "minimize — not maximize — harm to litigating parties." However, the court's decision does the exact opposite. By granting the stay requested by the government, the court is enabling the administration to "inflict maximum predecision damage" on nearly half a million noncitizens. This approach, as Justice Brown Jackson rightly points out, goes against the very purpose of court-ordered stays and reveals a disturbing willingness on the part of the majority to side with government requests, irrespective of the human cost.

Conclusion: A Call for Justice and Humanity

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson's dissent is not just a rebuke of her colleagues' decision but a clarion call for justice, humanity, and adherence to legal standards. It serves as a reminder that the Supreme Court should be a bastion of fairness and protector of the vulnerable, not an instrument for political agendas or a facilitator of harm.

As we analyze this decision and its ramifications, it's essential to remember the broader implications for immigration policy, the treatment of noncitizens, and the credibility of the Supreme Court itself. The court's ruling is a stark reminder of the power it wields and the profound impact its decisions can have on the fabric of American society. It's high time the court reevaluates its priorities and aligns its decisions more closely with the principles of justice and equity it is supposed to uphold.
