'Warning to Americans': Trump is reportedly destroying his 'most important relationship'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 19, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump is asking a federal appeals court to lift a ruling which stopped his administration’s ability to deny the Associated Press’ access to the Oval Office, Air Force One, and other White House spaces.

The restrictions came after an AP report referred to the Gulf of Mexico in a story, instead of calling it the Gulf of America. This week's Friday Read from Politico breaks down Trump's lifelong relationship with the AP, and other media outlets, as well as the implications this lawsuit has across the media landscape.

“It might sound like some farcical sitcom plot, but it’s also the deadly serious front line of what’s become Trump’s all-out war on the mainstream media,” Penned POLITICO’S Michael Kruse. “in his long, hyper-public life, this marks in Trump’s means of ascent nothing short of a massive shift. Because his relationship with the media is his most important relationship.”

“Throughout his entire life, the media has played many roles for Trump: confidant, foil, and tacit (and sometimes not so tacit) partner,” Kruse wrote. He recalled comments Trump, himself has said about the media. Phrases like: “I use the media, way the media uses me — to attract attention.” Another view Trump previously had of the media was “We give each other what we need.”

However, this changed after the 2016 election, and even more so after 2024. “Trump has turned his performative anti-media schtick into actual anti-media deeds. Wielding lawsuits, executive actions, and the unleashing of allies and aides, he’s attempting to starve, squelch, or shutter network television stations, global news agencies, and reporters in Washington and beyond,” opined Kruse.

Yes, press briefings still happen, and Kruse recognized Trump is “far more accessible and available to the media than his predecessor. But he’s also more aggressively than ever prioritized and rewarded friendlier, more acquiescent outlets and individuals.”

Jill Abramson, a former executive editor of the New York Times, called Trump’s moves against the media “An all-fronts assault on the basic constitutional tenets that make America, America.”

Others told the outlet it’s simpler and more personal. “It’s about enemies. I don’t think he sits around and says, ‘Oh, I’m going to be an authoritarian,’” Hank Sheinkopf, a New York political strategist, said. “[Trump] says, ‘I have to even scores.’”

Former AP White House reporter Ron Fournier told Kruse. “[Trump] has now gone from just treating the press as a foil to now treating the press as an enemy that literally needs to be stomped out. My warning to all Americans is this is just a step.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Media War: A Dangerous Precipice

In an audacious move that blurs the lines between petty vendettas and authoritarian overreach, President Donald Trump has escalated his long-standing feud with the media to alarming new heights. His latest legal maneuver to block the Associated Press (AP) from accessing pivotal White House spaces—over a semantic disagreement, no less—marks not just a petulant response to unfavorable coverage but a sinister attempt to erode the foundational pillars of a free press.

The Gulf of America Controversy: A Thinly Veiled Excuse

At the heart of this debacle is Trump's objection to the AP's reference to the Gulf of Mexico, insisting, bizarrely, on the term "Gulf of America." This isn't just a matter of geographical semantics but a revealing glimpse into Trump's propensity to weaponize any perceived slight as a pretext to quash dissenting voices. The fact that such a trivial issue could lead to punitive measures against a major news outlet is both laughable and deeply concerning.

A Lifelong Media Manipulator

POLITICO's Michael Kruse adeptly outlines Trump's complex relationship with the media, characterizing it as a dance of mutual utility turned sour. Trump, who once reveled in his ability to command media attention, has, according to Kruse, transformed his "performative anti-media schtick" into tangible, aggressive actions aimed at undermining the press's ability to operate freely. This shift from exploiting media dynamics for personal gain to actively seeking to dismantle the press's infrastructure is a dangerous pivot that threatens the very essence of democratic discourse.

An Assault on Constitutional Tenets

Jill Abramson's characterization of Trump's tactics as "an all-fronts assault on the basic constitutional tenets" is no hyperbole. The freedom of the press is enshrined in the Constitution not as a courtesy to journalists but as a bulwark against tyranny. Trump's efforts to punish and silence outlets that dare to critique him or his administration represent a clear and present danger to that principle.

Personal Vendettas vs. Authoritarian Impulses

While some, like Hank Sheinkopf, might argue that Trump's actions are driven more by personal vendettas than by a coherent strategy of authoritarian control, the distinction becomes moot when the result is the same: a weakened, intimidated press that hesitates to fulfill its role as a check on power.

Ron Fournier's warning that Trump's hostility towards the press is a mere "step" towards something more sinister should not be taken lightly. The trajectory from treating the media as a "foil" to an "enemy that literally needs to be stomped out" is one that history has shown us leads to dark places.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

This isn't just about the Associated Press or the absurd "Gulf of America" dispute. It's about the integrity of our democratic institutions and the essential role of a free press within them. Trump's actions are a stark reminder of the fragility of these institutions and the ease with which they can be undermined by those in power.

The media landscape, indeed, finds itself on the "deadly serious front line" of a battle for its very survival, as characterized by Kruse. As consumers of news and defenders of democracy, our response to this assault cannot be apathy or resignation but a renewed commitment to supporting investigative journalism and holding power to account.

In the face of Trump's all-out war on the press, silence and inaction are luxuries we can ill afford.
