The 'callous' Trump remark that will make MAGA turn on him: analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 3, 20265 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump walks after a Medal of Honor ceremony at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 2, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Donald Trump doomed himself with just one “callous” sentence, with a new analysis from The i Paper’s James Ball predicting that his MAGA base will “viciously” turn against him as a result.

On Saturday, the U.S. military joined with Israel in a joint operation against Iran, launching strikes initially focused on key leadership targets. This marked the largest overseas military operation yet launched by Trump in second term, despite consistently campaigning in the past against U.S. involvement in costly foreign wars.

Allies close to the president have expressed concern about the potential political backlash Trump could endure from his MAGA base over this reversal on that key pledge. However, some said that he might avoid that situation if the operation in Iran is quick and results in few casualties. As of Monday, four U.S. servicemembers have reportedly been killed as a result of Iran’s counterattacks.

Writing for The i Paper on Monday, Ball argued that Trump’s “callous” reaction to those casualties, in the form of a video shared to Truth Social on Sunday afternoon, had already doomed him.

“We pray for the full recovery of the wounded and send our immense love and eternal gratitude to the families of the fallen,” Trump said in the video. “And sadly, there will likely be more before it ends. That’s the way it is. Likely be more, but we’ll do everything possible where that won’t be the case.”

According to Ball, Trump’s one sentence in that statement about casualties being “the way it is” showed him “trying to shrug something off as just the way of the world, when instead it’s the direct consequence of his own actions.”

Ball noted that Trump has frequently denigrated the value of military service in the past, highlighting his mockery of the late Sen. John McCain’s time as a prisoner-of-war in Vietnam and his avoidance of a military cemetery he said was “filled with losers.” Trump managed to avoid much political fallout from those past comments, but this time, Ball stressed, will be different.

“The American troops fighting and dying today are doing so at Trump’s behest – by skipping Congressional approval, he has no one to hide behind,” Ball wrote. “Everyone knows this war is happening because Trump personally wanted it.”

He continued: “Trump’s base voted him in to fix the economy, close the borders and end foreign wars. They were willing to forgive him almost anything if he delivered on that. If the deal falls apart, they will turn on him just as viciously as they’ve turned on every other politician. And he won’t like that one bit.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Reckless Gamble: A Fateful Sentence

In a striking revelation from The i Paper’s James Ball, we witness the unraveling of Donald Trump’s political fortress, meticulously built on promises to his MAGA base. Ball’s analysis centers on Trump’s “callous” response to American casualties in a recent U.S.-Israel joint operation against Iran, a sentence that might just be the harbinger of his political downfall.

The Betrayal

Trump’s presidency, in its second term, ventured into unchartered and hypocritical territory with the largest overseas military operation of his tenure against Iran. This move is not only a stark departure from his previous stance against costly foreign wars but a direct contradiction to the very principles that hoisted him into the Oval Office.

“We pray for the full recovery of the wounded and send our immense love and eternal gratitude to the families of the fallen,” Trump said, before casually adding, “And sadly, there will likely be more before it ends. That’s the way it is.” This statement, as Ball rightly points out, is not just callous but a gross underestimation of the gravity of sacrificing American lives.

The Unforgivable Casualness

Trump’s nonchalance towards the repercussions of his own actions lays bare a disturbing detachment from the consequences of war. His past mockery of military service and valor, such as the deplorable comments about Sen. John McCain and American war dead, already painted a grim picture of his respect for the military. Yet, his latest blunder—suggesting that the loss of American lives is just “the way it is”—is particularly egregious because these servicemembers are fighting and dying at his behest, without Congressional approval, leaving him nowhere to hide.

A Base Betrayed

Trump’s MAGA base, a group that has shown an almost unbreakable loyalty, voted for him under the promise of economic revival, secured borders, and an end to foreign entanglements. They were willing to overlook almost any flaw, any scandal, as long as he delivered on these fronts. However, as Ball emphasizes, this base is as fickle as it is fervent. The moment Trump deviates from his promised path, their support transforms into vicious opposition.

Conclusion: A Political Miscalculation of Epic Proportions

James Ball’s analysis in The i Paper sheds light on a crucial political misstep by Donald Trump. By engaging in the very foreign wars he vowed to avoid and responding to the resultant American casualties with a shrug, Trump has not only contradicted himself but has also betrayed the trust of his staunchest supporters. This isn’t just a deviation from his campaign promises; it’s a glaring exhibition of disrespect for the very lives he’s put at risk. If history is any indication, Trump’s base will not take this betrayal lightly. The sentence, “That’s the way it is,” might just be remembered as the beginning of the end for Trump’s political stronghold. In his attempt to project strength and resolve, Trump has inadvertently exposed his greatest vulnerability: his callous indifference to the cost of his actions.
