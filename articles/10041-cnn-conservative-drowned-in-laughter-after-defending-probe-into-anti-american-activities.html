CNN conservative drowned in laughter after defending probe into 'anti-American activities'

A senior official in the Department of Justice has directed federal prosecutors around the country to investigate a group funded by billionaire Democratic donor George Soros, a longtime bogeyman to conservatives who Trump has said belongs in prison, and Rob Bluey, executive editor of The Heritage Foundation’s Daily Signal website, justified the move on “CNN This Morning.”

“I think when you see the writing on the bullets in the Charlie Kirk assassination and the attack on on the ICE facility this week, there are legitimate questions that are being raised or why are these shooters putting these messages, these cryptic messages sometimes, and what do they mean exactly?” Bluey said. “So not only do you have the president making this move from the White House, but you also have Congressman Chip Roy in the U.S. Congress who wants to form a select committee to do an investigation. So I’m all for investigating and trying to get to the bottom of this.”

Host Audie Cornish asked what the Texas Republican congressman was trying to investigate, and Bluey was happy to explain.

“Chip Roy, he wants to form a select committee to look into what he calls anti-American activities that are happening on our own soil,” Bluey said, and was immediately interrupted by laughter from his fellow panelists.

New York Times podcaster Lulu Garcia-Navarro stepped up to point out that Roy’s request for a select committee to investigate what he calls anti-American ideology sounded similar to the infamous House Un-American Activities Committee that former senator Joseph McCarthy exploited to persecute alleged communists in the 1950s.

“Anti-American, actually – interesting, anti-American,” Garcia-Navarro said. “Does that not bring back a happy memories of the past?”

Bluey stammered as he tried to regain his footing.

“You brought up George Soros earlier, and I think the reason that Trump is looking at Soros is he has funded a lot of prosecutorial races in this country for individuals who have what the right would say gone easy on criminals,” Bluey argued.

Vox podcaster Noel King argued that the conservative U.S. Supreme Court paved the way for Soros to fund any candidate he wanted in its landmark 2010 decision in the Citizens United case, which ruled the government cannot restrict political spending.

“He’s an he’s an American billionaire,” King said. “That is his right, we made this decision in Citizens United. We said money belongs in politics. George Soros is playing the same game as every other billionaire in America.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of Absurdity: Trump, Soros, and the Echoes of McCarthyism

In an era where political discourse often teeters on the brink of the surreal, the latest episode involving a conservative commentator’s defense of President Donald Trump’s attacks on George Soros and the subsequent call for investigations into “anti-American activities” is a stark reminder of how far we’ve ventured into the absurd. The scene, as reported, unfolded on “CNN This Morning,” where laughter emerged not as a dismissal of the gravity of these allegations but as a natural response to the sheer ludicrousness of the arguments presented.

The Specter of Soros: Once again, George Soros finds himself at the center of conservative conspiracy theories, a position he’s occupied with an almost mythic persistence. Trump’s fixation with Soros, coupled with the call for his imprisonment, is a dangerous flirtation with authoritarian tactics—vilify the opposition, then call for their heads. Rob Bluey’s defense of these moves under the guise of investigating “cryptic messages” on bullets is a thinly veiled attempt to legitimize baseless accusations. The irony is palpable; in a twist that would be humorous if not so deeply troubling, these conspiracy theories are presented as earnest concerns needing federal investigation.

Echoes of McCarthyism: The proposal by Congressman Chip Roy to form a select committee to investigate “anti-American activities” is nothing short of a McCarthy-era revival tour, complete with all the paranoia and witch-hunting that characterized that dark period in American history. Lulu Garcia-Navarro’s comparison to the House Un-American Activities Committee isn’t just apt—it’s a dire warning. The laughter that interrupted Bluey’s explanation wasn’t mockery; it was disbelief. Disbelief that in the 21st century, we are witnessing an attempt to resurrect one of the most shameful chapters in American political history.

Citizens United and the Billionaire’s Game: Noel King’s intervention during the discussion brings a crucial perspective to the fore. By highlighting the Supreme Court’s decision in Citizens United, King underscores a fundamental hypocrisy in the conservative outcry against Soros. If money in politics is the game, Soros is far from the only player—yet he is vilified disproportionately. The Citizens United ruling indeed opened the floodgates for unlimited political spending, and conservatives have benefited from this ruling as much, if not more, than progressives. The selective outrage against Soros’s involvement in prosecutorial races exposes a glaring double standard: it’s only a problem when the other side plays the game.

Conclusion: The Laughter of Despair

The laughter that filled the room on “CNN This Morning” is emblematic of a broader societal response to the absurdity now characteristic of political discourse. It’s a laughter tinged with despair, a coping mechanism for a reality that’s become too bizarre to confront any other way. Yet, beneath this laughter lies a sobering truth: the tactics of vilification, conspiracy theories, and calls for investigations into “anti-American activities” are not merely relics of the past but active threats to the present.

As we navigate these treacherous waters, it’s imperative to remember the lessons of history, to recognize the dangers of unchecked paranoia and the vilification of political adversaries. The spectacle might occasionally provoke laughter, but the stakes are too high for us to forget the consequences of surrendering to the absurd.
