'This affair is far from over': Disney shareholders are now revolting over Jimmy Kimmel

By Paulo Santos

Disney shareholders are demanding answers following the short-lived ousting of Jimmy Kimmel, and alleging that the late-night talk show host’s removal may have amounted to “capitulating to government pressure” — and is a potential violation of their shareholder rights.

The American Federation of Teachers sent a letter to Disney CEO Bob Iger on behalf of two shareholders dated Sept. 24, in which they demand answers about whether the company “was involved in any wrongdoing in relation to the Kimmel suspension,” Variety reported.

Kimmel was suspended “indefinitely” last week after receiving scrutiny over a comment he made on the suspected killer of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, comments that suggested the suspect was aligned with MAGA. The decision that was reportedly made by Iger and other top-Disney executives.

While Kimmel would go on to return to television within a week, the decision to suspend him in the first place has left some Disney shareholders demanding answers.

“There is a credible basis to suspect that the [Disney] Board and executives may have breached their fiduciary duties of loyalty, care, and good faith by placing improper political or affiliate considerations above the best interests of the Company and its stockholders,” the letter reads, according to the report.

“[We reserve the right to file a lawsuit] in the event that members of the Board or Disney executives did not properly discharge their fiduciary duties.”

In the letter, the group – on behalf of the shareholders – allege that Disney’s stock “suffered significant declines” as a result of Kimmel’s suspension, and point to comments from President Donald Trump as potential proof that the company caved to his administration.

“I think we’re going to test ABC on this,” Trump wrote in a social media post after Kimmel’s return to television, a comment highlighted in the letter. “Let’s see how we do. Last time I went after them, they gave me $16 Million Dollars.”

While Kimmel has returned to television, about 23% of U.S. households with televisions were unable to watch, with around 70 ABC affiliates operated by Sinclair and Nexstar still refusing to air the program.

The ongoing blackout for Kimmell in some American households, alongside the precedent the suspension set, that demands accountability, argues Clayton Weimers, executive director of Reporters Without Borders USA, who also wrote a letter on behalf of shareholders.

“Despite Jimmy Kimmel’s return to late night, this affair is far from over,” Weimers said in a statement, as reported by Variety.

“The FCC continues to threaten media organizations over content it doesn’t like and, in the case of Kimmel, dozens of affiliates are still refusing to air his show. The public needs to know how government actions toward the media unfolded in this instance, so we can stop this reckless assault on the First Amendment from going any further.”

Our Analysis:

The Disney Dilemma: A Case of Corporate Capitulation or Shareholder Sabotage?

In the latest saga of corporate America’s dance with democracy, Disney finds itself at the center of a storm that’s as much about the First Amendment as it is about fiduciary duty. The short-lived ousting of Jimmy Kimmel has sparked a fiery debate, with allegations flying that Disney’s actions might not just be a knee-jerk reaction to a controversial joke, but a full-blown capitulation to governmental pressure. This isn’t just about a late-night talk show host’s temporary suspension; it’s a mirror reflecting the troubled relationship between corporate power, political influence, and shareholder rights.

The Allegations: Capitulating to Government Pressure

The American Federation of Teachers, stepping into the ring on behalf of two shareholders, has thrown a spotlight on a murky area of corporate governance. Their demand for answers from Disney CEO Bob Iger is not just a quest for transparency but a battle cry against what they perceive as a potential violation of shareholder rights. The accusation? That Disney, by suspending Kimmel, may have bowed to external pressures, sacrificing the company’s and its shareholders’ best interests on the altar of political expediency.

The Kimmel Conundrum

Jimmy Kimmel’s suspension, described as “indefinite” and resulting from comments about the suspected killer of a right-wing influencer, has been the flashpoint in this controversy. The decision, attributed to Iger and top Disney executives, begs the question: Where does the line between responsible content management and undue political influence lie? Kimmel’s swift return to television does little to dispel these concerns; instead, it adds fuel to the fire, with shareholders left pondering the cost of this fiasco.

The Fallout: Shareholder Rights and Stock Declines

The stakes are high, with the letter from the American Federation of Teachers highlighting the “significant declines” in Disney’s stock following the suspension. This isn’t merely about the financial repercussions of a week-long suspension; it’s about the precedent it sets and the message it sends to investors. When President Donald Trump’s comments about testing ABC post-Kimmel’s return are thrown into the mix, one can’t help but wonder: Did Disney inadvertently validate a dangerous precedent where governmental comments can sway corporate decisions, affecting market performance?

The Blackout: A Lingering Shadow

The ongoing refusal of approximately 70 ABC affiliates to air Kimmel’s show underscores the lingering effects of this debacle. This isn’t just a blip on the late-night TV radar; it’s a significant blackout affecting 23% of U.S. households with televisions. Clayton Weimers’s statement on behalf of shareholders underscores the gravity of the situation, framing it as an ongoing assault on the First Amendment that demands accountability.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

This incident transcends Jimmy Kimmel and Disney, touching on broader issues of corporate governance, shareholder rights, and the increasingly blurred lines between political influence and corporate decision-making. The demand for transparency and accountability is more than justified; it’s essential in a democratic society where corporate giants wield significant influence. As this story unfolds, one thing is clear: The balance between corporate responsibility, shareholder interests, and political pressures is more precarious than ever.

Disney’s response to these allegations will not only shape the future of its relationship with shareholders but also set a precedent for how corporate America navigates the treacherous waters of political influence. The eyes of the world, and more importantly, of the shareholders, are watching.
