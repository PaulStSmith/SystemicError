'Should be ashamed': MAGA goes into tailspin as 60 Minutes anchor targets Trump

60 Minutes’ Scott Pelley. (Shutterstock)

The anchor of CBS' "60 Minutes" made a thinly veiled attack on President Donald Trump during Wake Forest University commencement speech — and MAGA immediately went into a tailspin.

Scott Pelley didn't mention Trump by name but he did warn “freedom of speech is under attack” and that “insidious fear” is creeping into schools, the Daily Beast reported.

“To move forward, we debate, not demonize; we discuss, not destroy. But in this moment, our sacred rule of law is under attack,” Pelley said in the viral address. “Journalism is under attack. Universities are under attack. Freedom of speech is under attack. And insidious fear is reaching through our schools, our businesses, our homes, and into our private thoughts. The fear to speak, in America.”

The comments come as CBS News is being sued by Trump. The lawsuit accuses the network of editing an interview with then-Vice President Kamala Harris to portray the Democrat in a more favorable light weeks before the election.

“The supposed titans of TV journalism are just sanctimonious theater majors with mid-level IQs and world-class egos,” Buck Sexton posted on X, and shared the video of the remarks.

“This self-important, sermonizing propagandist is what passes for a legacy media ‘journalist,’” the Western Lensman X account posted.

Pro-Trump podcaster Scott Adams added: “Liberals who once controlled speech and the power to censor are now upset that a majority of Americans reject their lies and deception. 60 Minutes’ Scott Pelley raged at Trump in an angry, unhinged commencement address at Wake Forest: as he speaks openly and freely in America.”

Curtis Houck, managing editor of NewsBusters, said Pelley “Delivers Hateful, Pompous Commencement Address Smearing Trump, MAGA”

“It is very sad that CBS/60 Minutes’ Scott Pelley chose one of the happiest days for graduates and their families to display his undeniable case of TDS [Trump Derangement Syndrome],” one MAGA account with more than 220,000 X followers posted. “Wake Forest University should be ashamed that it allowed this to take place.”

CBS News did not immediately respond to a request for comment from The Daily Beast.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Veiled Critique and the MAGA Meltdown

In a notable commencement speech at Wake Forest University, Scott Pelley, anchor of CBS' "60 Minutes," ignited a firestorm without even mentioning President Donald Trump by name. The thinly veiled critique of Trump's impact on American values such as freedom of speech and the rule of law sent MAGA supporters into what can only be described as a tailspin. This reaction is emblematic of the broader political climate, where indirect criticisms invite direct and often disproportionate counterattacks.

The Attack on Freedom of Speech

Pelley's warning that "freedom of speech is under attack" and the spread of "insidious fear" through American institutions—schools, businesses, and even into our private thoughts—is a damning commentary on the state of the nation. It's a bold move, especially considering CBS News is currently embroiled in a lawsuit with Trump, who accuses the network of biased editing. This legal battle underscores the increasingly fraught relationship between the media and political figures, where journalism itself becomes a target for those it seeks to hold accountable.

MAGA's Counterattack: A Study in Irony

The response from MAGA supporters and Trump-aligned media personalities was swift and predictably vitriolic. Buck Sexton's dismissal of journalists as "sanctimonious theater majors" and Scott Adams' claim that liberals are upset about losing control over speech are particularly rich in irony. These comments not only attack Pelley's character but also attempt to delegitimize the entire profession of journalism—a tactic often employed to divert attention from substantive critiques.

Adams' assertion that Pelley's speech was "angry" and "unhinged" is a fascinating choice of words, considering Pelley's call for debate over demonization and discussion over destruction. It's as if the mere suggestion that America's freedoms are under threat is enough to be labeled as deranged. This knee-jerk defensiveness reveals a concerning trend: the inability or unwillingness to engage with criticism in a constructive manner.

The Real Grift: Misrepresenting Critique as Hatred

What's most alarming about the MAGA reaction is the portrayal of Pelley's speech as "hateful" and "pompous." It's a classic example of the grift in political discourse today: framing legitimate concern and critique as personal attacks, thereby undermining the critic's credibility. This tactic is not just misleading; it's dangerous. It shifts the focus from the issues at hand—such as the erosion of foundational freedoms—to the personalities involved, muddying the waters and stifacing meaningful conversation.

The Unspoken Irony

Lastly, the indignation over Pelley's choice of venue—a commencement speech—is peculiar. Commencement speeches are traditionally platforms for addressing societal issues, encouraging new graduates to think critically about the world they're inheriting. Criticizing Pelley for using this platform to highlight concerns about freedom and democracy is not just missing the point; it's an attempt to silence a critical voice on a day dedicated to celebrating education and free thought.

Conclusion

Scott Pelley's speech at Wake Forest University, and the subsequent MAGA meltdown, is a microcosm of the larger battle being waged over America's soul. It's a battle between those who seek to preserve and protect the freedoms that define the nation and those who would attack those freedoms—either directly or through attempts to silence their defenders. The irony of claiming victimhood in the face of a plea for rational discourse and the defense of liberty is both tragic and telling. In their rush to defend Trump from any and all criticism, MAGA supporters reveal their true priorities—and freedom of speech, it seems, is not among them.
