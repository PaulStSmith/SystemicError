No One Wants to Work — for You

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

“No one wants to work anymore.” It’s the rallying cry of out-of-touch executives and LinkedIn pundits clinging to a fantasy where workers are lazy, entitled, and ungrateful. But peel back the whiny veneer and you’ll find the truth: people want to work. They just don’t want to work for you.

What they don’t want is to break their backs for companies that treat them like disposable parts in a malfunctioning machine. You refused to give raises that kept pace with inflation, yet expected gratitude for a stagnant paycheck that lost buying power every quarter. You kept wages flat while prices climbed, and then acted surprised when morale plummeted. You cut benefits, eliminated training programs, and piled more responsibility on fewer and fewer workers, as if burnout was part of the job description.

You handed out massive bonuses and stock options to your executives while telling frontline staff to “tighten their belts.” You celebrated shareholder value while hollowing out your company’s human core. You demanded loyalty from workers even as you treated them as liabilities instead of assets. And now you’re shocked they’ve started returning the favor.

You demanded employees return to the office under the pretense of “team spirit” and “collaboration,” all while ignoring legitimate health concerns, rising commute costs, and the fact that productivity didn’t suffer while they worked from home. Then you had the gall to install spyware to monitor them remotely, treating adult professionals like delinquent teenagers. You punished remote workers for moving to more affordable cities with arbitrary pay cuts, not because their performance declined, but because you could. That wasn’t policy—that was punishment.

You replaced loyalty with layoffs. You swapped trust for surveillance. You traded pride in work for relentless grind. The only thing you’ve innovated is new ways to alienate the people who keep your business running. And when those people finally walk away from your toxic culture, you don’t do any soul-searching. You don’t reflect. You just double down and blame them.

This so-called “labor shortage” you keep whining about? It isn’t a shortage of people willing to work. It’s a shortage of people willing to be exploited. The talent pool didn’t dry up. It just got fed up. People didn’t vanish. They woke up. They realized they don’t owe you their exhaustion, their weekends, or their sanity.

People still want to work. They want to build, to contribute, to be part of something meaningful. But they also want to be paid fairly, treated with respect, and given the autonomy to live a life that isn’t devoured by the job. They want careers, not just contracts. Growth, not just grind. Security, not just stock photos of smiling coworkers.

And they’re done settling. They’re done with the gaslighting, the performative HR seminars, the lip-service to “mental health” while workloads increase and headcounts shrink. They’re done being told that pizza parties are perks and burnout is character-building.

So no, it’s not that no one wants to work. It’s that no one wants to work for you — until you make it worth it.

And right now? You’re not worth it.

Fix your culture. Fix your priorities. Or get used to the silence when your job postings go unanswered.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
