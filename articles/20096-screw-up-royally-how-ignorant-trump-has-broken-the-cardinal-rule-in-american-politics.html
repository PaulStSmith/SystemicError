'Screw up royally': How 'ignorant' Trump has 'broken the cardinal rule in American politics'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Although James Carville is a scathing critic of President Donald Trump, the veteran Democratic strategist acknowledges that he knows how to work a crowd and often uses "smoke and mirrors" to his political advantage.

But in an op-ed published by the New York Times on April 14, the 80-year-old Carville emphasizes that Trump's "smoke and mirrors" won't work if his tariffs cause prices to soar and send the U.S. economy into a painful recession.

"The problem is that smoke and mirrors only work until you screw up so hard that no act of lunacy can pull the American people's attention elsewhere," Carville argues. "And boy, did the president just screw up royally. In what will certainly be recorded as one of the most ignorant acts of political leadership in American history, the president of the United States has now willfully damaged the global economy with his tariff chaos."

Carville adds, "Not only was this an act of economic warfare, it has broken the cardinal rule in American politics: Never destabilize the economy. With it, the Trump Administration is causing enormous damage to itself — and there can be no more distraction from this naked truth. This is where the Democrats have an opening. "

Democrats, according to Carville, will need to avoid political "distractions" and stay laser-focused on the economy.

"In the coming weeks and months," Carville explains, "many Americans are going to experience pressure and pain with the tariffs on China and the remaining tariffs on an array of goods and countries. Prices could rise sharply, consumer spending may well dry up, and we are already seeing evidence of surging mortgage rates and a weakened bond market. The Trump Administration will not be competent enough to dig us out."

Carville continues, "The path to stabilizing and strengthening the country starts when Democrats can take back the economic narrative from the Republican Party and persuade the majority of Americans to close the book on the Trump chaos. This can only be done if we avoid the distractions — whether it's Mr. Trump's third-term talk or Democratic infighting on social issues — and instead focus on the economic foundations that matter to Americans most. My fellow Democrats, it's time we transform our party into a projector for the economic pain of the American people."

Our Analysis:

Carville's Cruel Carnival: The Tariff Debacle and the Democratic Response

In what reads like a clarion call to the Democratic ranks, James Carville, a name synonymous with razor-sharp political strategy, has thrown down the gauntlet in the New York Times. His target? None other than President Donald Trump, a figure as polarizing as he is ensconced in the art of the spectacle. Carville, with the acerbic wit that's defined his career, slices through Trump's "smoke and mirrors" with a precision that leaves no room for ambiguity or doubt.

Trump's Tariff Turmoil

Carville's biting critique centers on Trump's recent foray into economic manipulation - namely, tariffs. Described by Carville as "one of the most ignorant acts of political leadership in American history," this move by Trump isn't just a blunder; it's an act of "economic warfare." Carville's choice of words here is neither accidental nor hyperbolic. It's a calculated indictment of a policy move that threatens to destabilize not just the U.S. economy, but the global market at large.

The consequences of these tariffs, as Carville rightly points out, are far-reaching. From soaring prices to a painful recession, the domino effect of this decision cannot be overstated. Yet, Carville's critique goes beyond mere economic analysis. He hits at the heart of Trump's political strategy - distraction. "Smoke and mirrors only work until you screw up so hard that no act of lunacy can pull the American people's attention elsewhere," he asserts. It's a stark reminder that the theatrics of politics can only shield so much before reality bites back.

The Democratic Counter: A Call to Arms

Perhaps the most compelling aspect of Carville's op-ed is his call to action for the Democratic Party. He perceives the tariffs debacle not just as a failure on Trump's part, but as an opportunity for Democrats. The strategy? Economic narrative reclamation. Carville's vision for the Democratic response is clear - avoid the distractions, sidestep the infighting, and focus on the economic pain of the American populace.

This strategy, while straightforward, is far from simplistic. It's a rallying cry for the Democratic Party to coalesce around a unified message, one that speaks directly to the economic anxieties of the American people. Carville's advice to his fellow Democrats is to transform the party into a "projector for the economic pain of the American people." It's a powerful image, one that suggests not just empathy, but action.

Final Thoughts

In the final analysis, Carville's op-ed is more than just a critique; it's a strategic blueprint for the Democratic Party. His message is unambiguous: Focus on the economy, stupid. It's a callback to his own successful strategy in the 1992 presidential campaign, but with a twist that's uniquely suited to the challenges of today's political landscape.

The question that looms large, however, is whether the Democratic Party will heed Carville's call. The opportunity is there, laid bare by Carville's scathing analysis, but seizing it will require a level of discipline and focus that has often eluded the party in recent times. As the economic repercussions of Trump's tariffs begin to make themselves felt, the political fallout will undoubtedly follow. Whether the Democrats can capitalize on this to "close the book on the Trump chaos," as Carville suggests, remains to be seen. But make no mistake, the Carville playbook is one that demands attention, grounded as it is in the realities of political warfare and economic pain. The ball, as they say, is now in the Democrats' court.
