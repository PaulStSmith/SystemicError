'I was speechless': Ex-White House reporter stunned by Trump supporter's reply

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unwavering Allegiance to Donald Trump: A Reflection of America's Polarized State

People sit wearing MAGA hats at The Grove”, in Los Angeles, California, U.S., April 14, 2025. REUTERS/Mario Anzuoni

A former White House reporter who covered Donald Trump's first term was shocked by an answer provided by a survivor of the recent floods in Texas.

Brian J. Karem, who famously asked Trump if he would commit to a peaceful transfer of power if he lost the 2020 election, wrote a piece on Substack called, "Donald Trump - the ultimate pimp." In the article, he argues that "the Texas floods are an allegory for the United States in 2025."

He goes on to describe a recent trip to Texas, in which he interviewed those impacted.

"Unfortunately, many of them still love Trump. In Kerrville I ran across some of them," he wrote. "I asked a woman who stood along the road to get a glimpse of her 'favorite President' if she thought the federal government was doing enough for those suffering from the floods. 'Hell no,' she said as she waved a Donald Trump sign."

“We tried to call FEMA and couldn’t get through, and it’s been a week and people are still missing. Those poor families. My cousin’s daughter is still missing," the woman said.

Karem replied, “President Trump says the federal government has responded very well."

Her response is what stunned the veteran reporter.

“Well, President Trump knows best,” she replied, according to the article.

"I was speechless," Karem wrote Sunday. "Elsewhere, near Ingram, I ran across people who had, by word of mouth, alerted others to the floods, had driven to affected campgrounds and risked their own lives to help evacuate others."

Karem also said there was plenty of blame to go around.

"As much as the response to this year’s flood is a reflection on Donald Trump - (who no longer wants to eliminate FEMA after witnessing the devastation in the Hill Country firsthand) and about Texas governor Greg Abbott as well as all the local, state and federal legislators who could have done something over the years but didn’t – it is more importantly a reflection of who we are," he wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Unwavering Allegiance to Donald Trump: A Reflection of America's Polarized State

In a recent article that reads more like a cautionary tale than a simple report, former White House reporter Brian J. Karem offers an eye-opening perspective on the unwavering support for Donald Trump, even in the face of adversity. Karem's piece, titled "Donald Trump - the ultimate pimp," uses the tragic backdrop of the recent floods in Texas as an allegory for the United States in 2025, painting a grim picture of blind allegiance and systemic failure.

The Dichotomy of Disaster and Devotion

Karem's journey to Texas, intending to cover the aftermath of the devastating floods, turns into an exploration of the psychological grip Trump continues to hold over many Americans. His encounter with a woman in Kerrville, who, amidst the chaos and suffering brought on by the floods, still stands loyally by Trump, is both shocking and revealing. Her reaction to the federal government's inadequate response—“Well, President Trump knows best,”—highlights a disturbing trend of unwavering devotion that transcends logic and reality.

A Misplaced Faith in Leadership

This anecdote is a stark example of the cognitive dissonance prevalent among Trump's base. Despite her own admission that the federal government, under Trump's leadership, failed to provide timely assistance during a crisis, the woman's faith in Trump remains unshaken. This scenario raises critical questions about the influence of political leaders and the dangers of cult-like loyalty.

The Bigger Picture: A Nation Divided

Karem's article goes beyond the floods and Trump's fandom, touching upon a broader issue plaguing the United States: the failure of its leaders across the board. By implicating not only Trump but also Texas Governor Greg Abbott and various state and federal legislators for their inaction over the years, Karem points to a systemic problem of negligence and unaccountability.

The Role of Media and Public Perception

What Karem’s piece also subtly underscores is the complex role media plays in shaping public perception. Through his recounting and analysis, readers are prompted to consider how narratives are constructed and the impact they have on public opinion and individual belief systems. The portrayal of Trump as “the ultimate pimp” is a bold, yet thought-provoking metaphor that encapsulates the manipulation and exploitation of trust for personal or political gain.

Conclusion: A Nation at a Crossroads

In essence, the article serves as a mirror reflecting the deeply entrenched polarization within American society, where allegiance often overshadows reason. The floods in Texas, as described by Karem, are not just a natural disaster but a metaphor for a nation struggling to navigate through its divided soul. The unwavering support for Trump, despite evident failures, is a symptom of a larger, more complex issue of identity, belief, and the human condition.

As we move forward, the challenge lies not only in addressing the immediate crises that face us but in bridging the vast chasm that divides us. Karem’s piece, through its vivid storytelling and critical analysis, invites us to reflect on our values, our leaders, and ultimately, the direction in which we are headed as a society.
