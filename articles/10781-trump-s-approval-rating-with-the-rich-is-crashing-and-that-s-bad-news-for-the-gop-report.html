According to a report from Newsweek, following his return to the Oval Office after four years in the wilderness, Trump sat at + 6 with 49 percent approving of his job performance among those polled earning yearly incomes of more than $100,000.

Since that time it has been nothing but bad news for the president who was underwater with that demographic by -10 percent in July before taking a stunning dip to -16 percent in August.

According to Newsweek’s Kate Plummer, Republicans running for re-election in the 2026 midterms may end up being collateral damage, taking the brunt of the blame for Trump, who will not be on the ballot, yet looming over every voter.

According to Thomas Gift of the University College London Centre on U.S. Politics, "Many wealthier voters are likely to be concerned about the potential impact of Trump's more populist and confrontational style on markets. Trump's on-again, off-again tariffs are doing nothing to calm the nerves of investors, especially as markets whipsaw in response to statements he's made about the macroeconomy."

Newsweek’s Plummer added that Trump’s “Liberation Day” announcement appears to have been a turniunbg point with that voters, writing, “in April, the net approval rating fell to -14 percentage points with 43 percent approving and 57 percent disapproving. At the time, stocks had plunged after Trump announced his ‘Liberation Day’ tariffs.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Policies: A Tale of Discontent Among the Wealthy

In an era where political decisions reverberate through the economic landscape with unprecedented speed, the recent article from Newsweek sheds light on a rather unexpected fallout from President Donald Trump's economic policies. It's a tale not just of a trade war that's left American consumers grappling with the consequences, but of a demographic that was assumed to be the beneficiary of Trump's economic strategies expressing their discontent. Wealthy Americans, long perceived as the stalwarts of Republican economic policy, are turning their backs on Trump, a movement that's as telling as it is surprising.

The Disenchantment of the Wealthy

The core of the issue lies in Trump's megabill and his rather controversial trade policies, including a series of on-again, off-again tariffs that have sent the markets into a tizzy. Initially sitting at a +6 approval rating among Americans earning more than $100,000 annually, Trump has seen this number plummet to a startling -16 percent. This sharp decline isn't just a number—it's a manifest sign of disenchantment among a demographic that was once considered Trump's stronghold.

The Ripple Effects of Populism

Thomas Gift's comments to Newsweek encapsulate the crux of the problem. Trump's populist and confrontational approach, particularly his erratic tariff policies, have left investors jittery. The markets despise uncertainty, and Trump's economic maneuvers have been anything but predictable. The term "whipsaw" used by Gift isn't just metaphorical; it's an accurate depiction of the volatile state of the markets in response to Trump's policies.

Collateral Damage and Republican Concerns

The implications of this growing discontent among wealthy voters are far-reaching. With the 2026 midterms on the horizon, there's a palpable concern among Republicans about being caught in the crossfire of Trump's unpopularity. The fact that Trump himself won't be on the ballot does little to alleviate these worries, as his shadow looms large over the party and its electoral prospects. The term collateral damage used in the article is apt, suggesting that the repercussions of Trump's policies and his diminished approval ratings could extend far beyond his own political career.

The Turning Point: "Liberation Day"

One can't discuss the eroding support for Trump without mentioning the so-called "Liberation Day" tariffs, which appear to have been a turning point. The announcement led to a significant drop in stocks, a tangible indicator of the market's reaction to policy instability. This moment is highlighted as a pivotal point in Trump's declining approval ratings among the wealthy, underscoring the direct impact of his trade policies on his political fortunes.

Conclusion: A Stark Reckoning

The story unfolding from the Newsweek article is one of stark reckoning. It challenges the assumption that wealthier Americans are unwavering in their support for Trump, highlighting a broader discontent with his economic policies. As the 2026 midterms approach, the Republican Party finds itself in a precarious position, navigating the turbulent waters of Trump's legacy. The dissatisfaction among wealthy Americans serves as a potent reminder of the complex interplay between politics, economics, and voter sentiment. As always, the true measure of these policies will be felt not in the corridors of power but in the lived experiences of the American people, across all income brackets.
