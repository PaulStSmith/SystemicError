Blood Money and Chalk Dust: The Sinister Economics of ICE's Paycheck Priorities

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Follow the Money, Follow the MoralityWho Benefits From This?The Wage Gap Between Service and SurveillanceThis is By DesignAbolish ICE. Fund the Future.

Let’s start with the hard truth: ICE, the Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency, is dangling six-figure salaries to hire people with no educational requirements to run facilities that critics and human rights advocates rightly call concentration camps. Supervisory roles are being advertised with pay between $132,000 and $185,000. Managerial positions at these detention centers, where families are torn apart and children are locked in cages, reportedly reach a staggering $306,000 a year.

Meanwhile, the average starting salary for a teacher in the U.S. is $46,526. Nurses, who bear the full weight of public health on their backs, start at around $68,485. Both require not just a bachelor’s degree, but often a master’s or multiple certifications. Teaching requires passion, skill, and emotional resilience. Nursing requires clinical expertise, empathy, and the endurance of a saint. ICE, apparently, just requires a pulse and a willingness to participate in human suffering.

This is not a case of bureaucratic oversight. It’s not an economic constraint. It’s a strategic and ideological investment in state violence over public welfare. It is the clearest articulation yet of where American priorities lie: not with education, not with healthcare, but with control.

Follow the Money, Follow the Morality

The lie that we "can’t afford" to pay teachers and nurses more is exactly that: a lie. The federal government has no problem allocating massive funds to ICE and its network of private contractors. These companies are profiting handsomely from misery, backed by taxpayer dollars. GEO Group and CoreCivic, two of the largest private prison contractors, rake in hundreds of millions each year from immigration detention alone. And they pay their facility administrators accordingly.

But let’s call it what it is: blood money.

Contrast that with the public education system, where teachers buy school supplies with their own money and juggle overcrowded classrooms for wages that can barely cover rent. Or the public hospitals where nurses pull double shifts and suffer burnout-induced trauma for salaries that pale in comparison to the trauma they endure.

The disparity is obscene, not just economically but morally. We have created a system where brutality is rewarded and compassion is punished. Where tearing families apart is a fast-track to a six-figure income, and saving lives or educating children is a dead-end financial decision.

Who Benefits From This?

A sick, uneducated population is easier to manipulate, easier to pacify, and easier to imprison. If you starve the educational system, you choke off critical thinking. If you starve the healthcare system, you keep people too busy surviving to resist. And if you pump billions into ICE, prisons, and police, you build the infrastructure of authoritarianism brick by brick.

This is not a bug in the system. It is the system.

Let’s remember that the same apparatus that overfunds ICE also underfunds mental health care, cuts social safety nets, and allows teachers to strike for the bare minimum of dignity. It creates a permanent underclass, criminalizes poverty, and weaponizes immigration as a pretext for state-sanctioned terror.

The Wage Gap Between Service and Surveillance

We love to talk about how teachers and nurses are “heroes.” But apparently, they’re not heroic enough to deserve a livable wage. Meanwhile, the enforcers of cruelty are offered lucrative deals with zero educational barriers. This isn’t just wage disparity; it’s wage warfare. It tells every young person in this country that empathy is for suckers, and cruelty is a career path.

America doesn't just tolerate this disparity; it enshrines it. It builds it into the DNA of its institutions. And while teachers' unions fight tooth and nail for a few thousand dollars more per year, ICE signs off on contracts with obscene bonuses for people willing to preside over suffering.

This is By Design

If the state wanted to prioritize education and healthcare, it would. But that’s not where the political power lies. The beneficiaries of this system—corporate prison contractors, fear-mongering politicians, and racially motivated enforcement bureaucracies—have no interest in raising an enlightened, healthy populace. They want obedience, not brilliance. They want detention, not dignity.

And so we get what we fund: a militarized border, not an educated society. A bloated deportation machine, not a public health infrastructure. Razor wire, not pencils.

Abolish ICE. Fund the Future.

The answer is not reform. It’s abolition. ICE is not a rogue actor; it is functioning exactly as designed. No amount of tinkering around the edges will turn a punitive surveillance agency into a humanitarian one. It must be dismantled, root and stem.

Those funds must be redirected. Pay teachers like they build the future—because they do. Pay nurses like they save lives—because they do. Stop rewarding cruelty. Start investing in care.

We are not broke. We are being looted. And the thieves wear uniforms with badges and bank statements bloated with blood money.

It’s time we fought back with ballots, bullhorns, and unrelenting clarity: we choose schools over cages. We choose healing over harm. We choose the future over fear.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.

Written and published by Paulo Santos for Systemic Error.
