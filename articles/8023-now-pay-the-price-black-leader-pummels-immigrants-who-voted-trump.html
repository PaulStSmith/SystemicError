'Now pay the price!' Black leader pummels immigrants who voted Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 6, 20255 min read

The Trump administration’s recent decision to end immigration protections for Haitians shocked many in the migrant community, but for Caribbean-U.S.-based journalist Felicia Persaud, it was a predictable outcome driven in part by pro-Trump immigrant voters.

The Department of Homeland Security announced last month that it would be terminating Temporary Protect Status for Haiti, first enacted under the Biden administration due to the ongoing political instability in the Caribbean nation that has endured centuries of exploitation and political meddling from Western nations.

The announcement was made hours after the deadly shooting in Washington, D.C. in which an Afghan migrant was suspected of shooting two National Guard members. The Trump administration also announced that every Green Card Holder from a list of 19 countries “of concern” would be reviewed for possible revocation; all of which, Persaud noted, were non-white majority.

“Whether Trump is threatening Afghans, Haitians, Venezuelans, Iranians, or Somalis, the message is the same: Brown and Black immigrants are the enemy,” she wrote in an analysis published Friday in the St. Kitts Gazette. “White ones are not.”

With the Trump administration’s termination of immigration protections for Haitians, more than 500,000 Haitians in the United States now face deportation when the TPS expires on Feb. 3, 2026. The DHS website encourages Haitian migrants to self-deport, and promises them a “complimentary plane ticket” and a “$1,000 exit bonus.”

While white voters made up a majority of Trump’s supporters in 2024, a significant share of hispanic and migrant communities backed the current president as well, and in unprecedented numbers for a Republican presidential candidate. To those voters specifically, Persaud had a message.

“Sadly, the many immigrants who helped him get elected are the ones whose families will now pay the price,” Persaud wrote.

“I’m referring especially to the Haitians For Trump and the Cubans, Somalians, Venezuelans and other Hispanic immigrants who despite hearing the xenophobic rhetoric for years and seeing it in action in the first administration, voted for him. Meanwhile, the truth is simple, as Jeremy McKinney of the American Immigration Lawyers Association noted: ‘Radicalization and mental illness don’t know nationality.’

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of a Predictable Tragedy

In an era where the pendulum of political decisions swings wildly, the Trump administration’s recent move to terminate immigration protections for Haitians isn’t just a shockwave through the migrant community; it’s a stark revelation of the predictable nature of xenophobic policies under a guise of national security. The Department of Homeland Security’s announcement to end Temporary Protect Status (TPS) for Haiti, a country still grappling with the scars of political instability and exploitation, is not merely a policy shift but a testament to a broader, more sinister agenda.

The Illusion of Security at the Expense of Humanity

The timing of this decision—coming hours after a tragic shooting in Washington, D.C., involving an Afghan migrant—reeks of opportunistic policy-making, exploiting a moment of national grief to push through measures that further demonize non-white immigrants. By casting a shadow of suspicion over Green Card holders from 19 countries of concern, all non-white majority, the administration sends a clear message: Brown and Black immigrants are not welcome here.

Felicia Persaud’s analysis in the St. Kitts Gazette cuts through the administration’s rhetoric with precision, highlighting the racial undertones of these policies. The revocation of TPS for over 500,000 Haitians living in the U.S. is not just a policy change; it’s a life-altering nightmare that forces families to face deportation or self-deport with a so-called ‘complimentary plane ticket’ and a ‘$1,000 exit bonus.’ This move is not about law and order; it’s about using policy as a weapon against the marginalized.

A Betrayal of Supporters

What’s even more disheartening is the betrayal felt by pro-Trump immigrant voters, some of whom are from the very communities now under threat. The irony is bitter and cruel. These communities, including Haitians For Trump and others, showcased their support in unprecedented numbers for a Republican presidential candidate, only to be repaid with policies that threaten to tear their families apart. The administration’s message to these supporters is clear: Your vote was valued, but your presence in this country is not.

The Misguided Conflation of Radicalization and Nationality

Persaud’s message is a wake-up call to those who believed they could align with xenophobic policies without facing consequences. The conflation of radicalization with nationality, as noted by Jeremy McKinney of the American Immigration Lawyers Association, is a dangerous and misguided approach that undermines the very fabric of America’s identity as a melting pot of cultures and nationalities.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in the Making

The Trump administration’s decision to end TPS for Haitians is not an isolated incident but a part of a larger, more disturbing trend of policies that target non-white immigrants. This decision, made under the pretext of national security, is a stark reminder of the systemic errors plaguing our political landscape. It exposes the administration’s willingness to exploit racial prejudices and fears to achieve its objectives, sacrificing the principles of diversity and inclusion on the altar of political expediency.

In this moment of reckoning, it’s crucial to remember that the strength of a nation lies not in its ability to exclude, but in its capacity to embrace. The termination of TPS for Haitians is a policy failure that will be remembered not for its supposed intent of safeguarding the nation, but for its undeniable impact on tearing apart communities and families who sought nothing but safety and a chance to contribute to the tapestry of American life.
