Officials walk back announcement 2 National Guard troops died in DC shooting

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Grave Miscommunication: The Perils of Premature Announcements

Law enforcement members gather in a cordoned-off area after two National Guard members were reportedly shot near the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 26, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

State officials have walked back an initial announcement that two National Guard troops deployed from West Virginia died after being shot near the White House on Wednesday.

West Virginia Gov. Patrick Morrisey initially announced their deaths in a statement on X.

“It is with great sorrow that we can confirm both members of the West Virginia National Guard who were shot earlier today in Washington, DC have passed away from their injuries. These brave West Virginians lost their lives in the service of their country. We are in ongoing contact with federal officials as the investigation continues. Our entire state grieves with their families, their loved ones, and the Guard community. West Virginia will never forget their service or their sacrifice, and we will demand full accountability for this horrific act.”

He later walked back that claim.

“We are now receiving conflicting reports about the condition of our two Guard members and will provide additional updates once we receive more complete information.Our prayers are with these brave service members, their families, and the entire Guard community.”

At least one of the troops was shot in the head. It appeared they were targeted, John Miller, chief law enforcement and intelligence analyst for CNN, reported on air.

The troops exchanged gunfire with the shooter before being shot, two law enforcement sources told CNN. The shooter, whose name was not released, remained in critical condition.

Our Analysis:

A Grave Miscommunication: The Perils of Premature Announcements

In a recent incident that has sent shockwaves through the community and the nation at large, state officials, led by West Virginia Gov. Patrick Morrisey, found themselves in a whirlwind of confusion and retraction following an erroneous announcement regarding the tragic fate of two National Guard troops deployed near the White House. Initially declared deceased, the governor’s premature statement not only sparked nationwide grief but also a storm of questions regarding the accuracy and reliability of official communications in times of crisis.

“It is with great sorrow that we can confirm both members of the West Virginia National Guard who were shot earlier today in Washington, DC have passed away from their injuries...” This initial announcement, laden with finality and despair, was later retracted, revealing a glaring lapse in the verification process of critical information. The correction, albeit necessary, casts a shadow of doubt on the efficiency of information dissemination at the state level and raises concerns about the implications of such miscommunications on the morale of the service members’ families and the general public.

The governor’s retraction, “We are now receiving conflicting reports about the condition of our two Guard members and will provide additional updates once we receive more complete information,” underscores a chaotic flow of information and a distressing lack of coordination among the involved authorities. This debacle not only tarnishes the credibility of the governor’s office but also highlights a systemic flaw in the management of sensitive information during crises.

Further complicating the narrative is the account of the incident provided by John Miller, chief law enforcement and intelligence analyst for CNN, who reported that the troops were seemingly targeted, with at least one troop shot in the head. According to Miller, the troops exchanged gunfire with the shooter before being shot, an element that adds a layer of heroism to the already dramatic event but also begs for a more nuanced and careful relay of facts by official sources.

The condition of the shooter, who remains in critical condition and unnamed, adds another layer of intrigue and speculation to the incident. The lack of disclosure regarding the shooter’s identity and motive leaves a gaping hole in the story, fueling speculation and potentially endangering the integrity of the ongoing investigation.

In conclusion, this incident not only represents a tragic moment of violence but also serves as a stark reminder of the critical need for accuracy, transparency, and timeliness in official communications. The premature announcement by Gov. Morrisey, followed by a hasty retraction, is a textbook example of how not to handle crisis communication. It reflects a systemic error in the processing and dissemination of information that demands immediate rectification to restore public trust and ensure the dignity of service members and their families is preserved. In an era where information travels faster than the speed of light, the entities at the helm of such narratives must wield their power with a heightened sense of responsibility and utmost care for the truth.
