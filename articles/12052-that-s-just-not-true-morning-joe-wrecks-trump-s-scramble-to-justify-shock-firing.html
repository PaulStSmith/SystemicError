'That's just not true!' Morning Joe wrecks Trump's scramble to justify shock firing

Joe Scarborough. (MSNBC)

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough knocked down President Donald Trump's justification for firing a labor statistician after the latest jobs report showed an economic slowdown.

The president abruptly fired Erika McEntarfer as commissioner of labor statistics Friday, hours after the agency reported job growth in the U.S. had slowed to a near-halt. He claimed the Joe Biden appointee had cooked the books in favor of the Democratic administration ahead of last year's election, only to say those numbers were wrong after votes were cast.

"The more he talks about it, the more he just proves how wrong it was to fire this woman," the "Morning Joe" host said. "First of all, he just said all the numbers leading up to this report were positive. He always praised her numbers. If he's going to blame this on her instead of actually data, it wasn't her."

Trump has long praised McEntarfer for her work, and Scarborough said his claim about her job numbers before the 2024 election misrepresented the facts.

"He keeps going back to this, this line about, 'Oh, they put out a blockbuster report right before the election' – no, they didn't," Scarborough said. "That's just not true. I mean, from the reports I saw, it was actually tepid and weak right before the election. So again, I know, I know, facts don't matter in this case. But, again, it seems as far as fluctuations go, and that always happens, that has always happened. It always will happen, as far as the numbers being revised the next month or two."

"The numbers are revised not because of any political bias, and not because of any political bias over the past two decades," he added, "but because of employers not participating as much and it taking another month to get more details and pulling it out of the employers, and then they revise it a month later. So, again, on all of these points, they just don't hold any water."

Our Analysis:

The Firing of Erika McEntarfer: Unpacking the Pretext and the Reality

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough recently delivered a scathing critique of President Donald Trump's decision to fire Erika McEntarfer, the commissioner of labor statistics, following a jobs report indicating an economic slowdown. Trump's rationale? A baseless accusation that McEntarfer, a Joe Biden appointee, had manipulated the data to benefit the Democratic administration. This move, and the justification behind it, encapsulates the Trump administration's penchant for attacking the credibility of professional civil servants and institutions whenever the narrative doesn't align with its agenda. Let's dissect the layers of this egregious act.

The Illusion of Justification

Trump’s decision to fire McEntarfer hours after the release of a less-than-stellar jobs report is not just impulsive but reckless. His accusation that she had "cooked the books" prior to last year's election reeks of desperation and a misunderstanding (or deliberate misrepresentation) of how labor statistics are compiled and revised. The labor statistics process, as Scarborough aptly points out, is subject to fluctuations and revisions, not due to political bias but because of the nature of data collection and the participation rate of employers. This is a crucial fact that Trump conveniently ignores or, perhaps more accurately, attempts to obscure with his baseless allegations.

Trump's Selective Praise and Condemnation

The hypocrisy of Trump's actions cannot be overstated. Previously lauding McEntarfer's work when it suited him, Trump's sudden volte-face is a testament to his transactional view of truth and loyalty. Scarborough highlights this inconsistency, noting that Trump's current criticism starkly contrasts with his earlier praise of the labor statistics under McEntarfer's stewardship. Trump’s selective condemnation based on a single report that doesn't fit his narrative is not just intellectually dishonest; it's a dangerous precedent for how critical economic data is perceived and politicized.

The Political Weaponization of Economic Data

Scarborough's critique sheds light on a broader, more disturbing trend: the politicization of economic data. When the president of the United States attacks the integrity of the Bureau of Labor Statistics and its commissioners based on unfounded claims, it not only undermines the institution but also erodes public trust in the data it produces. This is not a simple matter of disagreement with data interpretation; it's an assault on the very foundations of factual governance.

The Reality of Data Fluctuation and Revision

The essence of Scarborough's argument—that labor data is subject to revision not because of political bias but due to the inherent complexities of data collection—cannot be emphasized enough. This reality is well-known to economists, statisticians, and anyone with a basic understanding of how labor markets are analyzed. By ignoring this, Trump not only displays a profound ignorance (or willful disregard) of the process but also attempts to manipulate public perception for his own political gain.

Conclusion: The Dangerous Game of Disinformation

In the end, Scarborough's analysis is a damning indictment of Trump's attempt to discredit a professional statistician for doing her job. The firing of Erika McEntarfer under such pretenses is not just an attack on her but on the integrity of the labor statistics process and the factual basis of economic policy-making. In a political era increasingly characterized by disinformation, such actions pose a real threat to democratic governance and the informed electorate that sustains it.

In Trump's world, it seems, loyalty and the perpetuation of his narrative trump facts and the professionals dedicated to uncovering them. This is not just systemic error; it's systemic erosion of trust in facts—a dangerous path with far-reaching consequences.
