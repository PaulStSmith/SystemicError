President Donald Trump’s reaction to the release of the Epstein files shows that he is living in an “alternate reality,” Chris Brennan wrote for USA Today.

Nothing better exemplifies this, wrote Brennan, than when Trump responded to the controversy over the files by saying “I thought that was finished” and “There’s tremendous backlash. A lot of people are very angry that pictures are being released of other people that really had nothing to do with Epstein.”

“Trump was right that the Epstein files have prompted a backlash. But that anger is directed at the Department of Justice for not obeying the law passed in November to force the release of the Epstein files,” wrote Brennan. “That law required the full release by Dec. 19. The Department of Justice didn’t meet that legal deadline.”

Trump went on to say that this controversy was manufactured by “mostly Democrats and a couple of bad Republicans.” In reality, Brennan noted, “Trump knows every Republican in the House and Senate, with just one exception, voted with all the Democrats in both chambers last month in favor of the law requiring the release of the Epstein files. Trump signed that into law on Nov. 19 after spending months trying to intimidate Republicans into dropping their support for the measure” — which became all but impossible as survivors of Epstein testified in Congress and made their stories known.

Further revelations dropped this week with the release of new files, including an alleged letter from Epstein to fellow sex offender Larry Nassar naming Trump as a fellow traveler with young girls, which the DOJ has said is fake.

All of this, wrote Brennan, stands in stark contrast with how former President Bill Clinton, “who was also notoriously chummy with Epstein, reacted to the files this week. Clinton spokesperson Angel Ureña, in a statement posted on social media, called out the Department of Justice for selectively releasing some files while not following the law. ‘Someone or something is being protected,’ Ureña wrote while declaring, ‘We need no such protection.’”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s “Alternate Reality” and the Epstein Files Controversy: A Critical Analysis

In an article penned for USA Today, Chris Brennan skillfully dissects the bewildering reaction of President Donald Trump to the release of the Epstein files, portraying a man seemingly entrenched in an “alternate reality.” Brennan’s analysis, sharp and meticulously researched, highlights the chasm between Trump’s perception and the stark, uncomfortable truths of the Epstein scandal.

Trump’s Reaction: A Study in Deflection

Trump’s bewildering statements, “I thought that was finished” and his comments on the “tremendous backlash” against the release of certain photographs, illuminate a desperate attempt to divert attention. His insistence that the outrage is directed at the release of photographs of innocents is not just a gross misrepresentation; it’s a glaring attempt to overshadow the real controversy. The actual backlash, as Brennan rightly points out, is aimed squarely at the Department of Justice for its failure to comply with the law requiring the full release of the Epstein files by December 19. Trump’s narrative doesn’t just bend the truth; it snaps it in half.

A Manufactured Controversy?

Trump’s claim that the controversy is “manufactured” by Democrats and a few rogue Republicans is a slice of fiction so fantastical it would make George Orwell blush. Brennan’s rebuttal is grounded in irrefutable fact: nearly every Republican in Congress, alongside their Democratic counterparts, voted for the law mandating the release of the Epstein files. Trump himself signed this into law, a fact he conveniently omits in his narrative. This bipartisan support, spurred by the harrowing testimonies of Epstein’s survivors, stands in stark opposition to Trump’s portrayal of partisan witch-hunting.

The Fake Letter and Selective Outrage

The release of an alleged letter from Epstein naming Trump as a “fellow traveler with young girls,” swiftly debunked by the DOJ as fake, adds layers of intrigue and disinformation to the saga. Trump’s focus on this fake letter, and his omission of the bipartisan frustration over the DOJ’s handling of the Epstein files, underscores a selective outrage that benefits only him.

Clinton’s Contrasting Reaction

In stark contrast, former President Bill Clinton’s response to the partial release of the Epstein files, as articulated by spokesperson Angel Ureña, calls out the Department of Justice for its selective compliance with the law. Ureña’s statement, “Someone or something is being protected,” juxtaposed with Trump’s narrative, paints a damning picture of selective transparency and accountability.

Conclusion: The Real “Alternate Reality”

Brennan’s analysis exposes the dangerous “alternate reality” Trump occupies, one where facts are malleable and accountability is an alien concept. This isn’t just about Epstein or the salacious details contained within his files. It’s a glaring symptom of a broader disease plaguing Trump’s presidency: a chronic aversion to the truth when it inconveniences or implicates him.

In this narrative, Trump isn’t just a former president caught in the web of Epstein’s dark legacy. He’s a maestro of misinformation, conducting an orchestra of diversion and deception. Brennan’s article isn’t just a critique; it’s a clarion call for accountability, transparency, and the unyielding pursuit of truth, no matter how uncomfortable it may be.
