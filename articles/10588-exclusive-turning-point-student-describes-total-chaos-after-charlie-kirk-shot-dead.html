Exclusive: Turning Point student describes ‘total chaos’ after Charlie Kirk shot dead

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Tragedy: The Death of Charlie Kirk

Before conservative activist Charlie Kirk took the mic at Utah Valley University on Wednesday, 19-year-old Beck Dishman captured a 6-second video of a packed crowd of supporters in red Make America Great Again hats, waving American flags on the sunny afternoon.

Shortly after noon local time, Kirk, the co-founder and CEO of conservative youth organization Turning Point USA was shot in the neck.

Kirk, 31, died after the shooting, according to President Donald Trump and reports.

Dishman, the vice president of the university’s Turning Point USA chapter, told Raw Story in a phone interview he was standing at the back of the audience near a fountain when Kirk was shot.

“People, when you heard the shot, they just kind of trampled through the fountain. There's like a stampede,” Dishman said.

“Obviously traumatic.”

Beck Dishman (provided photo)

Dishman said he didn’t see Kirk get shot himself, but that his 17-year-old sister did, who was at the event with her high school.

“Just a horrific event. I pray for Charlie Kirk and pray for his family and hope they're doing okay,” Dishman said before the news of Kirk’s death was announced.

“The aftermath was just total chaos.”

Dishman said he was able to call and reunite with his sister. Everyone was evacuated from campus, and he witnessed SWAT teams on his way to a friend’s apartment near campus, Dishman said.

“We just regrouped and are just recovering a little bit,” Dishman said.

“Praying and hoping and crossing our fingers.”

Of his involvement with the school’s Turning Point student chapter, Dishman said: “Being involved in the government and in the processes that make our democracy function so well is important to me and just being able to get involved, alone, is a lot more than others in my generation are willing to do,” Dishman said.

“It comes at costs, but we can't let other people scare us.”

Caleb Chilcutt, president of the Turning Point USA chapter, declined to comment when reached by Raw Story.

Dishman sent Raw Story further comment via text message:

“TPUSA UVU has always been committed to spreading American Values,” Dishman said.

“We cherish the constitution. We cherish democracy. And we cherish our families. Today was an attack on all three. We are praying for Charlie and his family and we will never, regardless of the opposition, relent.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Tragedy: The Death of Charlie Kirk

Before diving into the heart of the matter, let's address the elephant in the room: the tragic and senseless loss of Charlie Kirk, the co-founder and CEO of Turning Point USA, is a devastating blow not only to his family and friends but to the political discourse in America. Kirk's death, as reported, following a shooting at Utah Valley University, is a chilling reminder of the volatility that can permeate political events.

The Setting and the Incident

The article paints a vivid picture of the event's atmosphere before the tragedy struck: a sunny afternoon filled with supporters donning red Make America Great Again hats, waving American flags—a hallmark of conservative gatherings. Amidst this setting, the violence that ensued is not only shocking but deeply ironic. Here was a gathering, presumably celebrating freedoms and American values, that became the backdrop for a heinous act that undermines those very principles.

The Immediate Aftermath

The chaos and panic described by Beck Dishman, the vice president of the university’s Turning Point USA chapter, in the wake of the shooting, are palpable. His account of people stampeding through a fountain to escape, and the traumatic experience of not only witnessing such an event but also fearing for loved ones, like his 17-year-old sister, captures the horror and confusion that violence breeds.

A Missed Opportunity for a Deeper Conversation

However, where the article falls short is in its exploration—or lack thereof—of the broader implications of such violence in political contexts. Dishman's comments about involvement in government processes and the importance of not being scared away by opposition are important. Yet, they skirt around the larger, more disturbing questions of how and why political spaces have become arenas where disagreements potentially end in bloodshed.

Turning Point USA's Response

The response from Turning Point USA, as relayed by Dishman, is noteworthy for its emphasis on American values, democracy, and family. However, it's also emblematic of a missed opportunity to address the underlying issues of political violence, gun control, and the increasingly divisive nature of American politics. The statement, "We will never, regardless of the opposition, relent," feels like a rallying cry when perhaps a call for reflection, dialogue, and a reevaluation of the politicization of virtually every aspect of American life is what's truly needed.

In Conclusion

The article, while informative, serves as a microcosm of a larger problem: the reluctance to confront the root causes of political violence and the ease with which it is normalized. The death of Charlie Kirk is a tragedy that should prompt serious questions about the state of American political discourse, the proliferation of guns, and the safety of public figures and citizens alike. Instead, we are left with a narrative that, while touching, barely scratches the surface of a deeply systemic issue.

In moments like these, it's crucial to remember that cherishing democracy means also challenging it to be better, safer, and more inclusive. Without this critical examination, we risk being trapped in a cycle of violence that threatens the very foundation of the democratic values we hold dear.
