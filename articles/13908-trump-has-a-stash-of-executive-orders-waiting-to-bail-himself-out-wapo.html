Trump has a 'stash' of executive orders waiting to bail himself out: WaPo

Prior to starting his second term, Donald Trump's inner circle began creating a "stash" of presidential executive orders and proclamations to not only dramatically alter how the government runs, but also for use when he needs to distract voters or change the narrative when he doesn't like what the press is reporting.

That is according to a report from the Washington Post's Natalie Allison, Emily Davies and Michael Birnbaum, who wrote that a travel ban order by the president on Wednesday was not tied to the terror attack on pro-Israel demonstrators in Boulder, Colorado, but was in initiated back in early January.

The Post is reporting, "The announcement was the latest example of how the Trump administration has dipped into its large reserve of proclamations and executive orders — many of which have been long in the making — to drive its chosen narrative, push the president’s priorities and sometimes change the subject when news coverage focuses on topics that Trump officials prefer to downplay."

According to the report, the president has a "stash" of such orders and other announcements at the ready as the mood strikes him or if he needs a distraction when things aren't going his way.

The report notes that Trump is on a record pace, reporting, he has signed 157 executive orders and 62 proclamations, during his second term, before adding, "Trump has already signed more orders than President Barack Obama in either term, and at the current clip, is likely to surpass this year Biden’s sum of 162 orders over four years."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: Trump's Executive Order Stash

In a brazen yet unsurprising revelation, The Washington Post has peeled back another layer of the Trump administration's playbook, uncovering a "stash" of presidential executive orders and proclamations. This cache, far from being a strategic reserve for national crises, seems to be a calculated tool for narrative control and political maneuvering. Let's dissect this report with the critical eye it demands.

Strategic Distraction or Governance by Whim?

At the heart of this report is an unsettling strategy: the use of presidential power not as a solemn duty to govern, but as a lever to manipulate public attention and discourse. The idea that executive orders are being pre-crafted, not in response to emerging national needs, but for the purpose of distraction or narrative control, is a stark deviation from the expected stewardship of the highest office in the land.

The travel ban highlighted in the article, purportedly unlinked to any specific or immediate threat, serves as a prime example of this tactic. Initiated in January, its announcement timing appears to be a calculated attempt to divert attention from unfavorable news coverage. Such actions not only undermine the gravity and purpose of executive orders but also erode public trust in the presidency's role as a guardian of national well-being.

A Record of Diversion

The sheer volume of executive orders and proclamations issued by Trump, as cited by The Washington Post, is staggering. With 157 executive orders and 62 proclamations in his second term alone, Trump eclipses the use of such powers by his predecessors. This prolific issuance, however, is not inherently commendable. Rather, it raises questions about the motivations behind this flurry of activity.

The comparison to Obama and Biden's use of executive orders is particularly telling. Surpassing Obama's count in either term and on track to outdo Biden’s four-year total, Trump's record suggests not a more efficient or proactive governance but a reliance on executive orders as a personal tool for agenda-setting and distraction.

The Ramifications

This strategy of using executive orders as distractions has far-reaching implications. First, it dilutes the potency and significance of these instruments of executive power. When orders are issued not based on necessity but on the desire to shift media narratives, it trivializes the executive's role in addressing actual national issues.

Furthermore, this approach to governance by distraction can lead to policy whiplash, where directives are given not with a long-term vision but with an eye on the next news cycle. This not only hampers coherent policy development but also undermines the stability and predictability essential for both domestic and international confidence in U.S. leadership.

Conclusion

The revelation of Trump's executive order "stash" and its reported use as a means to manipulate public discourse and distract from unfavorable news coverage is both a symptom and a cause of the erosion of democratic norms. It represents a shift from governance guided by principle to governance driven by personal agenda and expediency.

As the public becomes more aware of these tactics, the challenge will be to demand greater accountability and transparency in the use of executive powers. The presidency, after all, is a position of service to the nation, not a platform for narrative control or personal vendetta. This report from The Washington Post not only sheds light on a troubling strategy but also serves as a call to vigilance in preserving the integrity of our democratic institutions.
