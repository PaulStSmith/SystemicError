'Baffling': Inside the bizarre MAGA entourage given prime White House seats

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt takes a question during a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 22, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

A new report from The Guardian delves into the "new media" personalities given prime seats in the White House press room that many say are perpetuating propaganda through "fawning, softball, or otherwise baffling questions.”

One example is Podcast host Tim Pool, who joined the press room last week.

A known far-right conspiracy theorist, Pool was paid almost $10 million by Russian state media operatives to publish videos promoting Moscow’s interests and agenda, according to the Guardian.

In his question to Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, Pool attacked media outlets by asking, “I’m wondering if you can comment on their unprofessional behavior as well as elaborate if there’s any plans to expand access to new companies?”

“We want to welcome all viewpoints into this room,” Leavitt replied.

Another pro-Trump ‘influencer’ allowed into the press room is Dominick McGee. Known as Dom Lucre, the Guardian described him as a “self-styled Black Maga influencer.”

He is known to disseminate conspiracy theories on social media, including amplifying Trump’s false claims that the 2020 election he lost was fraudulent, and promoting the QAnon fiction that the so-called deep state was conspiring to usurp the president.

He asked Leavitt, “Is there any possibility for names such as Barack Hussein Obama, Hillary Rodham Clinton, to ever possibly get investigated for … any of the wrongdoings they might have done?”

The press secretary called the question “refreshing.”

One softball question Leavitt received came from TikTok creator Link Lauren.

Known as “Maga Malfoy” because of his resemblance to the Harry Potter character Draco Malfoy, he asked, “You’re a very high-profile young mother who seems to juggle and balance it all beautifully. What advice do you have to young parents out there who are starting their careers, having kids, building families, and trying to find that balance so desperately?”

Leavitt had no advice to offer.

The Propaganda Parade: A Closer Look at the "New Media" in the White House Press Room

In a recent exposé, The Guardian sheds light on the infiltration of "new media" personalities into the White House press room, revealing a disturbing trend that blurs the lines between journalism and propaganda. Among these personalities is podcast host Tim Pool, whose presence in the press room is nothing short of a mockery to journalistic integrity. Pool, a figure known for far-right conspiracy theories, was allegedly paid a staggering $10 million by Russian state media operatives to disseminate content favoring Moscow's agenda. This revelation not only raises questions about the ethics of granting such individuals a platform but also the potential implications for national security and public perception.

Pool's question to Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt about the "unprofessional behavior" of media outlets and the expansion of access to new companies is a masterclass in irony. It's akin to an arsonist inquiring about fire safety protocols. Leavitt's response, welcoming "all viewpoints," seemingly disregards the essence of journalistic responsibility — to challenge, investigate, and report truthfully, not to provide a stage for the dissemination of unfounded claims and foreign propaganda.

Moreover, the article introduces us to Dominick McGee (aka Dom Lucre), a "self-styled Black Maga influencer," whose inquiry about investigating Barack Hussein Obama and Hillary Rodham Clinton for nebulous "wrongdoings" reeks of political vendetta rather than a pursuit of truth. Leavitt's characterization of McGee's question as "refreshing" is bewildering, at best, and at worst, indicative of an administration's flirtation with authoritarian tactics — delegitimize the press, then replace it with loyal sycophants.

Then there's Link Lauren (or "Maga Malfoy"), whose softball question about balancing career and family to Leavitt could be dismissed as benign if it weren't so painfully staged. It serves as a distraction, a feel-good moment meant to humanize and soften the image of an administration that, in the same breath, entertains baseless accusations and conspiracy theories.

The inclusion of these personalities in the press room is not a trivial matter. It represents a strategic endeavor to reshape the narrative, to control the flow of information, and to delegitimize the very institution of the press. When characters like Pool, McGee, and Lauren are given prime seats, it's not diversity of thought that's being championed; it's a concerted effort to dilute fact-based reporting with entertainment, opinion, and outright falsehoods.

The Guardian's report is a chilling reminder of the ongoing battle for truth in an era where the lines between news and propaganda are increasingly blurred. As these "new media" personalities are welcomed into the fold, one must ask: where do we draw the line? When does the quest for varied viewpoints become a circus that undermines the very foundation of democracy?

In a healthy democracy, the press serves as a check on power, not a cheerleading squad for it. The White House press room should be a forum for tough, insightful questions that hold officials to account, not a stage for propagandists and influencers whose primary allegiance is to their benefactors or their own brand, rather than to the pursuit of truth.

The trajectory we're on is dangerous, and The Guardian's report is a wake-up call. It's high time we demand better — for the sake of our democracy, our security, and our right to the truth.
