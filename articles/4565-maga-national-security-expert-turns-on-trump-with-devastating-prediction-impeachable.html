MAGA national security expert turns on Trump with devastating prediction: 'Impeachable!'

A self-identified “America First” national security expert who has long supported Donald Trump now has some harsh words, and a major prediction, for the president’s new war in Iran.

CNN’s Natasha Bertrand recently reported that the CIA “has been working to arm Kurdish forces with the aim of fomenting a popular uprising in Iran,” while noting that “Iranian Kurdish opposition forces are expected to take part in a ground operation in Western Iran in the coming days and they expect US and Israeli support, a senior Iranian Kurdish official told CNN.”

That report caught the attention of longtime Trump voter and national security analyst David Pyne, who has a picture of Trump on his social media profile banner. Pyne is a former U.S. Army combat arms and H.Q. staff officer with an M.A. in National Security Studies from Georgetown University. He currently serves as Deputy Director of National Operations for the EMP Task Force on National and Homeland Security, according to his bio for The National Interest.

Pyne said, “As his hopes for a quick war have been dashed by Iran’s refusal to stop fighting after the US foolishly turned their supreme political and religious leader into a martyr, Trump is reportedly considering sending in US Special Forces into Iran along with Kurdish militia forces. Trump is now planning to invade a country that is nearly four times larger than Iraq with twice as many people.”

He then made a prediction:

“The result will likely be a war that could last a year or more with several thousand US military servicemen coming home in body bags just in time for the November mid-term elections fueling Democrat calls for his third impeachment.”

Pyne further said, “I’m guessing that Trump didn’t fully think through the likely outcomes of his Iran forever war folly before he made the rash decision to start the biggest regional war in the Middle East in thirty-five years.”

In a separate post, he said, “George W. Bush employed the US military as little more than an arm of the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps while he spent eight years fighting a war to transform Iraq from a secular republic into an Iranian vassal state and Islamist republic that cost the lives of tens of thousands of US lives including war related suicides,” before concluding with, “Now, President Donald Trump is subordinating the US military to Israel to further its expansionary designs against Iran rather than advance US national security interests, risking the lives of millions of Americans for no good reason in the process. This is an impeachable offense!”

Our Analysis:

The Folly of “America First” in Iran: A Critical Analysis

In a remarkable turn of events, a self-identified “America First” national security expert, David Pyne, who has long supported Donald Trump, has issued a scathing critique of the president’s new military strategy in Iran. This analysis will dissect Pyne’s major predictions and criticisms, juxtaposing them with the broader implications of Trump’s Iran policy on U.S. foreign relations and national security.

Trump’s “Iran Forever War”

Pyne’s disillusionment stems from what he perceives as Trump’s misguided military adventurism in Iran. He highlights a critical flaw in Trump’s strategy: the underestimation of Iran’s resilience and the nation’s refusal to capitulate after the U.S. escalated tensions by targeting its supreme political and religious leader. This miscalculation, according to Pyne, has led Trump to consider deploying U.S. Special Forces alongside Kurdish militia forces, thereby risking a protracted conflict in a country significantly larger and more populous than Iraq.

A War of Unprecedented Scale

Pyne’s prediction of a war “that could last a year or more” and result in “several thousand US military servicemen coming home in body bags” is not only a grim forecast but also a poignant reminder of the human cost of military interventions. This is particularly relevant as the timeline aligns dangerously close to the November mid-term elections, potentially galvanizing Democrat calls for Trump’s third impeachment.

A Failure of Strategic Thinking

The crux of Pyne’s argument lies in his assertion that Trump “didn’t fully think through the likely outcomes of his Iran forever war folly.” This critique points to a broader issue within the Trump administration: a propensity for impulsive decision-making without a comprehensive understanding of regional dynamics or the long-term consequences of military engagement.

Subordinating U.S. Military Interests

Perhaps the most scathing indictment is Pyne’s assertion that Trump is “subordinating the US military to Israel to further its expansionary designs against Iran rather than advance US national security interests.” This allegation of prioritizing foreign interests over American security is a sharp rebuke of Trump’s foreign policy, suggesting a betrayal of the “America First” doctrine.

The Echoes of Past Mistakes

Pyne’s comparison of Trump’s Iran strategy to George W. Bush’s Iraq policy is particularly striking. He argues that just as Bush’s efforts transformed Iraq into an Iranian vassal state, Trump is now risking American lives for a foreign policy that does not serve U.S. interests. This historical parallel serves as a cautionary tale of the potential consequences of misjudging the complexity of Middle Eastern geopolitics.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

David Pyne’s critique represents a significant departure from his previous support of Donald Trump, signaling a growing concern among “America First” proponents about the direction of U.S. foreign policy under Trump’s leadership. Pyne’s analysis underscores the need for a strategic reassessment of U.S. military engagements abroad, prioritizing American lives and national security interests over misguided foreign adventures.

In this context, Pyne’s call for impeachment over Trump’s Iran policy is not just a critique of a single foreign policy decision but a broader indictment of a presidency that, in his view, has repeatedly sacrificed American interests on the altar of political expediency and foreign influence. Whether one agrees with Pyne’s assessment or not, his arguments demand serious consideration and debate among policymakers, scholars, and the American public alike.
