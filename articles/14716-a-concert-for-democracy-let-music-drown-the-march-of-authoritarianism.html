A Concert for Democracy: Let Music Drown the March of Authoritarianism

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 19, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Contrast Couldn’t Be ClearerThe Symbolism of the Lincoln StepsWhy I’m Writing This

On June 14th, the streets of Washington, D.C. are slated to echo with the thunder of M1 Abrams tanks, military flyovers, and the spectacle of a man-child’s ego on parade. Branded as a celebration of the U.S. Army’s 250th anniversary, it just so happens to land on the birthday of a man who’s made a career of dodging service and demanding fealty: Donald J. Trump.

But what if, on that same day, on the same soil, a very different kind of America stood up and sang?

What if Bruce Springsteen and Taylor Swift—two icons who’ve never shied from speaking truth to power—stood together on the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his dream to a nation desperate for justice? What if instead of a militarized circus rolling down Constitution Avenue, the world turned its eyes and ears toward a Concert for Democracy?

The Contrast Couldn’t Be Clearer

On one side: a parade that reeks of authoritarian cosplay. Tanks retrofitted for optics. Soldiers in wool uniforms sweltering for a billionaire’s PR reel. A grotesque waste of tens of millions in taxpayer dollars—all to stroke the insecurities of a man who once called veterans who died in service “suckers” and “losers.”

On the other: guitars, piano, and microphones. Lyrics that inspire, protest, and remember. Artists who use their platforms to elevate the people, not trample them under steel tracks.

The Symbolism of the Lincoln Steps

The Lincoln Memorial is not just a monument—it’s a stage where American ideals have been proclaimed in their rawest, truest form. To host a concert there on June 14 wouldn’t just be an act of art. It would be a reclaiming.

It would remind the country, and the world, that patriotism isn’t about pageantry or parades. It’s about protecting freedom, dissent, and dignity for all.

Let the tanks rumble by. Let the jets scream overhead.

Let the music rise higher.

Why I’m Writing This

I’m not a political operative or concert promoter. I’m a writer, a citizen, and—perhaps like you—someone watching the creeping normalization of militarized nationalism with rising dread.

I'm far from the marble monuments of Washington. But I still believe in the power of ideas to cross oceans. I believe someone out there can make this happen.

So maybe I can’t organize it. But maybe I can spark it.
Maybe you’re the one who can.

#ConcertForDemocracy

Let’s make it trend. Let’s call on artists to take the stage—not for themselves, but for the people.

Let’s show the world that democracy doesn’t march in jackboots.
It sings.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
