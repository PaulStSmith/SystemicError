'Never seen anything like it': Expert stunned by Pam Bondi's bizarre responses

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 8, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi looks on as she testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C. on Oct. 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

A legal expert called Attorney General Pam Bondi‘s reaction to questions from members of Congress on Tuesday during a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing “remarkably, completely antagonistic.”

Former Deputy Assistant Attorney General Harry Litman told CNN that Bondi “refused to give answers at every turn.”

“She gave no answers, really,” Litman said. “Anything she could deflect, she did, but was really remarkable. You’ve seen fiery moments with AGs at other hearings. She came in guns a blazing with pre-drafted soundbites, and just whenever there was something she needed to answer, she substituted instead the sort of ‘when did you stop beating your wife’ kind of slurs.”

He pointed to Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and his fiery interaction with Bondi after he asked her about a reported 2024 bribery scheme involving White House Border Czar Tom Homan receiving a bag of cash with $50,000. It led to an FBI probe, though Homan has not been charged, and senators pressed Bondi about what happened to the money.

She deflected “from some really basic questions” during the hearing, Litman said. “Ask the FBI, she said.”

Litman called Bondi’s reactions “a concerted strategy, both not to give any responses, but also to have a sort of, you know, outrage, bombastic kind of presentation that she was even there and that I, I assume she hopes, observers who aren’t paying a lot of attention just take away from it.”

He said it was unusual.

“But it was remarkably unresponsive,” Litman added. “And remarkably like I’ve never seen anything like it. Completely antagonistic and contemptuous to the senators”

At some points, Bondi said she would not say anything, then “proceeded to give details, self-serving details, and then and only then shut things down.”

“In any event, the notion I think it’s just puzzling,” he said. “But what really was salient to me is she’ll talk a little bit — she’ll talk a little bit about Epstein and then shut things down again with personal insults. You know, Schiff was making a real point. It’s an oversight hearing. This is where you give us answers. And with this sort of calculated bombast, she refused to give answers at every turn.”

Our Analysis:

The Antagonistic Antics of Attorney General Pam Bondi: A Masterclass in Deflection

In a display that could only be described as a calculated circus of evasion, Attorney General Pam Bondi turned a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing into a spectacle of obfuscation and contempt. Her performance, if one could grace it with such a term, was not only a disservice to the principles of transparency and accountability but also a brazen showcase of political theater at its most cynically effective.

Bondi’s Strategy: Deflect, Distract, Dismiss

Former Deputy Assistant Attorney General Harry Litman’s observations to CNN encapsulate the bewildering nature of Bondi’s testimony. Describing her approach as “remarkably, completely antagonistic,” Litman paints a picture of an Attorney General who seemed less interested in addressing the concerns of the Senate Judiciary Committee and more focused on deploying a smokescreen of soundbites and slurs.

The interaction between Bondi and Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) serves as a prime example of her tactics. When pressed about a reported 2024 bribery scheme involving White House Border Czar Tom Homan, Bondi’s response was to essentially tell the senators to “Ask the FBI,” a deflection that does little to clarify the situation and much to avoid accountability.

A Concerted Strategy of Obfuscation

What Litman identifies as a “concerted strategy” on Bondi’s part to evade giving any substantial responses underlines a troubling trend in political discourse: the prioritization of outrage and bombast over substance and clarity. Bondi’s performance, characterized by its antagonism and contempt, was not a failure of communication but a deliberate choice to prioritize political theater over the democratic process.

The most alarming aspect of Bondi’s testimony was her refusal to engage in meaningful dialogue. At points, she offered nothing more than self-serving details before abruptly shutting down the conversation with personal insults. This not only undermines the very purpose of an oversight hearing but also disrespects the senators and, by extension, the public they represent.

The Erosion of Democratic Accountability

What stands out from Litman’s critique and the article’s portrayal of the event is the broader implication for democratic accountability. When officials like Bondi choose to adopt a posture of disdain towards the oversight process, it erodes the foundational checks and balances that safeguard our democracy. The Senate Judiciary Committee hearing was intended as a mechanism for oversight and accountability, yet Bondi’s responses—or lack thereof—highlight a disturbing contempt for this process.

In the end, Bondi’s testimony can be seen as emblematic of a larger problem: the increasing tendency of political figures to engage in performative antagonism at the expense of substantive governance. As Litman rightly points out, the calculated bombast employed by Bondi served only to obfuscate and deflect, leaving the public with more questions than answers.

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Accountability

Bondi’s antagonistic performance should serve as a wake-up call for those committed to the principles of transparency and accountability. The tactics of deflection, distraction, and dismissal have no place in a democratic society that values open and honest dialogue. As observers and participants in this democracy, it is our responsibility to demand better from our public officials, insisting on a standard of conduct that respects the democratic process and prioritizes the public interest over political spectacle.
