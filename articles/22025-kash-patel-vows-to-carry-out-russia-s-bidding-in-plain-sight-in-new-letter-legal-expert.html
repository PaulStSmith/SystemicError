Kash Patel vows to carry out 'Russia's bidding in plain sight' in new letter: legal expert

The FBI Chief Is Smearing the People Who Detected Russian Influence

The Setup

According to the reporting, Kash Patel is using an unsigned, undated letter to attack the FBI unit that helped flag Russian efforts to frame Democrats before the 2020 election. The same unit, Marcy Wheeler notes, also identified attempts by John Solomon and Rudy Giuliani to launder foreign influence operations aimed at Joe Biden. Patel’s move is not a correction of the record. It is a political reversal of it.

Who Has Power

The real power here sits with Patel himself. He runs the FBI. He gets to disband units, direct investigations, and decide which institutional narratives get amplified. That makes his complaint about “abuses” from a “different era” less like accountability and more like a power-holder reaching backward to criminalize the people who caught foreign interference in the act.

What the Letter Is Doing

The letter’s language tries to launder a simple retaliation into a neutral institutional review. It talks about “balanced” briefings, “all foreign threats,” and “accountability,” but the target is obvious: the bureau personnel who investigated Russian influence and the political actors tied to it. The absence of FBI stationery, the lack of a date, and the reliance on a spreadsheet from Tulsi Gabbard released by Bill Pulte all point in the same direction. This is not a serious evidentiary process. It is a propaganda object.

Blame Is Being Flipped

The misdirection is the point. The story does not present confused bureaucrats stumbling around a hard problem. It describes a deliberate campaign to recast the exposure of foreign influence as institutional misconduct. Patel is, by the account given here, treating the investigators as the suspect class and the compromised material as if it were a fresh revelation. That is how authoritarian politics protects itself: it punishes the people who document the crime and elevates the people who enabled it.

Russia Is Still the Frame

The reporting also places this in a larger and uglier context. Patel is reportedly preparing to travel to Moscow and St. Petersburg, and a Russian outlet with a history of airing FSB material was already speculating about kompromat and a backchannel to Vladimir Putin. That is not background noise. It is the environment this propaganda push is operating in. The message is being broadcast in plain sight, wrapped in official language and timed before the midterms.

The Larger Pattern

This is the governing logic of contemporary right-wing state capture: use the institutions of law enforcement to delegitimize oversight, recycle discredited informants, and turn foreign interference into a partisan smear against Democrats. The Democratic Party is not the villain in that script; it is the target because it remains the institutional barrier to authoritarian consolidation. When an FBI director moves to punish the people who caught Russian influence operations, the story is not bureaucratic confusion. It is a state apparatus learning how to protect power by rewriting the record.
