The Fog of Trump’s War: Lies, Memes, and Authoritarian Fantasies

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 8, 20256 min read

Inspiration for This Piece

This article is inspired by the grotesque display of Trump’s authoritarian cosplay in early September 2025. On September 6th, Trump posted a Truth Social entry that fused a Vietnam War fever dream with his obsession over “deportations.” The AI-generated image showed him dressed as a U.S. Army officer from Apocalypse Now, helicopters over the Chicago skyline, flames licking the horizon, with the caption: “Chicago about to find out why it’s called the Department of WAR.” A day later, when reporters confronted him about the obvious meaning of his words, Trump raged at them and declared their questions “fake news.” What followed was a cascade of denials, spin, and excuses from his allies, all while the original post remained visible to anyone with an internet connection.

This article digs into the meaning of these events, how they expose Trump’s authoritarian ambitions, his inability to keep his own lies straight, and the danger of normalizing this grotesque behavior.

Act I: The Meme That Wasn’t Just a Meme

Let’s start with the Truth Social post itself. Trump wrote:

“I love the smell of deportations in the morning...”

“Chicago about to find out why it’s called the Department of WAR 🚁🚁🚁”

The attached image was unmistakable: Trump styled as Lieutenant Colonel Kilgore from Apocalypse Now, Chicago engulfed in fire, helicopters swarming the skies. The text scrawled across the bottom read: “Chipocalypse Now.” This was no subtle policy message. It was a fantasy of urban war, complete with cinematic napalm. The “Department of WAR” reference tied into Trump’s ongoing push to rename the Department of Defense—because in his mind, America should stop pretending to defend and start embracing its role as an aggressor.

No serious observer could misinterpret this. Trump was explicitly equating deportations with warfare, targeting Chicago, and glamorizing violence. If Barack Obama had ever posted an AI image of himself torching Dallas with attack helicopters, the right would have had aneurysms for a decade. But for Trump, this was business as usual.

Act II: The Denial

The next day, September 7th, Trump was confronted about the post. NBC’s Yamiche Alcindor asked what seemed like the most obvious question in the world:

“Are you trying to go to war with Chicago?”

Trump’s response was predictable: rage, gaslighting, and contempt.

“When you say that, darling, that’s fake news.”

“Be quiet. You don’t listen. You never listen. That’s why you’re second-rate. We’re not going to war. We’re going to clean up our cities. That’s not war, that’s common sense.”

So, within 24 hours, Trump had transformed his own words into “fake news.” The very imagery he had commissioned, the very text he had typed, suddenly didn’t exist. Anyone repeating it was the problem. In Trump’s world, the lie is not just an option—it is the reality, and pointing to the truth makes you “second-rate.”

This is the hallmark of authoritarian gaslighting: he creates a spectacle of violence, then punishes anyone who dares to believe him.

Act III: The Spin Machine

Of course, Trump’s allies scrambled to clean up the mess. Former ICE director and current White House border czar Tom Homan appeared on CNN and offered this gem:

“The president’s words were taken out of context. We’re going to war with the criminal cartels, with illegal aliens, with public-safety threats.”

This was laughable on its face. The post mentioned Chicago by name. Twice. The imagery was Chicago’s skyline. It wasn’t Juárez. It wasn’t Tijuana. It wasn’t some drug lord’s compound. It was the Windy City under siege. Homan’s excuse was a desperate attempt to retrofit “serious” policy onto what was, in reality, an unhinged propaganda poster.

Governor J.B. Pritzker put it plainly: “The President of the United States is threatening to go to war with an American city.” Exactly. That’s what the post communicated. Any attempt to spin it otherwise is insulting to basic intelligence.

Act IV: The Lies Collapse Under Their Own Weight

The real problem here isn’t just that Trump lied. It’s that he lied so sloppily that his own lies can’t hold together. He couldn’t even keep a consistent story across 24 hours. On Saturday, he was a warlord general threatening Chicago. On Sunday, he was a misunderstood statesman fuming at the press for repeating his words. By Monday, his allies were inventing fantasies about cartels and “public-safety threats.”

This is Trump’s fatal weakness: his ego demands that he always be the alpha male, always the biggest show in town, always the hero of his own meme. But when those memes are too grotesque to defend, he panics, denies, and lashes out. He wants all the benefits of being an authoritarian without the responsibility of standing behind his threats. His lies devour themselves because they’re built on nothing but impulse.

Act V: The Authoritarian Test Balloon

It’s tempting to dismiss this episode as Trump being Trump—loud, stupid, and unserious. But that misses the point. Posts like these are test balloons for authoritarianism. By framing deportations as “war,” Trump is normalizing the militarization of immigration enforcement. By plastering Chicago under attack, he’s signaling that entire cities can be treated as battlefields. By renaming the Department of Defense the “Department of WAR,” he’s openly discarding even the pretense of restraint.

And ICE is the real engine behind this fantasy. Under Trump, ICE becomes not just a deportation agency but a domestic military force—a Praetorian Guard for the MAGA regime. That’s why ICE must be abolished. Left unchecked, ICE is the perfect vessel for transforming Trump’s memes into reality: raids, roundups, citywide crackdowns, all justified as “common sense” war.

Act VI: The Media’s Dilemma

Trump’s game puts the media in an impossible bind. If they take him literally, they’re accused of hysteria and “fake news.” If they ignore him, his authoritarian fantasies metastasize unchallenged. The press has been here before. “Take him seriously, not literally” was the mantra of 2016. That framing gave him cover to say whatever he wanted without accountability. Now, nearly a decade later, the same problem persists.

But the correct approach is clear: take him both seriously and literally. When a president posts a picture of himself declaring war on Chicago, that’s news. When he then denies it, that’s also news. The contradiction itself is the story—the gap between Trump’s fantasies and his denials is where authoritarianism takes root.

Act VII: The Real Cost

This is not harmless posturing. Every time Trump posts “jokes” about deportations as war, real people suffer. Immigrant communities feel terrorized. Cities get painted as enemies. Federal agencies like ICE are emboldened to act as shock troops rather than public servants. And the political culture erodes further, as violent rhetoric becomes just another meme in the churn of online propaganda.

The danger isn’t just that Trump might actually send helicopters over Chicago. The danger is that his supporters internalize the idea that their fellow Americans are enemies, that cities can be bombed in effigy, that war against immigrants is patriotic. Once that mental shift takes place, real violence is inevitable.

Conclusion: A Man Who Can’t Keep His Own Lies Straight

Trump’s September 6th-7th debacle is more than just another example of his instability. It’s a case study in how authoritarianism feeds on lies, and how those lies collapse under their own contradictions. He wants to be the general in his own meme war, but when confronted with the consequences, he lashes out and denies reality. His inability to keep his own lies in order is not a trivial detail—it’s a reflection of a chaotic, dangerous mind that cannot be trusted with power.

ICE, his weapon of choice, remains a standing threat to democracy. To abolish ICE is to strip away the machinery that makes Trump’s war fantasies possible. To hold Trump accountable is to refuse the gaslighting, to insist that his words and images mean exactly what they say.

We are not obligated to treat his violent memes as harmless jokes. We are not required to let him wriggle out of his own words. We owe it to the truth—and to every community he targets—to expose the lie, hold it up to the light, and refuse to let it pass as anything but what it is: authoritarian propaganda from a man unfit to govern.
