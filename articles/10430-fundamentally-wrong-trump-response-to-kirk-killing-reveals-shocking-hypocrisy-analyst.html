'Fundamentally wrong' Trump response to Kirk killing reveals shocking 'hypocrisy': analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Hypocrisy UnveiledThe Dangerous PrecedentConclusion

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump concludes an onstage interview with moderator Charlie Kirk, founder of Turning Point USA, during a youth forum titled Generation Next, at the White House in Washington, U.S. March 22, 2018. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst/File Photo

Sarah Longwell, publisher of The Bulwark, said on Monday that there is something "fundamentally wrong" with the way the Trump administration has responded to the assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk.

Kirk was killed last week when a gunman shot him in the neck during a talk at Utah Valley University. A 22-year-old suspect has been arrested in the case, and is allegedly not cooperating with authorities, according to officials.

Kirk's death set off a firestorm on the right, with some political leaders calling for a permanent national divorce. President Donald Trump has described the Democratic Party as an extremist organization and Vice President JD Vance said during a podcast interview on Monday that there can be "no unity" with people who celebrated Kirk's murder.

Longwell noted that there is a "hypocrisy" behind the Trump administration's posturing on the issue during a new episode of "Bulwark Takes" on Monday.

"The hypocrisy is obviously self-evident," Longwell said. "I think the bigger problem is that it's pretextual."

Longwell said the Trump administration and others on the right have equated Kirk's murder with George Floyd's death in 2020, which set off nationwide protests.

After Floyd's death, private citizens publicly shamed people who celebrated the death on social media, which caused some people to lose their jobs. Republicans protested the shaming then, but are now using the powers of government to the same ends, Longwell argued.

"It's deeply asymmetric, but also wrong. It is just fundamentally wrong," Longwell said. "But that's what they are doing. They are trying to use this as a reason to shut down funding and chill funding structures on the left so that they can try to keep people from beating them in elections."

Our Analysis:

The Dichotomy of Response: Trump Administration and the Assassination of Charlie Kirk

The recent assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk has opened up a Pandora's box of political rhetoric, with the Trump administration's response highlighting a profound hypocrisy that is as transparent as it is troubling. Let's dissect the layers of contradiction and the dangerous precedent it sets for the political discourse in the United States.

The Hypocrisy Unveiled

Sarah Longwell, publisher of The Bulwark, hits the nail on the head when she points out the "fundamentally wrong" stance of the Trump administration. The comparison of Kirk's murder to George Floyd's death and the subsequent reactions are not just apples and oranges; they are indicative of a broader, more systemic issue of political opportunism at its worst.

The death of Charlie Kirk is, without a doubt, a tragedy. However, the manner in which the Trump administration and its allies have leveraged this event is not just hypocritical; it's dangerously so. After George Floyd's death, the outcry and the nationwide protests were a response to systemic racial injustice and police brutality. The shaming of those who celebrated Floyd's death was a grassroots reaction to a blatant disregard for human life.

In stark contrast, the response to Kirk's assassination by the Trump administration and some political leaders on the right is an orchestrated attempt to weaponize tragedy. The calls for a "permanent national divorce" and the labeling of the Democratic Party as an extremist organization by President Trump are not just inflammatory; they are tactically divisive.

Vice President JD Vance's comment that there can be "no unity" with those who celebrated Kirk's murder only adds fuel to the fire of division. This is not a call for justice or an appeal to better angels; it is a calculated move to further polarize an already deeply divided nation.

The Dangerous Precedent

The real danger lies not just in the hypocrisy but in the strategy behind it. Longwell's observation that this is a pretextual maneuver to "shut down funding and chill funding structures on the left" is a stark warning. It's a clear indication that the tragedy is being used as a political tool, not just to gain sympathy, but to actively suppress opposition.

This strategy of leveraging tragedy for political gain and to suppress dissent is not just "fundamentally wrong"; it's a threat to the democratic principles of free speech and political participation. When the powers of government are used to silence or hinder political opponents, democracy is weakened, and the path to authoritarianism is paved.

Conclusion

The assassination of Charlie Kirk and the response it has elicited from the Trump administration reveal a disturbing trend of exploiting tragedy for political gain. The hypocrisy of comparing Kirk's death to George Floyd's, the dangerous rhetoric calling for division, and the strategic attempt to suppress opposition funding are all indicative of a broader systemic problem.

It's a stark reminder that in the current political climate, tragedy is not just mourned; it's mined for political capital. This is not just "deeply asymmetric"; it's a clear and present danger to the principles of democracy and free discourse. The exploitation of tragedy for political gain must be recognized, condemned, and most importantly, countered with steadfast commitment to truth, justice, and the democratic process.
