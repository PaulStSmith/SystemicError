'Absolute disregard': Trump skewered over 'reckless' botched covert mission

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20254 min read

In his first term, President Donald Trump personally approved a Navy SEALs team to secretly plant an electronic device on a North Korean fishing boat. The device would let the United States intercept the communications of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un during high-level nuclear talks with Trump.

But in an explosive report, The New York Times wrote that the team failed their mission in a deadly way. Fearing that they had been spotted, the SEALs opened fire on the boat, killing the innocent fishermen onboard.

Trump never publicly acknowledged or notified key members of Congress who oversee intelligence operations, either before or after the mission. That lack of notification may have violated the law, reports the Times.

Social media exploded at the news.

“Yet another example of Trump's reckless and failed approach to dealing with nuclear-armed North Korea,” posted Arms Control Association Director Daryl Kimball on X.

“If this had happened under Obama or Biden the media would have crucified them,” said another X commentator.

Constitutional law professor Anthony Michael Kreis called the maneuver “an absolute disregard for Article I powers,” on X, adding, “Good thing we’re giving the Dept. of Defense nicknames.”

Another critic snidely described the drama on X as “future Nobel peace prize laureate” Donald Trump killing “a boat full of fishermen then didn’t tell Congress about it.”

“Dude… can the U.S. negotiate in good faith… like, at all?” demanded another critic on X.

The NY Times said the White House declined to comment.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's Shadowy Operations: A Critical Dissection

In an era where transparency in governance is more a buzzword than practice, the revelation of President Donald Trump's personal authorization of a Navy SEALs operation to plant an electronic device on a North Korean fishing boat plunges us into the murky waters of clandestine operations and their ethical ramifications. According to an explosive report by The New York Times, this mission aimed to intercept communications of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un but ended in a tragic massacre of innocent fishermen, highlighting a disturbing disregard for human life and legal protocols.

A Mission Gone Horribly Wrong

The operation's objective might sound like a plot lifted from a Cold War spy novel: intercept high-level nuclear talks by any means necessary. Yet, the real-world consequences of such missions bear a heavy toll. The SEALs, fearing compromise, resorted to lethal force against civilians, an action that starkly contrasts with any semblance of a moral high ground the U.S. claims to hold on the global stage. The killing of innocent fishermen isn't just a tragic collateral damage; it's a glaring testament to the failure of oversight and accountability mechanisms that are supposed to govern such covert operations.

Silence and Violation: The Legal and Ethical Quagmire

Trump's failure to notify key members of Congress about the mission is not just an oversight; it's a potential violation of the law. This omission points to a larger, more systemic issue within the administration's approach to foreign policy and intelligence operations—a cavalier attitude towards legal norms and a disturbing penchant for unilateral decision-making. The silence from the White House in the wake of the NYT report is deafening, serving only to underscore the administration's apparent indifference to accountability.

Public Outcry and the Hypocrisy of Media Coverage

The reaction on social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter) underscores the public's outrage and disbelief. Critics rightly point out the double standards in media coverage and the political discourse surrounding the incident. Had such a debacle occurred under Obama or Biden, the ensuing media frenzy and political fallout would likely have been apocalyptic. This disparity in treatment reveals the deep-seated biases in our media ecosystem, where the sensational often overshadows the substantive.

Moreover, the snide remarks about Trump's "future Nobel peace prize laureate" status juxtaposed with the grim reality of the operation's outcome highlight the absurdity and cynicism pervading public discourse. It begs the question: can the U.S. ever hope to negotiate in good faith on the international stage when its actions betray a profound disregard for human life and international norms?

A Reflection on Power and Responsibility

This incident is a stark reminder of the immense power wielded by those in the highest echelons of government and the catastrophic consequences when that power is misused. It raises fundamental questions about the nature of executive authority, the checks and balances purportedly in place to curtail its abuse, and the ethical considerations that must guide clandestine operations.

The saga of the failed Navy SEALs operation under Trump's aegis is not just a tale of a mission gone awry; it's a grim tableau of the systemic failures that allow such operations to be conceived, approved, and executed with little regard for legal constraints or human life. As we dissect this episode, let us not lose sight of the larger picture—a world where power unchecked by accountability or ethical considerations leads to tragedy and injustice. The need for transparency, accountability, and a reevaluation of the norms governing covert operations has never been more acute.
