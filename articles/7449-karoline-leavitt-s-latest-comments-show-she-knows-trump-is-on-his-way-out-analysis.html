Karoline Leavitt's latest comments show she knows Trump is 'on his way out': analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 20, 20255 min read

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt holds a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 26, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

As President Donald Trump’s support reaches new lows 11 months into his second term, White House officials continue to lavish the president with “obsequious praise,” a sign that top administration officials know Trump “is kind of on his way out,” The New Republic discussed Friday in its “Daily Blast” podcast.

One particularly “obsequious” example of Trump praise came from White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt on Thursday, who announced that the “highly respected Board of the Kennedy Center” had voted “unanimously” to rename the cultural center in Trump’s honor.

While the Kennedy Center Board holds no authority to rename the center, Leavitt’s language, The New Republic’s Greg Sargent argued, was telling of something greater going on behind the scenes.

“She’s echoing his language there, using phrases like ‘highly respected’ and saying ‘Donald J. Trump’ exactly the way he does all the time,” Sargent said. “I don’t think it’s a coincidence that she’s slathering him with this obsequious praise just after he was mercilessly mocked for his terrible speech to the nation the other night.”

Moira Donegan, a columnist for The Guardian, agreed with Sargent’s assessment, but suggested that Leavitt’s praise was indicative of White House officials being aware that Trump’s one iron-clad grip on the Republican Party was slipping away.

“I think that there is a real sense in Trumpworld that Trump needs to be bolstered, right? That he needs encouragement or sort of reassurance in public of his importance,” Donegan said.

“There is a sense, I think, definitely in the pundit class and in the Beltway, and I think this is inevitably beginning to penetrate the White House itself, that Trump is kind of on his way out. And that’s partly because of his really cratering poll numbers, even on issues like the economy and immigration that used to be his strongest polling issues.”

The apparent realization among White House officials that Trump is losing his grip on his own party, Donegan argued, and his subsequent need for praise, was due to the recent culmination of several factors.

“It’s about the Democratic sweep of the special elections in November. It’s about the fracturing within his coalition, which has really intensified following the death of Charlie Kirk,” she continued.

“And it’s about his, like, visible aging, his visible physical decline, which is becoming harder to ignore and really conspicuous after he made such a big deal in the 2024 cycle over the age and infirmity of President Joe Biden.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Enigma: A Symphony of Obsequious Praise and Political Decline

In a recent episode that could only be scripted in the surreal corridors of the Trump White House, we’ve witnessed yet another bizarre spectacle that epitomizes the current state of American political reality. According to The New Republic, the spectacle unfolds with White House officials, in what can only be described as a desperate bid for favor, showering President Donald Trump with “obsequious praise” amid declining support 11 months into his second term. This scenario is not just a reflection of the political circus that surrounds Trump but a glaring indicator of a deeper, more systemic rot within.

The Kennedy Center Farce

The episode involving White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt announcing the renaming of the Kennedy Center in Trump’s honor is particularly telling. This act of sycophancy, aside from being factually incorrect—given the Kennedy Center Board’s lack of authority to rename the center—serves as a stark reminder of the lengths to which Trump’s administration will go to stroke the president’s ego. Leavitt’s mimicry of Trump’s language, as pointed out by Greg Sargent of The New Republic, isn’t merely an act of flattery; it’s an orchestrated attempt to cushion the blow of Trump’s faltering image.

The Crumbling Facade

Moira Donegan of The Guardian takes the analysis a step further, suggesting that this pattern of praise is indicative of a growing realization within Trump’s inner circle that the man’s “iron-clad grip” on the Republican Party is loosening. This isn’t merely about Trump’s plummeting poll numbers; it’s about the symbolic dismantling of his cult of personality. The factors contributing to this decline are multifaceted—ranging from the Democrats’ victory in special elections to the visible signs of Trump’s aging and the fracturing within his base following the death of Charlie Kirk.

The Political Implications

What’s unfolding is not just the political decline of a president but a revelatory moment for the Republican Party and, by extension, American politics. The desperate cling to power, manifested in the baseless adulation of Trump, underscores a party in disarray, struggling to reconcile its identity in the wake of its leader’s fall from grace.

The obsequious behavior of Trump’s officials is a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend: the idolization of political figures to the point of absurdity, where facts are twisted, and reality is manipulated to preserve the fragile ego of a leader. This isn’t just about Trump or the Republican Party; it’s a cautionary tale about the dangers of personality cults and the erosion of democratic norms.

Concluding Thoughts

As the Trump administration continues to navigate its twilight amidst a cacophony of praise, one must wonder about the long-term implications of this spectacle. The farce surrounding the Kennedy Center, the unabashed sycophancy of Trump’s officials, and the president’s declining influence are not mere political aberrations; they are indicative of a systemic error in American politics where power, personality, and perception trump reality, ethics, and accountability.

In this era of political theater, the Trump spectacle serves as a stark reminder of what happens when the lines between leadership, loyalty, and lunacy are not just blurred but obliterated.
