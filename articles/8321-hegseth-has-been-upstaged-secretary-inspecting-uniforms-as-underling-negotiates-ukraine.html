'Hegseth has been upstaged': Secretary inspecting uniforms as underling negotiates Ukraine

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of the Absurd: Pete Hegseth’s War on Decorum

Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth and President Donald Trump attend the U.S. Army’s 250th Birthday parade. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

Self-styled “secretary of war” Pete Hegseth has been reduced to threatening uniform inspections as one of his subordinates takes part in talks to bring Russia’s brutal invasion of Ukraine to an end.

The secretary of defense has been publicly attacking Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) for taking part with other Democratic lawmakers in a viral video that President Donald Trump wants investigated as sedition, and Hegseth first threatened to court martial the retired U.S. Navy captain and astronaut and then taunted him online over an alleged uniform violation, reported NOTUS.

“These new probes centered on the video come as both Hegseth and FBI Director Kash Patel are under increasing internal pressure over a variety of issues,” the outlet reported in its newsletter.

“Hegseth has been upstaged, in a sense, by Army Secretary Dan Driscoll,” the report added. “Driscoll, a Yale Law School friend of Vice President JD Vance, was dispatched to Ukraine to try to end its war with Russia. Patel has been under withering criticism for his use of local FBI SWAT teams to protect his girlfriend. MSNOW reported that Patel’s days leading the FBI may be numbered.”

The defense secretary threatened to recall Kelly to active duty so he can face court-martial proceedings for taking part in the viral video reminding service members of their duty to follow “lawful orders’’ under the Uniform Code of Military Justice, and all six lawmakers revealed Tuesday the FBI wants to question them.

“In addition to Kelly, the other Democrats are: Reps. Jason Crow (Colo.), a former Army officer; Chrissy Houlahan (Pa.), a former Air Force captain; Chris Deluzio (Pa.), a former Navy lieutenant who served in Iraq; Maggie Goodlander (N.H.), who served in the Navy reserves; and Sen. Elissa Slotkin (Mich.), who worked at the CIA,” NOTUS reported.

Slotkin said she heard from the FBI’s counterterrorism division, and the lawmakers said the administration’s response indicated a need to remind service members of their oath to support and defend the U.S. Constitution.

“No amount of intimidation or harassment will ever stop us from doing our jobs and honoring our Constitution,” the four House members said in a joint statement.

Hegseth’s attacks on Kelly could potentially elevate him as a possible 2028 presidential candidate, and the individuals who post in Reddit’s military forum were among many critics who wondered why the defense secretary was concerning himself with political matters.

“What leader of ANY department EVER got into a social media pissing war with ANY former service member?” posted Reddit user Roy4Pris. “It’s f------ disgraceful.”

“What f------ general-level (and up) is busy scrutinizing the order of medals on someone’s uniform?” agreed Reddit user ObjectiveRodeo. “Guy with platoon-level thinking, that’s what.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Pete Hegseth’s War on Decorum

In a spectacle that would make Kafka blush, the saga of Pete Hegseth, self-styled “secretary of war,” unfolds with all the subtlety of a sledgehammer at a house of cards. The latest chapter in this tragicomic opera pits Hegseth against Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ) in a battle that transcends the physical realm, venturing into the absurdities of uniform inspections and sedition accusations. It’s a story so rich in irony and devoid of self-awareness, one might be forgiven for mistaking it for satire. But alas, this is the reality we find ourselves in.

Hegseth’s War on Political Enemies

At the heart of this debacle is Hegseth’s public attack on Senator Kelly for participating in a viral video that President Donald Trump has deemed sedition-worthy. The video, which reminded service members of their duty to follow lawful orders under the Uniform Code of Military Justice, has somehow become the hill Hegseth is willing to die on. His response? Threatening to recall Kelly to active duty for a court-martial over an alleged uniform violation. This move is not just a gross misuse of power; it’s a farcical attempt to weaponize military protocol against political adversaries.

The Distraction Tactic

Let’s not overlook the convenient timing of these probes and attacks. Hegseth, along with FBI Director Kash Patel, is under “increasing internal pressure over a variety of issues,” according to the NOTUS report. While Hegseth plays the role of a petty tyrant over uniform regulations, real issues like the ongoing war in Ukraine and the alleged misuse of FBI SWAT teams by Patel to protect personal interests are sidelined. It’s classic misdirection—focus on the trivial to distract from the substantive.

A Symphony of Hypocrisy

The crowning jewel of this absurdity is the outcry from both the public and military forums alike. The consensus is clear: Hegseth’s actions are not just unbecoming of his position; they’re an embarrassment. The notion that a high-ranking official would stoop to scrutinizing the order of medals on a veteran’s uniform or engage in social media spats over perceived slights is ludicrous. It betrays a startling lack of priorities and perspective, reducing the solemn responsibilities of his office to the level of a schoolyard quarrel.

Elevating Kelly, Diminishing the Office

Ironically, Hegseth’s attacks could serve to elevate Senator Kelly’s standing, potentially positioning him as a presidential contender in 2028. By choosing to engage in this petty warfare, Hegseth inadvertently shines a spotlight on Kelly, granting him a platform and public sympathy. It’s a strategic blunder of epic proportions, showcasing Hegseth’s inability to grasp the long-term implications of his actions.

Conclusion: A Call to Reason

In the end, this saga is more than just a series of missteps by Hegseth; it’s a symptom of a deeper malaise within our political and military leadership. The readiness to engage in trivial disputes, the weaponization of military protocol for political vendettas, and the glaring disregard for the pressing issues facing our nation are alarming signals. They point to a need for a return to dignity, respect, and professionalism—qualities conspicuously absent in Hegseth’s current campaign of distraction and defamation.

As we navigate these tumultuous waters, let us demand more from our leaders. Let us not be distracted by the theater of the absurd but instead focus on the substantive issues that will define our future. It’s time to raise the bar, not lower it to the depths of pettiness and spite.
