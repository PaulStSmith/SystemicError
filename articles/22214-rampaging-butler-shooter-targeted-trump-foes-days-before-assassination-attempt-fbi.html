Rampaging Butler shooter targeted Trump foes days before assassination attempt: FBI

Based on an article by Jordan Green, Investigative Reporter

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The File Is Not the Story

What the Reporting Actually Shows

The new FBI files add texture, not clarity: Thomas Crooks was seen tearing down anti-Trump signs days before he shot at Donald Trump in Butler, he had some anti-woke and Republican-leaning talk in school, he trained with guns, and his father sold him the rifle he used. That is the factual core. Everything else in the coverage tries to turn a political attack into a psychological puzzle.

Power Was in the Arena

The relevant institutional power in this story was not a confused teenager’s personality profile. It was the political machinery around Trump, the campaign rally, the Secret Service, and the gun culture that made a rooftop shot possible at all. Crooks pulled the trigger, but the setting was produced by institutions that normalize armed spectacle and then act shocked when it turns violent.

The consequence was immediate and real: Corey Comperatore was killed, two others were seriously injured, and Trump was elevated into a near-mythic figure days before his nomination. That political afterlife matters. Violence did not just injure bodies. It fed a martyr narrative for a man already built out of grievance, domination, and grievance theater.

The Motive Fog Is a Choice

The article’s weak point is its fixation on ambiguity. Two years later, the motive remains officially unclear, so the file is treated like a mystery box. But the more important question is not whether Crooks was a committed partisan. It is how the reporting keeps drifting toward personality diagnostics when the material facts are already plain: access to guns, training, fixation on distance shooting, and a direct line from private grievance to public assassination attempt.

That is not “confusion.” It is the standard American pattern of political violence being treated as an individual aberration instead of an outcome of the systems that arm, stage, and exploit it.

The Party of Martyrdom

The conservative ecosystem wants the Butler shooting to function as proof of Trump’s quasi-sacred status, not as evidence of the violent political culture it has helped cultivate. The source material notes that Trump was elevated to near-mythic status after the shooting. That is not incidental. That is the point. Authoritarian politics thrives on martyr stories, not accountability.

Meanwhile, the FBI release was pulled into circulation by Judicial Watch, a conservative nonprofit. Even the disclosure pipeline is political. The story is being fed through actors who prefer spectacle, insinuation, and post hoc narrative control over a serious accounting of how such an attack became possible.

The Real Lesson

The most revealing detail is not the Myers-Briggs test or the adolescent chatter about politics. It is the banal chain of access: a father selling a rifle, a young man practicing long-range shooting, a rally with a rooftop within reach, and a security state that only appears omnipotent after the shots have already been fired.

That is the larger pattern. American institutions are excellent at producing retrospective explanations and terrible at preventing the conditions that make political violence ordinary. They would rather catalog a killer’s personality than confront the armed, theatrical, and increasingly fascistic political culture that keeps turning public life into a target range.
