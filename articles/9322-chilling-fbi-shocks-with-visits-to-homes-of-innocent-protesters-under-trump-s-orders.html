'Chilling': FBI shocks with visits to homes of innocent protesters under Trump's orders

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A person passes by the FBI seal on the wall of the FBI headquarters, days after the Trump administration launched a sweeping round of cuts at the Justice Department, in Washington, U.S., February 3, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Under orders from President Donald Trump, the FBI has started visiting the homes of innocent protesters, according to a report.

In one case, special needs teacher Miles Serafini, 26, told independent journalist Ken Klippenstein that agents visited his home after he participated in a June protest at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement office in Arizona. The two agents, who only identified themselves as “James” and “Keith”, were recorded on Serafini’s security camera.

“We came out here to ask you questions regarding a protest that happened on the 11th of June,” one of the agents explained. “We’ve been just basically going around asking questions for a few people … and your name was brought up.”

Last month, Trump issued National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 (NSPM-7), which authorized the Department of Justice to treat “extremism on migration” as an indicator of terrorism. Attorney General Pam Bondi cited Trump’s memorandum in a Sept. 9 directive adding any necessary FBI agents to a “temporary ICE Protection Task Force.”

The president’s order suggested that “law enforcement can intervene in criminal conspiracies before they result in violent political acts.”

Serafini said the agents that visited his home “were trying to figure out the shadowy entity behind the protest.”

“What a waste of their time to go after s—t that doesn’t exist,” he added. “And they kept drilling me on the fact that I showed up alone, [that I didn’t] remember where I saw the flyer, and didn’t know anybody there. They told me that’s unusual and pretty suspicious — as if I was holding back information about whoever organized the protest. They kept insinuating that I was lying to them.”

Before leaving, the agents told Serafini that it might not be the last time they saw him. The warning left him concerned enough that he decided not to attend the recent No Kings protests.

“That is exactly the chilling effect on speech that the FBI investigating political matters risks creating,” Klippenstein noted.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Dance of Power: FBI’s Intimidation Tactics Under Trump’s Directive

In a move that reeks of authoritarian tactics, President Donald Trump’s administration has ostensibly weaponized the FBI against peaceful protesters, under the guise of combatting “extremism on migration.” A report detailing the experience of Miles Serafini, a 26-year-old special needs teacher, has shed light on this disturbing strategy. Serafini’s encounter with the FBI, after participating in a June protest at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) office in Arizona, is a chilling testament to the lengths the Trump administration is willing to go to suppress dissent.

The Veil of National Security

Under the National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 (NSPM-7), Trump has authorized the Department of Justice to categorize “extremism on migration” as a potential indicator of terrorism. This broad and alarmingly vague categorization is a red flag, signaling the administration’s intention to clamp down on any form of protest under the pretense of national security. The directive, as utilized by Attorney General Pam Bondi, has led to the creation of a “temporary ICE Protection Task Force,” effectively blending the lines between national security, immigration enforcement, and the suppression of free speech.

A Misguided Mission

The account of the FBI agents’ visit to Serafini’s home is a classic example of misguided priorities and a disproportionate response. Agents “James” and “Keith,” as identified on Serafini’s security camera, were on a mission to unearth the “shadowy entity” behind a protest—a protest that, by all accounts, was a legitimate exercise of free speech. Serafini’s bafflement at the agents’ insistence on finding a sinister organizer behind his solitary participation is both humorous and horrifying. The idea that attending a protest alone, without remembering where one saw the flyer, is considered “suspicious” is a stark illustration of the absurdity of the FBI’s approach.

A Chilling Effect on Free Speech

The warning issued to Serafini by the FBI agents—that their visit might not be the last—serves as a dire warning to all who dare to exercise their First Amendment rights. The fear instilled in Serafini, to the point of deterring him from attending future protests, encapsulates the “chilling effect” on speech that journalist Ken Klippenstein rightly identifies. This effect, a deliberate consequence of the FBI’s actions, undermines the very foundation of democratic engagement and the right to dissent.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The Trump administration’s use of the FBI to investigate and intimidate protesters under the thinly veiled pretext of combating “extremism on migration” sets a dangerous precedent. It not only misallocates valuable federal resources but also significantly encroaches upon the civil liberties of American citizens. The case of Miles Serafini is a stark reminder of the lengths to which this administration will go to silence its critics, under the guise of national security. This strategy, if left unchecked, poses a grave threat to the fundamental principles of democracy and free speech.

In a nation that prides itself on its democratic values, the actions of the Trump administration and its manipulation of federal law enforcement agencies stand in stark contrast to the ideals of freedom and liberty. It is imperative that such overreaches are scrutinized, criticized, and ultimately curtailed, to preserve the sanctity of the democratic process and the rights of individuals to voice their dissent without fear of retribution or intimidation.
