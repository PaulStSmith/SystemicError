Even Fox News seems rattled by Trump's latest rage boil

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Trump Is Testing the Soft Spots

Not a Meltdown, a Method

Trump’s latest election rant is being packaged as another episode of grievance theater. That framing is too generous. The actual story is power: a president using the machinery of government, and the threat of it, to pressure networks into carrying lies that can be fed back into the public bloodstream.

The source facts are plain. NBC, CNN, and some ABC affiliates refused to air the speech. Trump responded by escalating his attacks and dangling the possibility of pulling network licenses through the FCC. Fox, normally eager to launder Republican deceit, is now visibly wary because its Smartmatic litigation has made the cost of lying harder to ignore.

The Real Leverage

Trump is not asking for airtime. He is trying to turn state power into a compliance device.

That distinction matters. When a president can make broadcast access feel contingent on obedience, the issue is no longer a rude complaint about coverage. It is coercion. Ryan Enos calls it authoritarian for a reason: the point is to make resistance expensive enough that future refusals never happen. The threat does not need to be carried out to do its work. Intimidation is the mechanism.

The Networks Are Not the Heroes

The media framing often flatters the press for “standing up” to Trump. Fine, but let’s keep the scale straight. This is not a morality play where television executives discover courage. It is a calculation by corporations that understand legal exposure, reputational damage, and the risk of becoming accessories to fraud.

Fox’s caution is especially revealing. It is not suddenly committed to truth. It is constrained by lawsuits and by the simple fact that even its own audience is not infinitely pliable. Dominion and Smartmatic did what polite commentary rarely does: they imposed a cost. The network’s reluctance is evidence that consequences work when they are large enough.

Republicans Know It’s False

The most corrosive detail in the source is not Trump’s insistence on the 2020 lie. It is the behavior of Republican officials who know the claim is false and still feel obligated to keep orbiting it.

That is not confusion. That is submission. The party’s elected officials are telling themselves they want him to move on while refusing to cross him on the one issue that matters most to his power over them. That makes them participants in the lie, not accidental bystanders. They are helping preserve a criminal political reality because they fear the base, fear Trump, and fear what honesty would demand of them.

Democracy Under Pressure

Enos’s point lands because it cuts through the performance. If a defeated politician can keep trying to impose his preferred outcome after the electorate has rejected him, and if he can use the state to punish resistance, then the democracy is not healthy enough to absorb the abuse.

That is the larger pattern here: authoritarian politics does not always arrive wearing boots and a flag. Sometimes it arrives as a licensing dispute, a court threat, a cable segment, a forced replay of a lie, and a party too cowardly to say no. The system is not being misled. It is being probed, intimidated, and trained to flinch.
