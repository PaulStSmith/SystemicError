Fox admits to 'error' after burying clip of Trump wearing hat to fallen soldier ceremony

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he boards Air Force One to travel to Palm Beach International Airport, at Miami International Airport in Miami, Florida, U.S., April 3, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Fox News over the weekend apologized and admitted to an “error” after failing to air footage of Donald Trump wearing what some deemed a controversial choice of attire to the dignified transfer of deceased soldiers.

On Saturday, Trump came under fire for his behavior during a solemn moment in which six fallen soldiers killed in the president’s onslaught against Iran were honored. The president was seen during the event standing upright while others bowed their heads. He was also wearing a white baseball cap during the typically solemn event, which was broadcast in complete silence on CNN.

Instead of playing that video for its audience, Fox News said it played an old video from a different dignified transfer. A host later apologized.

“We inadvertently aired video from an older dignified transfer instead of the ceremony that took place yesterday,” he said. “We deeply regret the error.”

The development was flagged by attorney and journalist Aaron Parnas, who wrote, “Fox News admits to airing an old dignified transfer video on its program this morning, one that did not show Donald Trump wearing a white ball cap.”

Our Analysis:

Fox News’ “Error”: A Convenient Oversight or A Deliberate Act?

In what can only be described as a blatant disregard for journalistic integrity, Fox News ‘apologized’ for what they termed an “error” after failing to air footage of Donald Trump’s controversial attire during the dignified transfer of deceased soldiers. This so-called mistake not only raises questions about the network’s commitment to truthful reporting but also highlights a potentially deliberate attempt to shield Trump from criticism.

The Controversy: Trump’s Attire and Behavior

Donald Trump, a figure no stranger to controversy, attended a solemn event to honor the fallen soldiers killed in an onslaught against Iran. Yet, it was his choice of attire—a white baseball cap—during this dignified transfer that sparked outrage. This event, meant to pay respects to those who have made the ultimate sacrifice, was sullied by Trump’s decision to stand out rather than show reverence.

Fox News’ Convenient “Error”

Fox News, a network often criticized for its unabashed bias towards conservative figures, “inadvertently” aired an old video from a different dignified transfer, conveniently omitting Trump’s recent faux pas. This “error” was later acknowledged with an apology that felt more like a slap on the wrist rather than a sincere admission of wrongdoing.

The Apology: A host stated, “We inadvertently aired video from an older dignified transfer instead of the ceremony that took place yesterday. We deeply regret the error.” This statement, while acknowledging the mistake, lacks depth and fails to address the underlying issue: Why was the wrong footage aired in the first place?

The Bigger Picture: Fox News and Media Responsibility

This incident is not merely about a network making an “error”; it’s a glaring example of the challenges facing today’s media landscape. Fox News’ decision to air incorrect footage—whether by mistake or design—serves as a convenient way to shield Trump from the backlash of his inappropriate behavior.

Media’s Role in Shaping Perceptions

Media outlets have a profound responsibility to present facts accurately and without bias. When networks like Fox News fail to do so, it not only undermines their credibility but also contributes to a polarized and misinformed public. This “error” is a stark reminder of the need for vigilance and critical thinking when consuming news.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

Fox News’ “apology” for failing to air the correct footage is a band-aid solution to a much larger problem. This incident should serve as a wake-up call for the network and its viewers: Journalistic integrity and accountability are non-negotiable.

In an era where misinformation can spread like wildfire, it’s imperative that news organizations uphold the highest standards of reporting. Anything less is a disservice to the public and to the principles of a free and honest press.
