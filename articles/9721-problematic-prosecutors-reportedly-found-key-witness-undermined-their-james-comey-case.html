'Problematic': Prosecutors reportedly found key witness undermined their James Comey case

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 9, 20254 min read

FILE PHOTO: Former FBI Director James Comey is sworn in prior to testifying before a Senate Intelligence Committee hearing on Russia’s alleged interference in the 2016 U.S. presidential election on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., June 8, 2017. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst//File Photo

Federal prosecutors concluded a central witness would actually undermine their case against former FBI Director James Comey, according to reporting.

Law professor Daniel Richman, who prosecutors allege Comey authorized to leak information to the media, told investigators the former FBI director had instructed him at least twice not to engage with journalists and unequivocally did not authorize him to provide information to a reporter before the 2016 election, according to sources who spoke to ABC News.

“According to prosecutors who investigated the circumstances surrounding Comey’s 2020 testimony for two months, using Richman’s testimony to prove that Comey knowingly provided false statements to Congress would result in ‘likely insurmountable problems’ for the prosecution,” the network reported.

Investigators stated their concerns about the case last month in a lengthy memo advocating against criminal charges, according to sources familiar with the document, but President Donald Trump replaced the lead investigator with a loyalist aide, Lindsey Halligan, who quickly presented the case to a grand jury and secured an indictment.

Halligan alleges that Comey intentionally misled Congress in 2017 and 2020 when he testified that he’d never authorized anyone to provide information anonymously to reporters, but Richman told investigators last month that he had never served as an anonymous source or acted on Comey’s direction when he led the FBI, and he said Comey told him at least twice specifically not to speak with the press.

Investigators who looked at material from Comey’s emails, including messages to Richman, could not find any instance where he approved the anonymous leak of information to a reporter, sources said.

Our Analysis:

The Comey Conundrum: A Tale of Shifting Narratives and Legal Labyrinths

In what can only be described as a judicial rollercoaster, the case against former FBI Director James Comey has taken a series of dramatic and, frankly, bewildering turns. Central to this case is the allegation that Comey authorized law professor Daniel Richman to leak sensitive information to the media. However, as recent reports have emerged, the very foundations of this case are not just shaky but seem to be crumbling under the weight of their contradictions.

The Witness Who Wasn’t

Daniel Richman, the so-called central witness in this legal drama, has effectively turned the tables on federal prosecutors. According to sources cited by ABC News, Richman was adamant that Comey never authorized him to leak information to the media. In fact, Comey apparently did the exact opposite, instructing Richman not once, but twice, to steer clear of engaging with journalists. This revelation doesn’t just poke holes in the prosecution’s case; it blasts through it like a wrecking ball through a house of cards.

What’s astonishing here is not just Richman’s testimony but the prosecutorial response to it. Investigators, after spending two months poring over the details and examining Comey’s 2020 testimony, concluded that leveraging Richman’s words would lead to “likely insurmountable problems” for their case. Yet, in a twist worthy of a legal thriller, President Donald Trump’s intervention led to a drastic shift in the prosecutorial approach.

The Loyalty Over Logic Shift

Enter Lindsey Halligan, a “loyalist aide” to Trump, who stepped in as the lead investigator after Trump replaced the previous one. With what can only be described as zealous haste, Halligan presented the case to a grand jury, securing an indictment against Comey. This move, fueled by allegiance rather than evidence, marks a troubling departure from the principles of justice and due process.

Halligan’s allegations that Comey intentionally misled Congress in 2017 and 2020 clash head-on with not only Richman’s testimony but also with the absence of any concrete evidence in Comey’s emails or communications with Richman. The investigators’ inability to unearth any instance of Comey approving an anonymous leak adds another layer of doubt to an already questionable indictment.

The Bigger Picture

This legal skirmish is not just about Comey or the allegations against him. It’s a glaring spotlight on a justice system that appears to be under the sway of political maneuvering and loyalty tests. When the pursuit of truth is sidelined for strategic indictments, the very bedrock of the legal system is under threat.

The Comey case, with its turns, twists, and tumultuous developments, serves as a stark reminder of the dangers of allowing political interests to dictate the course of justice. It’s a cautionary tale that underscores the need for a steadfast commitment to evidence-based prosecution, free from the tethers of political loyalty or ambition.

As this saga continues to unfold, one thing remains clear: the truth is often stranger than fiction, and in the case of James Comey, it’s a truth that’s still desperately seeking the light of day.
