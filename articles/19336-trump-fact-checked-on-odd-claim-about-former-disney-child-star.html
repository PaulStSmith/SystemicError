Trump fact-checked on odd claim about former Disney child star

President Donald Trump endorsed the potential political ambitions of former Disney Channel child star Jake Paul — and also made a statement so bizarre about Paul’s career, he had to get fact-checked.

“I just want to say I predict, I’m going to make a prediction, that you will be in the not too distant future, running for political office, OK?” Trump said about Paul while the two appeared together at a Wednesday rally in Kentucky. “And you have my complete and total endorsement, OK?’’

USA Today’s Josh Peter, who covers sports and often fact-checks false athletic claims, did not focus on the president’s political prognostication, but took issue with Trump’s subsequent characterization of Paul as “a hell of a fighter.”

“False,” Peter wrote. “Paul is 12-2 with 7 knockouts and, before his KO loss to Joshua, benefitted from careful matchmakers.”

The “Joshua” in question is Anthony Joshua, a 6-foot-6 boxer with an 82-inch reach who won the Gold Medal at the 2012 Olympics representing the United Kingdom. By contrast, Paul began his own career as a social media influencer in 2013, posting numerous viral videos on social media platforms like Vine and YouTube. Thanks to his social media success, the Disney Channel hired him to play a fictionalized version of himself called Dirk Mann for their TV series “Bizaardvark” from 2016 to 2018. Paul did not begin his career as a boxer until 2018, and even that was against a fellow YouTuber (Deji Olatunji).

“He built his record with victories over aging MMA fighters, an ancient Mike Tyson, a retired NBA player and a YouTuber,” Peter wrote. (Peter also incorrectly identified Paul’s Disney Channel career as being a “stint with the Disney Channel from 2005 to 2007.”)

Peter concluded, “This is not to say Paul is a terrible boxer. But ‘a hell of a fighter’ is a big stretch.”

Paul is an outspoken supporter of Trump, often appearing on talk shows to criticize Trump’s political opponents like President Joe Biden and claimed Trump did not die during the 2024 assassination attempt against him because “when you try and kill God’s angels and saviors of the world it just makes them bigger.” He has leveraged his relationship with Trump to promote his boxing career, even though respected boxing commentators referred to his fights with legitimate boxers like Joshua with terms like “clown show.” When Joshua knocked out Paul, he broke his jaw cleanly in two separate places.

In the case of his 2024 fight against Mike Tyson, a 59-year-old who literally broke his back during his last boxing victory in 2003, Paul won but only after many questioned the ethics of the fight itself even occurring.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Trump’s Endorsement of Jake Paul and Fact-Checking Dispute

Institutional Power and Decision-Makers

The story revolves around President Donald Trump, a figure wielding significant political and social influence, and his public endorsement of Jake Paul, a former Disney Channel star and current social media influencer turned boxer. Trump’s endorsement in a political context and his commentary on Paul’s boxing career are central to the narrative. The decision-maker here is Trump, who chooses to leverage his platform to endorse Paul and comment on his boxing skills.

Misdirection and Responsibility

The article suggests a bizarre blend of politics, celebrity culture, and sports, with Trump’s statements and the subsequent fact-check by USA Today’s Josh Peter. However, it’s crucial to dissect whether the framing misdirects responsibility or critique.

  1. Trump’s Endorsement: The endorsement of Paul’s potential political ambitions by Trump is a strategic move, appealing to Paul’s sizable following and Trump’s base. The responsibility for this endorsement lies solely with Trump, leveraging his influence to potentially shape future political landscapes.

  2. Characterization of Paul as a Boxer: Trump’s characterization of Paul as “a hell of a fighter” is contested by Peter, pointing out Paul’s record and the nature of his opponents. This debate falls outside the direct sphere of political influence and into personal opinion and sports commentary. Trump’s opinion, while influential, does not hold institutional power in sports analysis.

  3. Fact-Checking: Peter’s fact-check addresses Trump’s claim and corrects an error regarding Paul’s Disney Channel career. The responsibility here is on journalistic integrity and the importance of accurate reporting, a duty Peter fulfills. However, this aspect is tangential to the broader political implications of Trump’s endorsement.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating

The article does not overtly commit false equivalence but does conflate distinct arenas: political influence, celebrity endorsements, and sports performance. By focusing on Trump’s inaccurate sports commentary, there’s a risk of diverting attention from more consequential discussions about the implications of his political endorsements and the broader trend of celebrities transitioning into political figures.

Conclusion

Donald Trump, with his institutional power as a former President and influential political figure, made the decision to endorse Jake Paul for unspecified political ambitions. This endorsement is the primary action with potential systemic implications, considering Trump’s significant base and Paul’s social media influence. While the fact-checking of Trump’s claim about Paul’s boxing career by Josh Peter is valid, it arguably misdirects attention from the more impactful issue of Trump’s political influence and the blending of celebrity status with political power. The article, therefore, strays into areas of limited consequence (Paul’s boxing record) while underscoring the ongoing erosion of traditional political boundaries, exemplified by Trump’s endorsement.

In this context, criticism should be more sharply focused on the implications of Trump’s political endorsements and the merging of celebrity with politics, rather than the accuracy of his comments on Paul’s boxing prowess.
