Trump's 'cratering approval' will drag family trade down with him: Nobel Prize winner

The declining approval rating of Donald Trump will affect his family businesses, a Nobel Prize winner has claimed.

Paul Krugman believes the negative effects of the president’s approval ratings will cause a collapse in the crypto market, which received a surge in the post-election space. Trump had backed a cryptocurrency, $Trump, and has taken actions in office to inflate the value. Top holders of share price in the coin, for instance, were invited to a dinner with the president as a result of their investment.

Despite the boom in interest following Trump’s return to office, confidence is waning as his approval rating declines. Economist Krugman believes there is no coming back for the crypto business.

He wrote in his Substack, “But there was also a huge financial payoff: Crypto-friendly policies and the perception that the U.S. government would actively promote crypto assets helped fuel a huge rise in the prices of Bitcoin and other assets.

“The chart at the top shows that a large part of the rise in crypto values since the previous crypto winter came in a post-election surge. As I wrote last October, crypto has become a Trump trade.

“Now almost all of that surge is gone. Bitcoin sold for about $69,000 just before the 2024 election; it reached a peak of almost $125,000; but just before this post went live it was under $71,000.

“How much of that reversal reflects Trump’s cratering approval and doubts about whether he can or will deliver the crypto-friendly policies the industry wants? It must be part of the story. And crypto is unlikely to regain the level of political influence it had a few months ago.”

Krugman also criticized the claims of Michal Burry, the investor who predicted and profited from the subprime mortgage crisis. Burry believes there will be a “death spiral” when it comes to asset prices should Bitcoin continue declining.

Krugman disagrees, writing, “I think this is exaggerated: Crypto is still a fairly small piece of financial markets, and, without getting into too much detail, crypto hoarders like Strategy may eventually be forced to sell, but they won’t be facing immediate margin calls.

“In fact, if we’re going to have a crypto crash, best to get it over with now, before the industry becomes too big — or too politically powerful — to fail.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Crypto Connection: A Political and Economic Quagmire

The recent analysis by Nobel Prize winner Paul Krugman sheds light on a disturbing confluence of politics, personal business interests, and the volatile world of cryptocurrency, which, if not scrutinized, could spell disaster not just for the involved parties but for the wider financial market.

The Intersection of Political Power and Personal Gain

At the center of this debacle is the declining approval rating of Donald Trump and its ripple effects on the crypto market, particularly the $Trump cryptocurrency. The audacity to intertwine the presidency with personal business ventures, such as inviting top holders of a personally backed cryptocurrency to dinner, isn’t just ethically murky—it’s a glaring conflict of interest. The blurring lines between state affairs and personal gain under Trump’s administration are not just worrying; they are indicative of a systemic rot that prioritizes profit over policy, influence over integrity.

The Crypto Market’s Political Puppetry

Krugman’s analysis suggests a disturbing trend: the manipulation of crypto market values through political influence. The post-election surge in crypto values, tied closely to Trump’s return to office and his administration’s crypto-friendly policies, highlights a dangerous precedent where market stability is at the mercy of political whims. The subsequent decline in Trump’s approval rating and the correlating dip in crypto values underscore the vulnerability of financial markets to political turbulence—a situation Krugman rightly critiques.

Krugman vs. Burry: The Debate Over Crypto’s Future

In his critique of Michal Burry’s “death spiral” theory regarding asset prices and Bitcoin’s decline, Krugman provides a measured counterargument, suggesting that while the crypto market’s crash is concerning, it might not be the apocalypse Burry predicts. Krugman’s call to address the potential crash now, “before the industry becomes too big—or too politically powerful—to fail,” is a stark warning against allowing financial markets, particularly those as speculative as cryptocurrency, to become intertwined with political power.

Conclusion: A Call for Separation and Scrutiny

The insights provided by Krugman are not just a commentary on the current state of affairs but a call to action. The dangerous liaison between political influence and market dynamics seen in the Trump-crypto saga should serve as a cautionary tale. It’s high time to dissect and critique these intersections with the sharpness they demand, advocating for stricter boundaries between political office and personal business interests. As Krugman suggests, letting the crypto crash unfold now may prevent a future where political machinations can unduly influence market dynamics, holding the economy hostage to the whims of the powerful few. The Trump-crypto connection, therefore, is not just a symptom but a symbol of a larger systemic error that desperately needs correction.
