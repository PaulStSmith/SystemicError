'Run this in every ad': Karoline Leavitt ridiculed after revealing Trump's 'main priority'

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters on Thursday that President Donald Trump’s “main priority” at the moment is constructing a $200 million ballroom addition to the White House.

When she was asked whether Trump had any plans for additional White House renovations, she replied, “Not to my knowledge, no, but he’s a builder at heart, clearly. And so his heart and his mind is always churning about how to improve things here on the White House grounds. But at this moment in time, of course, the ballroom is really the president’s main priority.”

Leavitt possibly only meant “priority” in the narrow sense of what Trump wants to do with the White House, and not government in general — but the ambiguous way she phrased it prompted immediate criticism and attacks from Democratic lawmakers, staffers, and pundits, who seized on her remarks on social media to mean that Trump cares more about adding luxury amenities onto the White House than he does about reopening the federal government, paying millions of civil servants, or restoring health care subsidies to working people.

“Honestly, you can’t make this s--- up,” wrote Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) to his X account.

“Maybe literally anything else Americans are struggling with could be a priority? Cost of living? Health care? National security? Just a thought,” wrote the House Natural Resources Committee Democrats account.

“It’s clear national security is not his main priority,” wrote the House Homeland Security Committee Democrats account.

“Run this in every single ad next year. Every. Single. One,” wrote Democratic House staffer Brian Zilm.

“BREAKING: In a shocking moment, Karoline Leavitt admits that, as Americans are suffering behind high grocery prices and insurance premiums, ‘the ballroom is really the president’s main priority.’ And #RepublicansAreAWOL. Nobody voted for this,” wrote the account for Really American PAC.

Tommy Scarangelo, the legislative affairs director for New York Gov. Kathy Hochul, simply posted a GIF of the famous “I’m mad as hell and not going to take it anymore” scene from 1976’s The Network.

Our Analysis:

The Ballroom Blunder: A Misplaced Priority or a Misunderstood Plan?

In a recent disclosure that can only be described as befuddling, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt illuminated the nation on President Donald Trump’s “main priority”—a $200 million ballroom addition to the White House. This statement, ambiguous at best and aloof at worst, has ignited a firestorm of criticism from Democratic lawmakers, staffers, and pundits, all too eager to interpret Leavitt’s words as an admission of the administration’s misplaced priorities amidst myriad pressing national issues.

The Heart of the Matter

The core of the controversy lies not just in the proposed extravagant expenditure but in the timing and the optics of such an announcement. As the country grapples with monumental challenges—ranging from the urgent need to reopen the federal government and compensate millions of civil servants, to addressing the healthcare needs of working Americans—Leavitt’s revelation paints a picture of an administration seemingly out of touch with the reality of its constituents.

The Critic’s Corner

Criticism was quick and scathing. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer’s reaction, “Honestly, you can’t make this s--- up,” alongside comments from various Democratic entities, encapsulates a collective disbelief and frustration towards what is perceived as a flagrant display of indifference to the nation’s more pressing needs. The suggestion to highlight this prioritization of a ballroom in political ads by Democratic House staffer Brian Zilm underscores the perceived political folly and the ammunition it provides to opponents.

A Builder at Heart?

Leavitt’s defense, citing Trump’s background as a “builder at heart,” does little to quell the uproar. While it’s understandable for a president to contemplate improvements to the historic residence, prioritizing such a grandiose project at a time of national distress is, to put it mildly, tone-deaf. The ambiguity of Leavitt’s statement—whether it pertains solely to White House renovations or reflects the broader priorities of the Trump administration—has only added fuel to the fire of criticism.

The Opposition’s Playbook

The Democratic response, while expectedly critical, raises valid concerns about the administration’s focus and sensitivity to the plight of everyday Americans. The flurry of reactions on social media and the call to leverage this incident in future political campaigns highlight a broader discontent with what is seen as the administration’s often ostentatious and out-of-touch agenda.

In Conclusion

In the grand scheme of governance, the priorities set by those in power send a strong message about their values and their responsiveness to the needs of the populace. When juxtaposed with the myriad challenges facing the nation, a $200 million ballroom addition is not just a questionable investment but a stark symbol of misplaced priorities. Whether Leavitt’s comments were taken out of context or represent a genuine insight into the administration’s agenda, the backlash serves as a reminder of the critical eye with which such decisions are viewed. It’s a ballroom blunder that encapsulates the often glaring disconnect between political leadership and the lived realities of the citizens they serve.
