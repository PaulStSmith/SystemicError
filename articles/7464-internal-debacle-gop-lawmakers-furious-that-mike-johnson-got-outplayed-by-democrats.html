'Internal debacle': GOP lawmakers furious that Mike Johnson 'got outplayed' by Democrats

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 20, 20255 min read

U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), following the vote on releasing the Epstein files. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Democrats repeatedly outmaneuvered Republican leadership and stalled the GOP agenda to close out the legislative year, and House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) is facing heat.

Under House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Democrats shifted the congressional agenda toward their priorities while exposing fractures within the GOP majority, and the centerpiece of Democratic success was a deliberate September strategy to frame the government shutdown around healthcare rather than President Donald Trump’s federal spending cuts, reported Politico.

“The party knew well that health care was a minefield for Republicans — one that many in both parties blamed for the GOP’s massive House losses in the 2018 midterms,” Politico reported.

“Ultimately, the shutdown ended after 43 days when a handful of Democratic senators decided the standoff had run its course,” the report added. “But House Democrats strategized to keep health care center stage, with Jeffries filing a discharge petition seeking a vote on a straight three-year extension of the expiring tax credits before moderate Republicans started filing their own discharge petitions.”

Although Democrats failed to secure an extension of enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies, they achieved their broader objective: forcing Republicans to debate Democratic priorities instead of their own agenda. Johnson ultimately kept the House out of session roughly one-fifth of the year to secure the shutdown deal, inadvertently fueling GOP frustration.

“I think the House as a whole has not been nearly as proactive as we should have been in recent months,” complained Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-CA).

Democrats weaponized discharge petitions — one of the minority’s few legislative tools — with unprecedented effectiveness. Three petitions gathered the required 218 signatures this year alone, forcing votes on releasing Epstein files, restoring federal workers’ collective bargaining rights, and extending healthcare subsidies for three years.

Jeffries noted Democrats had “won more discharge petitions in the last three weeks than have been successful in the last 30 years.”

The healthcare discharge petition proved most damaging to Johnson. When moderate Republicans sought a floor vote on compromise proposals, Johnson refused, leaving vulnerable GOP members no choice but to join Jeffries’ effort. Four Republicans, including Pennsylvania Reps. Rob Bresnahan and Ryan Mackenzie, crossed party lines — humiliating leadership.

“The completion of the discharge petition has amounted to an internal debacle for Johnson, whose members are now unhappy that GOP leaders got outplayed and will now vote on a measure that the vast majority of them loathe,” Politico reported.

Republicans did secure some wins — ending the record shutdown without costly subsidies and passing a modest healthcare package and permitting reform bill. However, these accomplishments paled against Democratic momentum heading into the 2026 midterms.

GOP leaders underestimated how far moderate Republicans would go to resist leadership, and their miscalculation left them increasingly vulnerable to further Democratic exploitation of internal divisions.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Maneuvers: Democrats vs. Republicans in the Legislative Labyrinth

In the latest episode of what could only be described as political chess, the Democrats, under the strategic command of House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, have once again shown that they are not to be underestimated in the art of legislative maneuvering. The GOP, with House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) at the helm, found themselves outplayed and their agenda stymied, demonstrating a stark underestimation of the opposition’s cunning and the detrimental impact of their own internal fractures.

The Healthcare Gambit

The Democrats’ masterstroke was to pivot the narrative surrounding the government shutdown towards healthcare, a topic that has historically been akin to navigating a minefield for Republicans. This is not merely a strategic pivot but a calculated exploitation of the GOP’s Achilles’ heel. The 2018 midterms served as a clear warning sign, which the Republicans seem to have ignored, to their peril. By framing the shutdown around healthcare, Democrats not only shifted the conversation to their turf but also laid bare the GOP’s vulnerability on the issue.

The Power of Discharge Petitions

Perhaps the most fascinating tactical development has been the Democrats’ use of discharge petitions. This procedural maneuver, often seen as a last resort given its complexity and the difficulty in achieving the necessary support, was wielded with unprecedented effectiveness. Garnering the required 218 signatures on not one, but three different occasions, Democrats forced votes on critical issues, from the Epstein files to healthcare subsidies. This not only showcased their legislative acumen but also placed Republicans in a precarious position, having to publicly position themselves on contentious issues.

The healthcare discharge petition, in particular, serves as a glaring illustration of the GOP’s internal discord. When moderate Republicans, pushed to the brink, sought a compromise, Johnson’s refusal to entertain such proposals left them with no choice but to side with the Democrats. This not only led to a significant embarrassment for the GOP leadership but also to a rare legislative loss on an issue of critical importance to their platform.

The Fallout for the GOP

Speaker Mike Johnson’s decision to keep the House out of session for a significant portion of the year, ostensibly to secure a shutdown deal, did little but fuel frustration within his own ranks. Rep. Kevin Kiley’s (R-CA) lamentation that the House has not been as proactive as it should have been is a damning indictment of the leadership’s strategic failures.

Despite the GOP managing to end the shutdown without conceding to costly subsidies, and passing modest healthcare and permitting reform bills, these victories feel hollow in the wake of Democratic gains. The momentum is clearly with the Democrats as they look towards the 2026 midterms, leaving the GOP to reckon with their underestimation of moderate Republicans’ willingness to break ranks.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Legislative Warfare

The ultimate takeaway from this legislative saga is a lesson in the importance of strategic foresight and the peril of underestimating one’s opponents. The GOP’s failure to anticipate the Democrats’ moves, coupled with an inability to quell internal divisions, has left them vulnerable to further exploitation. As they lick their wounds and regroup, one wonders if this will serve as a wake-up call or merely another chapter in a continuing saga of miscalculation and missed opportunities.

In essence, the Democrats, through shrewd strategy and a keen understanding of both the political landscape and their adversaries’ weaknesses, have not only advanced their agenda but have also laid the groundwork for future legislative battles. The GOP, meanwhile, finds itself in a precarious position, its internal cohesion frayed and its strategic competence in question. The chessboard is set, and the Democrats have made their move. How the GOP responds will be critical to their future prospects.
