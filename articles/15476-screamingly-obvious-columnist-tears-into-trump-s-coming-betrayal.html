'Screamingly obvious': Columnist tears into Trump's coming betrayal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 30, 20255 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media on board Air Force One on the way to West Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., April 13, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

New York Times columnist Michelle Goldberg says Trump’s real motives are clear behind his battle against antisemitism.

“About a decade ago, conservatives would often denounce Muslim immigration on the grounds that it threatened Western progress on gay rights. This posture, sometimes called homonationalism, got its start in Europe, then made its way into American politics with Donald Trump’s first presidential campaign,” she writes in the New York Times.

In his acceptance speech at the 2016 Republican National Convention, Trump decried the murder of 49 people in a gay nightclub in Orlando, Florida, by Islamist fundamentalist Omar Mateen, but then spent his term stacking courts with judges hostile to LGBTQ+ people and rolling back their workplace protections. Trump later rode a wave of MAGA backlash against trans rights all the way into the White House, and he’s been stripping “federal funding from almost anything with 'LGBT' in it.”

The president’s betrayal of the LGBTQ+ community is a lesson to anyone “tempted to take his campaign against antisemitism seriously, when it is screamingly obvious that it’s just a pretext to attack liberal institutions,” writes Goldberg, who was born into a Jewish family in Buffalo, New York.

Goldberg notes the “derangement” at the way Israel’s defenders conflate all but the mildest criticism of Israel’s war on Palestine with antisemitism.” This, she says, leads Trump supporters “to vastly overstate the scale of antisemitism on the left and, in turn, to rationalize away Trump’s authoritarianism as he attempts to crush progressive” protesters, many of whom are Jewish.

Anti-Defamation League head Jonathan Greenblatt, for example, cheered Trump’s push to exercise political control over Harvard, saying, “It is a good thing that President Trump is leaning in.”

But “… if your presuppositions about Israel lead you to sanctify Trump, they bear rethinking,” says Goldberg, and even Jews who “delight in Trump’s animosity toward the Palestinians should be aware of the bargain they’re making.”

“In the right-wing nationalist movement that Trump leads, gutter antisemitism is … considered a cheeky transgression and a sign of in-group belonging,” she warns. Meanwhile, Holocaust denial is making an appearance on Tucker Carlson and Joe Rogan podcasts.

A decade ago, Trump aligned himself with gay rights but now bans or discourages the mere use of the word “gay” or the abbreviation “LGBT."

“I’m not sure why anyone, let alone a scholar of the Holocaust, thinks Jews will fare better,” Goldberg said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Antisemitism Crusade: A Cloaked Agenda?

Michelle Goldberg's recent piece in the New York Times slices through the facade of Donald Trump's stance against antisemitism, revealing a strategy not unlike the wolf in sheep's clothing. Goldberg illuminates how Trump, under the guise of combating antisemitism, wields this claim as a weapon to undermine liberal institutions and consolidate his authoritarian grip, all while betraying the very principles he pretends to uphold. Let's dissect this duplicitous maneuver and its implications.

Homonationalism: The Trojan Horse of Trump's Campaign

Goldberg traces Trump's manipulation of social issues back to his first presidential campaign, where he appropriated the concept of homonationalism. This strategy, which first gained traction in Europe, seeks to frame Muslim immigration as a threat to Western progress on gay rights. Trump's utilization of this narrative, Goldberg points out, was a mere smokescreen. His acceptance speech at the 2016 Republican National Convention, where he decried the Orlando nightclub shooting, was a strategic ploy. The reality that followed was a presidency marked by a systematic dismantling of LGBTQ+ rights, from packing courts with judges hostile to LGBTQ+ communities to stripping away their workplace protections.

Antisemitism: The New Front in Trump's Culture War

Goldberg argues that Trump's current crusade against antisemitism is reminiscent of his earlier exploitation of LGBTQ+ rights. This move, however, is not born out of genuine concern for Jewish communities but is a calculated attack on liberal institutions. By amplifying the perceived scale of antisemitism on the left, Trump and his followers aim to discredit progressive movements, many of which include Jewish activists fighting against his authoritarian tendencies.

The Complicity of the Anti-Defamation League

One of the most alarming points Goldberg raises is the complicity of figures like Anti-Defamation League head Jonathan Greenblatt, who praised Trump's exertion of political control over institutions like Harvard. This endorsement, as Goldberg suggests, is a clear misstep for those tasked with guarding against antisemitism. It underscores a dangerous willingness to overlook Trump's broader agenda in exchange for short-term alignment on Israel's policies.

The Bargain with the Devil

Goldberg warns of the perilous bargain made by those in the Jewish community who find delight in Trump's aggressive stance towards Palestinians. The broader movement Trump spearheads is steeped in antisemitism and even Holocaust denial, as evidenced by its presence on platforms like Tucker Carlson and Joe Rogan's podcasts. This toxic alliance exposes a glaring contradiction: how can a movement that harbors antisemitic elements be trusted to fight against antisemitism?

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Goldberg's analysis is a stark reminder of the cunning strategies at play in Trump's political machinations. It serves as a call to action for anyone genuinely committed to fighting antisemitism, LGBTQ+ rights, or any form of bigotry. The lesson here is clear: Trump's battle against antisemitism, much like his purported defense of gay rights, is nothing but a pretext for attacking liberal values and tightening his authoritarian control. It's a classic case of using one form of bigotry to camouflage another, and it's imperative that we see through this deceit. The stakes are too high, and the cost of naivety is a price we cannot afford to pay.
