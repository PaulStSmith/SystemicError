Ticket sales plummet at Kennedy Center amid Trump’s takeover: ‘Costs are huge!’

Ticket sales for the Kennedy Center’s annual performance of “The Nutcracker” have plummeted under the new leadership of President Donald Trump, who took over as the organization’s chairman earlier this year, CNN reported Saturday.

According to internal sales data obtained and reviewed by CNN, ticket sales for the Kennedy Center’s performance of “The Nutcracker” topped out at around 10,000 seats across the production’s seven performances, around a 33% drop off from the around 15,000 ticket sales for the production seen in 2001 through 2024.

A person with knowledge of the internal workings of the Kennedy Center told CNN that even if the performance of “The Nutcracker” were to have sold out this year, the costs for the performance would still outweigh the revenue from ticket sales.

“Selling every ticket to ‘The Nutcracker’ is absolutely not paying your bills,” the person told CNN on the condition of anonymity. “We have 19 unions here. The production costs are huge.”

Additionally, other performances have been cancelled due to both a new policy imposed on the center of requiring all performances to “break even,” and from artists resigning from their leadership roles or pulling out of previously scheduled events, such as the cancellation of a performance of the musical “Hamilton” that was planned for earlier this year.

“One of the financial lifelines of the center is Broadway,” said a former Kennedy Center employee, speaking with CNN on the condition of anonymity. “Broadway tours are looking at where they should be playing, and in many cases, they are choosing not the Kennedy Center.”

Under the Kennedy Center’s new Trump-approved board of trustees, performers had also been vetted for their gender identity, according to Marc Bamuthi Joseph, the former head of the center’s social impact team whose role was terminated after Trump was sworn into office.

“They would ask us specifically if any of the artists were trans,” Joseph told CNN. “They never explicitly said, ‘Don’t do that,’ but they would make the conditions impossible for trans artists and gay artists to safely come.’”

Our Analysis:

A Stark Decline in Cultural Diplomacy: The Kennedy Center Under Trump’s Chairmanship

The recent report by CNN on the plummeting ticket sales at the Kennedy Center under the new leadership of Donald Trump is a glaring testament to the adverse effects of politicizing cultural institutions. The steep 33% drop in sales for the center’s annual performance of The Nutcracker is not just a number—it is a reflection of a broader disillusionment and a significant diversion from the center’s legacy.

Trump’s Leadership: A Mismatch for the Arts

Donald Trump’s tenure as the chairman of the Kennedy Center has been marked by a series of decisions that starkly contrast with the institution’s foundational values. The imposition of a “break-even” policy for all performances is a myopic approach that undermines the intrinsic value of the arts. Art is not merely a commercial transaction; it is a medium of expression, cultural exchange, and societal reflection. By reducing performances to mere financial equations, the new leadership under Trump has stripped the Kennedy Center of its soul.

The Impact of Financial Decisions on Artistic Integrity

According to the internal sales data obtained by CNN, the Kennedy Center’s decision to prioritize financial viability over artistic merit has led to the cancellation of other performances, including a much-anticipated production of Hamilton. This decision not only deprives the audience of diverse artistic experiences but also sends a chilling message to the broader arts community about the changing priorities of the center. The former Kennedy Center employee’s insight that Broadway tours are now reconsidering their venues is a direct consequence of these misguided policies.

A Discriminatory Vetting Process

Perhaps the most alarming revelation from CNN’s report is the alleged vetting of performers based on their gender identity. Marc Bamuthi Joseph’s account of the center’s inquiry into the gender identity of artists under the Trump-approved board of trustees is a stark deviation from the inclusivity that the arts sector strives to champion. This discriminatory practice not only violates the principles of equality and freedom of expression but also alienates a significant portion of the artistic community and the audience that supports them.

Conclusion: A Call for Reevaluation

The decline in ticket sales for The Nutcracker, coupled with the cancellation of other performances and the alleged discriminatory vetting practices, paints a grim picture of the Kennedy Center’s current trajectory. The arts have always served as a bridge, bringing together diverse voices and fostering a shared understanding of our collective humanity. Under Trump’s chairmanship, the Kennedy Center appears to be veering away from this noble mission, prioritizing financial considerations and discriminatory practices over artistic integrity and inclusivity.

As supporters of the arts, it is our responsibility to challenge these developments and advocate for leadership that recognizes the true value of cultural institutions. The Kennedy Center, under its new administration, must reevaluate its priorities and policies to ensure that it remains a beacon of cultural diplomacy and artistic excellence. Failure to do so not only diminishes the center’s reputation but also erodes the very fabric of our cultural landscape.
