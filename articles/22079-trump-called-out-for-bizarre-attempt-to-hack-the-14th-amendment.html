Trump called out for 'bizarre' attempt to hack the 14th Amendment

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Trump’s Citizenship Gambit Is Not Confusion. It Is Executive Power Testing Its Limits.

The Source of Power

The relevant fact is not that a Cato libertarian thinks the order is “bizarre.” The fact is that Donald Trump used presidential power to direct agencies to rewrite the practical meaning of citizenship through executive order. That is the institutional center of the story: the White House ordering the bureaucracy to treat rights as conditional, revocable, and administratively redefined.

That is not a legal curiosity. It is an assertion that the president can decide who counts as inside the constitutional order and who does not.

What the Order Is Doing

According to the reporting, the new order goes well beyond the usual anti-immigrant theater. Bier says it would reach current U.S. citizens when agencies issue citizenship documents, applies retroactively to births before the order, and narrows protections by making citizenship hinge on the status of either parent. It also strips out protections that had covered children of lawful permanent residents in the earlier version.

That is the real structure here: not a clean rule, but a moving target. The point is to make citizenship depend on whatever category the executive branch wants to enforce that day.

Misdirection as Method

The article treats the controversy as a knot of technical drafting problems. That framing is too soft. This is not primarily about sloppy language. It is about deliberate ambiguity used as a weapon. Vagueness is useful when the goal is to intimidate, delay, and force people to prove they still belong.

Bier’s examples make that plain. The order is written so that a child’s rights can turn on parental “intent,” immigration status, and shifting agency interpretation. That is not clarity gone missing. That is power refusing to commit itself to a stable rule because instability is the point.

The Legal Theory Is the Attack

The most revealing part of the source is Bier’s description of the “extraterritorial fiction.” Trump is not just narrowing a category. He is claiming the president can declare people outside U.S. law in order to evade the Constitution’s jurisdictional limits.

That is the constitutional move worth naming directly: if the executive can say someone is not subject to U.S. law, then rights become optional and the border becomes a machine for selectively suspending them. The issue is not whether this theory is coherent. It is whether a president can use incoherence to manufacture obedience.

The Class Logic Underneath

There is another layer the article gestures at and then half buries: the order is built for arbitrary exception-making. Bier’s jab about “immunity cards” for donors points to the logic. Once rights become a discretionary status managed by executive interpretation, wealth and access become the real safeguard.

That is how authoritarian politics often works in practice. It does not abolish rules for everyone at once. It makes the rules pliable for the powerful and terrifying for everyone else.

The Larger Pattern

This story is not about immigration paperwork. It is about a president using the language of legality to train the public to accept administrative cruelty as constitutional reasoning. That is the pattern: take a basic democratic guarantee, wrap it in procedural sludge, and dare the courts and the public to keep up.

The danger here is not only what Trump is trying to do. It is how easily executive power can turn citizenship into a site of managed exception, where the state decides who belongs by inventing new categories fast enough to keep resistance behind the curve.
