'Obviously a lie': Trump officials caught contradicting each other about airspace closure

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Roger Penske, businessman and former race car driver, U.S. Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy, and U.S. Secretary of the Interior Doug Burgum, react as U.S. President Donald Trump speaks in the Oval Office, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., January 30, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

The Trump administration is facing blowback after putting out conflicting reports from top officials at multiple agencies — and after offering no explanation initially — as it closed a U.S. airport, initially for ten days, only to reopen it hours later. Reports revealed communications snafus between the Department of Defense and the FAA, and alleged causes being foreign drones or a “party balloon.”

“A U.S. official confirmed to Fox News that the U.S. military earlier this week shot down what was later determined to be a party balloon near El Paso, Texas, after initially assessing it as a possible foreign drone,” the news outlet reported. “The misidentification eventually led to a total shutdown of airspace around the El Paso, Texas, airport.”

“A separate U.S. administration official had told Fox News that Mexican cartel drones breached U.S. airspace near El Paso, Texas, and that counter-drone measures were taken to disable them,” Fox added.

CNN reported that a “Pentagon plan to use a high-energy, counter-drone laser without having coordinated with the Federal Aviation Administration about potential risks to civilian flights prompted Wednesday’s unprecedented airspace shutdown over El Paso, Texas.”

CBS News reported that the “unexpected but brief airspace closure” had “stemmed from disagreements” between FAA and Pentagon officials over drone-related tests.

“Two sources identified the technology as a high-energy laser,” CBS added.

The FAA Administrator, Bryan Bedford, “on Tuesday night decided to close the airspace — without alerting White House, Pentagon or Homeland Security officials, sources said.”

But Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth offered a different explanation, saying that the FAA and the Pentagon “acted swiftly to address a cartel drone incursion. The threat has been neutralized, and there is no danger to commercial travel in the region. The restrictions have been lifted and normal flights are resuming.”

Critics blasted the administration.

Pointing to one administration official’s claim about “Mexican cartel drones,” Aaron Fritschner, deputy chief of staff for U.S. Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA) wrote: “As everyone knows, the standard response to an incursion by ‘Mexican cartel drones’ is a sudden unexplained and poorly communicated 9/11 esque shutdown of all air traffic for ten days that is clumsily revoked a few hours later.”

“This is so obviously a lie,” he charged.

Veteran journalist Kevin Baron, who has a background in global security, defense, intelligence, and foreign policy, called the closure and rapid re-opening, “unnecessarily alarming.”

Astrophysicist and associate professor Robert Rutledge remarked, “We are being lied to by @SeanDuffyWI [Secretary Sean Duffy] and the FAA. This is obviously b — —.”

“There have now been three different explanations given by government sources to reporters for the shut-down of the El Paso airport,” he continued. “If any one of them were correct, the shut down would still be a completely bonkers, unacceptable situation.”

Responding to an FAA statement, former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance asked, “Who is running this clown show?”

Meteorologist Justin Stapleton remarked, “y’all need to get your story straight. This isn’t ok.”

Our Analysis:

The Circus of Confusion: Analyzing the El Paso Airport Shutdown Fiasco

In a bewildering display of administrative incompetence, the Trump administration has once again found itself entangled in a web of confusion and misinformation. This time, the debacle centers around the abrupt closure and subsequent hasty reopening of a U.S. airport in El Paso, Texas. The reasons for this turmoil? A mishmash of conflicting reports involving foreign drones, a “party balloon,” and alleged Mexican cartel drone incursions. Let’s dissect this chaos.

The Heart of the Matter: A “Party Balloon” or a Foreign Drone?

Fox News brings to light the initial confusion by stating that the U.S. military shot down what was thought to be a foreign drone near El Paso, which later turned out to be a mere party balloon. This misidentification led to a drastic decision to shut down the airspace around the El Paso airport. The question that immediately springs to mind is: How does a military trained to identify and assess aerial threats confuse a party balloon with a drone? The level of incompetence or, at best, the glaring gap in threat assessment protocols is astounding.

Conflicting Reports: The FAA vs. The Pentagon

CNN and CBS News add layers to this perplexing situation by highlighting a lack of coordination between the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the Department of Defense. CNN points out a “Pentagon plan to use a high-energy, counter-drone laser” as the cause of the shutdown, without proper communication with the FAA. CBS News reveals disagreements between the two entities over drone-related tests. It’s a classic tale of the left hand not knowing what the right hand is doing, except this isn’t a mundane office error; it’s a serious lapse in national airspace management.

Unilateral Decisions: FAA’s Lone Wolf Move

Bryan Bedford, the FAA Administrator, decided to close the airspace without alerting significant bodies such as the White House, Pentagon, or Homeland Security. This decision-making in a silo is not only reckless but also highlights a concerning breakdown in communication and protocol within federal agencies charged with ensuring the country’s safety and security.

The Discrepancy in Explanations

Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth’s statement offers a stark contrast, attributing the shutdown to a swift response to a “cartel drone incursion.” This narrative flip-flop from a party balloon to a cartel drone incursion only adds to the public’s confusion and erodes trust in the administration’s ability to manage and communicate crises effectively.

Public Outcry and Criticism

Critics and observers, from congressional staff to veteran journalists and academics, have lambasted the administration for its handling of the situation. The rapid about-face from a 10-day closure to reopening hours later, coupled with the muddled explanations, has been rightly criticized as a “clown show.” The administration’s inability to provide a coherent and unified explanation for such a significant action is, as Aaron Fritschner succinctly puts it, “so obviously a lie.”

Conclusion: The Unacceptable State of Affairs

What this fiasco lays bare is not just a singular event of miscommunication or misidentification. It’s symptomatic of a broader issue of disarray and lack of coordination among key federal agencies. When the safety and security of American airspace are at stake, such blunders are not just embarrassing; they’re downright dangerous.

The Trump administration owes the public a detailed explanation and, more importantly, a plan to ensure such a “bonkers” situation never recurs. But given the track record, one might say: don’t hold your breath. This circus of confusion is yet another act in the ongoing saga of administrative incompetence, and the audience, unfortunately, is the American public.
