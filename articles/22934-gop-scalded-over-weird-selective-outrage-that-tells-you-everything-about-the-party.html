GOP scalded over 'weird' selective outrage that 'tells you everything' about the party

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosSeptember 2, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republicans Want Antisemitism as a Weapon, Not a Principle

The Story

CNN’s Audie Cornish highlighted a basic contradiction: JD Vance and other Republicans will posture as guardians against antisemitism when it is useful to attack left-wing critics like Hasan Piker, but they keep making room for Laura Loomer, who has been widely criticized for racist attacks on Black Democrats and who says Vance got her into a closed-door Republican Jewish Coalition event.

Power, Not Confusion

The relevant power is not held by the influencer ecosystem. It sits with the people who open the doors, issue the invitations, and decide whose ugliness gets converted into party access. Loomer is not hovering outside the Republican machine as some uncontrollable fringe force. According to the source, she was in proximity to Vance and to a closed-door Republican gathering because Republicans let her in.

That matters because it strips away the favorite excuse of political cowardice: “we don’t endorse everything people say.” Fine. Then stop giving those people institutional access, legitimacy, and proximity to power. If a politician can denounce antisemitism in one breath and platform a racist in the next, the problem is not inconsistency. It is strategy.

The Double Standard Is the Point

Republicans have tried to turn Hasan Piker into a political cudgel because of his criticism of Israel’s war in Gaza. That is not principled concern about antisemitism. It is selective enforcement, aimed downward at a critic with a large audience and aimed sideways at Democrats who can be smeared by association.

Meanwhile, Loomer’s racism toward Black politicians is treated as manageable noise. That split reveals the actual rule: antisemitism is condemned when it can be used to discipline the left, and ignored when it comes packaged inside the Republican coalition. Moral language becomes a weapon for factional gain, not a constraint on behavior.

The Misdirection Machine

Cornish was right to ask why Republicans rush to present themselves as defenders of Jews and then refuse to denounce Loomer’s racist behavior. The answer is not subtle. They are not confused about the standard. They are choosing which hatred is politically useful and which hatred is inconvenient.

That is why this framing matters. The story is not about an unruly internet personality accidentally breaking decorum. It is about a party apparatus willing to launder extremism when it serves its politics, then turn around and call its opponents antisemitic for criticizing Israeli policy. The moral outrage is real only when it can be pointed at enemies.

What They Are Protecting

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s line that Trump “owns” Loomer is revealing because it captures what this relationship actually is: ownership, patronage, and permission. Loomer is not outside the system. She is useful to it. Republicans do not merely tolerate this ecosystem of racist provocation. They harvest it.

That is the larger pattern here: authoritarian politics does not need coherence, only selective enforcement. It can excuse racism from its allies, criminalize dissent from its opponents, and call both moves “values.” The point is not truth. The point is control.

The Systemic Error

The deeper failure is not one influencer’s racism or one vice president’s hypocrisy. It is a party structure that treats bigotry as an asset when it helps consolidate power and as a scandal only when it cannot be leveraged against the left. That is how democratic language gets hollowed out: not through a sudden collapse, but through repeated bargains with people who know exactly what they are doing.
