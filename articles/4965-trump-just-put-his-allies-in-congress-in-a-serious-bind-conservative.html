Trump just put his allies in Congress in a serious bind: conservative

Conservative commentator Charlie Sykes (Image: Screengrab via Matt Lewis Can’t Lose / YouTube)

Donald Trump’s new plan to enact sweeping tariffs could put the GOP in a major bind, according to conservative commentator Charlie Sykes, by forcing them to make unpopular votes with the midterms only months away.

On Friday, the Supreme Court ruled that Trump did not have the authority to levy his sweeping global tariffs under the law that he had been using up to that point, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. Amid the insults and accusations he leveled at the Court in the wake of this ruling, Trump also announced that he would be replacing the overturned tariffs with a 15 percent tariff on all nations under Section 122 of the 1974 Trade Act.

This section gives a present narrow power to enact tariffs to address certain “balance-of-payments” deficits, but this authority is notably limited compared to the way Trump had been using tariffs prior to the SCOTUS ruling. The section prohibits tariff rates higher than 15 percent, removing Trump’s ability to levy rates as high as the triple-digits in some cases. These tariffs are also temporary, as they can only last 150 days, or about five months, unless Congress votes to extend them.

It was this latter restriction that Sykes, an anti-Trump conservative, argued would put the president’s allies in Congress in a serious bind during a Monday appearance on the podcast of fellow commentator Matt Lewis. In order for these new tariffs to last longer than five months, the Republican-majority chambers of Congress would need to give their approval to them, effectively breaking the party’s staunch opposition to raising taxes.

Given the timeline required by Section 122, this vote would also need to take place less than four months out from the midterms. The closer such an unpopular move would be to the elections in November, the worse it could be for the GOP, which is already tipped to take huge losses.

“The new tariffs will require Republicans in Congress to vote for massive taxes on their own constituents,” Sykes explained.

“They would after 5-6 months, right?” Lewis asked.

“So where are we going to be in 5-6 months?” Sykes responded. “Right around the midterms.”

Sykes also noted that various legal scholars have argued that Trump’s new application of Section 122 is also illegal, and faces the distinct likelihood of being shot down in court as well. The conservative National Review laid out just this argument over the weekend, noting that the “balance-of-payments” issues required for Section 122 are not the same as the “trade deficits” that Trump has claimed as a national emergency requiring tariffs.

“Our overall payments are in balance,” National Review’s Andrew McCarthy wrote. “There is no crisis.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tariff Turmoil: A GOP Quagmire

Donald Trump’s latest foray into the realm of tariffs is more than just a simple policy shift; it’s a veritable minefield for the GOP as the midterms loom ominously on the horizon. Conservative commentator Charlie Sykes’ analysis hits the nail squarely on the head, revealing the impending disaster that Trump’s tariff plan spells for his own party. Let’s dissect this debacle step by step, shall we?

The Supreme Court’s Reality Check

First off, the Supreme Court’s ruling is a blunt reminder of Trump’s penchant for overreaching his authority. The Court’s decision underlines a critical point: Trump’s use of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act to levy global tariffs was, simply put, unlawful. Yet, in typical Trump fashion, instead of a moment of introspection, we get a barrage of insults and a hasty pivot to Plan B.

Trump’s Plan B: A Misguided Missile

Trump’s announcement to enact a 15 percent tariff on all nations under Section 122 of the 1974 Trade Act is nothing short of a misguided missile aimed at the heart of global trade relations. While he might view this as a clever workaround, it’s essentially a band-aid solution to a self-inflicted wound. The limitations of Section 122 - a cap of 15 percent on tariffs and a temporary duration of merely 150 days unless Congress intervenes - highlight the desperate, cornered nature of this strategy.

The GOP’s Looming Nightmare

Sykes is spot on in his assessment of the conundrum facing the GOP. Trump’s new tariffs maneuver forces Republican lawmakers to choose between supporting an unpopular policy that directly taxes their constituents or breaking ranks with Trump, a decision fraught with political peril as the midterms approach. This Catch-22 situation is a masterclass in how not to prepare for an impending electoral battle.

The Irony of Raising Taxes: For a party that has built much of its identity around staunch opposition to tax increases, the prospect of voting for what amounts to a massive tax hike is both ironic and politically toxic. The closer this decision comes to the November elections, the more damaging it could be for the GOP’s chances. The optics of this scenario are disastrous, to say the least.

Legal and Logical Gymnastics

The legal skepticism surrounding Trump’s application of Section 122 is yet another layer in this chaotic policy proposal. The argument, as laid out by legal scholars and the conservative National Review, that Trump’s justification for these tariffs does not meet the legal requirements of Section 122, is a testament to the shaky ground upon which this plan is built. Andrew McCarthy of National Review succinctly points out the absence of a true “balance-of-payments” crisis, further undermining the legal and logical foundation of Trump’s tariff strategy.

Conclusion: A Self-Made Quagmire

In the end, Trump’s tariff plan is not just a policy misstep; it’s a self-made quagmire that threatens to engulf the GOP in the upcoming midterms. By forcing his party into a corner where they must either support a harmful policy or distance themselves from their leader, Trump has once again demonstrated a remarkable disregard for the broader implications of his actions. As the midterms inch closer, the GOP finds itself in an unenviable position, caught between the proverbial rock and a hard place, thanks to Trump’s ill-conceived tariff gambit.
