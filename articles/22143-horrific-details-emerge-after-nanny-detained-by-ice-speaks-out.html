Horrific details emerge after nanny detained by ICE speaks out

Based on an article by Robert Davis

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

ICE Doesn’t “Process” People. It Breaks Them.

The Real Power Here

The institution with actual power in this story is not the nanny, not the lawyerly language of “custody,” and not the press release trying to sanitize the damage. It is ICE, operating under Trump’s political authority, moving a woman through multiple facilities, binding her in restraints, and deciding how she would be labeled after the fact.

Iliana Lick is the subject of the punishment. ICE is the author of it.

One Short Fact Pattern

Lick, an Argentinian nanny working for two Philadelphia families, was arrested by ICE on an allegation that she overstayed her visa. After release on a $10,000 bond, she described being forced through multiple facilities, made to wear a black-and-white striped jail uniform, and kept in ankle cuffs and shackles. ICE then tried to smear her publicly as an “illegal alien,” while she says she had paid her fees, attended her hearings, and was never out of status.

That is the factual core. The rest is institutional theater.

Misdirection by Design

The most revealing move in the story is ICE’s attempt to turn a detained worker into a character problem. That is not an accident. It is a standard state tactic: punish first, then flood the zone with language that makes the punishment seem deserved.

“Illegal alien” is not neutral description here. It is a political weapon. It is meant to collapse legal ambiguity, human agency, and bureaucratic error into one dehumanizing label. The point is to make readers look at the person and not at the machinery that restrained her.

The Cage Is the Message

The striped uniform, the shackles, the ankle monitor, the transfer through facilities: none of that is incidental. It is the point. ICE does not just enforce immigration law; it stages domination. It performs criminality onto people whose only offense may be an administrative dispute or a pending case.

That matters because the punishment in this system precedes adjudication in spirit, if not always in paperwork. The body is treated as guilty before the hearing. The state gets to inflict humiliation while still pretending it is merely “processing” a case.

What This Story Really Shows

This is not a story about one allegedly misplaced arrest. It is a story about how immigration enforcement manufactures legitimacy through cruelty. The system depends on public detachment: if the person can be reduced to a category, then the restraints feel procedural instead of violent.

ICE should be abolished. Not “reformed,” not “trained better,” not “made more accountable.” Abolished. An agency built to convert administrative status into carceral force will keep doing exactly that, because that is what it exists to do.

The Larger Pattern

This is the deeper political pattern: authoritarian state power does not always arrive as overt spectacle. Sometimes it arrives as forms, bonds, uniforms, and official language that hide coercion behind routine. The cruelty is not a glitch in the system. It is how the system teaches obedience, one detained body at a time.
