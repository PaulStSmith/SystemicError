Civil contempt trial may be in store for Trump admin as judge's anger grows: legal expert

Federal judges continue to grow increasingly frustrated with the government as it continues to defy court orders. It’s something that could result in a civil contempt charge, one legal analyst warned Tuesday.

Speaking on MSNBC, legal expert Lisa Rubin brought up scathing comments in the Voice of America case after a judge said his rulings were violated.

U.S. District Judge Royce Lamberth accused the Trump administration of falsifying their plans to gut VOA in previous hearings. He accused the government of disrespecting defendants and even threatened a civil contempt hearing after the government wasted time and resources.

The case was decided in April, but the government has dragged its feet, said Rubin. The government never disputed that it had a statutory obligation to meet the program mandates. It never appealed the ruling.

So, there “could be” a civil contempt hearing, Rubin said, “because they’ve had contempt for the government’s orders to provide additional information, as well as comply with that April order, ordering them to restore certain programming. He has found that in a variety of different ways, they’ve not only failed to do that, but they’ve taken opposite actions.”

An example, she said, was stripping VOA staff of their civil service protections, which would “allow them to move forward with reductions in force that are contrary to being able to restore that programming.”

She recalled a footnote in the case in which Lamberth blasted the director, Kari Lake, writing that her “brazen disinterest in the unambiguous statutory obligations implicates her competence to implement the president’s directives in a manner consistent with fundamental tenets of administrative law.”

Rubin said that it’s a nice way of saying, “you don’t have the foggiest idea what you’re doing. And that’s a real problem under the law.”

Our Analysis:

The Judiciary’s Growing Impatience: A Deep Dive into Government Defiance

In a revealing and almost theatrical display of governmental hubris, federal judges, notably U.S. District Judge Royce Lamberth, have expressed an escalating frustration with the government’s blatant disregard for court orders. This saga, most pointedly illustrated in the Voice of America (VOA) case, not only underscores a dangerous flirtation with legal boundaries but also raises alarming questions about the administration’s respect for the rule of law.

The Core of Contempt

At the heart of this legal drama is an administration that, according to legal expert Lisa Rubin on MSNBC, appears to view court orders not as mandates but mere suggestions. Judge Lamberth’s accusations that the Trump administration falsified their plans to overhaul VOA, and his subsequent threat of a civil contempt hearing, are not just footnotes in legal journals. They are blazing headlines in the ongoing narrative of governmental overreach.

The government’s laissez-faire attitude towards the court’s April ruling is not just a procedural faux pas; it’s a glaring exhibition of contempt. Not appealing the ruling, failing to restore mandated programming, and even moving to strip VOA staff of civil service protections—all while under the guise of obeying court orders—is audacity at its peak.

The Blatant Disregard for Statutory Obligations

Rubin’s commentary shines a light on a critical aspect of this debacle: the government’s unabashed disregard for its statutory obligations. The refusal to comply with the April order, coupled with actions diametrically opposed to those orders, suggests a dangerous precedent. If the administration can selectively ignore legal mandates, what does this say about the state of our democracy?

Kari Lake’s “Brazen Disinterest”

The footnote regarding Kari Lake, the director accused of a brazen disinterest in statutory obligations, is particularly damning. Judge Lamberth’s scathing critique not only questions Lake’s competence but also encapsulates the broader issue at hand: a systemic failure to adhere to the principles of administrative law. Rubin’s interpretation of Lamberth’s comments as an indication of Lake’s ignorance of legal obligations is more than just a reprimand; it’s a red flag signaling a profound disconnect between the administration’s actions and the foundational tenets of governance.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

This situation transcends the specifics of the VOA case. It’s a stark reminder of the fragility of our legal system when faced with an administration willing to test its limits. The potential for a civil contempt charge is not just about penalizing non-compliance; it’s about reaffirming the judiciary’s role as a bulwark against executive overreach.

The Voice of America case, as dissected by Lisa Rubin, is not merely a legal battle; it’s a litmus test for the health of our democracy. The government’s actions, as outlined, are not just in defiance of a court order—they’re a challenge to the very principles of accountability and transparency that underpin our democratic institutions.

As we navigate these tumultuous waters, one thing becomes clear: the judiciary’s growing impatience is a symptom of a much larger issue—a systemic error that threatens the checks and balances that safeguard our democracy. It’s time for a recalibration, lest we find ourselves adrift in uncharted legal and moral territories.
