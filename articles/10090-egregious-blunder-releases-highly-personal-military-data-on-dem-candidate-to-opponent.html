CBS News said that the release of the entire record of Rep. Mikie Sherill’s time in the military academy “potentially violates the Privacy Act of 1974 and exemptions established under the Freedom of Information Act.”

Sherill is running to be New Jersey’s governor against Republican opponent Jack Ciattarelli, who was able to obtain the files from ally and fellow Republican Nicholas De Gregorio, a former Marine who had a failed bit represent the state in Congress.

The file shows Sherill’s social security number on nearly every page, according to the report. It also clearly displays “the home addresses for her and her parents, life insurance information, Sherrill’s performance evaluations and the nondisclosure agreement between her and the U.S. government to safeguard classified information.”

The social security numbers of her former superiors were redacted, however.

CBS also obtained the file from the National Personnel Records Center, which is a branch of the National Archives and Records Administration, which maintains every record of service members and civil servants across the entire U.S. government.

The report called it an “egregious blunder.”

CBS discovered the file as it was working on an investigation into a 1994 Naval Academy scandal in which midshipmen were accused of cheating in an exam. Sherill, who was at the academy at the time, was not accused of cheating but refused to inform on those that were.

“The technician should NOT have released the entire record,” wrote Grace McCaffrey of the National Archives and Records Administration, in an email to CBS.

The report found that the National Personnel Records Center didn’t follow procedures for releasing information and that only part of the personal information should have been released under the Freedom of Information Act rules.

The Archives is now investigating “how and why” the staffer refused to follow the rules.

The New Jersey Monitor cited an Emerson College poll showing the gubernatorial election in a “dead heat” with 11% of voters still undecided. Sherill has edged ahead of Ciattarelli in other polls, the report acknowledged.

Privacy Violation or Political Warfare? Analyzing the Unredacted Release of a Democratic Candidate’s Military Records

In a democratic society where the rule of law is supposed to reign supreme, the recent debacle surrounding the release of unredacted personal military records of Democratic governor candidate Rep. Mikie Sherill to her Republican opponent is not just a breach of privacy—it’s a glaring testament to the lengths some will go to in their quest for political power. According to a Thursday report by CBS News, this act “potentially violates the Privacy Act of 1974 and exemptions established under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA).” Let’s dissect this egregious violation and its implications.

The Unwarranted Exposure

The released records of Sherill, who is vying for the New Jersey governorship against Republican Jack Ciattarelli, were not just any documents. They included sensitive information like her social security number, home addresses, life insurance details, performance evaluations, and a nondisclosure agreement safeguarding classified information. All of this was handed to Ciattarelli’s camp by Nicholas De Gregorio, a former Marine and Republican ally. The sheer volume of personal data exposed is not only reckless but dangerously invasive.

Why It Matters: The exposure of such detailed personal information without consent is a stark invasion of privacy. It’s alarming to see how data that should be closely guarded can be mishandled and used as political ammunition.

Legal Boundaries Crossed

The release of these records potentially treads on several legal landmines. The Privacy Act of 1974 was designed to safeguard individuals against unwarranted invasions of privacy by federal agencies. Additionally, FOIA exemptions exist to protect personal privacy. However, the National Personnel Records Center, a branch of the National Archives and Records Administration tasked with maintaining these records, failed spectacularly in adhering to these legal frameworks.

Why It Matters: This incident raises significant concerns about the safeguarding of personal information by government entities. If laws and protocols can be so easily bypassed or ignored, what guarantees do citizens have that their privacy is being protected?

A Failed System

Grace McCaffrey of the National Archives and Records Administration admitted that “the technician should NOT have released the entire record,” acknowledging a failure to follow procedures. The fact that the Archives is now investigating “how and why” this failure occurred is cold comfort to those whose privacy has been compromised.

Why It Matters: This “egregious blunder” underlines a systemic failure within the mechanisms supposed to protect personal information. It’s not just about a single technician’s mistake; it’s about a breakdown in the adherence to privacy protocols.

Political Ramifications

The timing of this release, amidst a gubernatorial election described as a “dead heat,” cannot be overlooked. This act of privacy violation has morphed into a weapon of political warfare, potentially influencing the election’s outcome. While Sherill has edged ahead of Ciattarelli in some polls, the damage to her privacy and personal safety cannot be underestimated.

Why It Matters: In an era where information is power, the misuse of private data for political gain is both unethical and dangerous. It undermines the democratic process and contributes to the erosion of trust in public institutions.

In Conclusion

The release of Rep. Mikie Sherill’s unredacted personal military records is a disturbing breach of privacy, potentially violating federal laws and showcasing a dangerous precedent in political competition. It’s imperative to question and hold accountable those who bypass legal and ethical boundaries for political gain. As we move forward, protecting individuals’ privacy, especially in the political arena, must be paramount to preserve the integrity of our democratic institutions.
