Trump teases 2028 run with new slogan

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 29, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Delusional 2028 Tease: A Masterclass in Political Absurdity

President Donald Trump continues to tease out a possible 2028 run, despite the constitutional prohibition on a third term. On Friday, the 79-year old unveiled a new “slogan,” and his new name for Trump Republican voters.

Trump has acknowledged the constitutional block on a third term, recently telling reporters that Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, a constitutional attorney, told him a third term is not allowed — a fact he appeared to accept.

The post, on his Truth Social website, also says, “Trumplicans!”

“There is a new word for a TRUMP REPUBLICAN, which is almost everyone,” he recently wrote. “It is, TEPUBLICAN??? Or, TPUBLICAN???”

Apparently, “Trumplicans” won out.

Health care activist Melanie D’Arrigo remarked on Wednesday that “Trump is workshopping names for his cult, while Americans struggle to afford the rising costs of groceries, healthcare and housing.”

Reporting on Trump’s musings, TIME on Thursday noted that his new MAGA moniker comes “amid high-profile divisions within the MAGA base.”

Were Trump to run for a third term, he would be 82 on Election Day in 2028.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Delusional 2028 Tease: A Masterclass in Political Absurdity

In what reads like a script out of a political satire, former President Donald Trump once again flirts with the boundaries of reality and the United States Constitution. His latest antics? Teasing a 2028 Presidential run, a move so blatantly unconstitutional it almost transcends the realm of absurdity. Trump’s disregard for constitutional limits is not just audacious; it’s a dangerous precedent for democratic norms.

Trump, at the age of 79, unveiled a new “slogan” and a novel designation for his followers: “Trumplicans.” This move is a clear attempt to brand a faction of the Republican Party as his own, merging identity politics with a cult of personality to an extent rarely seen in American politics. The term “Trumplicans” itself is a testament to Trump’s relentless pursuit of personal aggrandizement over party or country.

His acceptance of the constitutional prohibition against a third term, as recounted in his conversation with Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, is laughably juxtaposed with his promotion of a “Trump 2028, Yes!” campaign slogan. This contradiction isn’t just political theatrics; it’s a deliberate attempt to muddy the waters of political discourse and test the limits of his support base’s loyalty.

The AI-generated meme of an older Trump brandishing a “Trump 2028” sign is emblematic of a deeper issue: the manipulation of digital media to propagate unrealistic political narratives. It’s a stark reminder of the age of misinformation we live in, where AI and social media can be weaponized to distort political realities and undermine democratic processes.

Moreover, Trump’s fixation on rebranding his followers as “Trumplicans” amidst pressing national issues such as rising costs of groceries, healthcare, and housing is a glaring demonstration of his priorities. Health care activist Melanie D’Arrigo’s remark that Trump is “workshopping names for his cult, while Americans struggle” is a poignant critique of Trump’s disconnection from the real issues facing the country.

The reporting on Trump’s musings, including the divisions within the MAGA base, is indicative of a broader existential crisis within the Republican Party. The new MAGA moniker, “Trumplicans,” may be an attempt to consolidate his base, but it also highlights the fracturing and identity crisis within the party.

At 82 years old by the 2028 Election Day, Trump’s age adds another layer of impracticality to his suggested run. This isn’t just about the feasibility of an octogenarian running for the highest office in the land; it’s about the perpetuation of a cult of personality that seems to disregard any semblance of practicality or constitutional adherence.

In Conclusion:

Trump’s 2028 run tease is not merely political banter; it’s a testament to his relentless pursuit of power and influence, even at the cost of constitutional norms and democratic principles. The term “Trumplicans” and the promotion of a 2028 campaign are emblematic of a larger issue: the manipulation of political identity and the undermining of democratic institutions. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant against such absurdities, recognizing them for what they are: dangerous distractions from the pressing issues of our time.
