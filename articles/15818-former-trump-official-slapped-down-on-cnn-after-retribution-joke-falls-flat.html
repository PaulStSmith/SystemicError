Former Trump official slapped down on CNN after retribution 'joke' falls flat

Mike Dubka, Ashleyy Allison (CNN screenshot)

An attempt at what he called a joke on CNN didn't earn a former Donald Trump communications official any laughs on Sunday morning but it did get him a lecture.

During the panel segment on "State of the Union" with host Dana Bash, the topic turned to Donald Trump going after his perceived enemies as he promised before the 2024 election when said he would seek "retribution."

That prompted Mike Dubka to smirk and chime in with, "I will just say one thing. The ultimate retribution that we saw in the last year was Joe Biden on the Democratic Party by deciding that he was going to run for reelection. That was ultimate retribution."

"How is that retribution?" panelist Adrienne Elrod asked.

Pointing to fellow Republican Lance Trover, Dubka offered, "There's one panel member that gets that joke."

Former National Coalitions Director for Biden-Harris 2020 Ashley Allison wasn't having it.

'It's actually changing the definition of a word, which is what often happens when we spread mis and disinformation," she told the two conservative panelists. "Retribution is when you don't like what somebody says and so you use the power that you have through a democratic process or an authoritative process, and you go after people with the hope that you intimidate them to stand up against you."

"We should all agree retaliation, retribution –– Donald Trump said retribution," she added.

Our Analysis:

The "Joke" That Wasn't: Dissecting Dubka's Dubious Declaration

In a recent bout of what can only be described as political theatre on CNN's "State of the Union," Mike Dubka, a former communications official for Donald Trump, delivered a line so steeped in irony and ignorance it almost demands a standing ovation for its audacity. Attempting humor, Dubka quipped that Joe Biden deciding to run for reelection was "the ultimate retribution" against the Democratic Party. This statement, devoid of wit or wisdom, not only fell flat but also inadvertently highlighted the bizarre circus that is contemporary political discourse.

Dubka's Misfire: A Closer Look

Let's dissect the layers of absurdity here. Dubka's comment was a swing at humor that not only missed the ball but seemed to forget which game was being played. In the context of a discussion about Donald Trump's promise of "retribution" against his enemies, equating Biden's reelection bid to an act of vengeance is a leap of logic so grand it would make Evel Knievel blush. It's akin to comparing a chess move to an act of war - both involve strategy, but the scales and consequences are worlds apart.

The Reaction: A Reality Check

Adrienne Elrod's response, "How is that retribution?" wasn't just a question; it was a reality check. It was the equivalent of asking, "Are we really doing this?" Meanwhile, Ashley Allison's rebuttal was a masterclass in patience and precision. She didn't just call out the inaccuracy; she provided a necessary primer on what retribution actually means in a political context. Her definition underscored the gravity of Trump's threats compared to the normalcy of a presidential reelection campaign.

The Bigger Picture: Distortion and Diversion

Dubka's attempt at humor and the subsequent defense by his fellow conservative panelist aren't just isolated incidents of failed comedy. They're symptomatic of a broader strategy: the distortion and diversion of political discourse. By making such a ludicrous comparison, Dubka wasn't just trying to be funny; he was muddying the waters. It's a tactic straight out of the disinformation playbook: confuse the issue, shift the narrative, and hope nobody notices that the emperor has no clothes.

Conclusion: The Joke's On Us?

In the end, this incident serves as a stark reminder of the state of political dialogue today. When former officials treat serious discussions about democratic principles and the potential for authoritarian abuses as opportunities for cheap shots, everyone loses. The real punchline? That we're expected to take these moments as jest, rather than the jarring jolts to our collective consciousness they truly are.

So, let's not laugh off Dubka's comment as just another failed attempt at humor. Instead, let's see it for what it is: a glaring example of how far some are willing to stretch the bounds of reason to score political points. In a world where words matter and democracy is delicate, we can't afford to let these moments slide by without scrutiny.
