White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt insisted Tuesday that the Trump administration was “moving on” from the scandal surrounding the late accused sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein.

At the White House press briefing, one reporter noted that the Epstein files indicated that President Donald Trump talked to authorities about the notorious sex offender in 2006.

“The president has gone on record before, and he’s told reporters that he said that Jeffrey Epstein, that he had no idea about Jeffrey Epstein’s crimes,” the reporter said. “During this conversation with the police chief, however, he says, according to this recounting of it, that ‘thank goodness you’re stopping him.’ He also called Ghislaine Maxwell ‘evil.’”

“So we’re moving on from that.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by the White House

In a recent display of political gymnastics, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt insisted that the Trump administration was “moving on” from the scandal surrounding the late accused sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein. This statement, in itself, is a masterful stroke of evasion, designed to sidestep the real issues at hand and redirect the narrative. Let’s dissect the layers of this deflection and the subtle art of rewriting history.

The Alleged Conversation: A Convenient Amnesia?

A reporter, armed with facts from the Epstein files, noted that President Donald Trump had a conversation with authorities about Epstein in 2006, where Trump reportedly expressed his relief at Epstein being stopped and called Ghislaine Maxwell “evil”. This directly contradicts Trump’s previous claims of ignorance regarding Epstein’s crimes. Yet, when pressed for clarity on this discrepancy, Leavitt sidestepped the question with the dexterity of a seasoned politician. She insisted that Trump “remained consistent” and had severed ties with Epstein due to his unsavory character.

The Strategy of Consistency: A Cloak of Transparency?

Leavitt’s defense hinges on the claim of Trump’s “consistency” and his alleged “honesty and transparency” regarding his relationship with Epstein. This narrative conveniently glosses over the documented interactions between Trump and Epstein, including the now-infamous quote from Trump in 2002, where he described Epstein as a “terrific guy” who enjoyed women “on the younger side.” The selective memory of the administration is not just astonishing; it’s a strategic maneuver to rewrite the narrative in real-time.

The Narrative War: Who’s Really Winning?

Leavitt’s assertion that the revelation from the FBI report might shatter the media’s narrative about Trump is a fascinating study in the manipulation of public perception. By framing the administration as the victim of a long-standing smear campaign, Leavitt attempts to pivot from defense to offense. The implication that the media has been “trying to push” a false narrative about Trump’s association with Epstein is not just a deflection—it’s a counter-attack designed to discredit the press and rally the base.

Moving On: But At What Cost?

The statement “So we’re moving on from that.” is perhaps the most telling part of this entire charade. It signifies the administration’s desire to dictate the terms of the conversation, to decide unilaterally that this chapter is closed, regardless of the unresolved questions and contradictions. This approach is emblematic of a broader strategy to control the narrative at all costs, even at the expense of truth and accountability.

Conclusion: The Art of the Evasion

In this latest press briefing, the White House has demonstrated a masterful use of deflection, denial, and the strategic rewriting of history. By insisting on “moving on” from the Epstein scandal, the administration seeks to close the book on a chapter that remains riddled with unanswered questions. This tactic, while effective in the short term, raises serious concerns about the integrity of public discourse and the accountability of those in power. The art of evasion, as practiced here, is not just a political strategy; it’s a systemic error that undermines the very foundation of democratic governance.
