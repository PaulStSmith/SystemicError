Say Goodbye to Herd Immunity — RFK Jr. and the Slow-Motion Reboot of Old Plagues

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Let’s start with the obvious: we do ourselves no favors when we use falsehoods to attack people like Robert F. Kennedy Jr. It doesn’t matter that he’s a font of anti-science nonsense — if we fight dishonesty with our own dishonesty, we hand him a weapon. Accuracy is our armor, and the moment we discard it for shock value, we become what we claim to oppose.

So, let’s get this straight: smallpox was eradicated in 1980 after a monumental, worldwide vaccination effort coordinated by the World Health Organization. The virus exists only in two ultra-secure labs — one in the U.S., one in Russia — and has not circulated naturally for over four decades. The meme above is wrong to say otherwise. The problem with that mistake isn’t just pedantic fact-checking — it’s that it hands anti-vaxxers a ready-made “Gotcha!” moment they will gleefully use to dismiss all pro-vaccine arguments.

But while smallpox isn’t coming back unless human stupidity makes it come back, Kennedy’s policies invite a rogues’ gallery of other pathogens back to the party — measles, polio, mumps, rubella, whooping cough, and influenza, just for starters. Diseases we had beaten back to the margins will become seasonal staples again if his “optional vaccination” fantasy becomes policy.

RFK Jr.’s Anti-Vaccine Crusade: A Public Health Time Bomb

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has built a brand on conspiracy-mongering about vaccines. His platform promises to make all vaccines “optional” and to end insurance coverage for them. He paints this as a noble stand for “medical freedom.” In reality, it’s a direct assault on herd immunity.

Herd immunity works because the vast majority of people are immune, either through vaccination or prior infection, creating a firewall that keeps diseases from finding new hosts. Remove that, and the firewall crumbles. With highly contagious diseases like measles, immunity rates need to be around 95% to keep the virus from exploding through a community. Drop below that threshold, and you’re back to 19th-century epidemiology.

The fallout wouldn’t be hypothetical. We’ve already seen the early signs:

  • Measles outbreaks in undervaccinated schools.

  • Polio virus detected in wastewater in New York.

  • Mumps clusters on college campuses.

  • Whooping cough spikes in states with lax vaccine laws.

Kennedy’s policies would accelerate these into the mainstream. First, we’d see “localized” outbreaks — in insular religious communities, in states with loose exemption laws, in neighborhoods where Facebook pseudoscience has replaced pediatric advice. Then, as vaccination rates drop nationwide, these outbreaks would become more common, more widespread, and harder to control. The endgame? Endemic circulation — permanent reestablishment — of diseases we once came close to wiping out.

The Fraud of “Religious Exemptions”

Let’s be blunt: there is no god handing down anti-vaccine edicts. The universe does not care what ancient texts say about plagues. When a virus spreads, it doesn’t pause to check whether the next host has a medical note from the Almighty.

“Religious exemptions” to vaccination requirements are a dangerous farce. They give cover to free riders who benefit from the immunity of others while actively undermining it. They turn public health into a pick-and-choose buffet, where ideology trumps epidemiology.

The logic fails on every level:

  1. Religious freedom does not include the freedom to endanger others. Your beliefs stop where my lungs begin.

  2. Faith does not confer immunity. Prayer doesn’t neutralize measles particles or kill polio virus in the gut.

  3. Public health is collective. Vaccination is not just about protecting yourself; it’s about protecting those who can’t be vaccinated for genuine medical reasons — the immunocompromised, newborns, cancer patients.

By allowing non-medical exemptions, we erode the shield that protects these vulnerable people. That isn’t “freedom.” It’s negligent harm.

Why Accuracy Matters — Even Against People Like RFK Jr.

When we exaggerate or fabricate, we may get short-term shock value, but we lose long-term credibility. The truth is more than enough to expose Kennedy’s policies as dangerous nonsense.

The facts are damning:

  • Vaccines have saved more lives than any medical intervention in history.

  • Diseases come roaring back the moment vaccination rates drop.

  • Optional vaccination is a recipe for public health collapse.

We don’t need to invent a false threat of smallpox to make the point. The truth — that Kennedy’s vision would resurrect measles, polio, and other killers — is bad enough. And unlike the meme’s flawed premise, it’s backed by hard epidemiological reality.

A Future We’ve Seen Before

History books are full of epidemics that decimated populations. We ended that cycle not by divine intervention or blind luck, but through science, coordinated action, and — above all — vaccination. Undoing that achievement would be an act of staggering stupidity, one we would pay for in hospital wards and funeral homes.

RFK Jr.’s crusade against vaccines isn’t a harmless personal quirk. It’s an existential threat to modern public health. And “religious exemptions” aren’t a quaint nod to diversity; they’re a loaded gun pointed at the herd immunity we all depend on.

We’ve been here before. We know how it ends. And we know how to prevent it.

But only if we have the courage to confront the lies — his, and ours — with the unflinching truth.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
