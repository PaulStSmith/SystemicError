'Just saying': Trump family member gives devastating insight on Epstein card scandal

A banner of Jeffrey Epstein and President Donald Trump hangs in Grand Park during a protest against federal migration enforcement in downtown Los Angeles, California, U.S. August 2, 2025. REUTERS/David Swanson

The niece of Donald Trump smashed his argument that his signature was forged on a lewd message sent to celebrate pedophile Jeffrey Epstein's 50th birthday.

Tthe House Oversight Committee released the complete 238-page volume of a birthday book compiled by Ghislaine Maxwell Monday, reigniting a scandal that has had Trump's camp reeling for weeks.

The document contains a birthday note reportedly written by Trump, positioned near an illustration of a nude woman. The signature appears in a provocative manner, resembling pubic hair. Trump has vehemently denied involvement and has initiated a lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal for first publishing the story.

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt attempted to dismiss the allegations Monday, stating emphatically that President Trump neither drew the picture nor signed it. However, her denial was quickly challenged by Trump's own niece.

Mary L. Trump, a psychologist and author known for her critical stance toward her uncle, weighed in on social media. "That's definitely his signature," she wrote on X. "Just saying."

The assessment of the daughter of Donald's older brother Fred Trump Jr. carries particular weight given her professional background and intimate family knowledge. She previously authored a scathing book about Trump titled "Too Much and Never Enough: How My Family Created the World's Most Dangerous Man."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Trump's Alleged Lewd Message and the Ensuing Controversy

In a spectacle that seems ripped straight from the pages of a political thriller, the saga surrounding a lewd message purportedly penned by Donald Trump for Jeffrey Epstein's 50th birthday has taken another sordid turn. The intrigue, however, isn't just in the allegations themselves but in the vehement denials, familial betrayals, and the political ramifications that follow. Let's dissect this latest development with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned analyst.

The Heart of the Scandal

At the core of this controversy lies a birthday note, allegedly written by Donald Trump, nestled provocatively near an illustration of a nude woman in a 238-page birthday book compiled by none other than Ghislaine Maxwell. The note's signature, scandalously resembling pubic hair, is a visual pun that would be considered in poor taste at best, and at worst, a damning connection to the infamous pedophile Jeffrey Epstein.

Trump's vehement denial of involvement, coupled with his lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal, suggests a man backed into a corner, fighting against the ropes. Yet, the White House's defense, led by Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, appears to be a flimsy rebuttal, hastily assembled and promptly contradicted by an unlikely source: Mary L. Trump.

Mary L. Trump: The Family Whistleblower

Mary L. Trump, the psychologist, author, and estranged niece of Donald Trump, brings to the table not just her professional expertise but also her intimate family knowledge. Her assertion that the signature is indeed Donald Trump's carries a weight that cannot be easily dismissed. This isn't just an academic assessment; it's a familial condemnation.

Her previous work, "Too Much and Never Enough: How My Family Created the World's Most Dangerous Man," paints a picture of a family dynamic rife with dysfunction and toxicity, setting the stage for her credibility in recognizing Donald Trump's signature. Her simple statement on X (formerly Twitter), "That's definitely his signature. Just saying," reads less like casual commentary and more like the final nail in the coffin of Trump's denial.

The Politics of Denial

The denial by Trump and his team is a textbook example of political damage control. However, the strategy seems to be faltering under the weight of familial betrayal and the visual evidence presented. In the court of public opinion, the optics are disastrously against Trump. The association with Jeffrey Epstein, by itself, is a political albatross. When combined with a lewd message in a birthday book compiled by Ghislaine Maxwell, it becomes a potential career-ender.

Karoline Leavitt's dismissal of the allegations as baseless does little to assuage the doubts of onlookers. Instead, it serves to highlight the Trump administration's often criticized approach to controversy: deny, deflect, and attack. This time, however, the attack seems to be coming from within, making it all the more potent.

Conclusion: A Scandal with Deep Roots

This story is more than just a scandal; it's a window into the complex web of relationships, power, and accountability (or the lack thereof) that surrounds Donald Trump. Mary L. Trump's intervention adds a layer of familial betrayal to the narrative, suggesting that the truth may be closer to the surface than Donald Trump would like.

As this saga unfolds, one must ask: where do we draw the line between personal indiscretions and public accountability? For Donald Trump, that line seems to be blurring with each passing day. The implications of this scandal, both legal and political, could be far-reaching, but one thing is clear: in the murky waters of political scandal, the truth eventually rises to the surface, and sometimes, it bears the unmistakable signature of scandal.
