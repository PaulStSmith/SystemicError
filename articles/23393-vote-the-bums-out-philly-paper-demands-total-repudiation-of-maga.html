'Vote the bums out': Philly paper demands total repudiation of MAGA

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Election Is “Perfect” Only When Trump Wins

Power Is the Point

The central fact is not that Donald Trump distrusts elections. It is that the president commands executive power while his allies control Congress and have declined to restrain him. The reported campaign against mail voting, voter access, election-security agencies, and independent certification is therefore not merely rhetoric. It is an attempt to make federal power serve one faction’s preferred result.

A Strategy, Not Confusion

Trump’s standard is brutally simple: Republican victories validate the system; Republican defeats indict it. That is not uncertainty about election integrity. It is an effort to strip political opponents of any legitimate path to power.

The Philadelphia Inquirer anticipates chaos if Democrats win either chamber. But “chaos” is too foggy a word when identifiable actors are preparing to contest outcomes they dislike. Trump and his allies would be choosing confrontation, not stumbling into disorder.

The Enablers Hold Office

The story’s weakest turn is its reliance on the catchall label “election deniers.” Denial is only one part of the machinery. The more consequential actors are officeholders capable of changing district lines, restricting voting, weakening security institutions, deploying federal agents, or refusing to certify winners.

Congressional Republicans are not spectators trapped inside Trump’s narrative. According to the editorial, they have done little to stop it. Institutional cowardice is still agency: officials who possess formal power and refuse to use it make the outcome possible.

Pardons as Political Instruction

Trump’s pardons sharpen the message. The source reports that he freed all those charged with or convicted of January 6-related crimes, including people who attacked police officers, and pardoned prominent allies involved in election subversion.

Whatever legal power makes those pardons possible, their political meaning is plain: loyalty to Trump can bring protection even after violence or efforts to overturn an election. That creates an incentive structure. Punishment becomes temporary; service to the leader becomes the safer bet.

Voting Cannot Carry the Whole Burden

“Vote the bums out” is emotionally satisfying and strategically incomplete. It asks voters to defeat officials through the same electoral system those officials are accused of manipulating and preparing to discredit.

Elections matter, but exhortation is not an institutional defense. The immediate burden falls on election administrators, courts, legislators, law-enforcement leaders, and other officials with authority over ballots, polling places, certification, and federal deployments. Treating voters as the sole solution lets powerful enablers disappear into the audience.

Authoritarianism Through Conditional Legitimacy

The larger pattern is authoritarian, not because every disputed procedure proves a master plan, but because the reported conduct makes legitimacy conditional on loyalty. Elections count when they preserve Trump’s power. Criminal consequences can be erased for allies. Institutions are weakened, redirected, or intimidated before they can constrain him.

That is how democratic machinery is converted without formally abolishing it. The ballots remain. The offices remain. The ceremonies remain. What disappears is the governing premise that opponents are allowed to win.
