'Detached' Trump more dangerous than ever as he loses 'grip on reality'

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20263 min read

“Detached” Is the Alibi. Power Is the Story.

A President, Not a Spectator

Donald Trump did not arrive in Ireland as an uninformed tourist offering barroom speculation. He arrived as president of the United States, carrying the authority of an office that shapes alliances, markets, security calculations, and diplomatic expectations.

His declaration that Irish unification “might as well happen now” therefore cannot be reduced to an eccentric outburst. The constitutional future of Northern Ireland is governed by political consent, not an American president’s impulse. Trump treated that process—and the history of bloodshed surrounding it—as scenery for his own performance.

Confusion Is the Wrong Diagnosis

The source’s emphasis on Trump appearing “detached” or increasingly bizarre risks turning an exercise of power into a medicalized mystery. The reporting presented here does not establish cognitive incapacity. It establishes conduct: Trump made a reckless intervention, promoted expansionist AI fantasies, glorified himself, and spoke about another country’s future as though his preference carried its own mandate.

Calling this behavior incomprehensible softens its political meaning. Trump’s method has long depended on collapsing distinctions between desire, fact, and authority. He says an outcome is inevitable because he wants it, then lets officials, allies, and journalists calculate how seriously the world must take him.

The People Erased by the Performance

Trump claimed to have “friends on both sides,” as if personal acquaintance granted him standing to dispose of a conflict shaped by sovereignty, identity, religion, and generations of violence. That is not diplomacy. It is imperial vanity dressed as friendliness.

The remark also erased the people whose consent is supposed to determine Northern Ireland’s status. Unionists and nationalists became supporting characters in a presidential monologue. The British and Irish governments were reduced to interested observers in a settlement Trump casually pronounced desirable and inevitable.

The Enablers Hold Power Too

Trump made the statement. But the consequences are not his alone to contain. Senior officials, diplomats, party leaders, and political allies enable the pattern whenever they translate his provocations into respectable policy language or insist that audiences should not take his words literally.

That institutional cleanup operation is not damage control in any neutral sense. It protects the person creating the damage while transferring uncertainty to everyone else. Foreign governments must prepare for the possibility that presidential spectacle will become state policy; Trump’s allies merely prepare another explanation.

Expansionism as Content

The AI images of an intimidated Canadian prime minister, killer robots, and maps of American territorial absorption are not proof of a specific operational plan. They are evidence of the political imagery Trump chooses to circulate: domination abroad, mechanized force at home, territorial appetite, and personal supremacy.

Dismissing that material as posting excess misses its function. Propaganda need not announce a detailed program. It can normalize a hierarchy in which neighboring countries are props, borders are negotiable at the leader’s whim, and power is measured by whom he can frighten or absorb.

The System Prefers a Personality Story

The larger failure is the conversion of authoritarian political behavior into a question about whether one man has “lost his grip.” That framing is comforting because it isolates the problem inside Trump’s mind. It leaves the surrounding machinery—the office, the party, the aides, the diplomatic apparatus, and the media economy of endless provocation—conveniently intact.

The systemic error is not that an American president speaks recklessly and nobody understands why. It is that a powerful political structure repeatedly converts his impulses into international risk, then presents the result as another baffling episode in the life of an unusual man. Trump supplies the contempt. Institutions supply the reach.
