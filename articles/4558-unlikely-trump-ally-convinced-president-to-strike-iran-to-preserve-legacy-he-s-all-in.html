Unlikely Trump ally convinced president to strike Iran to preserve legacy: 'He's all in'

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump’s order to strike Iran had been pitched to him as a way to salvage his legacy by an unlikely ally.

The president’s administration carried out strikes on the Middle Eastern country earlier this week, confirming the death of ayatollah Ali Khamenei in the process. Six US military personnel have also been killed in counterstrikes carried out by Iran. The bombing campaign on Iran came to be when Lindsey Graham took Trump to task on what he wanted to be his legacy.

Graham, who had previously condemned Trump as a “race-baiting, xenophobic, religious bigot” has since become a close ally of the president during Trump’s second term.

Speaking with Politico, Graham said, “We were thinking about this early, early on about how Iran is a spoiler for expanding the Abraham Accords and stability in the Mideast. I told him before he took office… if you can collapse this terrorist regime, that’s Berlin Wall stuff.”

Graham also claims he managed to cut through the naysayers on bombing Iran and convinced the president it was the right course of action to back Israel in their campaign.

He said, “There was a real fight not to do it. Let Israel do it by itself, or just not do much. So we talked a lot about this: ‘Mr. President, you want to have your fingerprints on this. You want them to know America will fight.’ He’s a hard sell, but when you sell him, he’s all in.”

Graham went on to suggest a regime change would ultimately benefit the US, and that if the new Iranian government takes the same stance as ayatollah Khamenei, the military may intervene again.

“If they want to reconstitute their country, to build more nuclear weapons and more missiles to hit us, we’ll treat the new people like we did the old people,” he said. “I just don’t believe it. I think they’re going to find a way to ... be a different country.

“’You break it. You own it.’ That may be true for a consignment shop, but it’s not true for foreign policy. If there’s a threat, break it.”

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump’s Dubious Legacy: A Critical Analysis of the Iran Strike

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a dystopian novel, the recent military strike on Iran, orchestrated under the guidance of President Donald Trump, has sent shockwaves through the international community. The operation, which led to the death of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, has not only escalated tensions but has also resulted in the tragic loss of six US military personnel. This move, pitched by Senator Lindsey Graham as a means to “salvage” Trump’s legacy, reeks of political opportunism and a dangerous underestimation of international consequences.

The Unlikely Alliance: Lindsey Graham and Donald Trump

It’s almost Shakespearean to witness Lindsey Graham, a man who once condemned Trump as a “race-baiting, xenophobic, religious bigot,” evolve into one of the president’s staunchest allies. This metamorphosis from critic to collaborator underscores a worrying trend in American politics where principles are often overshadowed by partisan agendas. Graham’s justification for the Iran strike, as reported by Politico, paints a picture of a man more concerned with historical recognition - comparing it to “Berlin Wall stuff” - than with the intricate web of geopolitical ramifications.

Strategic Shortsightedness

The rationale behind the strike, as per Graham’s account, is fundamentally flawed. The belief that a military intervention could lead to a regime change that benefits the US is not only naïve but dangerously simplistic. History is littered with examples of how such strategies tend to backfire, often leaving countries in a worse state than before. Graham’s assertion that the US will simply “treat the new people like we did the old people” if they don’t align with American interests is a testament to a policy approach that is as arrogant as it is unrealistic.

The Fallout: A Dangerous Escalation

The immediate aftermath of the strike – the death of a prominent Iranian leader and the loss of American lives – is just the tip of the iceberg. The long-term consequences of such an aggressive move could potentially destabilize the region further, endanger global security, and strain diplomatic relations with allies and adversaries alike. The notion, as Graham puts it, that “if there’s a threat, break it,” is a gross oversimplification of international relations and a dangerous guideline for foreign policy.

Conclusion: Recklessness Disguised as Strategy

The Iran strike, as orchestrated by Trump and advocated by Graham, is a stark reminder of the perils of conflating military might with foreign policy wisdom. The operation’s purported aim to cement Trump’s legacy may very well do so, but not in the way intended. Instead of being remembered as a strategic masterstroke, it risks being etched in history as a cautionary tale of hubris and miscalculation.

In sum, the decision to strike Iran, far from being a legacy-defining moment of triumph, could very well be remembered as a significant misstep with far-reaching consequences. The pursuit of short-term political gains at the expense of long-term stability and peace is neither commendable nor strategic. It’s a systemic error that underscores a profound misunderstanding of the complexities of global leadership and the delicate balance required to navigate international relations.
