Wisconsin GOP suffers big blow as another conservative state Supreme Court justice flees

Republicans in Wisconsin got yet more bad news on Monday, with conservative state Supreme Court Justice Annette Ziegler announcing she will not seek re-election next year.

“Now is the right time for me to step away to spend more time with my husband, kids and grandkids,” said Ziegler, according to the Wausau Pilot and Review. “I am incredibly proud that in all my elections I had support from a broad spectrum of legal, civic, law enforcement and political leaders — both Democrats and Republicans — who believed in my commitment to fairness, ethics and the rule of law.”

Supreme Court justices in Wisconsin are nominally nonpartisan, but in practice, each party endorses a candidate in their elections. The race to replace Ziegler will take place in 2027.

Ziegler, who previously served as the state’s chief justice and was one of the most consistent votes on the court for Republican Party positions, had reportedly thrown a temper tantrum behind the scenes after liberals secured a majority on the court in 2023. Since liberals achieved a majority, they have moved quickly to strike down the state’s abortion ban and Republicans’ legislative gerrymander in the state legislature.

This comes as the GOP is already fighting an uphill battle to hold the seat being vacated by fellow departing conservative justice Rebecca Bradley, the election for which takes place next month. If Democratic-aligned Wisconsin Court of Appeals Judge Chris Taylor defeats Republican-aligned colleague Maria Lazar, the court would shift to a 5-2 liberal majority, ensuring a liberal hold until at least 2030 if all judges serve out their terms.

If liberals go on to also win Ziegler’s open seat next year, the only remaining conservative justice on the court would be Brian Hagedorn, who is already considered one of the most moderate members of the conservative bloc.

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Judicial Woes in Wisconsin: An Analysis

The recent announcement by Conservative state Supreme Court Justice Annette Ziegler that she will not seek re-election next year is nothing short of a seismic shift in Wisconsin’s political landscape. Ziegler, who cloaks her departure in the noble desire to spend more time with family, leaves behind a legacy that is as contentious as it is partisan. Her departure is emblematic of a broader trend of conservative woes in the state, particularly concerning the judiciary.

The Illusion of Nonpartisanship

First and foremost, let’s address the elephant in the room: the so-called “nonpartisan” nature of Supreme Court justices in Wisconsin. While justices might ride into office on a wave of impartiality, the reality is starkly different. Each party throws its considerable weight behind its preferred candidates, making a mockery of the notion of a nonpartisan bench. Ziegler’s tenure and her self-proclaimed “broad spectrum” of support across political lines belie the fact that she has been one of the most reliable votes for Republican Party positions. This charade of nonpartisanship is a thinly veiled attempt to mask the overt politicization of the judiciary.

The Temper Tantrum and Its Aftermath

Ziegler’s reported temper tantrum following the liberal majority’s ascension to the court in 2023 is a testament to the partisan underpinnings of her judicial philosophy. It’s ironic, really. The moment the scales of justice began to tip away from her ideological alignment, her commitment to “fairness, ethics, and the rule of law” seemingly gave way to petulance. Under the liberal majority, significant strides have been made to strike down the state’s abortion ban and the Republicans’ legislative gerrymander — moves that are not just victories for liberals but for any advocate of equitable governance and reproductive rights.

The Uphill Battle for the GOP

The GOP finds itself in an increasingly precarious position. The upcoming election to replace the also departing conservative Justice Rebecca Bradley further threatens to tilt the scales towards a 5-2 liberal majority. This scenario is not merely a temporary blip; it has the potential to solidify a liberal stronghold on the court until at least 2030, assuming all judges serve out their terms. The specter of losing Ziegler’s seat next year only adds to the GOP’s judicial nightmare, leaving Justice Brian Hagedorn as the lone conservative, who is ironically considered a moderate within the conservative bloc.

Conclusion: A Turning Tide

Ziegler’s exit, while framed as a personal decision, is emblematic of a broader ideological shift in Wisconsin’s judiciary. The potential for a liberal stronghold on the state’s Supreme Court poses a significant challenge to the GOP, one that could reshape Wisconsin’s legal landscape for years to come. The irony of Ziegler’s departure — a justice who has consistently supported partisan decisions under the guise of nonpartisanship — should not be lost on us. It underscores the inherent political nature of judicial appointments and the critical importance of these positions in shaping state and national policies. The GOP’s judicial woes are a stark reminder of the pendulum swing of political power, and the current trajectory suggests a liberal ascendancy that could have far-reaching implications for Wisconsin’s governance.
