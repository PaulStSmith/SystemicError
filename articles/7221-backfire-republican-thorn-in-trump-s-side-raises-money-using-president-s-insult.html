Backfire: Republican 'thorn in Trump's side' raises money using president's insult

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 29, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

A Republican lawmaker who serves as a “thorn in Trump’s side” raised thousands of dollars using the president’s Christmas insult against him, according to a new report.

Republican congressman Thomas Massie bucked the president with this co-authorship of the Epstein files act, which required the Department of Justice to release all of its files related to the deceased child sex abuser’s case. Since that time, Trump has backed a primary challenger to Massie in Kentucky.

But Massie isn’t taking that lying down. After Trump called the congressman a “lowlife” on Christmas, Massie used that insult to raise thousands for his campaign within two hours, the Guardian reports.

Under the subheading “Massie uses president’s insult to raise funds,” the outlet reported Sunday, “On Christmas, Trump posted a rant on his Truth Social platform that dismissed Congress’s interest in Epstein as a ‘scam’ while referring to Massie – the only lawmaker named in the post – as ‘one lowlife ‘Republican’.”

The Guardian continued:

“That prompted Massie to reply on X: ‘Imagine celebrating a blessed Christmas with your family … suddenly phones alert everyone to the most powerful man in the world attacking you … for fulfilling his campaign promise to help victims!’”

It further reported, “Massie’s account cited the ‘one lowlife Republican’ phrase in the post’s text, highlighted it in screenshots of Trump’s tirade – and asked X users to ‘please support me’ while providing a link to his campaign donations site. More than 40 people had donated nearly $3,000 within the first two hours, Massie’s account said in separate follow-up posts.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Turning Insults into Income: A Case Study on Thomas Massie

In a political landscape that often resembles a gladiator arena more than a forum for civil discourse, Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has demonstrated a masterful stroke of ingenuity. Leveraging an insult from none other than Donald Trump, Massie has transformed what many would see as a political low point into a fundraising boon. The irony is rich, the execution flawless, and the underlying message? That in today’s political circus, even the lions can be outsmarted.

The Context: Massie, painted as a “thorn in Trump’s side,” co-authored the Epstein files act, an initiative demanding the Department of Justice unveil all files related to the notorious child sex abuser’s case. This move, predictably, irked Trump, leading to an endorsement of a primary challenger against Massie in Kentucky. Yet, the congressman’s response to being labeled a “lowlife” by Trump on Christmas is where the story takes an intriguing turn.

Trump’s Insult: On a day celebrated for peace and goodwill, Trump chose to unleash a tirade on his Truth Social platform, dismissing the congressional interest in Epstein as a “scam” and singling out Massie as a “one lowlife ‘Republican’.” This attack, rather than being met with indignation or silence, provided fodder for Massie’s countermove.

Massie’s Counterstrike: The congressman, with a blend of indignation and savvy, took to X (formerly Twitter), lamenting the interruption of his Christmas celebration by the “most powerful man in the world attacking you.” But the real genius was in what followed. Massie didn’t just voice his grievance; he monetized it. Using Trump’s insult as a rallying cry, he appealed for support, linking to his campaign donation site. The result? Over $3,000 raised within two hours from more than 40 donors.

Analysis: What Massie accomplished here is nothing short of political judo. By using the momentum of Trump’s attack, he not only defended his position but turned it into an advantage. This incident underscores a critical lesson for modern politicians: in an age where social media amplifies every insult and controversy, the adept player can turn vitriol into victory.

The Bigger Picture: Beyond the immediate financial gain, Massie’s strategy reveals a deeper rift within the Republican Party, one where allegiance to Trump is no longer the unassailable asset it once was. It signals a potential shift in the GOP’s internal power dynamics, where dissenters can not only survive Trump’s wrath but can, in fact, profit from it.

Conclusion: Massie’s response to Trump’s Christmas insult is a case study in political acumen. It shows that in the digital age, the pen (or the tweet) can be mightier than the sword. By flipping the script on Trump, Massie not only bolstered his campaign coffers but also solidified his reputation as a formidable player who refuses to be cowed by the former president’s bluster. This incident is a stark reminder that in politics, as in life, sometimes the best way to win the game is to change the rules.
