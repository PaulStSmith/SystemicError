'Marjorie went bad': Trump launches ugly attack on MTG after surprise resignation

By Paulo Santos

Early Saturday morning, Donald Trump lashed out at Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who stunned Washington, DC, late Friday night with her plan to resign her seat in the House of Representatives in early January.

According to the controversial lawmaker who had been one of the president’s most ardent backers, “I’m going back to the people that I love to live my life to the fullest as I always have and I look forward to a new path ahead. I’ll be resigning from office with my last day being January 5, 2026, and I look forward to seeing many of you again sometime in the future.”

In her video posted to X, Greene also cited the fight over the Jeffrey Epstein files as a major contributing factor in her departure and which had become central to her fallout with the president.

Trump held his fire until early Saturday before jumping on Truth Social, where he lashed out — before thanking her for her service.

“Marjorie ‘Traitor’ Brown, because of PLUMMETING Poll Numbers, and not wanting to face a Primary Challenger with a strong Trump Endorsement (where she would have no chance of winning!), has decided to call it ‘quits.’ Her relationship with the WORST Republican Congressman in decades, Tom Massie of Kentucky, also known as Rand Paul Jr. because he votes against the Republican Party (and really good legislation!), did not help her,” he wrote.

He then added, “For some reason, primarily that I refused to return her never ending barrage of phone calls, Marjorie went BAD. Nevertheless, I will always appreciate Marjorie, and thank her for her service to our Country! President DJT.”

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Resignation: Trump vs. Greene

In a turn of events that could only be described as a political circus, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) has thrown in the towel, announcing her resignation in a move that has sent ripples through the corridors of power in Washington, DC. This isn’t merely a resignation; it’s a dramatic exit stage right, with all the flair of a seasoned performer bowing out for an intermission, promising a return to the spotlight in some unforeseen future act.

The Fallout

Greene, known for her unyielding support of Donald Trump, has cited the contentious battle over the Jeffrey Epstein files as a significant thorn in her side, leading to her fallout with the former president. Herein lies the juicy irony: Greene, once an ardent soldier in Trump’s political army, has found herself on the receiving end of his ire. The spectacle unfolded on Truth Social, where Trump, in his characteristic style, oscillated between lambasting Greene and thanking her for her service—calling her Marjorie “Traitor” Brown in a bewildering blend of condemnation and commendation.

Trump’s Tirade

Trump’s tirade on Truth Social is a masterclass in political double-speak. He attributes Greene’s resignation to “PLUMMETING Poll Numbers” and an aversion to facing a primary challenger with his endorsement—a claim that paints Greene as both a political coward and a victim of her own failing popularity. The mention of her relationship with the so-called “WORST Republican Congressman in decades,” Tom Massie (or should we say, Rand Paul Jr.?), is a cherry on top of this convoluted insult cake.

Between the Lines

What’s truly fascinating here is the subtext of Trump’s message. It’s not just about Greene’s resignation; it’s a warning shot to any other political ally who dares to stray from the Trumpian line. The message is clear: loyalty to Trump is paramount, and deviation will not be tolerated. This episode illustrates the fragile nature of political alliances in Trump’s orbit, where today’s confidant can become tomorrow’s “traitor.”

The Spectacle Continues

As Greene steps back “to live [her] life to the fullest,” one can’t help but wonder about the future of such political spectacles. Trump’s reaction is a reminder of the volatile, often unpredictable nature of American politics, where allies are disposable, and loyalty is a one-way street.

In this narrative of power plays and political fallout, the only certainty is that the spectacle is far from over. Greene’s resignation might be today’s news, but the underlying tensions and fractures within the Republican Party are the prologue to more drama, more fallout, and more political theater in the chapters to come.

In conclusion, the saga of Greene’s resignation and Trump’s response is not just a story of political intrigue; it’s a symptom of a deeper systemic error in American politics, where personal vendettas often overshadow policy and principle. As the curtain falls on this act, one thing remains clear: in the theater of politics, the show must go on, but at what cost to democratic integrity and public trust?
