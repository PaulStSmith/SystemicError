The White House was furious Thursday after CBS News and its MAGA-friendly boss, Bari Weiss, had added a new communications executive who formerly worked for Rep. Liz Cheney (R-WY).

Jeremy Adler was slated to join the network’s communications team, two sources told Axios. From 2019 to 2023, Adler was deputy chief of staff and senior communications adviser for Cheney, “one of the president’s biggest foils during his first term, when she led the House’s probe into the Jan. 6 Capitol siege.”

The Trump administration had harsh words surrounding the hiring decision.

“The idea CBS would hire Liz Cheney’s flack who has worked to jail President Trump and make it impossible for anybody who supported the president to get hired is insanity. What the hell is Bari Weiss thinking?” a White House official told Axios.

Tensions have been escalating between CBS News and the Trump administration.

The network has faced increasing scrutiny and tension from the Trump administration over its editorial decisions and coverage of White House policies, with the network maintaining its commitment to independent journalism despite pressure from the administration. The relationship between the White House and CBS News reflects broader conflicts between the Trump administration and mainstream media outlets over news narratives, fact-checking and access to administration officials.

Weiss’s news operation has taken some recent hits. Longtime journalists have opted to exit the news organization and not renew their contracts, including departures by Anderson Cooper and Scott MacFarlane.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the CBS News Hiring Controversy

The recent controversy surrounding the hiring of Jeremy Adler by CBS News, as reported, provides a rich case for dissecting the interplay of media, politics, and the allocation of blame or responsibility. This scenario involves several key players: CBS News and its executive Bari Weiss, former Rep. Liz Cheney (R-WY) and her aide Jeremy Adler, and the Trump administration.

Institutional Power Dynamics

Firstly, it’s crucial to identify who holds the institutional power in this situation. CBS News, as a major media outlet, has considerable influence over public discourse but operates independently of the government’s direct control. Bari Weiss, as an executive within the organization, wields decision-making power regarding hiring. The Trump administration, while possessing significant political power, does not have formal authority over CBS News’s operational decisions. Liz Cheney, as a former representative, and her aide, Jeremy Adler, are more peripheral figures in terms of institutional power within this context.

Decision-Making and Outcomes

The decision at the heart of this controversy is the hiring of Jeremy Adler by CBS News. This decision was made by CBS News’s leadership or its designated hiring executives, presumably including Bari Weiss given her mentioned role. The outcome, therefore, is a direct result of CBS News’s internal decision-making processes.

Article’s Framing and Misdirection of Responsibility

The article frames the situation as a contentious move by CBS News that has angered the Trump administration, emphasizing the political affiliations and past roles of Jeremy Adler. However, this framing potentially misdirects responsibility in several ways:

  • Misdirection Towards Bari Weiss and Liz Cheney: The focus on Bari Weiss and Liz Cheney’s political affiliations and past actions creates a narrative that may unduly politicize a hiring decision, which, in the realm of independent journalism, should ideally be based on merit and the ability to contribute to journalistic integrity.

  • Misdirection Towards Actors with Limited Control: By highlighting the Trump administration’s furious reaction, the article might inadvertently suggest that the administration has or should have a say in CBS News’s hiring decisions, which is not the case. The independence of the media from government influence is a cornerstone of democratic societies.

False Equivalences and Scapegoating

The article does not explicitly make false equivalences, but the intense focus on political rivalry and the implied criticism of CBS News for hiring someone associated with a political “foil” to the president could be seen as promoting a narrative where media independence is undervalued. Moreover, the Trump administration’s criticism positions Adler as a scapegoat for broader tensions between the administration and the media, diverting attention from the substantive issues of journalistic freedom and the right of media outlets to hire staff based on their qualifications and the needs of the organization.

Conclusion

In conclusion, while the hiring of Jeremy Adler by CBS News has certainly stirred political controversy, a critical analysis reveals that criticisms from the Trump administration are misplaced. The decision was within the purview of CBS News’s institutional autonomy. The framing of the article, while capturing the tensions between the Trump administration and CBS News, risks contributing to a misdirected discourse concerning the independence of media institutions and their personnel decisions. It is essential to uphold the principle that media organizations must operate independently of political pressures to maintain journalistic integrity and freedom.
