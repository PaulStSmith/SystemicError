'That is not the law': Professor schools JD Vance as he scrambles to defend Trump threats

U.S. Vice President JD Vance stands in the House Chamber prior to U.S. President Donald Trump’s speech to a joint session of Congress at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 4, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Yale Law School graduate JD Vance was on the receiving end of a lecture from a former U.S. attorney for jumping in late on Sunday to defend Donald Trump in the squabble about issuing illegal orders to U.S. military members.

The president has been at war with Democrats in Congress who have served in the military for advising their brothers and sisters in arms not to follow any demand of the president that they believe is illegal.

Over the weekend, the president blew up at the advice and wrote on Truth Social, “THE TRAITORS THAT TOLD THE MILITARY TO DISOBEY MY ORDERS SHOULD BE IN JAIL RIGHT NOW, NOT ROAMING THE FAKE NEWS NETWORKS TRYING TO EXPLAIN THAT WHAT THEY SAID WAS OK.”

He added, “IT WASN’T, AND NEVER WILL BE! IT WAS SEDITION AT THE HIGHEST LEVEL, AND SEDITION IS A MAJOR CRIME. THERE CAN BE NO OTHER INTERPRETATION OF WHAT THEY SAID!”

That came after he threatened on Thursday, “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH!”

With Trump being criticized for his over-the-top response, Vance ran to his defense on Sunday by linking to a clip of Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) pressing her case on ABC her case against the president.

The vice president responded on X by writing, “If the president hasn’t issued illegal orders, them (sic) members of Congress telling the military to defy the president is by definition illegal.”

That brought a swift response from the ex-US Attorney and University of Alabama School of Law professor Joyce Vance on her Substack platform, where she wrote, “That doesn’t make any sense.”

Noting Vance’s legal background, she added, “Anyone with a Yale Law School education should be in a position to understand that a) members of the military have an obligation not to follow an illegal order, b) that reminding them of that obligation neither violates the law nor instructs them to defy a legal order, and c) that using those false statements to claim that members of Congress who made the video they object to so strongly is not ‘by definition’ illegal, and certainly not for members of Congress who have speech and debate clause privilege even if there had been something incorrect about their statements.”

Having laid out her own legal opinion, she questioned the White House strategy for keeping the controversy alive.

“What is the administration’s point here? Do they contest that the military and the intelligence community should pursue concerns through their chain of command to prevent compliance with illegal orders? Do they want those public servants to believe they must follow any orders, no matter what?’ she wrote before bluntly stating, “Because that is not the law.”

Our Analysis:

Vance’s Vapid Defense: A Legal Farce

The spectacle of JD Vance, a Yale Law School graduate, leaping to the defense of Donald Trump’s outbursts against those advising military personnel to disregard illegal orders is as baffling as it is concerning. Vance’s assertion, especially coming from someone of his educational background, is not only legally unsound but borders on the absurd. Let’s dissect the farcical nature of this situation and expose the cracks in Vance’s armor of logic.

Trump’s Tirade: A Prelude to Vance’s Folly

Donald Trump’s incendiary comments on Truth Social, where he branded those cautioning the military against following unlawful commands as “traitors” deserving of imprisonment, sets the stage for what can only be described as a theatrical display of authoritarianism. Trump’s escalation to calling such advisories “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH!” is nothing short of alarming. It’s a textbook example of a leader attempting to weaponize his power against the foundational principles of legal and ethical conduct within the military.

Vance Rushes to Defend the Indefensible

Enter JD Vance, who rather than applying his purported legal acumen to critique Trump’s dangerous rhetoric, opts to parrot the former president’s baseless claims. Vance’s defense—that advising military personnel to refuse illegal orders is in itself illegal—is not just flawed; it’s a gross misrepresentation of the law. This stance, especially coming from a Yale Law School graduate, is either a display of willful ignorance or a deliberate distortion designed to curry favor with Trump and his base.

The Voice of Reason: Joyce Vance

Contrasting JD Vance’s bewildering defense is the response from Joyce Vance, a former U.S. Attorney and law professor, who succinctly points out the absurdity of JD Vance’s claims. Her analysis is a beacon of clarity in the fog of Vance’s confusion. She reminds us that: - Military personnel are under an obligation to disobey illegal orders. - Advising them of this duty neither breaks the law nor encourages defiance against lawful commands. - The notion that members of Congress could be acting illegally by reminding the military of this obligation is ludicrous, especially given their speech and debate clause privilege.

Joyce Vance not only dissects JD Vance’s argument with surgical precision but also questions the motive behind the Trump administration’s insistence on pursuing this narrative. Her inquiry points to a deeper, more sinister aim: to cultivate an environment where public servants feel compelled to follow orders, irrespective of their legality.

The Underlying Legal and Ethical Principles

At the heart of this controversy lies a fundamental principle of military conduct: the duty to refuse unlawful orders. This is not a matter of opinion but a legal and ethical cornerstone designed to prevent abuses of power and ensure that the military operates within the bounds of law and morality. JD Vance’s failure to acknowledge this principle, or worse, his attempt to undermine it, is a disservice to his legal education and a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Judgment

JD Vance’s defense of Trump’s inflammatory and legally baseless statements is more than just a political misstep; it’s indicative of a deeper systemic problem where loyalty to individuals or parties supersedes commitment to legal principles and ethical standards. This episode should serve as a wake-up call to those who might be tempted to follow Vance’s lead. It underscores the necessity of steadfast adherence to the rule of law and the protection of democratic values, especially in the face of attempts to erode them from within.

In a time when the principles that underpin our democracy are under assault, it is the duty of legal professionals and public servants to stand as bulwarks against the tide of authoritarianism, not to be complicit in its advance.
