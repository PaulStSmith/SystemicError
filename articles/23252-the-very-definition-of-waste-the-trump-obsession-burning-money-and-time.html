'The very definition of waste': the Trump obsession burning money and time

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Atlas of Vanity

The reporting here is straightforward enough: Trump keeps ordering places and institutions renamed, and the resulting bill lands on agencies, local governments, researchers, and taxpayers. Lake Ontario becoming “Lake America” is not policy. It is executive vanity with a procurement trail.

Power Writes the Map

The actual power in this story sits where it always does in this administration: at the White House. Trump issues the order, and the federal bureaucracy is forced to absorb the administrative mess. U.S. Geological Survey records, FAA systems, signage, printed materials, park markers, tourism brochures, and a long tail of public references all become compliance chores. That is not accidental fallout. It is the mechanism.

The article’s framing stays close to the money, which is useful but incomplete. The deeper issue is not whether this rename is expensive. It is that a president is using the machinery of government to force institutions to participate in his personal branding exercise.

Waste as a Governing Style

The waste is the point, not the bug. If a name change produces no lower cost, no better service, and no public benefit, then it is not governance. It is ritualized obedience dressed up as patriotic theater. Newsweek’s sources are right to call it wasteful, but the larger pattern is more corrosive: public capacity is being spent on meaningless symbols while real work gets displaced.

This is how power hides in plain sight. The order looks absurd, almost cartoonish, which encourages observers to treat it as a distraction. But the administrative burden is real. Someone has to update datasets, maps, signs, websites, and records. Someone pays for the printer, the labor, the software changes, the staff hours.

The Cost Is Pushed Downward

The burden does not stay in Washington. It spills into small cities, schools, municipal services, agencies, researchers, and ordinary people who depend on stable geographic names. Oswego’s mayor, a Republican no less, described the expense as unnecessary and significant for a city of 17,000. That matters because it shows the order is not some abstract national flourish. It is a mandate that gets translated into local bills.

This is the familiar authoritarian trick: centralize the decision, decentralize the pain. The president gets the spectacle. Everybody else gets the invoice.

Naming as Propaganda

The renaming spree is not about geography. It is about domination through language. “Gulf of America,” “Department of War,” “Lake America,” and now the suggestion that New Mexico should become “NEW AMERICA” all belong to the same project: turning state power into a stage set for nationalist bragging. The issue is not confusion. The issue is deliberate symbolic capture.

That is why the polling matters. Only 14 percent reportedly support the Lake Ontario rename. Public rejection does not stop the order, because the order is not meant to be popular. It is meant to demonstrate that institutional reality must bend to one man’s taste.

What This Reveals

This is a small story with a large shape. Trump is not merely renaming places. He is testing how much arbitrary command the state will absorb while its functionaries rationalize the damage as administrative necessity. That is the political pattern: spectacle at the top, waste in the middle, compliance at the bottom.

A healthy democracy does not let public institutions become props in a leader’s private mythology. When government spends its energy laundering ego into official language, the public should not mistake absurdity for harmlessness. It is governance by petty decree, and the cost is paid in money, labor, and the slow degradation of institutional seriousness.
