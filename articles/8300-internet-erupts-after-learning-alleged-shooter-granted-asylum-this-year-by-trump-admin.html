Internet erupts after learning alleged shooter 'granted asylum this year' by Trump admin

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

29 year-old Afghan national Rahmanullah Lakanwal was arrested Wednesday and accused of shooting two National Guardsmen in downtown Washington D.C., who both are currently hospitalized in critical condition. The alleged gunman’s motive has not yet been revealed, but President Donald Trump insinuated in a video address posted to Truth Social that the shooting could be attributed to former President Joe Biden’s immigration and refugee policy.

“The suspect in custody is a foreigner who entered out country from Afghanistan — a hellhole on earth. He was flown in by the Biden administration in September 2021,” Trump said. “... His status was extended under legislation signed by President Biden ... This attack underscores the single greatest national security threat facing our nation.”

However, CNN law enforcement and intelligence analyst John Miller said Wednesday that new information about Lakanwal suggests that the Trump administration may be more to blame for Lakanwal’s residency than Biden. He pointed out that the alleged shooter may have had his visa sponsored by U.S. military personnel as part of programs in place meant to help Afghanis fleeing the Taliban who helped the United States.

“He applies for asylum in December of 2024,” Miller said. “Now, he goes through another vetting process involving that, and he’s approved for asylum in April of this year, under the Trump administration.”

The new revelations sparked outrage on social media. Writer Wajahat Ali tweeted: “Why did Trump invite a man who shot the National Guard?”

“Am I doing this right, MAGA? I am, right? I’m following your logic,” he wrote. “So, yeah, Donald Trump should be blamed entirely.”

“The [alleged] shooter was granted asylum this year,” observed Colorado State University political science professor Maria Isabel Puerta Riera.

“WHY DID THE FBI LEAVE THIS OUT OF THEIR BRIEFING?” New York Times bestselling author Don Winslow wrote on X. “WHY DIDN’T KASH PATEL MENTION ANY OF THIS?”

Researcher Arif Ammar noted that there are still “many questions” on the nature of the Wednesday shooting including the “motive of the assailant,” “who sponsored his evacuation from Afghanistan” and any “possible adverse affiliation of the suspect.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Blame Game in D.C. Shooting: A Tangled Web of Political Finger-Pointing

The recent arrest of 29-year-old Afghan national Rahmanullah Lakanwal, accused of critically injuring two National Guardsmen in downtown Washington D.C., has swiftly morphed into a political circus, replete with blame-shifting and factual distortions. At the heart of the controversy is former President Donald Trump’s swift attribution of the incident to the immigration and refugee policies of his successor, Joe Biden. However, emerging details suggest a more complicated narrative, one that potentially implicates both administrations in varying degrees.

Trump’s Hastily Drawn Conclusions

In a characteristic display of rapid-fire finger-pointing, Trump wasted no time in connecting Lakanwal’s actions to Biden’s policies, framing the incident as a direct consequence of the latter’s immigration stance. Trump’s comments, made in a video address on Truth Social, encapsulated his argument by labeling Afghanistan as “a hellhole on earth” and insinuating that Biden’s administration was responsible for Lakanwal’s presence in the U.S. This narrative, however, crumbles under scrutiny.

The Reality Check

CNN’s John Miller introduces a critical counterpoint, highlighting that Lakanwal’s entry and subsequent asylum in the U.S. might actually trace back to the Trump administration’s own policies and actions. Specifically, the alleged shooter’s visa sponsorship by U.S. military personnel—a program designed to assist Afghans fleeing Taliban persecution—casts doubt on the simplicity of Trump’s narrative.

Moreover, Lakanwal’s asylum application process, initiated and concluded within the timeframe of the Trump administration, further complicates the former president’s attempt to shift blame onto Biden. This revelation, unsurprisingly, ignited a fury of reactions across social media platforms, with commentators and analysts pointing out the irony and hypocrisy in Trump’s rush to judgment.

The Blame Game and Its Implications

The eagerness of Trump and his supporters to leverage this tragic incident as a political weapon against Biden underscores a broader issue within American politics: the propensity to exploit national security concerns for partisan gains. Trump’s insinuations, devoid of a complete factual basis and prematurely assigning blame, exemplify this problem.

The controversy also exposes the pitfalls of a highly polarized political environment, where the truth often becomes collateral damage in the rush to score political points. The back-and-forth blame game between Trump and Biden supporters, fueled by incomplete information and speculative connections, distracts from the more pressing need to thoroughly investigate the incident and address any systemic flaws that it may reveal.

The Unanswered Questions

The case of Rahmanullah Lakanwal raises numerous questions, not least concerning the nature of his arrival in the U.S., the vetting processes involved in his asylum approval, and the broader implications for U.S. immigration and refugee policies. The focus on political blame-shifting risks overshadowing these critical issues, delaying necessary discussions on policy reforms and the implementation of more effective vetting mechanisms.

Furthermore, the incident highlights the challenges of integrating refugees and asylum seekers into American society, a task that demands comprehensive support systems and community engagement strategies to prevent radicalization and ensure successful integration.

Conclusion

The shooting incident involving Rahmanullah Lakanwal is a complex event that cannot and should not be reduced to simplistic partisan narratives. The rush to assign political blame, while ignoring the intricacies of the case and the systemic issues at play, serves only to deepen divisions and hinder constructive dialogue. A more nuanced, fact-based approach is essential to understanding the root causes of such incidents and preventing their recurrence. In the end, the truth is often more complex than convenient political narratives would have us believe.
