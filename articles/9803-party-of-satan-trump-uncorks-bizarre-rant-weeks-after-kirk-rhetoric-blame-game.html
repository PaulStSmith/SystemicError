‘Party of Satan!’ Trump uncorks bizarre rant weeks after Kirk rhetoric blame game

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of the Absurd: Trump’s Latest Social Media Circus

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media as he arrives at Republic Airport in Farmingdale, New York, U.S., September 26, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

In a late-night social media posting spree, President Donald Trump promoted a meme that declared Democrats are the “party of hate, evil, and Satan.” The attack on his political opposition comes just weeks after the President and his allies blamed Democratic rhetoric for the assassination of conservative commentator Charlie Kirk, despite providing no evidence to support their claims.

The meme the President posted also stated that the Democratic Party is “dead,” and has “no leadership,” “no message,” “no hope,” and, “their only message for America is to hate Trump.”

In an Oval Office address just after Kirk’s assassination, Trump declared:

“It’s long past time for all Americans and the media to confront the fact that violence and murder are the tragic consequence of demonizing those with whom you disagree day after day, year after year, in the most hateful and despicable way possible.”

“For years,” he continued, “those on the radical left have compared wonderful Americans like Charlie to Nazis and the world’s worst mass murderers and criminals. This kind of rhetoric is directly responsible for the terrorism that we’re seeing in our country today, and it must stop right now.”

HuffPost reported on Friday that Trump “presumably” had been in “a snit over the continued partial government shutdown,” and “literally demonized his political opponents while his allies promote lies about Democrats wanting to give health insurance to undocumented migrants.”

Overnight, the President also posted several other memes and videos, including one that The Daily Beast on Friday described as “presumably an AI-generated cover of Blue Öyster Cult’s 1976 hit ‘(Don’t Fear) The Reaper,’ portrays … OMB director and Project 2025 architect Russell Vought as the reaper in question.”

“The visuals, which are also AI-generated, show Vought as the Grim Reaper, cavorting around Washington, D.C., while wielding his scythe. Interspersed throughout are shots of a band led by Trump playing a cowbell—one is famously used on the original track—accompanied by Vance on the drums.”

Trump, who had disavowed any knowledge of Project 2025 throughout his 2024 campaign, on Thursday announced he was meeting “with Russ Vought, he of PROJECT 2025 Fame, to determine which of the many Democrat Agencies, most of which are a political SCAM, he recommends to be cut, and whether or not those cuts will be temporary or permanent.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump’s Latest Social Media Circus

In what can only be described as a late-night escapade into the realm of the bizarre and unsettling, President Donald Trump has once again leveraged the power of social media to launch an attack on the Democratic Party, branding it as the “party of hate, evil, and Satan.” This rhetorical bombshell, dropped amidst the digital ether, is emblematic of the increasingly surreal and divisive landscape of American politics. But let’s unpack the irony, the hypocrisy, and the downright falsehoods that permeate this narrative.

Firstly, the choice of words—hate, evil, and Satan—is, to say the least, rich coming from a President whose tenure has been marked by vitriolic attacks, divisive rhetoric, and an unparalleled capacity to stoke the fires of animosity within the body politic. To accuse the Democratic Party of being the harbinger of hate and evil, while simultaneously engaging in a campaign of demonization against them, is a masterclass in hypocrisy.

Secondly, the assertion that the Democratic Party is “dead,” devoid of leadership, message, or hope, is not only a gross misrepresentation but also a dangerous underestimation. Regardless of one’s political leanings, it’s intellectually dishonest to claim an entire party, representing a significant portion of the American populace, is without direction or purpose. This kind of blanket dismissal serves not the cause of constructive debate but rather the cultivation of a polarized and unbridgeable divide.

In the wake of the tragic assassination of conservative commentator Charlie Kirk, President Trump and his allies were quick to point fingers at Democratic rhetoric, yet conspicuously absent was any tangible evidence to support such claims. This tactic of blame-shifting is not new, but its use here is particularly insidious, exploiting a moment of national grief for political gain. The President’s call for Americans and the media to confront the consequences of demonizing political opponents rings hollow when he himself engages in the same with gusto and impunity.

The reference to Trump’s Oval Office address, wherein he lambasts the radical left for comparing Americans to Nazis and mass murderers, would be laughable if it weren’t so tragically ironic. This comes from a man whose rhetoric has often veered into the realm of the extreme, and who has shown little restraint in dehumanizing those who dare to oppose him.

Moreover, the President’s nocturnal social media activity, including the promotion of memes and AI-generated videos, verges on the surreal. The depiction of OMB director Russell Vought as the Grim Reaper, scything his way through Washington, D.C., accompanied by a band led by Trump, is a spectacle that defies rational analysis. It’s a bewildering mix of propaganda, fantasy, and dystopian satire, blurring the lines between political messaging and absurdist art.

The article also touches upon Trump’s sudden embrace of Project 2025 and his meeting with Russell Vought to discuss potential cuts to “Democrat Agencies.” This sudden pivot, after disavowing any knowledge of Project 2025 throughout his 2024 campaign, is indicative of a broader pattern of obfuscation, inconsistency, and a cavalier attitude towards governance.

In conclusion, Trump’s latest social media barrage is not merely an attack on the Democratic Party; it’s an assault on the very principles of rational discourse and democratic engagement. It’s a manifestation of a political strategy rooted in division, fearmongering, and the relentless pursuit of power at any cost. As observers, participants, and victims of this ongoing spectacle, we must ask ourselves: at what point does the theater of the absurd become intolerable?
