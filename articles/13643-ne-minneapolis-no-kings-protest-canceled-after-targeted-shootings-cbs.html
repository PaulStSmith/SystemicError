NE Minneapolis 'No Kings' protest canceled after 'targeted' shootings: CBS

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Demonstrators protest against U.S. President Donald Trump and his administration during a ‘No Kings’ rally near the U.S. embassy in Berlin, Germany, June 14, 2025. REUTERS/Fabrizio Bensch

A "No Kings" march scheduled for Saturday afternoon in northeast Minneapolis has been canceled after the shocking "targeted" shooting of two Minnesota lawmakers and their spouses by a man impersonating a police officer, CBS News reported.

With a manhunt underway for the shooter who left a list of lawmakers behind after police confronted him in front of the home of state Rep. Melissa Hortman (D) and her husband, both of whom were found dead, locals have been warned to shelter in place as city, state and federal law enforcement agents flood the area.

Noting that state Sen. John Hoffman (DFL) and his wife were also wounded at a separate location, Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz called the shootings a "politically motivated assassination," and stated in a press conference, "We don't settle our differences with violence or at gunpoint. In the state of Minnesota, and as recently as last week, in the most closely divided state legislature in the country, we sat down, shook hands and compromised, and we served the state of Minnesota together. It's possible in politically charged times to find compromise, democracy and civil discourse. I, Minnesota and across the country, stand against all forms of political violence."

That led the organizers of the anti-Trump "No Kings" event in northeast Minneapolis protest to call off their march.

In a statement, they explained, "Our thoughts are with the victims, their families, and the communities impacted by this violence. Some events in other parts of the greater Minneapolis area are still moving forward at this time, and we are actively monitoring the situation."

Our Analysis:

The Grim Intersection of Political Violence and Public Fear

The recent "targeted" shooting of two Minnesota lawmakers and their spouses is a chilling reminder of the volatile intersection between political discord and violent action. As reported by CBS News, this heinous act, perpetrated by a man masquerading as a police officer, not only claimed lives but has also instigated a pervasive state of fear across northeast Minneapolis. With the "No Kings" march—a planned demonstration against former President Trump—being canceled in the wake of this tragedy, we are forced to confront the dire consequences that emerge when political disagreements transcend the bounds of civil discourse and enter the realm of lethal violence.

The Unsettling Reality of Political Assassinations

Labeling the shootings as a "politically motivated assassination," Minnesota Governor Tim Walz's response encapsulates a fundamental principle that seems to have been disregarded: political differences should never be settled through violence. The governor's remarks, emphasizing the state's commitment to compromise and civil discourse, starkly contrast with the brutal reality of the situation. The act of violence not only silenced two public servants but also sent a chilling message to anyone who dares to engage in the political process.

The Implications of Canceling the "No Kings" March

The decision to cancel the "No Kings" march, while understandable in the context of ensuring public safety, carries with it significant implications. Firstly, it underscores the palpable fear that such acts of violence can instigate among those advocating for change through peaceful means. Secondly, it inadvertently allows the perpetrator's actions to achieve a form of success by halting the very expression of democratic dissent they seemingly aimed to suppress.

The Response and its Reflection on Today's Political Climate

The organizers of the "No Kings" event, in their statement, highlighted a sentiment that should resonate with all who cherish democratic values: the importance of community, empathy, and the unwavering rejection of violence as a means to address political grievances. As they monitor the situation and express solidarity with the victims and their communities, a crucial question emerges: how can society ensure that the foundational principles of democracy are protected against the corrosive effects of political violence?

In Conclusion

The tragic events in Minneapolis serve as a stark reminder of the fragility of civil discourse in today's politically charged environment. As we mourn the loss of life and grapple with the fear instigated by such violence, it is imperative that we reaffirm our commitment to resolving differences through dialogue, compromise, and mutual respect. Let us not allow the actions of a few to undermine the democratic values that bind us together. In the face of adversity, our resolve must be stronger than ever to stand united against all forms of political violence.
