On Saturday morning, MSNBC host Katie Phang invited Guardian reporter Hugo Lowell on to discuss his new report on the turmoil at the Pentagon under Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and quickly singled out a startling revelation of an accusation of cocaine use.

With Hegseth under the gun for what has been dubbed "Signalgate," new revelations that he included his wife, brother and lawyer in a separate Signal chat where he discussed war plan, and use of an unsecured internet commercial "dirty line" from his Pentagon office, Lowell reported that fighting among dismissed Hegseth aides has also gotten messy.

According to his report, the DOD has been "marked for weeks by ugly internal politics" with his now-ousted chief of staff Joe Kasper pointing the finger at the departed Dan Caldwell, deputy chief Darin Selnick and chief to the deputy defense secretary Colin Carroll, with them reciprocating.

According to MSNBC host Phang, what she fund intriguing was a passage where Lowell wrote, "The tensions among the former aides have continued since their collective ouster. Carroll has considered filing a defamation suit against Kasper and started making calls on the Monday after he was fired, asking people whether Kasper had ever been seen doing cocaine in a previous job. Kasper has complained that some of the calls went to his wife and previous clients, asking rhetorically to associates how he would have been able to hold a security clearance and pass regular drug tests."

"Your piece that just came out this morning is fantastic, but there was something that stuck with me was pretty hardcore," Phang prompted her guest. "I mean, Hugo, this is the Department of Defense, this is national security, this is the welfare of not only our armed forces and the people that are sacrificing to be able to defend us, but it's our safety. And this is the type of insanity that's going on under Pete Hegseth's's tenure."

"It was Colin Carroll, the chief of staff of the deputy secretary, who in the wake of his firing, you know, he was basically bundled out of the Pentagon along with two other aides who were seen as antagonistic or at odds with the chief of staff at the Pentagon, Joe Kasper and what we have seen in the days since is this kind of continuation of that interpersonal conflict," Lowell responded.

"And so the Monday, after Colin Carrol was was fired –– he was fired on the Friday –– he was busy calling up people around Kasper's life, including his wife and his in-laws and, and kind of former clients trying to chase down a tip that Kasper may have, been using cocaine in a previous job. and he claimed it was because he was trying to do research for his defamation suit," he elaborated.

"Now, we should say, you know, Joe Kasper, the chief of staff who has since left that role in the wake of all of this as well, he has strenuously denied using any cocaine," the Guardian reporter cautioned "And when reached by phone yesterday, told us that, you know, it's so 'egregiously. stupid' that I'm, you know, getting mired into this stuff and how could I have held a security clearance for so long if I was actually doing drugs? But the wider point is correct."

Our Analysis:

The Pentagon in Disarray: A Scathing Look at the Allegations Under Pete Hegseth's Tenure

The recent report by Hugo Lowell of The Guardian, as discussed on MSNBC with host Katie Phang, unveils a saga so convoluted and scandalous it could rival a soap opera, yet, tragically, this is not fiction – this is the current state of affairs at the Pentagon under Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The allegations, ranging from the misuse of secure communication channels to accusations of cocaine use among senior aides, paint a picture of an institution in turmoil, far removed from the principles of discipline and integrity it professes to uphold.

The Signal Scandal: A Breach of Security or a Family Chat Gone Wrong?

At the heart of Lowell's report is the startling revelation that Hegseth included his wife, brother, and lawyer in a separate Signal chat where he discussed war plans using an unsecured internet commercial "dirty line" from his Pentagon office. This alone should send shivers down the spine of anyone with a modicum of concern for national security. The audacity to use a platform, which although encrypted, was not cleared for such sensitive discussions, demonstrates a blatant disregard for the protocols that safeguard our nation's secrets. It's akin to discussing state secrets at a family barbecue, with the only difference being the catastrophic implications for national security.

Cocaine Allegations: The Tip of the Iceberg or a Distraction?

As if the security lapses were not concerning enough, the report delves into the ugly underbelly of internal politics at the Department of Defense (DOD). The ousted chief of staff, Joe Kasper, is at the center of a whirlwind of accusations, including alleged cocaine use. This accusation, while sensational, underscores a deeper issue of trust and integrity among the Pentagon's senior ranks. The fact that such allegations are even plausible enough to warrant discussion is a damning indictment of the culture Hegseth appears to have fostered during his tenure.

Colin Carroll, the chief of staff of the deputy secretary, in a move that smacks of a political thriller rather than the sober conduct expected at the Pentagon, reportedly made inquiries into Kasper's alleged drug use post-firing. This act of digging up dirt, ostensibly for a defamation suit, is emblematic of the toxic environment that seems to have taken root under Hegseth's watch.

The Real Casualties of This Turmoil

What is egregiously absent from this entire debacle is a concern for the real casualties – the principles of leadership, accountability, and the safety of the nation. As Phang aptly points out, this is not merely a matter of internal squabbles; this is about the national security of the United States and the welfare of its armed forces. The individuals involved are not just playing with their careers but the safety of millions.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The saga unfolding at the Pentagon under Pete Hegseth's leadership – if one can call it that – is a stark reminder of the dangers of nepotism, the erosion of ethical standards, and the potential fallout from a lack of accountability. The allegations reported by Lowell, ranging from security breaches to drug use, should not be dismissed as mere tabloid fodder. They are symptomatic of a systemic failure that requires immediate and decisive action.

The Pentagon is not a playground for power plays or a backdrop for personal vendettas. It is the cornerstone of America's defense infrastructure, and as such, it demands leadership that is beyond reproach. The revelations brought to light by Lowell's reporting are a call to action – a demand for transparency, integrity, and, most importantly, a return to the principled leadership that is the bedrock of military discipline and strategic command.

In a world fraught with challenges and adversaries keen to exploit any sign of weakness, the United States cannot afford the luxury of internal chaos at the helm of its defense. The time for accountability is now.
