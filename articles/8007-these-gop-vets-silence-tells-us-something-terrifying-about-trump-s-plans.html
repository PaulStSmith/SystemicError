These GOP vets' silence tells us something terrifying about Trump's plans

Those of us who came of age during the ’60s well remember the Vietnam War, its horrific toll of human life, its cost, the division it caused, and its long-term effects upon those who served and survived.

We also remember the phony incidents and propaganda that threw our young men and women into combat in what became our nation’s longest war … which we lost.

As noted in 2008 by the U.S. Naval Institute in reference to the Gulf of Tonkin incident: “Once-classified documents and tapes released in the past several years, combined with previously uncovered facts, make clear that high government officials distorted facts and deceived the American public about events that led to full U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War.”

Combined with the propaganda that the “Domino Theory” would see all of Southeast Asia under communist rule if the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, also known as “North Vietnam,” overthrew the government of South Vietnam, the dogs of war were unleashed.

We saw a similar concocted reason for the Iraq War in the assurance from President George W. Bush and Vice President Dick Cheney that “Weapons of Mass Destruction” — or WMDs — existed, just awaiting use against the United States. It was a flat out lie — but again, with horrifying consequences of death, destruction, cost, and long-term mental and health effects on the nation’s men and women in the military — all to virtually no positive outcome.

Comes now the next attempt to push our nation into yet another fabricated war — this time with Venezuela, which just coincidentally happens to have the largest proven reserves of oil on the planet at 303 billion barrels.

This time around it’s Donald Trump’s propaganda machine that declares drugs from Venezuela are a threat to our nation on such a scale that we now have dozens of warships, planes, and 15,000 troops in the region as the president threatens to invade that sovereign nation and has already drawn international condemnation for blowing up suspected drug boats and killing more than 80 individuals — without releasing a scintilla of verification to the public.

Which begs the question: Given that three of the four members of Montana’s Congressional Delegation are veterans, and that two of them – Rep. Ryan Zinke and Sen. Tim Sheehy — are former Navy SEALS, how can they stand silent while this deadly charade unfolds?

This is particularly poignant since other members of Congress — both Republican and Democrat — are openly questioning the fabricated rationale for the war. They’re also critical of a violation of the international Law of Armed Conflict by a “double tap” by SEAL Team 6 that killed two survivors left clinging to the wreckage of an alleged drug boat in the middle of the ocean.

As reported by the Associated Press: “I can’t imagine anyone, no matter what the circumstance, believing it is appropriate to kill people who are clinging to a boat in the water,” said Michael Schmitt, a former Air Force lawyer and professor emeritus at the U.S. Naval War College. “That is clearly unlawful.”

Given our past involvement in disastrous foreign wars fueled by propaganda and false information one would expect the veterans in Montana’s Congressional delegation to speak up, demand answers and proof, and honor the Oath of Office they took as soldiers and elected officials to uphold the Constitution’s mandate that only Congress may declare war.

Moreover, they have not even bothered to ask Montanans about sending their sons and daughters to yet another foolish foreign war — a “war” which they are aiding and abetting through their unconscionable silence.

Our Analysis:

The Endless Cycle of War: A Critical Analysis

Once more, the specter of war looms large over the United States, driven by the same old engines of deceit and propaganda that have marred its history. George Ochenski’s article casts a sharp light on the disturbing pattern of U.S. involvement in foreign conflicts under false pretenses, drawing a direct line from the Vietnam War through Iraq and now potentially to Venezuela. Let’s dissect the article’s key points, the historical context it provides, and the chilling implications of repeating these grave mistakes.

The Historical Echoes of War

Ochenski reminds us of the Vietnam War, a conflict synonymous with the misuse of governmental power, where the truth was the first casualty. The Gulf of Tonkin incident, a fabricated pretext for full-blown war, serves as a stark reminder of how easily a nation can be led astray by its leaders. The article correctly highlights the “distorted facts and deceived American public” narrative, which, sadly, is not confined to the annals of history but continues to find relevance in contemporary times.

Iraq: A Modern Misadventure

The Iraq War, initiated under the guise of eliminating Weapons of Mass Destruction (WMDs), is rightly cited as a modern parallel to the Vietnam debacle. Ochenski’s comparison is both scathing and accurate, underscoring the “flat out lie” that cost countless lives and inflicted long-term damage on U.S. military personnel, with “virtually no positive outcome.” This segment of the article serves as a painful reminder of the consequences when nations fail to learn from their past.

Venezuela: The Next Chapter in a Tragic Saga

The potential conflict with Venezuela, as outlined by Ochenski, is a frightening instance of history threatening to repeat itself. The invocation of a drug threat by the Trump administration, without substantial evidence, and the positioning of military assets around Venezuela, is eerily reminiscent of the pretexts used to justify previous wars. The article shines a critical light on the unquestioning acceptance of these maneuvers by certain members of Montana’s Congressional Delegation, despite their backgrounds as veterans who should, ostensibly, understand the gravity of war.

The Silence of the Veterans

Ochenski’s call to action for Rep. Ryan Zinke, Sen. Tim Sheehy, and other veterans in Congress, is a poignant critique of their “unconscionable silence.” It’s a damning indictment of their failure to uphold their Oath of Office and their apparent disregard for the will and welfare of their constituents. The mention of a “double tap” operation by SEAL Team 6, resulting in the killing of survivors from a suspected drug boat, is a stark example of the disregard for international law and human life that seems to pervade these military actions.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Reflection

George Ochenski’s article is not just a critique of potential military action in Venezuela; it’s a broader condemnation of the cycle of deceit and unnecessary warfare that has characterized much of U.S. foreign policy. It’s a call for reflection, accountability, and a renewed commitment to genuine security and peace. The silence of veterans in Congress, as highlighted by Ochenski, is particularly troubling, suggesting a disconnect between the lessons of history and the actions of the present.

In sum, Systemic Error stands with Ochenski in demanding transparency, truth, and a break from the destructive patterns of the past. It is incumbent upon all of us, especially those in positions of power, to remember the costs of war and to ensure that history does not repeat itself on our watch.
