Trump admin won't call Klansman who murdered civil rights leader a 'racist'

Mississippi Today reports that President Donald Trump’s National Park Service (NPS) is replacing visitor brochures from the Medgar & Myrlie Evers Home National Monument, in Jackson, Mississippi.

Among the anticipated changes? No longer calling his murderer a “racist.”

“Edits to the brochure have removed that reference to Byron De La Beckwith, according to NPS officials, who asked not to be named for fear of retribution. Other edits include eliminating the reference to Medgar Evers lying in a pool of blood after being shot,” reports Mississippi Today writer Jerry Mitchell.

In 1963, Beckwith shot civil rights leader Medgar Evers in the back as he stood in the driveway of the Evers family home in northwest Jackson. His children were inside the home awaiting their father at the time of his death, and they saw him bleeding out in the front yard.

The original brochures pulled from the home described Beckwith as “a member of the racist and segregationist White Citizens’ Council.” That council, according to history author Stephanie Rolph, “believed in the natural superiority of the Aryan race.”

“They even went so far as to say that civilizations failed because of racial amalgamation,” Rolph added.

Mississippi Today reports Beckwith also belonged to the nation’s most violent white supremacist group, the White Knights of the Ku Klux Klan, responsible for at least 10 killings in Mississippi.

“You can’t call Beckwith a racist?” said Jeff Steinberg, founder of Sojourn to the Past, which regularly provides civil rights tours to the home. “If you opened a picture dictionary and turned to the definition for ‘racist,’ you’d probably find a picture of Byron De La Beckwith.”

Mississippi Today reports NPS’ decision comes in the wake of Trump’s March 2025 executive order, “Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History,” which accused the Biden administration of a “widespread effort to rewrite our Nation’s history, replacing objective facts with a distorted narrative driven by ideology rather than truth.”

Furthermore, the order demanded the Secretary of the Interior “ensure” that all public monuments and properties within its jurisdiction “do not contain descriptions, depictions, or other content that inappropriately disparage Americans past or living.”

This apparently includes race-based assassins.

Mississippi Today adds that Trump initially hailed Evers, a World War II veteran, as a “great American hero” at the opening of the Mississippi Civil Rights Museum in 2017. But after his 2025 executive order, his U.S. Army removed Evers, and the names of other Black heroes, from a section on the Arlington National Cemetery website that honored non-white Americans who fought in the nation’s wars.

Our Analysis:

The Whitewashing of American History: A Critical Examination of the NPS Brochure Edits

In a move that could only be described as both astonishingly brazen and deeply troubling, the National Park Service (NPS), under the directive of President Donald Trump’s administration, has decided to sanitize American history to the point of rewriting the narratives surrounding the civil rights movement. Specifically, the edits to the visitor brochures of the Medgar & Myrlie Evers Home National Monument in Jackson, Mississippi, mark a disturbing step towards erasing the painful realities of racial violence in America.

Erasure of Racial Violence

The decision to no longer refer to Byron De La Beckwith, the murderer of civil rights leader Medgar Evers, as a “racist” is not just a glaring omission but a deliberate attempt to obscure the truth. De La Beckwith was not only a member of the White Citizens’ Council, a group that championed the “natural superiority of the Aryan race,” but also of the White Knights of the Ku Klux Klan, responsible for at least 10 killings in Mississippi. Removing such descriptions from the brochures effectively whitewashes the motivations behind Evers’ assassination and insults the intelligence of the public by suggesting that they cannot handle historical truths.

The Irony of “Restoring Truth and Sanity”

Trump’s March 2025 executive order, “Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History,” is a masterpiece of Orwellian doublespeak. Accusing the previous administration of distorting history while actively engaging in the most blatant form of historical revisionism is both ironic and deeply cynical. The order’s demand that descriptions, depictions, or content that “inappropriately disparage Americans past or living” be removed is a thinly veiled attempt to sanitize the ugliest parts of American history. In this context, “inappropriately disparage” seemingly translates to “accurately describe.”

The Disparagement of Black Heroes

The removal of Medgar Evers and other Black heroes from the Arlington National Cemetery website further demonstrates a systematic effort to diminish the contributions and sacrifices of Black Americans in the nation’s history. Evers, a World War II veteran and a martyr of the civil rights movement, is reduced from a “great American hero” in Trump’s 2017 speech to a non-entity in the narrative pushed by his executive order.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The edits to the Medgar & Myrlie Evers Home National Monument brochures are not an isolated incident but part of a larger, more concerning trend towards rewriting American history. This attempt to erase the racial violence and struggles that have shaped the nation is not just an affront to the memory of Medgar Evers but to all those who have fought—and continue to fight—for justice and equality in America.

It is incumbent upon us, as citizens and stewards of history, to demand accountability and transparency from our government and its agencies. We must resist attempts to sanitize or erase the uncomfortable parts of our past, for in those stories lie the lessons we must learn to ensure a more just and equitable future. The truth, no matter how painful, must not be sacrificed at the altar of political expediency.
