'Nutso stuff': New conspiracy theory implicating Trump flagged as a 'big breach' in MAGA

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 9, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Conspiracy Carousel Spins Again: Analyzing the Latest from Trump’s Orbit

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to members of the media as he arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 5, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

One of President Donald Trump’s biographers revealed on Tuesday that a new conspiracy theory surrounding the death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk has sundered Trump’s base.

Michael Wolff, a journalist who has written four books on Trump, claimed during a recent episode of the podcast “Inside Trump’s Head” that there are prominent voices within the MAGA movement who are questioning the official narrative about Kirk’s death. Kirk was shot in the neck while giving a speech at a Utah university in September.

The voices believe that the Trump administration had something to do with Kirk’s death, according to Wolff. Some MAGA-aligned personalities, like Candace Owens and Tucker Carlson have suggested that Israel and Kirk’s wife, Erika, may have played a role in his death as well.

“We have a set of these kinds of breaches in the MAGA wall, which is why this is interesting and why we’re taking it seriously, because there are lots of conspiracy theories out there that we don’t pay attention to,” Wolff said.

“This is a big breach,” he added.

Wolff argued that the Trump administration is particularly vulnerable to conspiracy theories because there are some many conspiracists in high-ranking positions, like Deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino and FBI Director Kash Patel.

“This is real nutso stuff, but never discount nutso on the right,” Wolff said.

Wolff himself has also been the source of some controversy. High-profile people like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity have denied quotes published by Wolff in his books.

Our Analysis:

The Conspiracy Carousel Spins Again: Analyzing the Latest from Trump’s Orbit

In the latest episode of what could easily be mistaken for a script from a political thriller, we delve into the murky waters where conspiracy theories and political narratives collide, courtesy of Michael Wolff, a figure no stranger to controversy himself. Wolff, who has penned four books on Donald Trump, recently stirred the pot on the podcast “Inside Trump’s Head,” revealing a new conspiracy theory that has supposedly split Trump’s base wide open regarding the death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk.

Charlie Kirk’s Tragic Demise: A Fodder for Conspiracists

Charlie Kirk, a conservative activist, met a tragic end, shot in the neck while delivering a speech at a Utah university in September. This incident, distressing on its own, has been transformed into a breeding ground for conspiracy theories, with some of the loudest voices in the MAGA movement questioning the official narrative. According to Wolff, allegations are flying from figures like Candace Owens and Tucker Carlson, suggesting that Israel and Kirk’s wife, Erika, might have had roles in his demise.

The MAGA Movement: A Hotbed for Conspiracy Theories

Wolff’s commentary sheds light on a deeper issue within the MAGA movement: its susceptibility to conspiracy theories. He points out that the Trump administration, with individuals such as Deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino and FBI Director Kash Patel in high-ranking positions, is particularly vulnerable to such theories. This statement is not just a casual observation but a scathing critique of the quality of individuals occupying positions of power within the administration. Wolff doesn’t mince words, describing the situation as “real nutso stuff,” a sentiment that is hard to argue against when looking at the facts laid bare.

Wolff: The Controversial Chronicler

It’s impossible to dissect this latest revelation without considering the source. Michael Wolff himself is a figure mired in controversy. High-profile individuals like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity have vehemently denied quotes attributed to them in Wolff’s books, casting a shadow of doubt over his claims. While Wolff’s track record might not be spotless, it does not automatically discredit the phenomena he describes. The MAGA movement’s flirtation with conspiracy theories is well-documented, making his assertions, at the very least, worth considering.

Analysis: Beyond the Smoke and Mirrors

The core issue here isn’t just about a single conspiracy theory but the environment that allows such theories to flourish. The MAGA movement, with its propensity for embracing narratives that challenge the official line, often without substantive evidence, represents a broader trend of political polarization and the erosion of public trust in institutions. When prominent figures within this movement entertain theories implicating foreign nations or even the victim’s spouse in acts of violence, it underscores a dangerous willingness to prioritize sensationalism over facts.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection on Political Discourse

This latest saga is a microcosm of the larger issues plaguing American political discourse. It’s a reflection of how far some are willing to go to affirm their biases, often at the expense of truth and decency. While Wolff’s commentary provides a glimpse into the conspiratorial underbelly of the MAGA movement, it also serves as a reminder of the critical need for a return to a more reasoned, fact-based public dialogue.

In conclusion, while the veracity of Wolff’s claims regarding the conspiracy theory surrounding Charlie Kirk’s death may be debated, the phenomenon he highlights is undeniably real. It’s a stark reminder of the divisive, often surreal political landscape we navigate today, where facts are frequently overshadowed by fiction.
