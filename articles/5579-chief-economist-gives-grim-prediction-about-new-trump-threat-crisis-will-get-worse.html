Donald Trump’s latest economic threat will backfire, according to an economist who said that, if the president follows through, a major crisis will be heightened.

Peter Schiff, a financial commentator and radio personality who has been raising alarms about America’s affordability crisis, weighed in over the weekend. Previously, Trump himself went nuclear when Schiff did an appearance on Fox News.

“Why would Fox and Friends Weekend (of all things?) put on a ‘Stockbroker’ named Peter Schiff, a Trump hating loser who has already proven to be wrong. Either the show made a mistake, or it is heading in a different direction,” he wrote in December.

But as it relates to foreign tariffs, Schiff says Trump is about to make a big mistake.

“Trump threatened to hit Americans with 25% tariffs on imports from countries that directly or indirectly do business with Iran,” Schiff said, noting the follow-up effects of that move.

“Since China does business with Iran and nearly every country does business with China, if Trump follows through the affordability crisis will get worse,” the chief economist and global strategist for Europac then added Sunday.

A user on X responded to Schiff by Asking Elon Musk’s AI chatbot if the take was a correct one. Grok replied, “Trump did post on Truth Social in January 2026 that countries ‘doing business’ with Iran would face 25% tariffs on their US trade. He signed an executive order on Feb 6 formalizing this... China trades with Iran, so it could be targeted, which might increase US import costs if implemented.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Threats: A Misguided Missile Set to Worsen the Affordability Crisis

In the latest episode of economic threats that feel more like self-sabotage than strategy, Donald Trump has launched a new salvo that could very well backfire, exacerbating an already dire affordability crisis. Financial commentator and known critic, Peter Schiff, has once again stepped into the spotlight, offering a grim forecast that should serve as a wake-up call to an administration seemingly hell-bent on economic brinkmanship.

Previously, Trump’s disdain for Schiff was made glaringly apparent in a December tirade following Schiff’s appearance on Fox News, branding him a “Trump hating loser” who’s “already proven to be wrong.” Yet, in the face of Trump’s latest economic theatrics, even Schiff’s critics might want to lend an ear.

Tariffs: A Dangerous Game

Trump’s threat, as outlined by Schiff, involves slapping a hefty 25% tariff on imports from any country that dares to engage in commerce with Iran—either directly or indirectly. This is not just a targeted strike; it’s a carpet bombing approach to economic policy that shows a reckless disregard for collateral damage. Given the interconnectedness of global trade, with China as a major hub that does business with Iran, the fallout from such tariffs would be widespread.

The Ripple Effects

The logic—or lack thereof—behind Trump’s tariff threat is baffling. China, a central player in this economic drama, is intricately woven into the fabric of global trade. To punish any country that interacts with China over its dealings with Iran is akin to penalizing virtually the entire world, including, ironically, American consumers and businesses. Schiff’s warning that this move would exacerbate the affordability crisis is not just speculation; it’s grounded in a clear understanding of economic interdependence.

A user’s engagement with Elon Musk’s AI chatbot on X (formerly Twitter) to verify Schiff’s take adds a layer of technological validation to the argument. The chatbot’s confirmation that Trump indeed formalized this threat through an executive order only serves to underline the imminent reality of these misguided tariffs.

The Bigger Picture

What’s at stake here is not just the cost of goods skyrocketing in the US due to increased import costs—a direct consequence of these tariffs—but the very stability of the global economic system. Such unilateral moves serve only to alienate trading partners, incite retaliatory measures, and undermine the principles of free trade.

In his relentless pursuit of a hardline stance against Iran, Trump seems willing to sacrifice economic stability on the altar of political posturing. This approach, characterized by a lack of foresight and a palpable disdain for global economic realities, could very well lead to a self-inflicted wound that the U.S. economy will struggle to recover from.

In Conclusion

As Schiff aptly points out, the affordability crisis is a ticking time bomb that requires careful, considered policy decisions—not the reckless imposition of tariffs that resemble more of an economic temper tantrum than a strategic maneuver. The potential backfire of Trump’s latest threat could not only deepen the affordability crisis but also isolate the U.S. on the global stage.

In this high-stakes game of economic poker, Trump’s bluff could see the U.S. folding under pressure, with American consumers ultimately paying the price. One can only hope that reason prevails before the damage becomes irreparable. But given the track record, optimism seems like a luxury we can ill afford.
