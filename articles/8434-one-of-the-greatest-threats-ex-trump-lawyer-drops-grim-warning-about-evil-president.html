A former Donald Trump White House lawyer on Sunday raised a red flag about “one of the greatest threats” that the “evil” president poses to democracy itself.

Ex-Trump lawyer Ty Cobb appeared on MS NOW over the weekend, where he was asked about what some have called Trump’s war on the judicial branch. The host asked, “What does this mean for the judiciary moving forward, that the Justice Department is taking this hostile action and rhetoric?”

Cobb replied, “I think that’s one of the greatest threats to our democracy at this stage of the game.”

“Keep in mind that Trump has neutered congress. Actually, they’ve probably neutered themselves through their cowardice and greed, but they have, you know, ceded basically all control to the president. And he dictates everything Mike Johnson does,” he added. “So I think that’s tragic because the way the constitution is designed, congress not the courts, were deemed to be the, you know, the first wave of of resistance to an evil president. But the article one powers include impeachment, spending, war, power, all those things. Basically, they have, you know, handed it to Trump in a basket with a bow on it, which is, which is dangerous.”

Our Analysis:

The Alleged Erosion of Democracy: A Scathing Analysis

In an era where political discourse often veers into the hyperbolic, a recent interview with Ty Cobb, a former lawyer for Donald Trump’s White House, cuts through the noise with a chilling assertion. Cobb, speaking on MS NOW, flagged “one of the greatest threats” to American democracy: the actions and influence of Donald Trump himself. Cobb’s commentary, while blunt, merits a deep dive for its implications on the judiciary, the legislative branch, and the balance of power as envisioned by the U.S. Constitution.

Trump’s Alleged War on the Judiciary

Cobb’s assertion that Trump poses a significant threat to democracy pivots on the accusation of a “war on the judicial branch.” This is a grave accusation, suggesting not only a disdain for the judiciary’s independence but an active effort to undermine it. The judiciary, designed as a check on executive power, is fundamental to the balance of power. If a president were indeed waging war against this branch, it would indeed be as Cobb describes: “one of the greatest threats to our democracy.”

The Self-Neutering Congress

Perhaps more scathing than the critique of Trump’s actions is Cobb’s indictment of Congress. His words, “Congress has neutered themselves through their cowardice and greed,” are a damning assessment of the legislative branch’s failure to live up to its constitutional role. The Framers envisioned Congress as the primary check against executive overreach, armed with the power of impeachment, control over spending, and war powers. Yet, according to Cobb, Congress has abdicated these responsibilities, handing them over to the executive branch “in a basket with a bow on it.”

This indictment is not just about Trump; it’s a broader critique of a systemic failure within American politics, where legislative inertia or complicity allows executive overreach to go unchecked.

The Dangerous Precedent

The implications of Cobb’s comments are profound. If Congress, through “cowardice and greed,” fails to exercise its constitutional duties, and the judiciary is under assault by the executive, the very foundations of American democracy are at risk. This isn’t merely about partisan politics; it’s about the structural integrity of the nation’s governance.

Cobb’s use of the term “evil president” is intentionally provocative, meant to shock the system into awareness. The choice of words is designed to highlight the severity of the threat posed by a president who seeks to undermine the checks and balances that define American democracy. This isn’t just about one man or one presidency; it’s a cautionary tale about what could happen when the safeguards built into the system are ignored or dismantled.

Conclusion: A Call to Action or a Cry in the Wilderness?

Cobb’s commentary, while deeply concerning, should not be seen as mere criticism of a former employer or a political figure. It’s a clarion call for vigilance and action to protect the balance of power that underpins the American democratic experiment. Whether his warning will be heeded remains an open question. Will it spur a reassertion of constitutional duties by Congress and a defense of judicial independence, or will it be dismissed as hyperbole in an already polarized political climate?

The answer to that question will shape the future of American democracy. What’s clear, however, is that Cobb’s alarm bell cannot be un-rung. The responsibility now lies with both the guardians of the system and the electorate to ensure that the balance of power is not just a feature of history textbooks but a living principle guiding the nation’s path forward.
