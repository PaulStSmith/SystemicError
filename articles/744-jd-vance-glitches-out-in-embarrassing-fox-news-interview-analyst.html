JD Vance 'glitches out' in 'embarrassing' Fox News interview: analyst

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 16, 20262 min read

Trump’s Heir Apparent Problem Is Not Charm. It Is Control.

A Brief Context

David Pakman mocked JD Vance after a Fox News interview with Jesse Watters, arguing that Vance’s awkward laugh and weak delivery showed why Donald Trump may not trust him as a successor. That is the surface event. The real story is the power struggle underneath it.

Trump Still Owns the Torch

The institutional power here does not belong to Vance. It belongs to Trump, who still defines the Republican hierarchy and decides who gets treated as a potential heir rather than a disposable mascot. Vance is not being evaluated like an independent political actor. He is being tested as a vessel for Trumpism after Trump.

That is why the question matters. A vice president is supposed to look like a governing alternative. Vance instead looks like a man auditioning for permission to inherit a brand he did not build.

Fox News Is Not a Neutral Stage

Watters did not accidentally create this moment. Fox News functioned exactly as it always does: as a controlled environment where Republican figures are asked to perform confidence on cue. Vance was not challenged on substance. He was handed a soft setup and still managed to produce a performance that invited ridicule.

That matters because it reveals how fragile the right’s media ecosystem has become. It is built to manufacture strength, but it cannot manufacture stature.

The Misframe Is the Point

Pakman’s criticism lands because the weakness is real, but the deeper issue is not whether Vance is funny or awkward. The issue is what that awkwardness conceals: a political class that treats charisma as a substitute for competence and loyalty as a substitute for legitimacy.

The article’s framing is narrow, almost celebrity-like, because that is how succession politics is often sold. But the true question is not whether Vance can land a line on Fox. It is whether a party built around one man has any serious mechanism for choosing power that is not based on imitation and obedience.

What Vance Represents

Vance is useful precisely because he is pliable. That is also his weakness. He is the kind of figure a decaying movement elevates when it wants continuity without risk. The result is a politician who can be placed near power but cannot convincingly project it.

This is how authoritarian political cultures often look in their late stage: not solemn, not disciplined, but brittle. The lie is that succession is orderly. The truth is that the heir is only as strong as the patron who names him.

The Larger Pattern

The real pattern is not one bad interview. It is a Republican system that still revolves around Trump’s personal authority while pretending to be a normal party with a bench. That system elevates functionaries, not leaders; performers, not governing minds.

Vance’s laugh was not the scandal. It was the leakage. It exposed a movement that still needs Trump’s shadow to confer seriousness, and a media machine willing to keep pretending that this is the same thing as strength.
