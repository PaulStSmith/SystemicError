'Would be devastating for him': Trump biographer speculates about president losing power

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Eternal Campaign: Trump’s 2028 Ambitions

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks during a Navy 250 Celebration in Norfolk, Virginia, U.S. October 5, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Journalist and author Michael Wolff believes there is a “very real possibility” that President Donald Trump will run again in 2028 because allowing the Democrats to take power would be “devastating” for him.

Wolff discussed the prospect of Trump running for office again during the new episode of “Inside Trump’s Head,” a podcast Wolff co-hosts with Joanna Coles, the chief content officer at The Daily Beast. Wolff predicted that Trump “will have to” run for office again because of the wide-ranging authoritarian actions his administration has taken.

“These are the moves of an authoritarian, clearly,” Wolff said. “And the problem with this is that an authoritarian breaks so many rules and makes so many enemies that in order to avoid retribution, he has to stay in power.”

Trump has previously denied that he will run for office a third time, which is unconstitutional under the 22nd Amendment. The last president to serve more than two terms was Franklin Roosevelt.

However, Trump and his surrogates have been distributing “Trump 2028” merchandise, which has led some to believe that the president is seriously considering running again.

“That is the nature of the game that is being played,” he continued. “So, if he has four years of dominating government, all branches of government...it becomes a very real possibility that he will undo the impediments to a third term as he pursues this course.

“He will have to do that because the consequences of the Democrats taking power would be devastating for him,” Wolff added.

Wolff himself has also been the source of some controversy. High-profile people like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity have denied quotes published by Wolff in his books.

Our Analysis:

The Eternal Campaign: Trump’s 2028 Ambitions

In the latest episode of political theatre that could only be conceived in the current era, journalist and author Michael Wolff has stoked the fires of speculative journalism by suggesting a “very real possibility” that former President Donald Trump might eye a 2028 run for office. This assertion, delivered on his podcast “Inside Trump’s Head,” co-hosted with Joanna Coles of The Daily Beast, paints a picture of a political landscape where constitutional amendments and democratic norms are mere hurdles for the ambitious and the authoritarian.

Trump’s Authoritarian Playbook: Wolff’s analysis hinges on the characterization of Trump’s administration as one marked by “wide-ranging authoritarian actions.” This isn’t a novel observation; the Trump era was indeed punctuated with moments that challenged the traditional boundaries of executive power and flirted with authoritarian tendencies. Wolff argues that such a playbook necessitates continuous power to avoid the consequences of one’s actions—a classic autocrat’s dilemma.

But here’s where Wolff’s narrative veers into the realm of speculative fiction: the notion that Trump, having already served a term and lost his bid for a second, could somehow maneuver around the 22nd Amendment. This amendment explicitly limits presidents to two terms, a provision that has stood unchallenged since the time of Franklin Roosevelt. Wolff’s suggestion that Trump could undo these “impediments to a third term” requires a suspension of disbelief and a cynical view of American democracy’s resilience.

Merchandise as Manifesto: The distribution of “Trump 2028” merchandise is presented as evidence of Trump’s intentions. Yet, in the grand tradition of political branding, merchandise is hardly a declaration of policy or intent. It’s provocative, sure, but to equate it with a serious campaign strategy is to give it undue weight. It’s the political equivalent of a clickbait headline—designed to stir controversy and keep the Trump brand in the public discourse.

Wolff’s Credibility Questioned: It’s crucial to note that Wolff himself is a controversial figure. His reporting methods and the accuracy of his quotes have been publicly challenged by high-profile figures like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity. This doesn’t invalidate his entire thesis, but it does suggest that his prognostications should be taken with a grain of skepticism.

The Bigger Picture: At its core, Wolff’s argument touches on a deeper anxiety within American politics about the erosion of democratic norms and the potential for authoritarian creep. It’s a valid concern, amplified by the real actions and rhetoric of Trump’s tenure. However, the leap to a Trump 2028 campaign undercuts the seriousness of these issues with sensationalism.

In sum, Wolff’s speculation about a Trump 2028 run serves more to entertain and provoke than to inform. It reflects a political landscape where the lines between reality and hyperbole are increasingly blurred, where the possibility of the absurd becomes fodder for news cycles. As observers and participants in this democracy, our focus should be on safeguarding the institutions and norms that prevent any would-be authoritarian from bending the rules to their will—not on entertaining far-fetched scenarios that distract from the real work at hand.
