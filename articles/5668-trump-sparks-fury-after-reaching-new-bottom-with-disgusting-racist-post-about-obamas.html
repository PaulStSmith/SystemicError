Trump sparks fury after reaching 'new bottom' with 'disgusting' racist post about Obamas

President Donald Trump sparked fury Friday morning after posting a blatantly racist video on social media depicting Barack and Michelle Obama as non-human primates, a post critics denounced as a shocking new low for the presidency.

“Disgusting behavior by the President,” wrote California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s press office Friday in a social media post on X. “Every single Republican must denounce this. Now.”

Posted late Thursday night to his social media platform Truth Social, the social media post in question is a roughly one-minute video supporting the president’s false claims that the 2020 election was “rigged.” At the end of the video, a short, two-second clip is seen in which the Obamas’ faces are superimposed onto the bodies of primates in the jungle, set to the 1961 song “The Lion Sleeps Tonight” by The Tokens.

The video also bears a watermark suggesting it was created by X user “Xerias,” a pro-Trump account with more than 45,000 followers who on X early Friday claimed credit for having made the video last October.

“Trump just posted an incredibly racist photo of the Obamas’ faces photoshopped onto the body of apes,” wrote Dean Withers, a political commentator on X with nearly 500,000 followers. “Every day is a new rock bottom for this ugly pig.”

Some critics, like journalist Wajahat Ali, took Trump’s latest post as indisputable evidence of both Trump’s and the Republican Party’s racism, arguing that in posting the video, and the GOP’s silence, both were “showing their true colors.” Others, like liberal political commentator Brian Krassenstein, were simply left baffled.

“Our RACIST President just posted a video which depicts Barack and Michelle Obama as Apes,” Krassenstein wrote early Friday morning in a social media post on X.

“But I’m sure MAGA will tell us it’s the black people who are the real racist ones. When is enough enough? When can you all admit that you voted for a terrible human being?

Our Analysis:

The Unabashed Racism of Donald Trump: A New Nadir

In an act that can only be described as brazenly racist, Donald Trump has once again demonstrated his utter disregard for the norms of decency and respect that are supposed to hallmark the office of the Presidency. The incident in question, involving a video shared by Trump that depicts Barack and Michelle Obama as non-human primates, isn’t just a “shocking new low” as critics have called it; it’s an alarming indicator of how racial animus continues to be fanned at the highest levels of American politics.

The Despicable Act

Let’s dissect the event with the clarity it demands. Late Thursday night, Trump decided it was appropriate to share a video on his platform, Truth Social, that not only propagated his unfounded claims about a “rigged” 2020 election but also included a dehumanizing and racist portrayal of the Obamas. The inclusion of a clip showing their faces superimposed onto the bodies of primates isn’t just offensive—it’s a calculated move to debase and ridicule based on race.

The Response

The outcry was swift and pointed, as it should be. California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s press office called out the behavior for what it is: “disgusting.” Yet, the statement that “Every single Republican must denounce this. Now.” underscores a recurring theme in this sordid tale—the deafening silence or tepid condemnations from Trump’s party.

Political commentator Dean Withers didn’t mince words, labeling Trump an “ugly pig” for his actions. But it’s Wajahat Ali’s commentary that strikes the heart of the matter, pointing out this incident as “indisputable evidence” of the racism that seems to pervade Trump’s actions and, by extension, the silence of the GOP, as revealing their “true colors.”

The Frustrated Pleas for Accountability

Brian Krassenstein’s exasperated comment encapsulates the frustration felt by many: “When is enough enough?” This question, rhetorical yet begging for introspection, highlights the broader issue at hand. It’s not just about Trump’s abhorrent behavior; it’s about the complicity and silence that enable it. The MAGA crowd’s predictable deflection, as Krassenstein sarcastically notes, misses the point entirely—it’s not about who’s “the real racist” in a tit-for-tat exchange. This is about accountability at the highest levels.

The Bottom Line

This incident isn’t merely another entry in Trump’s long ledger of controversial acts. It’s a glaring example of how racial prejudice is not only tolerated but seemingly endorsed through actions and inactions by figures of authority and their supporters. The argument that Trump is merely being “politically incorrect” or “trolling the left” falls apart under the weight of the racism so clearly displayed here.

The real question moving forward isn’t just about how the Republican Party will respond (or fail to). It’s about what this says regarding the state of political discourse in America and how such blatant acts of racism are still part of a strategy for some. For those who stand against racism, the path forward is clear: condemnation without reservation and a commitment to upholding the dignity of all, irrespective of race. Anything less is complicity in the face of undeniable racism.
