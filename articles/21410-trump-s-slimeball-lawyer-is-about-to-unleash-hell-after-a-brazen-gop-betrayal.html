Trump's slimeball lawyer is about to unleash hell after a brazen GOP betrayal

Based on an article by John Casey

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

Todd Blanche Is Not a Technocrat. He Is a Loyalty Test.

Power, Not Personnel

This story is not really about Todd Blanche’s résumé, or even about whether he is “qualified” for a permanent Attorney General job. It is about Donald Trump using the Justice Department as a personal holding pen and the Senate as a timing obstacle. Trump controls the leverage. Blanche controls the machinery. That is the real hierarchy.

The article’s basic facts are simple: Trump may pull Blanche’s nomination, keep him in an acting role, and wait for a more favorable Senate. That is not governance. It is a delay tactic built around executive power, partisan math, and the hope that enough institutional inertia will do the rest.

The Lie of “Acting”

The acting title matters because it lets Trump sidestep confirmation while still exercising control through a loyal subordinate. Under the succession law the article cites, Blanche can run DOJ for 210 days without a Senate vote. That is not a neutral administrative detail. It is a runway for abuse.

The point is not that Blanche is waiting in the wings for legitimacy. The point is that Trump can keep him there as long as the calendar is useful. The Senate becomes something to game, not something to respect. If Democrats take the chamber, the article says Blanche’s path closes. That is exactly why Trump wants to run out the clock.

The Senate Is Not the Main Character

The source frames Cornyn and Tillis as the immediate problem because they want written guarantees that the “anti-weaponization” fund is dead. That is useful context, but it also risks over-crediting a pair of Republicans for what is really a broader pattern of Republican complicity breaking down under pressure. They are not suddenly defenders of principle; they are policing a settlement they no longer want to bless.

Trump’s response is revealing. He is not backing away from the scheme because it is corrupt. He is looking for a different Senate arithmetic. The article notes that a Democratic majority is plausible after the midterms. That is the real threat to Trump’s plan, because democratic oversight, even in its limited and compromised form, can still interrupt a patronage operation.

Blanche as Disposable Help

Blanche is being sold as a legal operator, but the article makes clear he is really functioning as a compliant instrument. That is why his future is so bleak. Once a lawyer turns the Justice Department into a personal service arm, he stops being a professional asset and becomes a liability waiting for a calendar date.

The source cites a federal judge calling the IRS settlement he orchestrated a sham designed to manipulate the courts. It also notes an ethics complaint from more than 100 former judges and a referral to the New York State Bar. Those are not random smears. They are signs that Blanche’s entire value proposition is loyalty stripped of institutional legitimacy. He is not building a career; he is liquidating one.

Misdirection as Procedure

The article is strongest when it treats Blanche’s conduct not as confusion, but as deliberate alignment with Trump’s interests. The Epstein files episode, the retaliatory investigations into Comey and the Southern Poverty Law Center, and the subpoenaing of New York Times journalists are all presented as parts of the same pattern: the Justice Department used for punishment, intimidation, and containment.

That is the political meaning that matters. The problem is not merely that Blanche is abrasive or overzealous. The problem is that he is helping convert law enforcement into a shield for power and a weapon against criticism. The article is right to reject the soft language that would describe this as “controversial” or “aggressive.” It is retaliatory state power.

What This Actually Reveals

The larger pattern is plain: Trump does not just reward loyalty. He destroys the institutions that make loyalty expensive. He turns lawyers into expendable enforcers, the Senate into a timing challenge, and the Justice Department into a personal law firm with badges attached. The people who take those jobs are not misunderstood patriots. They are choosing to be used.

Blanche may be finished professionally, but that is not the point. The point is that the damage is done while he still has authority. That is how authoritarian systems work in practice: they do not need competent servants forever, only obedient ones long enough to inflict the harm.
