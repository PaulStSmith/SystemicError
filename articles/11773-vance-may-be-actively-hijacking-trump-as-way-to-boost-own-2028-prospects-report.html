The saga has become an ongoing headache for the Trump administration, as his own base — which peddled conspiracy theories about Epstein's death and his supposed "client list" of wealthy co-conspirators for years — has become frustrated that the administration can't deliver on what it promised.

"Vance chose to interrupt his vacation yesterday for an interview with Maria Bartiromo on the Fox Business channel," they wrote Monday. "Bartiromo predictably asked Vance about Epstein. Vance chose not simply to deflect and say blandly that the administration was handling the situation responsibly. Nor did he choose to defend Donald Trump and assert confidently that Trump had done nothing wrong."

Rather, they wrote, Vance said “President Trump has demanded full transparency” — even though little if any information has actually been released so far — before claiming there could be a number of Democrats in the files too, potentially generating still further interest from the base, and saying, “a lot of Americans want answers. I certainly want answers.”

"Perhaps Vance was simply being clumsy," they wrote. "But if one had a suspicious mind, one might think Vance knows what he’s doing. Perhaps he doesn’t mind seeing Trump embroiled in the Epstein scandal. Vance presumably had no connection to Epstein, so he’s at no risk if the files are released. And to the degree Trump gets damaged by the Epstein matter, it would make it harder for Trump to run again in 2028 — something Trump obviously wants to do, something Vance is intelligent enough to see that Trump wants to do, and something Vance presumably doesn’t want Trump to do."

Trump and his supporters have frequently entertained the idea of him seeking a third term, even though he would be in his 80s by that time, and even though the Constitution expressly forbids a president seeking more than two terms. Some of Trump's backers have suggested there might be a "loophole" in the 22nd Amendment that involve him running for vice president and having the next president resign immediately, but experts have made clear this is not allowed either.

Our Analysis:

JD Vance and the Epstein Saga: A Closer Look

In the labyrinth of MAGA politics, Vice President JD Vance's recent comments on the Jeffrey Epstein case files have ignited speculation and intrigue, suggesting a possible power struggle within the Trump camp. The analysis offered by Bill Kristol, Andrew Egger, and Jim Swift for The Bulwark serves as a foundation, but let's dive deeper and dissect the motives, implications, and the absurd theater of political maneuvering at play.

Vance's Calculated Move

JD Vance's decision to address the Epstein controversy on Fox Business channel was, contrary to some interpretations, anything but clumsy. His statement that "President Trump has demanded full transparency" while simultaneously highlighting the potential involvement of Democrats in the Epstein scandal is a masterclass in political double-speak. This isn't just about transparency; it's a strategic diversion. Vance is keenly aware of the tightrope he's walking within the MAGA ecosystem. By asserting a desire for answers and hinting at Democratic involvement, Vance is playing to the base's penchant for conspiracy and deflection, all while maintaining a safe distance from the fallout.

The Trump Dilemma

The article rightly points out the delicate dance Vance performs around Trump's embroiled legacy with Epstein. The suggestion that Vance may benefit from Trump being mired in the Epstein scandal is not far-fetched. It's a calculated bet. If Trump is damaged goods come 2028, it clears a path for Vance and others harboring presidential aspirations. However, this strategy is not without risk. The MAGA base's loyalty to Trump is fervent, if not fanatic. Vance's gambit implies a belief that this base will eventually pivot towards a more viable candidate if Trump is sufficiently tarnished. It’s a bold assumption and speaks volumes about Vance's ambitions and his estimation of Trump's grip on the party.

Misreading the Constitution

The article touches upon the fantastical musings of Trump and his supporters regarding a third term, citing the Constitution's 22nd Amendment. The notion of exploiting a "loophole" for Trump's return via the Vice Presidency is as ludicrous as it is constitutionally baseless. Yet, it reveals a deeper, more alarming truth about a segment of Trump's base: their willingness to distort or outright ignore constitutional safeguards for political gain. This is not merely a matter of legal interpretation; it's a symptom of the cult of personality that Trump has cultivated, where fealty to the individual supersedes allegiance to constitutional principles.

Conclusion

JD Vance's maneuvering within the MAGA hierarchy over the Epstein files is a revealing episode in the ongoing saga of Republican politics. It underscores the lengths to which individuals within the party will go to secure power, even if it means leveraging scandal and conspiracy. Vance's actions are a calculated bet on the future, one that anticipates the eventual decline of Trump's political viability. However, the broader implications for the Republican Party and American democracy are concerning. When political strategy is rooted in scandal manipulation and constitutional acrobatics, it's a stark reminder of the systemic errors eroding the foundations of our political discourse.
