Judge rages at Trump lawyers about 'Schrödinger's golf course'

The Cat Was Never the Point

Power, Not Physics

A federal judge had to mock the Trump administration’s lawyers for claiming there was no decision yet on East Potomac Park, even as Trump himself had already said the decision was made. That contradiction is the story. The administration is trying to keep the legal consequences of a decision while denying that the decision exists.

The Decision Was Trump’s

The actual institutional power sits with Trump and the administration he controls. Not with park users, not with D.C. residents, and not with environmental groups trying to defend a century-old public space. If the White House has already committed to the golf course, then the lawyers’ posture is not uncertainty. It is a litigation tactic built to blur responsibility long enough to move the project forward.

Public Land, Private Appetite

East Potomac Park is not empty land waiting for a luxury redesign. It is a 328-acre public park with trails, sports facilities, birdwatching, a cherry grove, and decades of use by Washington residents. The reported plan would strip out public value and replace it with a Trump golf course, which is exactly the kind of transaction that reveals the governing instinct here: treat shared civic land as a dealer’s asset.

Environmental Review as a Speed Bump

The lawsuit centers on NEPA, which exists because federal agencies are supposed to assess environmental damage before imposing major projects on public land. That requirement is not decorative. It is one of the few tools available to force the state to account for consequences to the Potomac ecosystem, migratory birds, and public recreation before the bulldozers arrive. When the administration acts as if the decision is undecided, it is not respecting process. It is trying to hollow process out.

The Misdirection

The framing invites a soft reading: lawyers confused, project controversial, debate ongoing. That is too generous. The dispute is not confusion; it is deliberate evasion wrapped in procedural language. The administration wants the benefits of having chosen, while pretending not to have chosen, so it can delay accountability and keep control of the outcome. The real harm is not administrative inconsistency. It is the effort to convert public land into a private luxury project while insulating the people responsible from the legal and political cost.

The Pattern

This is the recurring shape of authoritarian, business-first governance: capture public institutions, mask private gain as public planning, and force opponents to prove what power already knows. The judge’s joke about Schrödinger’s golf course lands because the administration is trying to make truth optional. When power depends on contradiction, the lie is not a bug. It is the operating system.
