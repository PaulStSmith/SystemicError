'Maggots and mold': Lawsuit alleges even Trump was revolted by state of his golf club

A manager who was fired from President Donald Trump’s Bedminster, New Jersey, golf club has filed an explosive lawsuit detailing horrific conditions, reported The Daily Beast on Friday, and noting that even Trump himself was disgusted by the state of the facility.

“The president was dragged into the state of the $350,000-a-year New Jersey club in a lawsuit brought by a fired manager, who claims she was forced out of her job for flagging the dire health violations at the New Jersey club’s clubhouse bistro restaurant,” reported Laura Esposito. According to the report, the lawsuit claims the club “had maggots and mold in [Trump’s] favorite self-serve ice cream, while it employed drunk kitchen staff and lecherous managers who drunkenly phoned female wait staff to demand they come to their homes late at night.”

Trump went out of his way to complain about the state of the kitchen, per the suit, particularly about the infestation of flies.

The suit also alleges that when the manager, Justine Sacks, arrived for her first day of work, her boss, general manager David Schutzenhofer, told her that fellow assistant manager Raul Sicoe had “an issue with women,” that Sicoe completely ignored her on shift to the point of not even looking at her, that she was instructed to only hire women with a particular “look,” and that when she called HR to complain about managers propositioning employees, Schutzenhofer berated her and called her a “miserable person.”

In the suit’s account, she tried to raise alarms about a number of health violations, including pests and mold, drunken employees, employees smoking in the kitchen, food being served past its expiration date, and an incident in which the club’s bistro had no running water for a whole day. However, after a health inspector flagged 18 violations and gave the facility the worst grade in the county, she was the one fired.

“Ms. Sacks was targeted because she refused to stay silent about sexual harassment, gender discrimination, and serious health and safety violations that put employees and patrons at risk,” wrote Sacks’ attorney.

Trump’s properties have faced similar allegations in the past. In 2017, his Mar-a-Lago resort was cited for 13 health code violations in the kitchen — including failure to treat raw fish for parasites and failing to properly refrigerate ham — just as the Japanese prime minister was set to visit.

Our Analysis:

The Bedminster Debacle: A Deep Dive into Trump’s Latest Scandal

In what reads like a subplot straight out of a dystopian novel, President Donald Trump’s Bedminster golf club has been thrust into the limelight, not for its luxury or exclusivity, but for reasons that sound alarm bells on multiple fronts. The latest lawsuit filed against this $350,000-a-year establishment paints a picture so grim, it makes one wonder about the standards of elite havens and the apparent impunity with which they operate.

Maggots, Mold, and Mismanagement: A Trifecta of Troubles

The lawsuit, as detailed by Laura Esposito for The Daily Beast, isn’t just another complaint; it’s an exposé of horrific conditions that even managed to disgust Trump himself. The allegations are as varied as they are vile: from maggots and mold in the self-serve ice cream station to drunk kitchen staff and lecherous managers making unsolicited, late-night calls to female waitstaff. These claims aren’t just disturbing; they’re a glaring indictment of the club’s disregard for basic human decency and health standards.

Trump’s own dissatisfaction with the kitchen’s state, particularly the fly infestation, is telling. It suggests that the issues were so egregious that they broke through the bubble of wealth and privilege to touch even the club’s owner. Yet, the lawsuit implies these complaints did little to spur meaningful action until legal action was taken.

A Pattern of Neglect and Discrimination

The lawsuit’s protagonist, Justine Sacks, paints a damning picture of her tenure at the club. From day one, she was introduced to an environment where gender discrimination seemed to be the norm, with instructions to hire women based on their looks and a pointed ignorance from male colleagues. Her efforts to combat sexual harassment and flag health violations were met not with support but with hostility and eventual termination.

This isn’t the first time a Trump property has been under fire for health and safety violations. In 2017, Mar-a-Lago was cited for 13 health code violations, just as the Japanese prime minister was set to visit. The pattern is unmistakable and points to a systemic problem within Trump’s establishments, where the allure of luxury masks a disturbing disregard for basic standards and regulations.

A Crisis of Accountability

What stands out in this entire debacle is not just the gross violations themselves but the response to them. Sacks’ firing, after she was targeted for refusing to stay silent, is a textbook example of shooting the messenger instead of addressing the message. It raises critical questions about accountability and the mechanisms in place (or lack thereof) for employees to report and rectify dangerous and unethical practices.

Conclusion: Beyond the Ice Cream

This lawsuit is a wake-up call, not just for Trump’s Bedminster golf club but for similar institutions worldwide. It’s a reminder that luxury does not equate to legality or ethicality. As readers and citizens, it’s crucial to look beyond the surface sheen of elite establishments and demand transparency, accountability, and, most importantly, a basic adherence to health and safety standards. The Bedminster incident should not be dismissed as an isolated case but seen for what it truly is: a systemic failure that requires immediate and unwavering action.
