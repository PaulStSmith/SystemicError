Live by the Gun, Die by the Gun: The Charlie Kirk Aftermath

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Kirk’s War on EmpathyThe Martyrdom MythThe Second Amendment Comes HomeThe Children and the Rest of UsChristianity as a WeaponThe Real Danger AheadRefusing the Script

Charlie Kirk is dead. Shot down while spewing the same bile he’d built his career on, he became the victim of the very forces he glorified. And now, as the political establishment wrings its hands and conservative media begins the hagiography machine, it’s worth asking: what exactly are we mourning here? The man? Or the rot he embodied?

Let’s be clear. This is not celebration. Dancing on graves makes us no better than the authoritarian movement Kirk nurtured. But neither is this mourning. To pretend his death is some grand tragedy that should unite us in prayer is to sanitize the reality of what he chose to be: a merchant of hate, a foot soldier of authoritarianism, and a cheerleader for the very gun culture that took his life.

Kirk’s War on Empathy

Charlie Kirk despised empathy. He said so openly. In his worldview, compassion was weakness, kindness was cowardice, and solidarity was an obstacle to the ruthless individualism he sold from every stage and studio. He sneered at the idea of helping the vulnerable, mocked those who wept for victims, and trained a generation of reactionaries to see human decency as a defect.

And now, as his allies plead for compassion, they insult our intelligence. Kirk’s legacy is not one of dialogue or “viewpoint diversity.” It is one of weaponized cruelty. He chose to make his fortune demonizing immigrants, dehumanizing queer people, and vilifying anyone who didn’t fit neatly inside his brittle Christian-nationalist box. He made empathy a punching bag. He doesn’t deserve it in death.

The Martyrdom Myth

Predictably, the canonization machine has begun. Right-wing media is hard at work transforming Kirk from a smug propagandist into a martyr. They’ll say he was just trying to have “a discussion.” They’ll say he believed in “the free marketplace of ideas.” But strip away the PR gloss, and the truth remains: he chose hate, over and over again. Every hateful word he uttered was a decision. No contract forced him. No editor compelled him. He volunteered to be the face of a movement that thrives on other people’s misery.

And let’s not pretend this is only a conservative trick. The reflex to canonize the dead infects all politics. But we don’t have to play along. Honesty matters more than performative grief. The truth is, Charlie Kirk was not an innocent victim of forces beyond his control. He was a co-architect of the very conditions that made his own assassination possible.

The Second Amendment Comes Home

Few things define Kirk’s public persona more than his worship of the Second Amendment. Guns, to him, weren’t a social crisis but the highest expression of American liberty. He infamously said gun deaths were “worth it” to preserve that sacred right. It was a grotesque calculation: accept the mass slaughter of children, the daily carnage of suicides and domestic shootings, because firearms supposedly guarantee freedom.

Well, the ledger has balanced. He lived by that calculation and died by it. He insisted that a few deaths were acceptable collateral for a fully armed nation. He simply never imagined one of those deaths would be his own. Like all the smug ideologues of his kind, he believed the bullets would always find someone else. The poor. The brown. The queer. The nameless.

But the world he helped build finally caught up with him. And no amount of crocodile tears from his defenders will change that.

The Children and the Rest of Us

Yes, Kirk had children. No child deserves to lose a parent to violence. The horror of watching a father gunned down is unimaginable. And that grief is real, even if the man himself was a parasite on public discourse. But here’s the pushback: Kirk’s children were already condemned to a life shaped by his influence. To grow up with Charlie Kirk as a father meant absorbing his cruelty, his paranoia, his hatred of empathy. Losing him in violence is tragedy; growing up under his tutelage is its own tragedy. The scales here don’t tip easily.

More importantly, while the right asks us to cry for Kirk, other lives go unmentioned. The kids shot in Colorado the same day. The victims of Gaza, whom Kirk dismissed as subhuman. The tens of thousands who die every year in the U.S. from gun violence, casualties of a system Kirk defended with every breath. These are the lives worth mourning. Not his.

Christianity as a Weapon

Kirk wrapped himself in Christianity, but it was never the Christianity of humility, grace, or love. It was the Christianity of bludgeon and brand. He wielded scripture as a cudgel, declaring moral laws that applied only to others, never himself. He lived as though the Bible was a weapon against enemies, not a guide for his own conduct. He ignored the simple verses—you reap what you sow, live by the sword, die by the sword—because in his mind, he was exempt. God was on his side. His cruelty was righteousness.

And yet here we are. The scripture he disregarded has proven prophetic. He reaped the whirlwind of the culture he sowed. He lived by the gun. He died by the gun.

The Real Danger Ahead

Charlie Kirk’s death is not just an endpoint. It is fuel. The far-right will seize this moment to double down, to vilify the left, to expand the surveillance state, to justify fresh rounds of persecution. Trump himself will undoubtedly wield it like a cudgel, demanding loyalty oaths, unleashing repression, and turning Kirk into a martyr-saint of the authoritarian crusade. The dead man will be more useful to them than he ever was alive.

And that’s the tragedy. Not the silencing of Charlie Kirk—he’s had more than enough airtime—but the silencing of everyone who will be crushed under the state’s new excuse for crackdowns. The immigrants he demonized. The trans kids he targeted. The protesters who will be smeared as accessories to his death. That’s where the grief belongs. That’s where the danger lies.

Refusing the Script

We don’t have to follow the script. We don’t have to light candles for a man who spent his career blowing out everyone else’s flame. We don’t have to pretend his death is a unifying moment. It isn’t. It’s a consequence. A grim punctuation mark on a life dedicated to making others suffer.

So no, we won’t celebrate. But we also won’t pretend. Charlie Kirk lived by hatred. He profited from cruelty. He preached that a few deaths were worth it to preserve America’s gun obsession. In the end, he became the collateral damage he always dismissed.

Call it fate. Call it irony. Call it justice. But don’t call it tragedy. The tragedy is everything else—the millions still living under the world Charlie Kirk helped create.
