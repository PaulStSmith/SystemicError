'Stupid and disgusting' Candace Owens buried by CNN's Enten in stunning rant

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unvarnished Truth Behind the Candace Owens Controversy

Jemele Hill, Harry Enten (CNN screenshot)

To the obvious delight of journalist Jemele Hill, CNN data guru Harry Enten dominated a panel discussion on conservative conspiracy-spewer Candace Owens who is being sued by Emanuel and Brigitte Macron for accusing the wife of the French president of being a man.

On Saturday's edition of CNN's "Table for Five," host Abby Phillip brought up the lawsuit in which the French president and his wife accused Owens of attempting "to promote her independent platform, gain notoriety, and make money."

With hardly a pause, Enten launched into a fierce rant that had the entire table sitting back and watching while he went off.

With Phillips calling Owens' bizarre accusations and harassment of the couple "amazing," Enten shot back 'That has got to be one of the dumbest arguments I have ever heard, first off."

He then exclaimed, "Second off, I mean, not only is she stupid, but she's also disgusting, right? I mean, Candace Owens has got to be one of the most disgusting folks who ever gets airplay on any major network."

"And this is actually not even the worst of Candace Owens," Phillips added.

"No! She's an anti-Semite, yeah, she's an anti-Semite. She's anti-gay, she's an anti-Semite. She has said stuff about the history of slavery in this country that's disgusting," he exclaimed as Hill shook her head in agreement.

"Yes, it's funny insofar, you know, that, you know, her statements make no sense," he added. "But when we really get to the core of the issue, she's just a disgusting human being and that's the bottom line."

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth Behind the Candace Owens Controversy

In a recent episode of CNN's "Table for Five," the discourse descended into a fiery condemnation of conservative commentator Candace Owens, a moment that not only shed light on the polarized state of our political landscape but also underscored the dangerous power of misinformation. Owens finds herself at the center of a legal storm with French President Emanuel Macron and his wife, Brigitte, over baseless accusations that serve as a stark reminder of the pernicious effects of conspiracy theories in our digital age.

The Essence of the Accusations

Candace Owens, a figure notorious for her controversial statements, has been accused of asserting that Brigitte Macron is a man—a claim as ludicrous as it is offensive. Such statements, beyond their immediate shock value, are indicative of a broader trend where public figures weaponize misinformation for personal gain. The lawsuit from the Macrons articulates this concern, suggesting Owens's motivations are to "promote her independent platform, gain notoriety, and make money."

Harry Enten's Righteous Indignation

Harry Enten, CNN's data guru, did not mince words in his critique of Owens, describing her arguments as "one of the dumbest" he has ever heard. Enten's impassioned response, characterized by both its vehemence and its clarity, underscores the frustration felt by many who witness the relentless propagation of baseless claims. His labeling of Owens as "disgusting" for her history of inflammatory remarks—including anti-Semitism, homophobia, and revisionist takes on slavery—reflects a broader exasperation with the normalization of such rhetoric in public discourse.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is emblematic of a larger, more disturbing reality: the capacity for unsubstantiated allegations to not only gain traction but to also cause real harm. Owens's comments about the Macrons are not isolated incidents but part of a pattern of behavior that exploits societal divisions for personal and financial gain. The incident raises critical questions about the role of media figures in perpetuating falsehoods and the responsibility of platforms to curtail the spread of misinformation.

A Scathing Commentary on Our Times

The conversation on "Table for Five" serves as a scathing indictment of the current state of political discourse, where facts are often overshadowed by sensationalism and personal vendettas. It's a stark reminder of the importance of critical thinking and the need for vigilance against the spread of unfounded claims. The legal action by the Macrons is not just about addressing personal grievances; it's a stand against the degradation of public conversation and the erosion of truth.

In conclusion, the controversy surrounding Candace Owens and her comments about the French President and his wife transcends mere celebrity gossip or legal drama. It's a symptomatic flare-up of the deeper malaise affecting our political and social systems—a malaise characterized by the relentless pursuit of controversy at the expense of truth. As observers, participants, and victims of this system, it behooves us all to demand better, to seek truth over sensationalism, and to hold those who peddle lies accountable for the damage they inflict on the fabric of our society.
