'Murderers’ row of has-beens': MAGA's alternate Super Bowl halftime show brutally mocked

The outrage over the NFL selecting reggaeton superstar Bad Bunny as the next Super Bowl halftime musical artist led Turning Point USA higher-ups to jump into the fray and propose an alternative “All American Halftime Show.”

And it’s not going well, reports Salon columnist Amada Marcotte, who suggested that fans of the late Charlie Kirk’s TPUSA appear to be out of touch with not only current music, but also are in a state of “complete cluelessness” about what people will watch.

As she wrote on Wednesday, TPUSA decided to let supporters provide input on the acts and style of music to be presented, and it has not gone well, and there is not the groundswell of enthusiasm they likely expected.

According to Marcotte, there is a divide between those who want a Christian-based halftime show and those who lean hard into older rock units in the twilight of their careers.”

“When TPUSA posted about their halftime show, there was a flurry of hyperbolic responses from MAGA followers, hoping that hate alone would somehow produce an entertaining alternative to one of the most popular artists in the world,” she wrote and added, “The results haven’t been announced yet, so we can’t know if ‘Classic Rock’ will beat out ‘worship’ music. But on X, there were plenty of eager Trump fans offering suggestions about the kind of artists they think will top Bad Bunny, who has been streamed nearly 100 billion times on Spotify.”

Pointing out a proposed lineup of “a murderers’ row of has-beens like Papa Roach, Nickelback, Staind and, yep, Creed,” the columnist quipped, “... basically, the same array of CDs you’d find in the floorboards of the least dateable guy you knew in the 1990s.”

With that in mind, she added, “Some people wanted music that’s even more unpopular. Multiple users demanded worship music, which is basically a blandly written verse and chorus about Jesus performed in an adult contemporary style that no one listens to outside of white evangelical circles,” before pointing out, “Many people, without having a clue who will perform, declared they would watch this instead of the real halftime show. Some of them seemed confused, thinking TPUSA had somehow canceled and replaced Bad Bunny with [fill in acceptably white person] at the Super Bowl.”

“All of this reflects what is a small ray of hope in our bleak political moment. MAGA’s relationship with pop culture only has two forms: Complete cluelessness and/or resentment that most people think their taste stinks,” she suggested, and noted that the right-wing donors behind the rise of TPUSA would be throwing their money away.

Writing that, “MAGA couldn’t be better designed to repel the creative urge. The whole movement is based on a notion that difference is scary, change is bad and everything that’s happened since you were 11 years old is a travesty,” she added, “Fearfulness and intellectual laziness are kryptonite to the imagination. How can you think of something new when your ideological position is that everything new is bad?”

Our Analysis:

The Tone-Deaf Symphony: TPUSA’s Misguided Musical Venture

In the latest saga of cultural disconnect, the decision by the NFL to appoint reggaeton superstar Bad Bunny as the next Super Bowl halftime musical artist has stirred the pot, leading to an uproarious backlash from the folks at Turning Point USA (TPUSA). Their proposed solution? An “All American Halftime Show” that seems to be floundering in a sea of irrelevance and nostalgia for a bygone era. Let’s dissect this cacophony of poor decisions and misplaced cultural warfare.

The Proposal That Fell Flat

The initiative by TPUSA to create an alternative halftime show is an emblematic display of their disconnect from contemporary music tastes and broader cultural shifts. Their approach, as reported by Salon columnist Amada Marcotte, reeks of a profound misunderstanding of the current entertainment landscape. The rallying of support for a Christian-based halftime show or the preference for rock bands whose prime was decades ago speaks volumes of their misalignment with today’s zeitgeist.

The MAGA Cultural Bubble

The reaction from MAGA followers to TPUSA’s call-to-arms, expecting that a concoction of hate and nostalgia would somehow trump the global appeal of Bad Bunny, is a testament to their bubble. The suggestion of acts such as Papa Roach, Nickelback, Staind, and Creed is laughably out of touch, serving as a stark reminder of the echo chamber’s inability to grasp the nuances of contemporary culture and entertainment.

The Misguided Pursuit of Popularity

The divide within the TPUSA ranks, between those clamoring for a return to classic rock and those pushing for worship music, underscores a broader struggle within the conservative movement. Their attempts to sway public opinion and garner enthusiasm for their “All American Halftime Show” are met with a lukewarm response at best, highlighting a fundamental misunderstanding of what appeals to the broader public today.

The Cultural Resentment

Marcotte’s observation that MAGA’s relationship with pop culture oscillates between cluelessness and resentment hits the nail on the head. This resentment isn’t just a benign preference for the past; it’s a deliberate rejection of the diversity and vibrancy that define today’s cultural landscape. Their aversion to anything that deviates from their narrow worldview is a sad commentary on their fear of change and difference.

The Creative Drought

The assertion that MAGA’s ideology is antithetical to creativity and innovation is poignant. A movement fueled by fear, nostalgia, and a resistance to change is inherently incapable of producing or appreciating anything that challenges its rigid boundaries. Their ideological stance is a death knell for creativity, as it confines them to a cultural echo chamber that is increasingly out of step with the rest of society.

In Conclusion

The debacle surrounding TPUSA’s attempt to counterprogram the Super Bowl halftime show with an “All American Halftime Show” is a microcosm of the larger cultural and political disconnects that plague the MAGA movement. Their failure to understand the pulse of contemporary society, coupled with their aversion to diversity and change, leaves them shouting into the void. The irony is palpable: in their quest to champion an “All American” ethos, they’ve managed to alienate themselves from the evolving fabric of America itself.
