'End of his political career!' Trump pounces on Dem contender in middle-of-night rant

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a House Republican members conference meeting in Trump National Doral resort, in Miami, Florida, U.S. January 27, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

President Donald Trump fired off at Maryland Governor Wes Moore (D) early morning Thursday following reports that the governor had mistakenly listed himself as a Bronze Star recipient in an application nearly two decades ago.

Moore and Trump have feuded publicly over the past week after Trump, following the federal takeover of Washington, D.C., threatened to send the National Guard and federal officers to Maryland’s capital city, Baltimore. Moore, citing Baltimore’s shrinking crime rate, invited Trump to “walk the streets of Maryland” with him to see the improvements in person, though Trump declined the offer.

Now, Trump is seizing on old reporting from The Hill that details what Moore admitted to being an “honest mistake.”

“But is that the end of his political career,” wrote Trump early morning Thursday on his social media platform Truth Social. “He was very disrespectful to the Office of the President!”

According to The New York Times, Moore had applied to a White House Fellowship in 2006 following his almost 20 years in the Army Reserve, and listed himself as being a Bronze Star recipient, despite never having been awarded the decoration.

In a statement on Thursday, Moore said he listed the Bronze Star at the urging of his deputy brigade commander, who advised him to include it on the application and assured him that senior officers had already approved the award, a story that was corroborated with to the New York Times by Lt. Gen. Michael Fenzel, described as a mentor to Moore.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: Trump vs. Moore

Once again, Donald Trump has proven himself to be the master of distraction and deflection. Early Thursday morning, Trump launched an attack on Maryland Governor Wes Moore (D), dredging up a nearly two-decade-old incident where Moore mistakenly listed himself as a Bronze Star recipient on an application. This skirmish between Trump and Moore has escalated following Trump's threats to send the National Guard and federal officers to Baltimore, a move opposed by Moore who pointed to the city's decreasing crime rate as evidence of its improvement.

The Bronze Star Blunder

The core of Trump's attack is an article from The Hill, which reports on what Moore has acknowledged as an "honest mistake." This mistake occurred in 2006 when Moore, following his service of almost 20 years in the Army Reserve, applied for a White House Fellowship and listed the Bronze Star among his accolades—a decoration he had never received. Moore's defense, as reported by The New York Times, is that he listed the Bronze Star based on the advice of his deputy brigade commander, who had assured him that the award was approved by senior officers. This claim is supported by Lt. Gen. Michael Fenzel, described as a mentor to Moore.

Distraction as a Strategy

The timing and focus of Trump's attack are peculiar but strategic. By highlighting this incident, Trump aims to discredit Moore's political standing and integrity, especially considering Moore's recent defiance against Trump's approach to Baltimore's security. This move is classic Trump, attacking an individual's character and past mistakes to divert attention from the current political discourse—in this case, the proposed federal intervention in Baltimore.

The Irony of Trump's Moral High Ground

The irony of Trump attacking Moore for a mistake made nearly 20 years ago should not be lost on us. Trump's presidency and subsequent actions have been marred by countless controversies, misleading statements, and divisive rhetoric. His seizing on Moore's admitted mistake as potentially career-ending is hypocritical, considering his own track record of unapologetic falsehoods and misrepresentations.

The Real Issue at Hand

While Trump's critique of Moore might entertain or enrage, depending on where one falls on the political spectrum, it distracts from the more pressing issue—the safety and improvement of Baltimore. Moore's invitation for Trump to witness the city's progress firsthand was met with rejection, not constructive dialogue or support. This move from confrontation to collaboration could have been an opportunity to address the complexities of urban crime and renewal, but Trump chose a different path.

Conclusion: The Art of Seeing Through Distraction

In this circus of political maneuvering, it's crucial to see through the smoke and mirrors. Trump's attack on Moore is a distraction from substantive issues—namely, the debate over the best approach to supporting and improving American cities like Baltimore. It's essential to question why Trump would rather focus on a decades-old mistake than engage in meaningful discussions on policy and progress. As observers and participants in the political process, we must focus on the issues that matter, not get lost in the art of distraction.
