'Red-alarm fire': State AGs 'running war games' to protect the midterms from Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 24, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Election Anxiety: Democrats Brace for Trump’s Legal Maneuvers

U.S. President Donald Trump in Rome, Georgia, U.S., February 19, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Following a series of double-digit election victories in late 2025 and early 2026, Democrats are feeling increasingly optimistic about their prospects in this year’s midterms. But Democratic elections attorney Marc Elias is worried about legal maneuvers that President Donald Trump and his allies are likely to make as November draws closer, and he warns that far-right “War Room” host Steve Bannon should be taken seriously when he calls for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents to “surround the polls” on Election Day.

In an article published on February 23, Politico’s Lisa Kashinsky reports that Democratic state attorneys general are ramping up their legal game plan for the midterms and are preparing for the worst.

“Democratic attorneys general are bracing for President Donald Trump to interfere in the midterm elections — and war-gaming how to stop him,” Kashinsky explains. “The party’s top prosecutors have been strategizing for months about how to counter a series of increasingly extreme scenarios they fear could play out this fall. They have huddled in hotel conference rooms and over Zoom meetings to run tabletop exercises anticipating the president’s moves and choreographing responses.”

Kashinsky adds, “They’re preparing for the administration to potentially confiscate ballots and voting machines, strip resources from the postal service to disrupt the delivery of mail ballots, and send military members and immigration agents to polling locations to intimidate voters. They’re readying motions for temporary restraining orders to preserve election materials and remove armed forces from voting sites.”

California Attorney General Rob Bonta warns fellow Democrats against underestimating Trump.

Bonta told Politico, “(Trump) wants to continue to have his party prevail, seemingly by whatever means necessary. So we have to be ready for that, sad and tragic as it is.”

Washington State Attorney General Nick Brown, similarly, described MAGA Republicans’ remarks on the midterms as a “red-alarm fire that people need to take very seriously.”

Brown told Politico, “He will try anything….. (So) we have to just sort of think creatively about: If you were the president and you were trying to invalidate an election or undermine an election, what are the oddball, ludicrous, unconstitutional theories that you might advance?”

Our Analysis:

Election Anxiety: Democrats Brace for Trump’s Legal Maneuvers

In the shadow of a series of significant electoral victories, the Democratic Party might have been expected to bask in the warmth of optimistic forecasts for the upcoming midterms. However, a chilling breeze seems to be blowing their way, courtesy of President Donald Trump and his cadre of far-right allies. According to a Politico article by Lisa Kashinsky, there’s a growing concern among Democrats, spearheaded by Democratic elections attorney Marc Elias, about potential legal and extrajudicial shenanigans as the midterms approach. The fear is palpable, and the alleged strategies range from the disturbingly authoritarian to the outright bizarre.

The Plot Thickens: Bannon’s Call to Arms

Steve Bannon, the “War Room” host with a penchant for the theatrical, has reportedly called for ICE agents to “surround the polls” on Election Day. Such a statement, whether bluster or genuine intent, should not be brushed aside as mere political theater. It echoes a darker, more insidious agenda aimed at voter intimidation and the erosion of democratic norms. The Democrats are right to take this threat seriously, as it speaks volumes about the lengths to which some factions are willing to go to cling to power.

Democratic AGs: The First Line of Defense

The response from Democratic state attorneys general is telling of the gravity of the situation. They’re not merely preparing for a legal battle; they’re bracing for what could be an unprecedented assault on the electoral process. The preparations, as detailed by Kashinsky, sound more like the plot of a dystopian novel than standard electoral safeguarding measures. Confiscating ballots and voting machines? Stripping resources from the postal service? Sending military and immigration agents to polling locations? These are scenarios one might expect in authoritarian regimes, not the United States.

Yet, here we are, with California Attorney General Rob Bonta and Washington State Attorney General Nick Brown sounding the alarm. Bonta’s comment that Trump wants his party to “prevail, seemingly by whatever means necessary,” underscores a disturbing willingness to flout democratic principles for political gain. Meanwhile, Brown’s call to think creatively about countering “oddball, ludicrous, unconstitutional theories” suggests a scenario where the rule of law is under direct attack.

The Underlying Issue: A Test of Democracy

What this Politico article lays bare is not just a political skirmish but a fundamental test of American democracy. The tactics being readied against the electoral process are not new to the annals of history, but their application in the context of a modern American election is both novel and deeply concerning.

The Democratic attorneys general are doing more than preparing for a legal battle; they’re gearing up to defend the very foundation of democratic governance against what they perceive as a clear and present danger. This isn’t merely about one election cycle or one political party’s dominance. It’s about ensuring that the pillars of democracy — free and fair elections, the peaceful transfer of power, and the rule of law — remain intact.

In a political climate already saturated with division and distrust, these developments add another layer of volatility to an already explosive mix. The Democrats’ war-gaming of extreme scenarios may seem like an overreaction to some, but given the stakes, can we afford to dismiss these concerns outright?

Conclusion

As the midterms draw near, the shadow of potential legal and extrajudicial challenges looms large. The actions and strategies being contemplated are not just attacks on the opposing party but on the democratic process itself. It’s a stark reminder that the battle for the soul of America is ongoing, and the price of liberty remains eternal vigilance.
