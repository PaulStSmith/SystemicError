Trump says Trump Jr. would rather hunt 'in some jungle' than attend his memorial

President Donald Trump joked Sunday that his own son, Donald Trump Jr., would prefer to be “in some jungle” hunting than attend his memorial “when I kick the bucket someday,” while also issuing his son a warning that “wildlife always wins.”

Trump was speaking Sunday at the White House in Washington, D.C. and spoke at length about a memoir by James Jones chronicling his experience in the Amazon Rainforest in Peru, where he was bitten by a venomous snake.

While on the topic of dangerous wildlife, Trump shared what he called his own personal theory: “wildlife always wins,” which he repeated specifically for Trump Jr. to hear.

“I’m saying this for my son by the way because I have a son who would rather be in the jungle than any place on earth,” Trump said.

“When I kick the bucket someday, I think he’ll be here for about two days, he’ll go and pay his respects and we’ll say ‘where’s Don’ – he’d rather be in some jungle, and he’s a really good hunter. But remember this: wildlife always wins, unfortunately as in this case.”

Trump Jr. is an avid big-game hunter, though has faced scrutiny over several of his hunting ventures, including his killing of an endangered sheep in 2019, a hunt that Humane World for Animals reported had cost American taxpayers $77,000. Trump Jr. has also faced accusations of illegally hunting a protected species of duck near Venice, Italy.

For well over a decade, Trump has made disparaging remarks about wildlife, particularly sharks, which in 2013 he declared to be “last on my list,” other than “perhaps the losers and haters of the world!” Trump made several disparaging remarks about sharks over the years, including on Sunday, where he suggested sharks that have attacked humans should be retaliated against.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Unsettling Jokes and Disturbing Wildlife Views

In a remarkably tone-deaf display of familial affection and environmental commentary, President Donald Trump recently turned heads—not for the usual reasons of policy or political maneuvering—but for his peculiar reflections on his own mortality, his son’s hobbies, and an unsettling perspective on wildlife. The disconcerting narrative unfolded during a White House event in Washington, D.C., where Trump, in a manner only he can, navigated through topics with the grace of a bull in a china shop.

Family Bonds and Bizarre Warnings

Trump’s joke about his son, Donald Trump Jr., preferring the jungle’s solitude over mourning his father’s eventual demise is as bizarre as it is telling. It’s an odd spectacle, witnessing a sitting president musing publicly about his own ‘kicking the bucket’ and his son’s supposed indifference to this future event. This moment, intended to be light-hearted, instead paints a peculiar picture of familial relations within the Trump dynasty. But the real kicker is Trump’s cryptic warning to his son that “wildlife always wins,” a phrase as enigmatic as it is ominous.

Trump Jr.: A Controversial Hunter

Donald Trump Jr.’s penchant for big-game hunting is no secret. His exploits have not only stirred controversy but have also highlighted the problematic entitlement and recklessness exhibited by those in power. The mention of his killing of an endangered sheep in 2019, with American taxpayers reportedly footing a $77,000 bill, is particularly egregious. This incident, among others, casts a long, dark shadow over the Trump family’s environmental legacy. Trump Jr.’s alleged illegal duck hunt in Italy only adds to this troubling pattern. These actions reflect a blatant disregard for wildlife conservation efforts and legal boundaries, all under the guise of sport.

Trump’s War on Wildlife

President Trump’s disdain for wildlife, especially sharks, is not new. His comments over the years reveal a deep-seated animosity towards certain species, which, frankly, is unwarranted and baseless. His suggestion that sharks attacking humans should be met with retaliation is not only scientifically ignorant but dangerously anthropocentric. Sharks, like all wildlife, play crucial roles in their ecosystems, and their behavior towards humans is largely misunderstood and sensationalized.

Conclusion: A Disturbing Worldview

This entire episode underscores a disturbing worldview held by the Trumps—one that sees wildlife and the environment not as treasures to be preserved but as adversaries to be conquered or commodities to be exploited. The flippant remarks about death, the glorification of hunting endangered species, and the vilification of vital predators like sharks, all serve to highlight a broader disregard for the natural world.

In the grand scheme of things, such attitudes are not just problematic; they’re dangerous. They signal to the public and the world at large that conservation and environmental respect are optional, secondary to human whims and sport. As we face escalating environmental crises, from climate change to biodiversity loss, the last thing we need is leaders who propagate such recklessly antiquated views.

In sum, Trump’s comments offer more than just a window into his and his son’s personal predilections; they reveal a systemic error in how we view and interact with the natural world. And for anyone paying attention, it’s clear that this error needs correcting—and fast.
