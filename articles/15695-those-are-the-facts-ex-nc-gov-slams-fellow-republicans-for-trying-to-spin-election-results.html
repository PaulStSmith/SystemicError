'Those are the facts': Ex-NC gov slams 'fellow Republicans' for trying to 'spin' election results

North Carolina showed how much of a swing state it is when, in 2024, voters elected a Democratic governor, Josh Stein, but favored Republican Donald Trump over Democrat Kamala Harris by roughly 3.5 percent in the presidential race. And the competition for a seat on the North Carolina Supreme Court was a real nail-biter, with Democratic Justice Allison Riggs defeating Republican Judge Jefferson Griffin by only 735 votes.

Griffin, however, never conceded and is hoping to overturn the election results if he can get enough votes thrown out.

Former North Carolina Gov. Pat McCrory, a Republican, is among the people on the right who is standing up for Riggs and urging Griffin to accept the election results.

In a late April video for Common Cause North Carolina, McCrory argued, "We all know how spin helps win elections. We see it in campaign commercials, stump speeches and candidate debates right up until the final votes are counted. But months after Election Day, Republicans in Raleigh are still spinning in the race for North Carolina Supreme Court."

McCrory continued, "It's the only election in the country that still hasn't been decided. Democratic Allison Griffin beat Republican Jefferson Griffin by 735 votes. But Griffin is suing to have thousands of ballots, mostly from military families stationed overseas, tossed out. Those are the facts. Now, here's the spin. Republicans are saying those votes for Supreme Court don't count because overseas military voters didn't show ID. In fact, the state never even asked them for ID."

The former North Carolina governor noted that Griffin is the only North Carolina candidate who "asked for those military overseas votes not to be counted."

"Look, I'm a Republican," McCrory said in the video. "I supported Jefferson for Supreme Court. I wanted him to win. But spinning your way into a Supreme Court seat isn't the way to do it…. Sorry, fellow Republicans, you can't spin your way out of this one."

Our Analysis:

North Carolina's Election Drama: A Microcosm of Political Polarization

North Carolina's recent election cycle has been nothing short of a political thriller, epitomizing the swing state's volatile identity and exposing the deep-rooted polarization gnawing at the fabric of American democracy. In an era where political division is as ubiquitous as the air we breathe, the state's election results from 2024 serve as a stark reminder of the complexities and contradictions that define our current political landscape.

The Dichotomy of Choice

At the heart of this electoral saga is the paradoxical decision by North Carolinians to elect a Democratic governor, Josh Stein, while simultaneously favoring Republican Donald Trump over Democrat Kamala Harris by a margin of approximately 3.5 percent in the presidential race. This dichotomy is not just a testament to the state's unpredictable swing status but also highlights a broader national trend of voters seeking balance through divided governance. However, one could argue that this balance is less about ideological moderation and more about the electorate's disillusionment with both parties' ability to govern effectively and without partisan bias.

The Supreme Court Stalemate

Further complicating the state's political narrative is the nail-biter of a race for a seat on the North Carolina Supreme Court. Democratic Justice Allison Riggs' victory over Republican Judge Jefferson Griffin by a mere 735 votes underscores the razor-thin margins that often decide the fate of judicial leadership in swing states. Yet, the refusal of Griffin to concede, coupled with his attempts to disenfranchise military voters under the guise of absentee ballot irregularities, lays bare the increasingly common tactic of weaponizing electoral integrity concerns for political gain.

Former Governor Pat McCrory's Stand

In a surprising twist, former North Carolina Gov. Pat McCrory, a Republican, emerges as a voice of reason amidst the post-election chaos. By publicly denouncing Griffin's efforts to invalidate overseas military ballots and urging him to accept the election results, McCrory distances himself from the all-too-familiar narrative of election denialism that has plagued his party in recent years. His assertion that "spinning your way into a Supreme Court seat isn't the way to do it" not only rebukes Griffin's actions but also sends a message to his party: integrity and respect for the electoral process must supersede partisan ambitions.

The Military Vote Controversy

The controversy surrounding the military ballots is particularly egregious. Griffin's lawsuit to discard thousands of ballots, predominantly from military families stationed overseas, on the grounds of lacking voter ID — despite the state's failure to request such identification — is a stark illustration of how low some candidates will stoop to secure a win. It's a slap in the face to those who serve abroad, ostensibly defending the very democratic principles that such legal challenges seek to undermine.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Our Democratic Health

As North Carolina grapples with its post-election reality, one thing becomes crystal clear: the health of our democracy is in peril. When election outcomes are no longer accepted at face value, when legal challenges become a tool for disenfranchising voters, and when political victories are prioritized over democratic integrity, we must pause and reflect on the path ahead.

The North Carolina election saga is not just a state issue; it's a national wake-up call. It's a reminder that the fabric of our democracy is only as strong as our collective commitment to upholding its principles. As we move forward, let us hope that the voices of reason, like McCrory's, become louder than those of division and denial. Only then can we begin to mend the tears in our democratic tapestry and ensure that the will of the people — all people — is respected and preserved.
