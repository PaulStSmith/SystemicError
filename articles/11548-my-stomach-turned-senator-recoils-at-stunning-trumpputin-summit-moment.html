'My stomach turned': Senator recoils at stunning Trump–Putin summit moment

By Paulo Santos

A Democratic senator flagged a moment between President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin that made his "stomach turn."

Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), a member of the Armed Services Committee, joined Anderson Cooper on CNN on Friday night following the conclusion of a Trump-Putin summit that many observers felt amounted to a giant "nothing burger."

Blumenthal shared that view.

"Senator, I mean, I've asked this question to a lot of people, but you have seen a lot of these kinds of summits. Did anything come out of this in your mind?" asked Cooper.

"Nothing came out of this summit. It was a nothing burger. It was a shrug of the shoulders," Blumenthal replied.

But Blumenthal said one moment stood out to him.

"But I will tell you, Anderson, my stomach turned when I heard the president of the United States characterize Vladimir Putin as his fabulously good friend," he said. "Vladimir Putin is a war criminal. He has directed soldiers to kill women and children and bury them in mass graves. He's kidnapped children as we speak. The reality on the ground is that people are bleeding and dying all around Ukraine because Putin is continuing to bomb them. And at the front, he's continuing to pummel the brave Ukrainian soldiers who are defending the Donetsk and Luhansk regions, the town of Pokrovsk."

Blumenthal said the reason there's no ceasefire and no deal is that Putin didn't want one.

"He doesn't want peace. The only way to convince him to stop this diplomatic rope-a-dope, playing the president and delaying any kind of ceasefire, is through strength."

He called for arming Ukraine with the interceptors for their Patriot systems — and giving them more of them — as well as more missiles, long-range artillery, and "scorching sanctions."

Blumenthal and Cooper threw Trump's words back at him.

"It seems like, if there were going to be some consequences or 'severe consequences,' he could have said something to that effect to Vladimir Putin face-to-face," said Cooper.

Blumenthal agreed.

"You’re absolutely right. He could have said face-to-face, 'There will be severe consequences.' We’ll never know — at least for now, in the short term — whether there was any kind of threat, veiled or otherwise," he lamented.

He said he hopes the world's reaction to the "optics triumph for Vladimir Putin will enrage Donald Trump, or at least annoy him enough that he will support more sales of the military."

He also called for "crippling" Putin's economy by placing sanctions against China, India, and Brazil to push them to stop buying Russian oil and gas.

"The way to stop fueling that war machine is through bone-crushing sanctions," he said, touting his bill that would do just that, and has the support of 85 senators.

Our Analysis:

The Summit of Absurdities: A Critical Dissection

The recent CNN interview with Senator Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) following a summit between President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin has once again exposed the ludicrous theater of international politics where the stakes are disturbingly high. Blumenthal's comments, analyzed under a discerning lens, reveal a mixture of genuine concern and political posturing that merits a closer examination.

The "Nothing Burger" Summit

Let's start with the characterization of the Trump-Putin summit as a "nothing burger." This term, catchy as it is dismissive, encapsulates the empty calories of diplomatic engagements that promise much and deliver little. But to dismiss the summit entirely is to ignore the undercooked meat hidden beneath the bun: the dangerous implications of Trump's fraternization with a figure like Putin. When Blumenthal's stomach turns at Trump calling Putin a "fabulously good friend," it's a reaction we should all have, considering Putin's rap sheet. War crimes, the targeting of civilians, the bombing of Ukraine—these are the actions of the man Trump is cozying up to. Yet, this bromance is served up to the public with a side of geopolitical negligence.

The Call for "Strength"

Senator Blumenthal's prescription is all too familiar: more military aid to Ukraine, "scorching sanctions," and a call for strength. This rhetoric, while understandable, skirts dangerously close to the edge of warmongering. The push for arming Ukraine with interceptors for their Patriot systems, more missiles, long-range artillery, and imposing crippling sanctions on not just Russia but also China, India, and Brazil, is a clear call to escalate tensions. One might argue, where is the call for diplomacy, for dialogue, for any solution that doesn't involve further militarization of the conflict?

The Optical Illusion of Diplomacy

The reference to the "optics triumph for Vladimir Putin" and the hope that it would "enrage" or "annoy" Trump enough to support more military sales is a cynical take on international relations where human lives are reduced to pawns in a geopolitical chess game. The reliance on optics, on the superficial appearance of diplomacy, rather than substantive engagement, is a systemic failure. It's a world where the appearance of doing something is mistaken for actual progress.

Sanctions: The Bone-Crushing Reality

Blumenthal's call for "bone-crushing sanctions" and his boast of a bill supported by 85 senators reflects a troubling consensus in Washington: that economic warfare is an effective and ethical tool of foreign policy. The reality, however, is that sanctions often hurt the most vulnerable and do little to change the behavior of those in power. The idea of crippling Putin's economy by targeting countries like China, India, and Brazil for buying Russian oil and gas is not only likely to be ineffective given global economic interdependencies but also morally questionable. It's a strategy that risks widespread suffering and exacerbates international tensions.

Conclusion: The Theatre of the Absurd

In the end, Blumenthal's interview with Anderson Cooper sheds light on the absurdity of current U.S. foreign policy: a mixture of hollow diplomacy, dangerous escalations, and a lack of genuine solutions. The real "nothing burger" may well be the belief that strength, sanctions, and military aid alone can address the complex challenges posed by figures like Vladimir Putin. It's high time for a reevaluation of strategies, one that prioritizes peace, dialogue, and the well-being of all citizens over the geopolitical gamesmanship that currently defines international affairs.
