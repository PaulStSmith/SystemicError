‘I didn’t!’ Insider tells of frantic Trump call after latest Epstein accusation

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of the Absurd: Trump, Epstein, and the Conspiratorial Carousel

A banner of Jeffrey Epstein and President Donald Trump hangs in Grand Park during a protest against federal migration enforcement in downtown Los Angeles, California, U.S. August 2, 2025. REUTERS/David Swanson

President Donald Trump called a friend within the last two days to vent about rumors that he ordered Jeffrey Epstein killed in his jail cell, his biographer said Saturday.

Michael Wolff, who wrote the Trump White House exposé “Fire and Fury,” said on the Daily Beast’s podcast that he’d spoken to the person who received the call.

“Yesterday, I had a conversation with someone who talks to Trump frequently. And this person had a conversation with Trump in the last 48—slightly, possibly more—hours, and it kind of gave me a chill,” Wolff said.

“So Trump called up this person and said, ‘They say I killed Epstein. I didn’t have Epstein killed,’ And then this person said, ‘Well, do you think he was killed?’ And then Trump said, ‘A lot of people wanted him dead.’”

Many conspiracy theorists believe that Epstein, who committed suicide in his cell before his trial for multiple sex crimes including trafficking underage girls, was deliberately killed.

It ties into the belief that Epstein had information that would link many powerful people to his sick antics.

Last month, Trump's Attorney General Pam Bondi sparked outrage among his MAGA base when she announced that Epstein did die of suicide and that he left behind no files identifying associates.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump, Epstein, and the Conspiratorial Carousel

The latest episode in the grand political circus comes courtesy of none other than former President Donald Trump, who, in a move that surprises exactly no one, decided to confront head-on the swirling rumors of his supposed role in the demise of Jeffrey Epstein. According to Michael Wolff, the man who penned the infamous "Fire and Fury" exposé on the Trump White House, Trump has been actively seeking a sympathetic ear to dispel the notion that he ordered a hit on Epstein, a convicted sex offender and financier.

Let's dissect this with the scathing clarity it deserves.

First, consider the source. Michael Wolff, a figure who has made a career threading the needle between insider gossip and journalistic investigation, claims to have indirect access to Trump's private venting sessions. Wolff's track record, while certainly attention-grabbing, invites skepticism. However, if true, this anecdote peels back another layer of the post-presidency Trump: a man seemingly obsessed with controlling the narrative, even when it comes to the most macabre of rumors.

Trump's alleged defense, as per Wolff, is as bizarre as it is telling. "They say I killed Epstein. I didn’t have Epstein killed,” Trump supposedly claimed, only to follow up with, “A lot of people wanted him dead.” This statement, intended or not, reads less like a denial and more like an acknowledgment of the dark underbelly of power politics where Epstein played and ultimately lost his game.

The Epstein saga, with its sordid blend of sex crimes, high-profile associates, and mysterious end, is ripe for conspiracy theories. And Trump, with his penchant for engaging directly with such theories (remember the Obama birther conspiracy?), only fuels the fire. It's almost as if he relishes the murky waters where truth and fiction swirl indistinguishably.

Last month, Trump's Attorney General Pam Bondi threw a supposedly definitive bucket of cold water on the conspiracy flames by announcing that Epstein indeed died by suicide, leaving no incriminating files behind. This announcement, expected to quell the rumors, did little but enrage the MAGA base, a group ever-ready to cry foul at any narrative not fitting their worldview.

The irony of Trump—himself a master of manipulation and misinformation—trying to distance himself from the Epstein narrative is rich. This is a man who has demonstrated time and again his willingness to bend reality to his whim. Yet, when faced with a conspiracy that implicates him in a heinous act, he seems to want the facts to matter again.

In the end, what are we to make of this? The saga of Jeffrey Epstein is a mirror to the ugly, power-hungry facets of society that many wish to ignore. Trump's alleged late-night phone calls of denial are but a sideshow to the larger, more disturbing truths about power, exploitation, and the lengths to which people will go to protect their secrets.

Let's not get lost in the sensationalism of Trump's possible defenses and forget the real issue at hand: the victims of Epstein's crimes, the systemic failures that allowed his predations to continue unchecked for so long, and the broader implications of such unchecked power dynamics.

In this circus, the clowns may change, but the show, it seems, goes on eternally.
