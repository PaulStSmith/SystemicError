'Reach out and smack him': Lindsey Graham faces voter rebellion for Trump pandering

Senator Lindsey Graham (R-SC) is facing a primary challenger that could tempt many of his former supporters, according to an article from the Washington Post.

“I feel like he just flows with the wind. I really do. Which is not a good thing,” Jane Rabon, a Republican who came to hear Graham speak in the Sun City retirement community, told the Post. “Sometimes I just want to reach out and smack him.”

Graham, along with his friend the late John McCain, was once viewed as a steadfast voice on foreign policy.

Now, the 69-year-old is navigating between the old-school foreign policy views that formed him — and the very different political demands of President Donald Trump.

Another voter at the town hall, Bruni Baker, said Graham is “kind of a RINO,” which means Republican in Name Only. “I have written him letters when I’m really p----ed at him,” she added.

Mark Salter, McCain’s former speechwriter, said Graham was spouting nonsense he did not believe as “Trump fluffery.”

“People do what they have to do with Trump, but Lindsey’s reputation was built on his foreign policy views,” Salter said.

“The idea that foreign leaders have respect for Trump’s strength is laughable,” Salter told the Post. “He’s destroying the world order that people like John McCain and, once upon a time, Lindsey Graham … built and resulted in the United States being one of the most powerful and wealthy countries on Earth.”

Trump endorsed Graham, saying, the senator, “has always been there when I needed him.”

Graham said, “This is his last term, and I won’t be around forever, and I’m trying to help him.”

How much longer he will be able to is up to the voters in the Palmetto State. Businessman Mark Lynch is a primary challenger for Graham and is hoping to capitalize on the unrest among the base.

“Trump’s endorsement of Lindsey Graham means nothing to South Carolina because we all know Lindsey,” Lynch said.

Our Analysis:

The Winds of Political Opportunism: Dissecting Lindsey Graham's Quandary

Senator Lindsey Graham's tenure epitomizes the classic tale of political metamorphosis, morphing with the prevailing winds rather than standing firm on the bedrock of principle. As the Washington Post article reveals, Graham's oscillation between his traditional foreign policy stances and the demands of the Trump era has not gone unnoticed, nor has it been particularly appreciated by the Republican base.

"I feel like he just flows with the wind." This sentiment from Jane Rabon is not just a casual observation but a damning indictment of Graham's political maneuverings. The metaphor of a weather vane comes to mind, spinning at the slightest breeze, with Graham's political convictions seemingly as malleable.

The RINO Accusation

The label of "RINO" (Republican in Name Only) is not just a catchy term but a significant marker of discontent within the party ranks. Bruni Baker's frustration, expressed through letters of discontent, signifies a deeper problem: the perceived abandonment of core Republican principles. When a senator's own constituents question their allegiance to the party's values, it's a clear signal of a representative adrift from their base.

The Trump Conundrum

Graham's relationship with Donald Trump is particularly perplexing. Once a vocal critic, Graham's subsequent alignment with Trump has been described as "Trump fluffery" by Mark Salter, McCain’s former speechwriter. This transformation from critic to ally raises questions about the authenticity of political convictions in the face of political expediency.

"People do what they have to do with Trump," Salter remarks, highlighting a broader issue within the Republican Party: the often uncomfortable embrace of Trumpism to maintain political relevance and support. This pragmatic, yet morally ambiguous strategy, epitomizes the challenges faced by traditional Republicans in the era of Trump.

The World Order at Stake

Salter's criticism of Trump's impact on global politics — "destroying the world order" — is no small accusation. It's a commentary on the seismic shifts in U.S. foreign policy under Trump's leadership, shifts that Graham once would have vehemently opposed. This underscores a profound transformation in Graham, from a defender of a certain global vision to a participant in its unraveling.

The Endorsement and Its Implications

Trump's endorsement of Graham, described as "always been there when I needed him," is telling. It's less a commendation of shared ideology and more an acknowledgment of loyalty. Graham's response, hinting at his own political mortality, "This is his last term, and I won’t be around forever," strikes a chord of resignation rather than defiance.

The Challenger Emerges

Mark Lynch's entry into the fray capitalizes on this growing disenchantment. His dismissal of Trump's endorsement as irrelevant to the sentiments of South Carolinians is bold and indicative of the shifting sands beneath Graham's feet. Lynch's challenge is not just political but symbolic, representing a broader contestation of what the Republican Party stands for in a post-Trump era.

In sum, Lindsey Graham's predicament is a microcosm of the larger Republican dilemma: the struggle between traditional conservative values and the populist wave ushered in by Trump. As Graham navigates these tumultuous waters, his story offers a cautionary tale of the perils of political opportunism and the fickle nature of political loyalty.
