Trump’s 'law school graduate' VP gets history lesson over 'absurd' constitutional claim

U.S. Vice President JD Vance attends a meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu (not pictured) in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, U.S., April 7, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

Former U.S. attorney Barbara McQuade on Friday gave the Trump administration a legal history lesson after Vice President JD Vance claimed it would not deliver Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) funds to hungry Americans despite a court order to do so.

U.S. District Judge John J. McConnell Jr. on Thursday “ordered the Trump administration to make a payment to fully fund” the program through November by Friday, ABC News reports.

Asked about the decision Thursday, Vance called it “an absurd ruling” and blamed SNAP funding on the Democratic Party.

“… You have a federal judge effectively telling us what we have to do in the midst of a government shutdown, which what we’d like to do is for the Democrats to open up the government,” Vance said Thursday. “Of course, then we can fund SNAP. We can also do a lot of other good things for the American people. But in the midst of a shutdown, we can’t have a federal court telling the president how he has to triage the situation.”

“We’re not going to do it under the orders of a federal judge,” Vance claimed.

Reacting to the vice president, McQuade didn’t mince words.

“Yeah. You know, JD Vance is a law school graduate — shame on him,” she said. “He knows that as far back as the seminal case of Marbury vs. Madison at the dawn of the Republic, the courts have said it is emphatically the province of the courts to say what the law is. It is the role of the courts to tell the president what to do when he is violating the law, the courts. The president’s remedy is to file an appeal, and if they get a different ruling there, that’s fine. But in the meantime, they are obligated to follow the court’s order.”

McQuade pointed to the judge’s remarks, noting it’s “obvious” there’s money to fund SNAP but the White House won’t do it “because they want to put pressure on Democrats in Congress.”

“President [Donald] Trump’s own social media posts were cited by the court to support that conclusion by the judge,” she noted. “And so this idea that somehow the president doesn’t have to follow the order of the court, that’s what’s absurd.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Defiance: A Lesson in Legal Obligations

The recent comments by Vice President JD Vance regarding the Trump administration’s refusal to comply with a federal court order to deliver Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) funds showcase a breathtaking misunderstanding or deliberate defiance of basic legal principles. Let’s dissect the layers of absurdity and the stark reminder of the administration’s approach to governance and legal accountability.

The Core of the Controversy

At the heart of this debacle is U.S. District Judge John J. McConnell Jr.’s order directing the Trump administration to fund SNAP through November, a directive stemming from the clear and present need to support hungry Americans. In a move that shocks the conscience, Vance labeled the ruling “an absurd ruling,” attributing the blame to the Democratic Party and suggesting that the administration is somehow exempt from following court orders during a government shutdown.

Vance’s Vehement Misstep

Vance’s statement, “We’re not going to do it under the orders of a federal judge,” is not only a flagrant display of contempt for the judiciary but also a disturbing echo of the administration’s penchant for placing politics over the rule of law. His comments not only betray a shocking disregard for the vulnerable populations dependent on SNAP but also a fundamental misunderstanding (or perhaps willful ignorance) of the role of the judiciary.

McQuade’s Merciless Retort

Former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade wasted no time in schooling Vance, reminding him and the audience that, since the landmark case of Marbury vs. Madison, it has been the unequivocal role of the courts to interpret the law and direct the executive branch when it oversteps or neglects its duties. McQuade’s pointed critique, “Yeah. You know, JD Vance is a law school graduate — shame on him,” underscores the expectation that those in the highest echelons of power should not only understand but respect the foundational principles of the U.S. legal system.

The Irony of the Administration’s Position

The administration’s stance becomes even more untenable when considering McQuade’s observation that the judge’s decision was supported by President Trump’s own social media posts, suggesting that the refusal to fund SNAP is not a matter of capability but rather a strategy to pressure Democrats. This revelation lays bare the administration’s willingness to use the well-being of millions of Americans as leverage in a political gambit.

The Absurdity of Ignoring Legal Obligations

The assertion that the president does not have to follow court orders is not just absurd; it’s dangerous. It sets a precedent that could erode the very fabric of our constitutional democracy by suggesting that the executive branch can operate above or outside the law. The very notion flies in the face of checks and balances that are integral to preventing the concentration of power and preserving the nation’s democratic institutions.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The refusal of the Trump administration to comply with a federal court order to fund SNAP in the midst of a government shutdown is not just a legal misstep; it is a moral failure. It reflects a disturbing trend of governing by defiance, where legal obligations are dismissed as inconveniences. The administration’s stance sends a chilling message about its commitment to the rule of law, the separation of powers, and, most importantly, the welfare of the American people.

It is incumbent upon all branches of government, and indeed upon all citizens, to hold those in power accountable to the principles that form the bedrock of American democracy. The law must apply equally to all, and no individual, not even the President, can be allowed to place themselves above it.
