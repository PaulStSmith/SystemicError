Trump gloats over huge YouTube settlement: 'All banned conservatives should be paid!'

After YouTube agreed to pay him $24.5 million, all of the Big Tech companies have now settled lawsuits the president filed against them in 2021 over bans imposed in the wake of the deadly U.S. Capitol riot. Trump shared an AI-generated post calling for other conservatives to be paid for their own bans from social media platforms.

“YouTube SURRENDERS! Pays Trump $24.5 MILLION for illegal ban!” Trump’s post on Truth Social reads, along with an emergency siren emoji and an image of a smiling Trump and what appears to be YouTube CEO Neal Mohan holding an oversized check. “This MASSIVE victory proves Big Tech censorship has consequences. Every shadowbanned patriot deserves justice!”

The House Judiciary Committee, led by Chairman Jim Jordan (R-OH), published multiple reports on alleged efforts to suppress conservative viewpoints on social media, but Twitter owner Elon Musk allowed Trump and other banned right-wing accounts back onto the platform after he bought the company and renamed it X.

“Trump fought for free speech and won!” the post adds. “Repost if ALL banned conservatives should be paid!”

Meta Platforms, which owns Facebook and Instagram, agreed in January to pay $25 million after similar bans, with most of that sum going to Trump’s presidential library. X agreed to pay $10 million, with most of it going directly to the president.

Executives at Google, which owns YouTube, had hoped to keep their settlement below the amount paid by rival Meta. About $22 million from the amount toward a nonprofit fund set up to pay for construction of a ballroom at the White House.

Another $2.5 million will go to other plaintiffs on the case, including the American Conservative Union and writer Naomi Wolf.

The Trump administration apparently engaged in its own suppression of speech earlier this month when Federal Communication Commission Chairman Brendan Carr publicly pressured ABC to drop talk show host Jimmy Kimmel over comments he made about slain right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, but the late-night host returned to the airwaves a week later in the face of public backlash.

Our Analysis:

The Midnight Gloat: Trump’s Big Tech Bonanza

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of irony and bravado, former President Donald Trump took to the digital streets of Truth Social to celebrate a monumental $24.5 million settlement with YouTube. The settlement comes in the wake of his post-January 6 ban from the platform, rounding off a series of legal victories against Big Tech companies for similar bans. But let’s dissect this for a moment, shall we?

The Price of Silence... Or Is It?

Trump’s triumphant post proclaimed, “YouTube SURRENDERS! Pays Trump $24.5 MILLION for illegal ban!” alongside an image of a grinning Trump and YouTube CEO Neal Mohan with an oversized check. This imagery alone is a masterclass in the art of self-aggrandizement—a skill Trump has polished to a high sheen. The settlement, he claims, is a “MASSIVE victory” against Big Tech censorship, calling for “justice” for every “shadowbanned patriot.”

It’s essential to unpack the massive victory narrative here. Trump, a figure whose presidency was marred by controversies over free speech and media manipulation, is now positioning himself as a martyr of free speech. The settlements, including $25 million from Meta and $10 million from X (formerly Twitter), supposedly vindicate his and other conservatives’ claims of being unfairly targeted by Big Tech.

The Alleged Suppression Saga

The House Judiciary Committee, under Chairman Jim Jordan, has been vocal about Big Tech’s alleged conservative suppression. Yet, it’s worth noting the convenient timing and selective outrage here. Twitter, under Elon Musk’s ownership, reinstated Trump and other right-wing figures, a move celebrated by Trump’s camp as a win for free speech. Yet, this narrative conveniently glosses over the context and consequences of the actions that led to the bans in the first place.

The Irony of Suppression Claims

The article mentions an intriguing counterpoint: the Trump administration’s own alleged efforts to suppress speech. The FCC’s Brendan Carr reportedly pressured ABC to drop Jimmy Kimmel over comments about Charlie Kirk, illustrating a stark hypocrisy. While Trump and his allies decry censorship, they seem all too willing to wield it against their critics. This duality is a glaring omission in Trump’s victory lap.

The Financial Windfall and Its Implications

Of the $24.5 million from YouTube, a significant portion is earmarked for a nonprofit fund for constructing a ballroom at the White House, with a smaller fraction going to other plaintiffs like the American Conservative Union and writer Naomi Wolf. This distribution raises eyebrows, not just for the allocations themselves but for what they signify about the priorities and motivations behind these lawsuits.

Conclusion: A Victory for Whom?

Trump’s settlement spree with Big Tech may be framed as a crusade for free speech, but a closer look suggests it’s more about personal vindication and financial gain than any principled stand against censorship. It underscores a broader strategy of leveraging claims of victimhood for political and monetary advantage, all while practicing the very suppression they decry.

In the end, one must ask: Is this a genuine victory for free speech, or a masterful exploitation of the system for personal gain? The answer, it seems, is as complex as the man at the center of it all.
