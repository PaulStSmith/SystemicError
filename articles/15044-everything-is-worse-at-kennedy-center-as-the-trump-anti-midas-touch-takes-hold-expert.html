'Everything is worse' at Kennedy Center as the 'Trump anti-Midas touch' takes hold: expert

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump, Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts (Photos by Yuri Gripas, Elizabeth Frantz for Reuters)

In an interview with Slate's Mary Harris, the New Yorker's Katy Waldman stated that nothing is going well at John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts since Donald Trump took over the venerable Washington D.C. institution.

Following up on earlier reports that crowd-pleasing –– and ticket-selling – performances like the musical "Hamilton" have bailed on appearing due to the president's meddling, Waldman bluntly claimed, "Everything is worse."

Singling out Richard Grenell who Trump put in place to oversee his vision of what the Kennedy Center should be, Waldman noted a purge of employees that followed and explained, " think the ways in which he has fired people or laid people off have been somewhat heartless. And also, there’s been a lot of slippery explanations," while describing his manner of dealing with people as " Seed any ground, come in hot, attack, attack, attack."

Asked about disputed reports coming the Trump administration that the arts center has been a money loser, she offered, "From what I could tell, it was flourishing. It had money, and it was doing what it was supposed to be doing pre-Trump. That doesn’t necessarily mean that it was always making more money than it spent on its programming. But because its sources of revenue were not just box office, but were also gifts and donations, it was still overall in the black."

With that in. mind, she added that financial difficulties are in the foreseeable future as ticket sales have collapsed.

"That’s a whole different part of the picture, which is no matter how well or badly the center was doing before Trump, the Trump anti-Midas touch is in operation here, and now everything is worse," she explained before adding, "Not only are ticket sales down, but donations have been paused, artists are pulling out. All of the spigots for money have slowed or have been turned off."

Our Analysis:

The Trumpian Decline of the Kennedy Center: A Sharp Analysis

The John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, once a beacon of cultural magnificence in Washington D.C., now finds itself embroiled in controversy and decline under the watch of former President Donald Trump. Katy Waldman's interview with Slate's Mary Harris paints a grim picture of the institution's current state, and it's a narrative we've seen all too often with Trump's touch—everything turning to lead, rather than gold.

The Grenell Factor: A Recipe for Disaster

The appointment of Richard Grenell by Trump to oversee the Kennedy Center was, bluntly put, a catastrophe in motion. Waldman's observations about the subsequent "purge" of employees under Grenell's watch highlight a broader trend of Trumpian leadership: chaos, heartlessness, and inefficiency. Describing Grenell's approach as "Seed any ground, come in hot, attack, attack, attack," Waldman captures the essence of a management style that is antithetical to the nurturing and promotion of arts. This is not just a failure of leadership; it's a deliberate dismantling of institutional integrity.

Financial Follies: The Art of Losing Money

The Trump administration's claim that the Kennedy Center has been a "money loser" is not just disputed; it's disingenuously misleading. As Waldman aptly notes, the Center was flourishing pre-Trump, buoyed by a diverse revenue stream that included gifts, donations, and box office sales. It's crucial to underscore that financial health in the arts is not solely measured by ticket sales—a fact conveniently ignored or misunderstood by the Trump administration.

Now, with Trump's "anti-Midas touch" in full effect, the Kennedy Center faces plummeting ticket sales and paused donations, a stark reversal of fortune. Artists pulling out and the drying up of financial resources underscore the broader implications of Trump's influence: a systematic erosion of cultural institutions' vitality and sustainability.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection on Trump's Legacy

The situation at the Kennedy Center is emblematic of a larger, more distressing pattern of Trump's impact on the arts and cultural institutions. The deliberate disregard for the arts' intrinsic value, coupled with a mercenary approach to management and governance, has left the Kennedy Center—and by extension, the nation's cultural landscape—in a state of disarray.

Waldman's interview, while focused on the Kennedy Center, invites readers to reflect on the broader implications of Trump's presidency on cultural institutions. The erosion of artistic and cultural integrity under his administration serves as a cautionary tale of what happens when leadership is devoid of vision, empathy, and understanding of the arts' crucial role in society.

In conclusion, the decline of the Kennedy Center under Trump is not just a case of poor management; it's a symptom of a systemic malaise that afflicted his administration's approach to governance. The arts, once celebrated as a unifying force and a beacon of national pride, have become casualties in Trump's America, leaving us all the poorer for it.
