'Never heard him so angry': MAGA senator posts – then deletes – story of furious Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 18, 20265 min read

Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) published – and immediately deleted – a post on social media Tuesday sharing details of a conversation he had moments earlier with President Donald Trump who, according to Graham, was absolutely furious over his pleas for other countries to aid his Iran war effort being largely ignored.

“Just spoke to President Trump about our European allies’ unwillingness to provide assets to keep the Strait of Hormuz functioning, which benefits Europe far more than America,” Graham wrote in a social media post on X Tuesday, a post that was deleted within minutes.

“I have never heard him so angry in my life. I share that anger given what’s at stake.”

Not long after Trump first authorized strikes on Iran, the Middle East nation vowed to attack any sea vessels aligned with the United States and its allies attempting to pass through the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial shipping route through which 20% of the world’s oil trade flows. Oil prices have skyrocketed as a result, reportedly sparking panic within the Trump administration and prompting Trump to call on other nations to support its war effort.

Those calls have mostly landed on deaf ears, however, and were met with “little in the way of immediate commitments,” The Wall Street Journal reported Sunday.

Trump’s apparent fury was shared by Graham, who wrote in his now-deleted social media post that the perceived snub from European nations made him “second guess the value” of U.S. alliances.

“The arrogance of our allies to suggest that Iran with a nuclear weapon is of little concern and that military action to stop the ayatollah from acquiring a nuclear bomb is our problem not theirs is beyond offensive,” Graham wrote.

“I consider myself very forward-leaning on supporting alliances, however at a time of real testing like this, it makes me second guess the value of these alliances. I am certain I am not the only senator who feels this way.”

Despite deleting the post, Graham re-published a revised version about 40 minutes later, and with minimal and inconsequential changes

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Deleted Post

In a recent flurry of social media activity, Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) inadvertently spotlighted the ongoing tensions between the United States and its European allies over the situation in the Strait of Hormuz. The deleted—and subsequently revised—post shared by Graham after a conversation with President Donald Trump sheds light not just on the frustrations boiling over within the administration, but also on the broader geopolitical chess game at play. A closer examination reveals not only the dynamics of power and decision-making at the highest levels of government but also the misdirections and oversimplifications that characterize much of the public discourse around international relations and security.

Who Holds the Power?

At the core of this situation are two main power centers: the President of the United States, Donald Trump, who has the executive authority to direct foreign policy and military action, and the collective body of European allies, whose cooperation—or lack thereof—can significantly impact the effectiveness and political legitimacy of U.S. actions abroad. Sen. Lindsey Graham, while influential as a senior senator and a member of the Republican Party, serves more as a conduit for expressing and amplifying the administration’s frustrations than as a direct actor in the decision-making process regarding military action or diplomatic strategy.

The Decision-Makers

The decision to press European allies for more support in the Strait of Hormuz, as well as the subsequent reactions to their reluctance, originates with President Trump and his administration. The president’s authority over foreign policy and military decisions places him at the helm of this issue. European leaders, deciding independently based on their nations’ interests and perspectives on the conflict with Iran, also play a critical role. Their collective hesitation to commit assets reflects a divergent appraisal of the situation and the appropriate response to it.

Misdirection of Responsibility

The framing of the article suggests a narrative of abandonment and betrayal by U.S. allies, positioning them as the primary roadblock to a successful strategy in the Strait of Hormuz. This narrative, amplified by Graham’s post and its dramatic deletion, obscures several key factors:

  1. Autonomy of European Decision-Making: European nations are sovereign entities with their own foreign policy goals and security assessments. Their reluctance is not merely obstinacy but a calculated stance on military intervention and its potential consequences.

  2. U.S. Foreign Policy Strategy: By focusing on European non-cooperation, the discussion shifts away from scrutinizing the Trump administration’s strategy toward Iran and its broader implications for regional stability and global oil markets.

  3. Complexity of International Relations: The situation in the Strait of Hormuz is a node in a complex web of diplomatic, military, and economic relationships. Simplifying it to a matter of allies failing to support U.S. actions bypasses the nuanced challenges of international diplomacy and consensus-building.

Conclusion

The deleted and revised post by Sen. Graham, while a fleeting moment in the digital age, encapsulates the current state of U.S.-European relations and the challenge of aligning diverse interests in a volatile international landscape. It serves as a reminder that, in the realm of global politics, power dynamics, decision-making authority, and the framing of events are critical in understanding the substance beyond the surface-level narratives.

The focus should not merely be on the frustration expressed by U.S. officials but on the broader strategic questions it raises: How can the U.S. effectively engage with its allies on shared security challenges? What are the implications of diverging strategies between the U.S. and Europe on Iran? And most importantly, how can a sustainable, cooperative approach be developed to address such critical international issues?

In dissecting the layers of this episode, we find a complex interplay of power, choices, and narratives that demands a more nuanced discussion than the one often presented. As observers and analysts, our duty is to peel back these layers, challenge the simplifications, and seek a deeper understanding of the forces at work.
