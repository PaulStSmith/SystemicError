Damning CNN report unveils real reason Trump opted to close Kennedy Center for renovations

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 5, 20264 min read

‘Can’t stand on that stage’: More artists are canceling Kennedy Center shows due to Trump

CNN is reporting that the real reason President Donald Trump announced the two-year renovation project for the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts is more about upcoming shows than about the project itself.

Host Dana Bash announced on her show Monday that there wouldn’t be much programming to announce for Trump’s newly renamed theater.

Several actors, singers, bands, composers, comedians and other performers are withdrawing from show commitments because they don’t support Trump. After Trump announced that the center would be closing, he was ridiculed on social media.

“The Kennedy Center does not have a 2026 2027 season,” a source told Bash. “There would not have been any programming to announce.”

The move comes after Trump made himself the chairman of the Kennedy Center board, fired several others on the board and replaced them with loyalists. Trump then ordered new letters to change the name to the Trump-Kennedy Center and the board voted to affirm the name change.

Democrats note that because Congress established the xenter by law, the name cannot be changed unless it is also by law.

White House Associated Press reporter Michelle Price said closing the center is an interesting move because most of the renovations Trump wanted were not expected to require closing the entire building. Trump even boasted on Truth Social that he could have done all of his renovations without disruption, such as updating the upholstery and redoing the carpet.

“But we got to the point where the list of cancellations was longer than the list of people” performing, she noted.

“And it seems that something has changed in the last few days that he is pushing for this change now to close it for two years,” said Price.

Last week, Trump’s head of the Kennedy Center quit after just two weeks on the job.

The Kennedy Center audience is typically made up of people who live in the tri-state area of Washington D.C., Virginia and Maryland. Those cities have all rejected Trumpism both in elections and in protests.

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Kennedy Center Fiasco: A Masterclass in Ego Over Practicality

In what can only be described as a theatrical saga dripping with irony, President Donald Trump’s decision to launch a two-year renovation of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts reveals less about a dedication to the arts and more about an ego-centric ploy to stamp his brand on a cultural institution. CNN’s recent report, courtesy of Dana Bash, unveils the convoluted web of Trump’s intentions with a clarity that exposes not only the superficiality of the renovation project but also the underlying political machinations.

The Absence of Substance: At the heart of this debacle is an astonishing admission: the so-called renovation, which necessitated the closure of the center, was not driven by a packed schedule of enriching performances but rather by an embarrassing scarcity. The Kennedy Center, rechristened with Trump’s name in a move of dubious legality, finds itself bereft of performers. Artists, in a commendable stand of integrity, chose to withdraw their commitments, rejecting the association with Trumpism—a decision that starkly highlights the cultural and moral chasm between the art community and the Trump administration.

A Board Hijacked: Trump’s maneuver to appoint himself chairman of the Kennedy Center board and populate it with loyalists is a classic power play that would make Machiavelli blush. The subsequent vote to affirm the renaming of the center to include Trump’s name not only disregards the institution’s legacy but also flouts legal norms, given that any change in name, as Democrats rightly point out, requires an act of Congress.

Renovation or Ego Trip? The justification for closing the center—purportedly for renovations that include such critical updates as new upholstery and carpeting—rings hollow, especially in light of Trump’s boast on Truth Social that these changes could have been accomplished without disrupting the center’s operations. This begs the question: Why insist on a complete shutdown? The answer, it seems, lies in the list of cancellations outstripping the list of performers willing to grace the Trump-Kennedy Center, a testament to the widespread rejection of Trump’s divisive brand.

Political Backlash: The Kennedy Center, historically a nexus for cultural and artistic expression in the tri-state area of Washington D.C., Virginia, and Maryland, now finds itself at the epicenter of a political backlash. These areas, having consistently rejected Trumpism at the polls and through protests, are unlikely to quietly acquiesce to the hijacking of a beloved cultural institution for Trump’s aggrandizement.

Conclusion: Trump’s attempt to refurbish the Kennedy Center in his image is a stark reminder of his administration’s proclivity for prioritizing personal legacy over cultural enrichment and legal norms. The widespread performer boycott and the legal challenges to the name change underscore a broader cultural repudiation of Trumpism. As this saga unfolds, one thing becomes clear: in the intersection of politics and the arts, integrity and cultural values hold sway over attempts at political commandeering. The Trump-Kennedy Center, rather than symbolizing a triumph, stands as a cautionary tale of overreach and resistance, a hollow monument to a misguided quest for legacy.
