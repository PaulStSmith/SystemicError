Jeanine Pirro put dance photographer in charge of indicting members of Congress

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Dance with Injustice: The Pirro Prosecutorial Farce

U.S. Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ) speaks with members on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

Fox News host-turned U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro’s attempt to criminally charge six members of Congress fell flat this week. That may be due to the personnel she put in charge of making the case to the grand jury.

Bloomberg Law reported Wednesday that one of the Department of Justice (DOJ) prosecutors Pirro tasked with convincing a grand jury to indict members of Congress — who recorded a video message encouraging active-duty military service members to honor their oath to disobey illegal orders — is a dance photographer with no prior DOJ experience.

According to Bloomberg, Steve Vandervelden currently maintains a photography studio, and his Instagram profile shows posts as recent as Wednesday morning. He previously worked for Pirro several decades ago when she was the district attorney in Westchester County, New York. The other prosecutor on the case, Carlton Davis, last worked for the DOJ in 2018 as an assistant U.S. attorney in the Eastern District of Virginia.

“I spent a good portion of my life peering into the darkness looking for the bad guy,” Vandervelven told the Rockland/Westchester Journal News in 2023, when explaining why he gave up his law career for photography. “Now it’s a joy to peer into the darkness and look for the light and find beauty as opposed to the grime.”

The outlet reported that it is “unprecedented” for the DOJ to assign a high-level investigation to an outside attorney. Typically, the Justice Department relies on longtime career prosecutors with multiple years of experience working on complex cases to ensure a suspect is indicted by a grand jury.

According to Bloomberg’s unnamed sources within the DOJ, career prosecutors were alarmed that Pirro prevented the Justice Department’s Public Integrity (PIN) section — which typically handles investigations of sitting elected officials – from the case involving the six Democratic lawmakers. However, PIN was effectively hollowed out last year with a wave of firings and resignations, and Bloomberg reported that PIN’s remaining staff may have not had any input on the case.

The six Democrats in the video are all veterans of either the U.S. military or intelligence agencies. Sens. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) and Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.) joined Reps. Jason Crow (D-Colo.), Maggie Goodlander (D-N.H.), Chris DeLuzio (D-Pa.) and Chrissy Houlahan (D-Pa.) to remind troops of the Uniform Code of Military Justice’s instructions to obey all orders from superior officers, except for orders that are illegal.

Our Analysis:

A Dance with Injustice: The Pirro Prosecutorial Farce

In what can only be described as a bizarre twist of events, Fox News host-turned-U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro’s efforts to levy criminal charges against six members of Congress have unraveled into a spectacle that undermines the gravitas of the United States Department of Justice (DOJ). The crux of this debacle lies not just in the audacious attempt to indict public officials for advocating military adherence to lawful orders, but in the utterly perplexing choice of personnel spearheading this legal crusade.

The Unlikely Prosecutor: From Capturing Dances to Courting Disasters

Let’s zoom in on Steve Vandervelven, a name that has bewildered many within and outside the legal realm. Vandervelven, a dance photographer with zero prior DOJ experience, was plucked from the artistic confines of a photography studio to the high-stakes arena of a federal grand jury. This decision reeks of not just nepotism—given his previous employment under Pirro in a different life—but of a glaring disregard for professional legal standards. Vandervelven’s own words to the Rockland/Westchester Journal News encapsulate this surreal transition, from seeking “the bad guy” in the legal field to finding “beauty” through the lens of a camera. While poetic, this does little to instill confidence in his prosecutorial capabilities.

A Departure from Tradition

The assignment of an “outside attorney” with no DOJ background for a high-profile investigation deviates so far from standard practice that it lands in the realm of the unprecedented. The DOJ, a fortress of legal procedure and decorum, traditionally relies on seasoned career prosecutors for such tasks. These are professionals with years, if not decades, of experience in navigating the complex and treacherous waters of high-level cases. The sidelining of the Public Integrity (PIN) section, a specialized unit designed to handle the intricacies of cases involving elected officials, raises serious questions about the motivations behind this prosecutorial charade. The evisceration of PIN under Pirro’s watch, coupled with the exclusion of its expertise, paints a picture of a deliberate attempt to circumvent the seasoned wisdom and oversight such cases demand.

The Targets: A Defiance of Illegality

The subjects of this ill-conceived indictment attempt are six Democrats, all of whom have served in the U.S. military or intelligence agencies. Their “crime”? Reminding active-duty service members of their duty to disobey illegal orders, a cornerstone principle of the Uniform Code of Military Justice. This group includes figures like Sens. Mark Kelly and Elissa Slotkin, along with Reps. Jason Crow, Maggie Goodlander, Chris DeLuzio, and Chrissy Houlahan. Their message, rooted in the foundational values of military service and legal obedience, has been twisted into the basis for a criminal charge, an irony so thick it’s palpable.

Conclusion: A Misstep of Judicial Proportions

In summary, Jeanine Pirro’s decision to employ a dance photographer with no DOJ experience for a high-stakes legal maneuver against sitting members of Congress is not just an error in judgment; it’s a farce. This scenario undermines the integrity of the judicial process, making a mockery of the principles of justice and due diligence. It is imperative for the legal community and the public at large to critically assess and challenge such deviations from legal norms and expectations. The DOJ is not a playground for political vendettas or amateur hour for aspiring prosecutors. The American people deserve better, and the sanctity of the legal system demands it.
