'Just heard a single vertebra!' Johnson finally responds to Trump’s cruel Reiner comments

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) holds a press conference in the U.S. Capitol Visitor Center in Washington, D.C. on Dec. 16, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

House Speaker Mike Johnson Wednesday was forced to respond to President Donald Trump‘s cruel comments on Hollywood legend Rob Reiner following the news that he and his wife were murdered.

Trump attacked Reiner, who was found stabbed to death with his wife Michele inside their Brentwood, California home, in a Truth Social post. He tried to suggest that Reiner’s “massive, unyielding, and incurable affliction with a mind crippling disease known as TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME” was responsible for his death.

In an interview with CNBC’s Squawk Box, Johnson finally responded — and blamed the Capitol Hill press corps. — to questions about the president’s remarks after journalist Andrew Ross Sorkin asked him to share his thoughts, prefacing it by saying he wouldn’t “love” the question.

“Well, I did comment at length. They just didn’t replay that part,” Johnson said. “No, no, no surprise here. Capitol hill press corps. But what I said was straight from the heart. I heard about it coming out of an event in the morning when the news broke. And my comment was that, I mean, obviously it’s an unspeakable family tragedy. And the Reiner family, I mean, just a horrible thing to think of.”

Johnson cited a Bible verse and Sorkin pushed back again, asking Johnson to address the president’s comments.

“Look, I know I’m asked to give running commentary on everything that the president says every five minutes of the day, as well as all of my colleagues here,” said Johnson, who appeared frustrated by the question.

“I don’t communicate the same way,” he said. “I am my own person, and I speak, you know, from my voice.”

“The president and I address issues differently sometimes,” Johnson said. “That’s not the way I would have done it. It’s not the way I have done it. You know, that’s my comment on it.”

People reacted to Johnson’s comments on social media:

“I think I just heard a single vertebra!” user Jeremy Winter wrote on X.

“Real profiles-in-courage moment, Skippy,” University of Illinois Assistant Director of Communications professor Vince Lara-Cinisomo wrote on X.

“Evasion speaks loudly. When pressed on rhetoric, leaders face a choice: clarify, condemn, or deflect. Accountability starts with direct answers,” journalist Qadir Khan Yousufzai wrote on X.

“You condemn his vile and immoral remarks Mike. That is what leadership of the House should do,” user Lee West wrote on X.

“He didn’t ‘love’ that question so it had to asked again; just to give him the chance to give the weakest ass response imaginable,” film sound editor John Kwiatkowski wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Hollow Echoes of Political Accountability

In a spectacle of evasion that would be comedic if it weren’t so grotesquely tragic, House Speaker Mike Mike Johnson found himself cornered by the basic expectation of human decency—and promptly sprinted in the opposite direction. The catalyst for this feat of moral gymnastics was none other than President Donald Trump’s comments following the brutal murder of Hollywood legend Rob Reiner and his wife Michele. Trump’s choice to attack Reiner, even in death, blaming his outspoken criticism of Trump (termed “TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME”) for his murder, sets a new low, even for him. Yet, it’s Johnson’s response, or the lack thereof, that we’re dissecting today.

When pressed by CNBC’s Squawk Box host Andrew Ross Sorkin to reflect on Trump’s remarks, Johnson performed an act of verbal contortion so profound it might have defied the laws of physics. His initial deflection blamed the Capitol Hill press corps for not broadcasting his “lengthy” comments, a maneuver as tired as it is transparent. The press, after all, is a convenient scapegoat for politicians unwilling to confront the substance of their or their allies’ actions.

Johnson’s statement that he is “his own person” and does not “communicate the same way” as the president is a fascinating study in the art of saying nothing. It’s the political equivalent of claiming to be a vegetarian between bites of a steak. The president and Johnson might indeed “address issues differently,” but when one of those issues involves the president making cruel and insensitive remarks about a recently murdered public figure, the difference in style should be the least of our concerns.

Social media reactions to Johnson’s non-answer were both swift and scathing, with comments ranging from the sarcastic to the outright condemnatory. These reactions underline a collective frustration with a political landscape where leaders routinely dodge accountability with the grace of a soap opera villain. Jeremy Winter’s mention of a “single vertebra” and Vince Lara-Cinisomo’s “profiles-in-courage moment, Skippy” comment capture the essence of Johnson’s response—a display of spinelessness masquerading as diplomacy.

Journalist Qadir Khan Yousufzai hits closer to the heart of the matter, pointing out that when pressed on rhetoric, leaders have a choice: “clarify, condemn, or deflect.” Johnson, in choosing the third option, failed not just the basic test of leadership but also of basic human empathy. Lee West and John Kwiatkowski echo the sentiment that Johnson missed an opportunity to show real leadership by unequivocally condemning Trump’s remarks.

The Verdict?

In the wake of tragedy, we look to our leaders for clarity, compassion, and condemnation of the indefensible. Mike Johnson’s response to Donald Trump’s vile comments on Rob Reiner’s murder was a masterclass in evasion—a reminder that, in the political arena, the courage to speak plainly and morally remains a rare commodity. Johnson’s refusal to directly condemn Trump’s remarks is not just a failure of leadership; it’s a stark illustration of the moral bankruptcy that plagues our current political discourse.

In a world where political expediency often trumps decency, Johnson’s actions are a systemic symptom rather than an isolated error. It’s time we demand more from our leaders than cowardly deflections and half-hearted expressions of sympathy. The soul of our politics, and indeed our nation, depends on it.
