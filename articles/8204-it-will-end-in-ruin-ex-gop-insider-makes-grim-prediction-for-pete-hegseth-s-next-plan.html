'It will end in ruin': Ex-GOP insider makes grim prediction for Pete Hegseth's next plan

Pete Hegseth (Reuters)

Pete Hegseth just admitted to murder and is heading toward executing a massive mission that one ex-GOP insider says will “end in ruin.”

Steve Schmidt, an independent political strategist who worked on former President George W. Bush’s campaign, on Sunday published a piece called, “Kill them all” is a confession to murder under American law, in which he discusses how the “United States of America stands at the edge of war against Venezuela.”

Schmidt begins by flagging “a letter from Armed Services Committee chair Roger Wicker and Democratic Senator Jack Reed,” which he said “seems to suggest that the Politburo takes a dim view of blatant murder, and may seek to assert its muted powers and end its eunuch status, by reminding the executive branch of its co-equal status within the government of the Republic.”

He then continued:

“However, anyone betting on these people to do their duty at this point is a fool waiting to be separated from their money. For the optimist, the best I can do is say ‘Trust, but verify.’ I say this based on precedent. If Trump favors invading a nation of 28.4 million without a strategy, plan or any debate under utterly incompetent leadership after dividing the nation, MAGA is for it.”

Schmidt further added, “When the inevitable disaster comes they will blame it on anyone from Conan the Barbarian to Chelsea Clinton to Mr. Magoo, but never Trump. Never Trump. After all, God can do no wrong — at least their God.”

Summarizing how he sees it, Schmidt says, “When Trump retreats and the bodies come home they will say it was a victory.”

“When the economy crashes they will call it prosperity. When your job disappears they will call it opportunity. When a masked secret police thug smashes a window and punches a woman or child they will call it justice. When an election is lost they will say it is stolen,” the strategist wrote. “The war in Venezuela has been concocted on a south Florida patio by the most incompetent gang of American leaders in American history. It will end in ruin.”

He goes on to say the U.S. is “going to war against Venezuela with no congressional debate, no public debate and scant media coverage on the back of war crimes being committed in the name of the American people — and executed by men who have likely committed murder under the Geneva Conventions.”

“Incredibly, the war will begin before the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks and before the 5th anniversary of the American withdrawal from Afghanistan,” he further stated.

Our Analysis:

The Mirage of Militaristic Triumph: A Scathing Analysis of Hegseth’s ‘Admission’ and the Venezuela Conundrum

In a world where the boundary between political rhetoric and actionable policy increasingly blurs, the recent revelations surrounding Pete Hegseth’s so-called “admission to murder” and the broader implications of a looming U.S. military escapade in Venezuela demand a rigorous, unflinching examination. Steve Schmidt, leveraging his considerable experience as a political strategist, offers a damning critique, titled “Kill them all” is a confession to murder under American law, which lays bare the reckless abandon with which certain factions within the American political landscape flirt with the idea of war. Schmidt’s analysis, while centered on the inflammatory comments and actions of specific political figures, underscores a systemic malaise that threatens not just the foundational principles of American democracy but also the very lives that such wars casually put on the line.

The Politburo’s Dim View and the Eunuch Status of Congressional Oversight

Schmidt’s invocation of a “dim view” by the Politburo, in reality, a metaphor for the supposed oversight capabilities of the U.S. Armed Services Committee, is a scathing indictment of the legislative branch’s abdication of responsibility. The reference to Senators Roger Wicker and Jack Reed’s letter as a potential spark for reclaiming the Congress’s co-equal status is met with well-earned skepticism by Schmidt. The historical precedent of congressional inaction and the almost ceremonial role it has played in matters of war and peace lend credence to his cynicism. Schmidt’s assertion that trusting in these institutions to act decisively is akin to a “fool waiting to be separated from their money” is not just a pithy remark but a dire warning against misplaced optimism in the face of overwhelming evidence to the contrary.

The Cult of MAGA and the Teflon Don

Schmidt’s critique of the MAGA movement’s blind allegiance to Donald Trump, especially in matters of war, is both poignant and painfully accurate. The portrayal of Trump as a deity incapable of wrongdoing, even as he purportedly contemplates invading Venezuela “without a strategy, plan or any debate under utterly incompetent leadership,” is a testament to the dangerous cult of personality that has taken root. The inevitability of disaster is met not with introspection or accountability but with a Kafkaesque blame game that would be laughable if it weren’t so tragically predictable.

The War on Reality: Propaganda, Prosperity, and Policing

Schmidt’s broader critique of the MAGA movement’s Orwellian manipulation of reality — where defeats are victories, economic crashes are prosperity, and authoritarian brutality is justice — is a searing condemnation of the current state of American political discourse. The planned aggression against Venezuela, devoid of any semblance of public debate or media scrutiny, is emblematic of a deeper malady: a government that operates in the shadows, unaccountable to its people, and engaged in actions that contravene both domestic and international law.

Conclusion: A Precipice of Ruin

In his essay, Steve Schmidt paints a grim picture of a United States teetering on the brink of moral and strategic bankruptcy. The war in Venezuela, conceived in hubris and executed in secrecy, is not just an indictment of a particular administration or political faction but a stark warning about the erosion of democratic norms and the perils of unchecked militarism. Schmidt’s call to action, though shrouded in despair, is a clarion call for vigilance, accountability, and, most importantly, a return to principled leadership that respects the rule of law and the sanctity of human life. As we stand on this precipice, the choice between ruin and redemption has never been clearer.
