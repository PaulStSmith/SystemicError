Steve Schmidt: Today's G7 meeting proves Trump is in serious decline

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Decline Is Not the Story. The Succession Machine Is.

The Source of the Alarm

The source material is built around a simple claim: Steve Schmidt and psychiatrist Henry Abraham say Trump appears to be deteriorating physically and mentally, while Louise Aronson pushes back on age as a lazy frame and says the real test is policy, outcomes, honesty, and record. That is the setup. The political meaning is elsewhere.

Who Actually Holds Power

The institutional power here is not in Trump’s posture, hands, or ankles. It is in the presidency itself, and in the network around it that can conceal weakness, route access, and shape succession. Trump is the officeholder. J.D. Vance is the constitutional replacement. Peter Thiel, as described in the source, sits outside the formal chain but inside the power structure that can profit from a handoff.

That is the real center of gravity. Not whether Trump looks frail in public. Whether the system around him is already normalizing a transition to a vice president presented as an obedient instrument.

The Useful Panic

The piece pretends to be about health, but it keeps drifting toward performance: slurred words, falling asleep, being steered through photo ops, being surrounded by doctors. That imagery is not accidental. It invites the reader to fixate on Trump’s body instead of the machinery that keeps him in place.

This is classic misdirection. A weakening leader is not the same thing as a weakened regime. If anything, a visibly diminished president can become easier for the surrounding apparatus to manage, exploit, and eventually replace without ever answering for what he enabled.

The Enabling Class

The source is clearest when it points at the people doing the enabling. Schmidt’s description of Vance as Trump’s “fascist understudy” and “puppet to Peter Thiel” is not neutral analysis; it is a warning about the real succession plan. If that characterization is even partly right, then the danger is not that Trump might fade. It is that a cruder, cleaner, more disciplined successor is waiting in the wings with donor-backed legitimacy and less need for improvisation.

The same applies to the medical theater. If Trump is being “poked and prodded” by specialists while his decline is handled as a public-relations problem, then the institution is already doing what institutions do best: converting serious dysfunction into managed optics.

Age Is the Wrong Shield

Aronson’s point matters because it blocks an easy escape hatch. Age is not the issue by itself. Power is. Health is relevant because it affects decision-making, vulnerability, and continuity. But reducing this to generic geriatric commentary would let the political operators off the hook.

The source is strongest when it refuses that dodge. The relevant question is not whether an older leader looks old. It is who benefits when his condition becomes a spectacle, who controls the narrative around it, and who is positioned to inherit the machinery once the performance breaks.

The Real Pattern

This story is a small version of a broader political pattern: authoritarian movements and personality cults always depend on a double lie. First, they insist the leader is singular and indispensable. Then, when the leader falters, they quietly prepare a replacement who is less chaotic, more disciplined, and often more dangerous.

That is the lesson here. The body may fail. The apparatus usually does not.
