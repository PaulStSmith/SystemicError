'Please kill us': Unusual request flagged in oversight board's budget request

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Erosion of Independent Oversight: A Critical Analysis of the CSB Budget Request

FILE PHOTO: A view of the White House in Washington, U.S., July 20, 2024. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt/File Photo

A former high-ranking federal flagged an unusual budget request by an independent agency that investigates chemical disasters.

White House budget documents released Friday show the Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board would shut down by October 2026 as “part of the Administration’s plans to move the Nation towards fiscal responsibility and to redefine the proper role of the Federal Government,” reported the Washington Post.

"President Donald Trump repeatedly called for zeroing out the agency’s funding during his first term, only for Congress to maintain or increase its budget," the Post reported.

"This time, however, Trump is trying to seize greater control of independent agencies, testing the limits of presidential power," the report added. "The Supreme Court last month refused to immediately reinstate a pair of independent regulators fired by the Trump administration, saying the president may have the authority to oust them.

Congress established the CSB through the Clean Air Act in 1990, and it operated last year on the relatively small budget of about $14 million, but the board asked for zero funding in its budget request to the White House for fiscal year 2026 because its work “duplicates substantial capabilities” of the Environmental Protection Agency and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration.

“CSB generates unprompted studies of the chemical industry and recommends policies that they have no authority to create or enforce,” the board said in its request. “This function should reside within agencies that have authorities to issue regulations in accordance with applicable legal standards.”

The request, and especially the language used by the board, struck Jordan Barab, the former deputy assistant secretary of labor at OSHA under Barack Obama, as highly unusual.

“The so-called budget request basically said, ‘We don’t deserve to live; please kill us,’” Barab said. “That indicates to me that the request did not come from the CSB leadership itself, but was rather imposed on the CSB by the White House. … So much for being an ‘independent’ agency.”

The CSB is supposed the have five members who are appointed by the president and then Senate-confirmed, but the board currently has just thee members, all appointed by Joe Biden, and has had two open seats since February 2023, but eliminating it will likely face opposition from Congress.

“If Trump gets his way, this will mean more chemical disasters choking our communities with toxic fumes, upending lives, and threatening the health and property of those living and working close by,” said Sen. Jeff Merkley of Oregon (D-OR), the top Democrat on the Senate Environment and Public Works subcommittee on chemical safety. “I will do everything I can to prevent this gutting of funds and will continue to push for stronger laws to prevent chemical disasters and keep our communities and workers safe.”

Our Analysis:

The Erosion of Independent Oversight: A Critical Analysis of the CSB Budget Request

In a move that can only be described as audacious and deeply troubling, recent revelations about the Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board's (CSB) budget request have laid bare the Trump administration's attempts to redefine the role of federal oversight in a manner that portends dire consequences for public safety and environmental protection.

The Unprecedented Request

At the heart of this controversy is a budget proposal that effectively signs the death warrant for the CSB, an independent agency tasked with investigating chemical disasters. According to the Washington Post, White House budget documents openly state the intention to shutter the CSB by October 2026, framing it as a stride towards "fiscal responsibility" and a recalibration of "the proper role of the Federal Government." This rationale, however, thinly veils a more sinister agenda.

It's worth noting that this isn't the first time the Trump administration has targeted the CSB; previous attempts to zero out its funding were thwarted by Congress, which instead chose to maintain or even increase its budget. The persistence in trying to dismantle the agency underscores a disturbing trend of undermining independent regulatory bodies that serve as crucial checks on industry malpractices.

The Role of the CSB

Operating on a modest budget of about $14 million, the CSB's mandate to conduct "unprompted studies of the chemical industry and recommend policies" is a cornerstone of America's chemical safety infrastructure. The board's assertion, as reported, that its functions "duplicate substantial capabilities" of other agencies like the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) is a misleading oversimplification. The unique value of the CSB lies in its independence and its ability to conduct thorough investigations without the influence of regulatory or political pressures that might affect other agencies.

A Troubling Indication of Influence

The language used in the CSB's budget request — suggesting that the agency "does not deserve to live" — raises red flags about the integrity and autonomy of the board's decision-making. As noted by Jordan Barab, former deputy assistant secretary of labor at OSHA, this language is highly uncharacteristic of an independent agency advocating for its own dissolution. It strongly indicates external pressures, likely from the White House, dictating the agency's stance on its own relevance and existence.

The Implications for Public Safety and Environmental Protection

The potential closure of the CSB is not merely a bureaucratic reshuffling; it represents a critical blow to public safety and environmental protection. The board's investigations into chemical accidents have been instrumental in identifying hazards and recommending essential safety improvements. Without the CSB's oversight, the likelihood of chemical disasters "choking our communities with toxic fumes" — as Sen. Jeff Merkley of Oregon aptly put it — markedly increases.

Moreover, the attempt to dismantle the CSB signals a broader assault on the framework of independent regulatory oversight in the United States. It tests the limits of presidential power over such agencies, challenging the very principles of checks and balances that underpin the federal government's operations.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's budget proposal for the CSB is a stark reminder of the ongoing efforts to erode independent oversight and prioritize industry interests over public health and safety. This move, if successful, not only endangers communities and workers across the nation but also represents a significant step backward in the fight for a safer, more accountable chemical industry. It is incumbent upon Congress and the public to staunchly oppose this proposal and safeguard the vital work carried out by the CSB. The cost of inaction, measured in human lives and environmental degradation, is far too great.
