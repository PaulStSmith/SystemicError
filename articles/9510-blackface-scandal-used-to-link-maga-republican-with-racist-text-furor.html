Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY) is the focus of the targeted five-figure ad called “Not for Us, which will air on YouTube and television in his district, Politico reports Thursday.

The ad will also mention Lawler’s use of blackface while he was a college student. Lawler has said it was “an homage to pop star Michael Jackson.”

“Mike Lawler keeps showing us exactly who he is — between now and Election Day we’re going to make sure Hudson Valley voters know he’s not fit for Congress,” a Black Caucus PAC spokesperson said.

The campaign is also working to connect Lawler to Peter Giunta, a member of the New York Young Republicans reportedly involved in the Telegram chats released by Politico earlier this week that included racist, antisemitic and homophobic comments, rape fantasies, and suggestions on how to drive their opponents to suicide.

“They referred to Black people as monkeys and ‘the watermelon people’ and mused about putting their political opponents in gas chambers. They talked about raping their enemies and driving them to suicide and lauded Republicans who they believed support slavery,” according to the report.

Giunta has claimed that the chat and its release are “a highly-coordinated year-long character assassination.” He apologized, saying, “I am so sorry to those offended by the insensitive and inexcusable language found within the more than 28,000 messages of a private group chat that I created during my campaign to lead the Young Republicans.”

Our Analysis:

The Blackface Scandal and MAGA Republican: A Deeper Dive

In a recent political maneuver that’s as unsurprising as it is distasteful, the Congressional Black Caucus PAC has launched an ad campaign that drags Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY) over the coals for his college days’ blackface incident. Yet, this is far from just a dredging up of past indiscretions. This strategic political play links Lawler to the broader, more malignant issue of racism festering within certain segments of the Republican Party, as evidenced by the repugnant racist text leak involving young Republicans.

The Scandal Unpacked

Rep. Lawler’s past use of blackface, described laughably as an “homage to pop star Michael Jackson,” is not just a juvenile mistake but a glaring indication of the pervasive, often dismissed, racial insensitivity within political ranks. The Congressional Black Caucus PAC’s decision to highlight this in their ad, “Not for Us,” is a calculated move to remind voters in Hudson Valley of the underlying racial biases that candidates like Lawler represent.

Connecting Dots: Lawler and the Racist Text Leak

However, the intrigue thickens as the campaign weaves Lawler’s past indiscretion with the nauseatingly racist comments unearthed from a Telegram chat created by Peter Giunta, a member of the New York Young Republicans. The contents of these chats, which include references to Black people as “monkeys” and “the watermelon people,” discussions about rape, suicide, and a nauseating yearning for slavery, are not just appalling; they are a window into the soul of a deeply troubled segment of the American political landscape.

Giunta’s defense, branding the release of these chats as “a highly-coordinated year-long character assassination,” and his apology to those “offended by the insensitive and inexcusable language” is a classic non-apology. It skirts around the issue, failing to acknowledge the deep-rooted bigotry and violence espoused in these messages.

A Reflection on Broader Implications

This saga is not just about Lawler or Giunta; it’s reflective of a larger, systemic issue within certain factions of the Republican Party that seem to be increasingly emboldened in their racist, antisemitic, homophobic, and sexist rhetoric. The attempt to connect Lawler with Giunta’s scandal is not merely political mudslinging; it’s a pointed critique of the complicity and silence within the party regarding these abhorrent views.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The Congressional Black Caucus PAC’s ad campaign, while a political strategy, underscores a critical need for accountability and a reckoning within the Republican Party. It’s not enough to apologize when caught or to claim ignorance. The patterns of behavior and belief evidenced by these scandals are indicative of a profound moral and ethical rot. Voters, and indeed all Americans, must demand better from their political representatives. The acceptance of racism, whether overt or covert, cannot and should not be a partisan issue. It’s a fundamental matter of human decency and morality.

In the final analysis, this episode is a stark reminder of the ongoing battle against racism in America. The political arena, unfortunately, reflects society’s broader struggles. It’s incumbent upon us all to stand against this tide, demanding not just apologies but real, substantive change.
