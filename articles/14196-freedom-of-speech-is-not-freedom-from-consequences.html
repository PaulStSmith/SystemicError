Freedom of Speech Is Not Freedom from Consequences

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 31, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Grievance GriftHypocrisy in High-DefFaux Naïveté and Performance PoliticsThe Martyrdom Industrial ComplexConsequences Are Not Censorship

One of the most persistent and disingenuous talking points in the modern right-wing playbook is the deliberate conflation of freedom of speech with freedom from consequences. This distortion isn’t merely a misunderstanding—it’s a carefully engineered strategy, designed to shield bad actors from accountability while weaponizing the language of liberty. It’s a rhetorical smokescreen meant to excuse everything from overt hate speech and bigotry to conspiracy-peddling, lies, and destabilizing propaganda.

The goal is simple: say whatever you want, however inflammatory or false, and if anyone dares respond with criticism or action—accuse them of censorship. It's a calculated effort to hijack the moral high ground by claiming persecution while simultaneously inflicting harm.

The Grievance Grift

At the heart of this rhetorical bait-and-switch is the modern right’s performative addiction to victimhood. The very people who rail against so-called “snowflakes”—those who supposedly crumble under pressure or offense—are the first to descend into histrionics when their own offensive tweets, bigoted diatribes, or unhinged rants spark backlash. Whether it’s a ban from Twitter, a sponsor pulling out, or a well-deserved public rebuke, they immediately cry foul. Suddenly, these self-proclaimed warriors of open discourse rebrand themselves as martyrs of cancel culture, painting every consequence as persecution.

But let’s be clear: the First Amendment of the U.S. Constitution protects citizens from government censorship. It does not entitle anyone to a microphone, a publishing deal, or an audience of millions. It doesn’t force social media companies to carry your racist tirades, nor does it compel businesses or academic institutions to provide a stage for your bile. When a private entity removes your podcast, rescinds your speaking invitation, or walks away from a toxic partnership—that’s not censorship. That’s society exercising its own free speech—the right to say, “We don’t support this.”

Hypocrisy in High-Def

What makes this grievance grift particularly galling is the level of hypocrisy baked into it. The same people who are screaming about cancel culture from their multi-million-dollar media empires are often the most enthusiastic proponents of actual, institutional censorship. They’re the ones pushing for book bans, suppressing discussion of race and gender in classrooms, passing draconian laws against protest, and demanding ideological purity tests in education and government.

Their concern isn’t about freedom—it’s about control. “Free speech” in this context isn’t a universal principle, it’s a partisan bludgeon. When they speak of liberty, they don’t mean for everyone. They mean for themselves—and for no one who disagrees.

Faux Naïveté and Performance Politics

Of course, some portion of the population genuinely doesn’t understand what the First Amendment does or does not protect. Civics education in the U.S. has long been a disaster. But among the pundit class, the influencers, the elected officials, and the media shock-jocks? The confusion is rarely sincere. It’s a performance.

Feigning ignorance about the Constitution plays well on social media. It turns legal nuance into binary outrage. The louder the cry of injustice, the more attention the post garners. When the inevitable backlash arrives, it’s spun into a martyrdom narrative: “Look at what the left is doing to us!”

It’s not about free speech. It’s about viral clips, culture war capital, and merchandising the persecution complex. It’s politics as grift, outrage as branding.

The Martyrdom Industrial Complex

In the current media ecosystem, there is no quicker path to right-wing celebrity than claiming you were “silenced”—even as you shout it from a national platform. Say something vile. Get ratioed or suspended. Then cry censorship and watch the donations, book deals, and guest spots roll in. This is the martyrdom industrial complex: a feedback loop where public condemnation becomes a badge of honor and a springboard for monetization.

It doesn’t matter whether what was said was deeply racist, dangerously misleading, or flat-out false. In fact, the more outrageous the content, the better it performs in the grievance economy. From failed politicians and disgraced pundits to fringe influencers, the formula remains the same: offend, provoke, play the victim, monetize. Rinse. Repeat.

Consequences Are Not Censorship

Freedom of speech is a foundational pillar of any functioning democracy. But that freedom is not, and never has been, a license to say anything without reaction or repercussion. Speech is a two-way street. When you say something, others are free to respond, reject, criticize, and yes—disassociate from you entirely.

You are free to speak your mind, no matter how ignorant, hateful, or absurd. But the rest of us are equally free to say you’re wrong, to walk away, to call you out, or to take action against the damage your words cause. That’s not tyranny. That’s accountability.

Speech without consequences is not liberty. It’s impunity. And no ideology, political stripe, or social movement is entitled to that. Not now. Not ever.
