Mad as hell Dems will incinerate their party before they accept this betrayal

Based on an article by David Fagin

Accountability Is Not a Mood

The source makes one useful point almost by accident: power is the issue, not vibes. Republicans currently hold the executive machinery and, depending on the chamber, the ability to block oversight. But the article’s sharper target is Democrats, because the question is not whether they can complain. It is whether they will use institutional power if voters hand it to them.

The Real Question

The article is about a basic test of governance disguised as a sermon about frustration. If Democrats win Congress, they would get subpoena power, committee gavels, and the authority to force facts into the open. That is not abstract symbolism. It is one of the few tools left for making abuse legible. If they refuse to use it, then the problem is not lack of power. It is lack of will.

“Move On” Is a Dodge

The most revealing line in the source is the urge to “move on.” That phrase sounds mature only to people who have never had to live under the consequences of unaccountable power. Moving forward means investigating, documenting, and imposing consequences. Moving on means protecting the people who benefited from the damage and asking everyone else to swallow it.

That distinction matters because the article itself names the pattern: Democrats funding ICE when resistance was demanded, answering outrage with “strong letters,” and treating obvious abuse as something to be managed rather than confronted. That is not prudence. It is institutional cowardice dressed up as restraint.

ICE Is Not a Staffing Problem

The source mentions Democrats funding ICE. That should end the conversation about reform theater. ICE is not redeemed by training, oversight, or a few expulsions of especially visible abusers. It is an enforcement apparatus built to terrify, detain, and normalize state cruelty. If Democrats want to claim democratic legitimacy while bankrolling that machinery, they are not offering a better version of the same system. They are underwriting it.

A democratic coalition cannot pretend the problem is merely managerial when the institution itself is the problem.

Power Talks, Process Hides

The Josh Turek example in the source is useful because it shows how Democrats launder paralysis through ceremonial language. “Guardrails.” “Prevent this from happening again.” “Move on.” These phrases are politically convenient because they sound responsible while evading the one word voters actually want: yes. Yes to oversight. Yes to subpoenas. Yes to consequences.

That evasiveness matters because it tells voters exactly what to expect. When Democrats behave as though oversight is rude, they teach people that elections are performance, not enforcement.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

This is larger than one race or one interview. It is the recurring Democratic habit of treating accountability as a tactical inconvenience instead of a governing obligation. That habit creates the vacuum authoritarian politics feeds on. If abuses never produce consequences, then corruption becomes a weather system: always present, never attributable, and therefore never punished.

The article is right about one thing and too timid about another. Oversight is not revenge. It is the minimum function of a legislature in a damaged democracy. If Democrats win and still decide to “move on,” they will not be offering healing. They will be confirming that power in Washington remains protected by the people elected to challenge it.
