Trump's attack dog who accused Fed official of fraud has parents who did same thing

By Paulo Santos

A home listed as purchased by Mark and Julie Pulte, on a quiet cul-de-sac in Bloomfield Hills, Michigan, U.S., September 4, 2025. REUTERS/Rebecca Cook

President Donald Trump's Federal Housing Finance Agency director Bill Pulte has emerged as an "attack dog" who has filed a series of complaints against various politicians and public servants Trump opposes, accusing them of mortgage fraud. His latest target is Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, whom he claims took "primary residence" status on two different properties at the same time — and Trump's Justice Department is now pursuing a criminal investigation of the matter.

A new investigation by Reuters reveals that Pulte's father and stepmother are doing the exact same thing.

"Mark and Julie Pulte, the father and stepmother of Bill Pulte, President Donald Trump’s appointee as director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, since 2020 have claimed so-called 'homestead exemptions' for residences in wealthy neighborhoods in both Michigan and Florida, according to the records," reported Marisa Taylor, Chris Prentice, and Mike Colias. "The exemption is meant to give a discount to homeowners on taxes for properties they use as their primary residence."

Furthermore, when Reuters started asking questions, the Pultes' tax breaks in Michigan were abruptly revoked.

"Local tax officials in both states told Reuters that claiming more than one home as a primary residence isn’t generally allowed in their jurisdictions and could be punishable by fines or back taxes," said the report. "After Reuters contacted tax officials in Bloomfield Township, Michigan, to inquire about the dual claims, Darrin Kraatz, director of assessing, on Thursday said the township 'as of today' would revoke the exemption on the Pultes’ residence there."

This isn't the first time Pulte, who has more broadly taken an aggressive stance against the Fed, has faced such scrutiny. Last month, reporting revealed Pulte's wife donated half a million dollars to a super PAC backing Trump, routed improperly through a Delaware shell company — although the Federal Election Commission did not find a criminal offense when it investigated.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Power: The Pulte Paradox

In a twist that could only emerge from the convoluted corridors of political power plays, Bill Pulte, President Donald Trump's appointee as director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, has been wielding his authority like a weapon. Pulte, transforming into Trump's proverbial "attack dog," has not shied away from launching complaints against the President's adversaries, accusing them of mortgage fraud. Yet, a stunning revelation by Reuters uncovers a glaring hypocrisy at the heart of the Pulte family itself.

A Case of Do as I Say, Not as I Do

The crux of the issue lies in the allegation against Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, accused by Pulte of claiming "primary residence" status on two different properties simultaneously. This act, according to Pulte's aggressive stance, warrants a criminal investigation by Trump's Justice Department. However, what unfolds next is a narrative so rich in irony, it borders on farce.

Mark and Julie Pulte, the father and stepmother of Bill Pulte, have been caught in the act of committing the very fraud their son vehemently opposes. Since 2020, they have claimed homestead exemptions—a tax break meant for homeowners on their primary residence—in both Michigan and Florida. This maneuver, aside from being evidently duplicitous, is not generally permitted in either jurisdiction and could lead to fines or back taxes.

The Sudden Revocation and the Shell Company Saga

The plot thickens as Reuters' investigation prompts the abrupt revocation of the Pultes' tax breaks in Michigan, showcasing a reactive rather than proactive governance system. Moreover, the investigation uncovers another layer of the Pulte family's entanglement in shady financial dealings. Last month, it was revealed that Pulte's wife funneled half a million dollars to a super PAC backing Trump via a Delaware shell company—a maneuver that, while not deemed criminally offensive by the Federal Election Commission, raises significant ethical red flags.

Conclusion: The Pervasive Stench of Hypocrisy

In this twisted tale of political maneuvering and personal gain, the hypocrisy is palpable. Bill Pulte's aggressive crusade against supposed mortgage fraudsters stands in stark contrast to his own family's actions. This saga not only undermines the credibility of his vendetta but also highlights a broader issue within the current political landscape: a disturbing pattern of do as I say, not as I do.

The Pulte paradox serves as a potent reminder of the systemic errors plaguing our governance. When those in power exhibit such blatant disregard for the rules they purport to uphold, it erodes public trust and undermines the very fabric of our democratic institutions. In the end, the real casualty is the integrity of our political system—a price too steep for any nation to bear lightly.
