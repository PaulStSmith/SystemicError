'Fear of retribution spreading': Experts warn of creeping authoritarianism under Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 23, 20256 min read

Many political observers and activists have been arguing that the American democracy is heading in a wrong direction under President Donald Trump's administration. An article in NPR published Tuesday quoted a number of American university professors, who warned that fear of government retribution is now spreading through society.

The article featured a key survey called Bright Line Watch, in which professors in the United States evaluated the state of American democracy on a scale from zero (total dictatorship) to 100 (ideal democracy). Following Trump's election in November, academics rated American democracy at 67. However, just a few weeks into Trump's second term, that rating dropped significantly to 55.

Many professors interviewed by NPR expressed concerns over the president's attempts to expand executive power.

John Carey, a professor of government at Dartmouth and co-director of Bright Line Watch, said, "There's certainly consensus: We're moving in the wrong direction."

Steven Levitsky, a professor of government at Harvard who co-authored the book How Democracies Die, said the U.S. has already slid into some form of authoritarianism. "It is relatively mild compared to some others. It is certainly reversible, but we are no longer living in a liberal democracy," he said.

Levitsky suggested another method for assessing authoritarianism: examining if opposing the government publicly carries repercussions. He argues that, during Trump's presidency, it does. An example of this is Trump's executive orders that prevent lawyers from firms he disapproves of from accessing government buildings and representing contractors for the government.

Kim Lane Scheppele, a Princeton sociologist who has studied Hungary for years, said the U.S. is "on a very fast slide into what's called competitive authoritarianism."

The NPR article further mentions that a researcher interviewed requested not to be named because he feared the Trump administration might retaliate by cutting funding for his research projects.

Meanwhile, in an article published in The New Republic on Tuesday, senior journalist and author Alexander Stille echoed this sentiment, saying that Trump resembles Italian fascist dictator Benito Mussolini.

Like Mussolini’s dictatorship, the writer says, Trump's style of governances "involves a desire for total dominance and an increasingly unhinged delusion of omnipotence: hence his repeated threats to take over Canada and invade Greenland; to turn Gaza into an American beach resort."

"Mussolini, like Trump, had a keen instinctive animal cunning that helped him intuit the public mood and vanquish his domestic political opponents. He was a brilliant demagogue who could electrify the crowd and who shrewdly understood and exploited his domestic opponents’ weaknesses," Stille added.

"Like Trump, Mussolini was an ardent protectionist, adopting a policy of 'autarky,' demanding that Italy should consume only products made in Italy. The policy was initially a peevish response to the boycott that other nations leveled at Italy following its unprovoked invasion of Ethiopia. But Mussolini elevated the practice to a central feature of Italian economic policy, even though it did little to improve the standard of living for Italians," he wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Slippery Slope of American Democracy Under Trump: A Critical Analysis

The narrative that American democracy is in peril is not new, but under President Donald Trump's administration, this warning has taken on a dire and palpable urgency. An article from NPR paints a grim picture of the current state of U.S. governance, bolstered by insights from academia and juxtaposed with historical parallels that should alarm any advocate of democratic principles. Let's dissect the key elements and implications of this discourse, which, frankly, reads like a prelude to an authoritarian dystopia.

The Diminishing Glow of Democracy

The Bright Line Watch survey serves as a damning indictment of the health of American democracy, falling from a mediocre 67 to a concerning 55 in the wake of Trump's re-election. This decline is more than a number; it's a reflection of a systemic erosion of democratic norms and values. The consensus among scholars that "We're moving in the wrong direction" is not just academic hand-wringing; it's a red flag signaling the dismantling of democratic safeguards.

Trump's Authoritarian Bent: A Reality Check

Steven Levitsky's assertion that the U.S. has "slid into some form of authoritarianism" is as bold as it is distressing. Levitsky, a noted scholar on the death of democracies, doesn't mince words, suggesting that the repercussions for opposing the government publicly under Trump's regime are indicative of authoritarian rule. The examples of executive overreach, such as barring lawyers from firms disfavored by Trump from government buildings, are manifestations of a government that views dissent as a threat to be quashed, rather than a necessary element of a healthy democracy.

The Shadow of Competitive Authoritarianism

Kim Lane Scheppele's comparison of the U.S.'s trajectory to "competitive authoritarianism" is particularly chilling. This term, often associated with regimes that maintain a veneer of democracy while systematically undermining it, seems increasingly apt. The fear of retribution that silences researchers and stifles academic freedom is a hallmark of a regime that has lost any moral compass it might have claimed to possess.

Historical Parallels: Trump and Mussolini

The comparison between Trump and Mussolini, drawn by Alexander Stille, is not just provocative; it's a cautionary tale. Both figures utilized a mix of populist rhetoric, disdain for democratic norms, and aggressive nationalism to consolidate power. Trump's fantastical musings about invading Greenland or transforming Gaza into an American beach resort are not just eccentric; they're symptomatic of a leader who views governance as an extension of personal whim, rather than a responsibility to the people.

The Cult of Personality and Protectionism

Like Mussolini, Trump's governance style is deeply intertwined with a cult of personality and protectionist policies. The policy of 'autarky,' as adopted by Mussolini, finds echoes in Trump's "America First" doctrine. However, history teaches us that such policies often lead to economic insularity and decline rather than prosperity.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

The NPR article and the sentiments echoed by scholars and journalists serve as a wake-up call to the dangers of complacency in the face of authoritarian tendencies. The descent into authoritarianism is often gradual, and by the time the alarm bells are ringing, the damage may be too difficult to reverse. The parallels to Mussolini's Italy should not be seen as hyperbolic but as a stark warning: the path towards autarky and the stifling of dissent is a path away from democracy.

In the face of these alarming trends, silence and passivity are tantamount to complicity. It's incumbent upon every advocate of democracy to recognize these warning signs and act to safeguard the democratic institutions and values that are under threat. The trajectory towards authoritarianism is not irreversible, but reversing course requires a concerted and vigilant effort from all sectors of society.
