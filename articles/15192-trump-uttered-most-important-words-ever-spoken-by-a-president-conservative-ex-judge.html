Trump uttered 'most important words ever spoken' by a president: conservative ex-judge

Conservative former Judge J. Michael Luttig (Photo: Screen capture via MSNBC video)

Conservative former Judge Michael Luttig pointed to the recent interview President Donald Trump did and called it possibly the "most important" statement made by a U.S. president.

The comment was part of Trump's interview with NBC News' Kristen Welker that aired Sunday. The questions involved the deported migrants sent to a prison in El Salvador and whether they received the due process required under the Fifth Amendment of the Constitution.

Trump said he didn't know. She then asked Trump if he believed he must uphold the Constitution, and again, he repeated that he was unsure.

“I don’t know. I have to respond by saying, again, I have brilliant lawyers that work for me, and they are going to obviously follow what the Supreme Court said. What you said is not what I heard the Supreme Court say. They have a different interpretation," Trump said.

On Monday, Luttig told MSNBC's Nicolle Wallace that Trump's comments "are perhaps the most important words ever spoken by a president of the United States." He added that the story is "one of the most important stories of our times."

"The president always says what's on his mind, with almost perfect clarity of what is on his mind. Now, sometimes what's on his mind is confusing, but the words that he chooses are perfectly clear as to his confusion," Luttig said.

"Now, the temptation here is to dismiss the president's words as just another gaffe, of which he makes many. But I don't think that we should do that," he explained. "I'm quite confident that the president was saying what is on his mind, and that is that he, the president of the United States, doesn't necessarily believe that he is obligated to uphold the Constitution of the United States, as it is interpreted by the Supreme Court."

He described a school of thought called "constitutional denialism," something he's followed and attempted to understand for most of his career.

"And that strain and school of constitutional thought believes that the Supreme Court's interpretation of the Constitution is not necessarily what the Constitution says or means," Luttig said.

Those who believe in that interpretation think that if the Supreme Court wrongly interprets the Constitution, then the president has "no obligation whatsoever to follow the Supreme Court's decisions and orders," the judge explained.

Luttig said he believes this is one of, if not the "single most important issue of our time," because the president must "tell the American people exactly what he does believe about his constitutional obligation to uphold the Constitution of the United States."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Constitutional Clarity, or Lack Thereof

In a recent interview that could only be described as a political earthquake, former President Donald Trump delivered statements that have sparked a firestorm of controversy and concern. Speaking with NBC News' Kristen Welker, Trump's ambiguity on his understanding and commitment to the U.S. Constitution has been laid bare for the nation to dissect. Conservative former Judge Michael Luttig's reaction to this interview underscores the gravity of Trump's words, branding them as "perhaps the most important words ever spoken by a president of the United States."

The Shocking Indifference to Constitutional Responsibility

When questioned about the due process for deported migrants sent to a prison in El Salvador—a fundamental right under the Fifth Amendment—Trump's response was a bewildering "I don’t know." This alone should raise alarms, but his further assertion of uncertainty about whether he must uphold the Constitution sends us into uncharted political waters. Trump’s reliance on his "brilliant lawyers" and their interpretation of Supreme Court rulings is a cop-out that undermines the very essence of his oath of office.

Luttig's Unsettling Analysis

Judge Luttig’s analysis of Trump's comments sheds light on a dangerous philosophy: constitutional denialism. This concept, as Luttig describes, rejects the authority of the Supreme Court's interpretations of the Constitution, suggesting that if the president disagrees with these interpretations, he is under no obligation to follow them. This radical idea strikes at the heart of the United States' constitutional framework, which relies on the separation of powers and the checks and balances among the branches of government.

The Implications Are Staggering

What we're witnessing is not just another political gaffe to be brushed aside or laughed off. It's a former president—and potential future candidate—publicly questioning the foundational principles that bind and guide the nation. If the adherence to the Constitution becomes a matter of personal interpretation or convenience for the highest office in the land, where does that leave the rule of law? Where does it leave the American people?

Luttig’s assertion that this issue is one of, if not the, "single most important issue of our time" is no exaggeration. The implications of Trump's stance are far-reaching, potentially setting a precedent that could erode public trust in the Constitution and the offices sworn to uphold it.

The Urgent Need for Clarity

Trump owes the American public a clear, unequivocal statement on his views regarding the Constitution. Not through the filter of his legal team or the selective interpretation of Supreme Court decisions, but a straightforward commitment to uphold the framework that has guided the United States since its inception. Anything less is a disservice to the office he once held and may seek again.

In essence, Trump's constitutional ambiguity is a wake-up call. It's a stark reminder of the importance of constitutional literacy and commitment among our leaders. Our democracy relies not just on the written words of the Constitution, but on the unwavering commitment to its principles by those in power. Anything less undermines the very fabric of our nation.
