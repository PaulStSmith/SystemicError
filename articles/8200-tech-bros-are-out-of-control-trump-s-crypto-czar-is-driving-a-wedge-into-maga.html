'Tech bros are out of control!' Trump's crypto czar is driving a wedge into MAGA

Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia, and Elon Musk stand with U.S. President Donald Trump during the U.S.-Saudi Investment Forum in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 19, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

President Donald Trump’s crypto czar, South African venture capitalist David Sacks, has pushed the Trump administration to enact several policies that have enriched his Silicon Valley friends – and potentially himself – driving a wedge in MAGA and “alarming” White House officials, The New York Times reported Sunday.

“The tech bros are out of control,” said Steve Bannon, Trump’s former White House chief of staff and MAGA influencer, The Times reported. “They are leading the White House down the road to perdition with this ascendant technocratic oligarchy.”

Sacks is denoted as a special government employee in the Trump administration, meaning he isn’t paid for his government work. Nevertheless, Sacks has made several recommendations that have paid off handsomely for Silicon Valley, and despite warnings from White House officials that some may pose national security risks.

One such recommendation was Sacks’ push for the United States to be able to sell Nvidia chips abroad, a push that ultimately led to a deal to sell 500,000 American-made artificial intelligence chips – the majority of them from Nvidia – to the United Arab Emirates. The deal was approved by Trump, despite it having “alarmed some White House officials” over national security concerns that China may gain access to the technology.

Sacks’ work in the Trump administration has also bolstered his personal standing. The “All-In” podcast, which Sacks hosts, has exploded in popularity since his time in office. The “All-In” podcast saw sales for its annual conference increase from around $6 million to $21 million this year, and in June, the podcast launched its own “All-In”-branded tequila, priced at $1,200 a bottle.

In its official capacity, however, the White House fired back at the Times for its reporting, with White House spokesperson Liz Huston calling Sacks “an invaluable asset” for Trump in a statement to the outlet, and Jessica Hoffman, a spokesperson for Sacks, labeling the “conflict of interest narrative” as “false.”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at the Crypto Czar’s Controversial Influence

In an era where the lines between politics, personal gain, and technological advancement blur, the case of David Sacks, President Donald Trump’s so-called crypto czar, is not just troubling—it’s a glaring example of the technocratic oligarchy that seems to be tightening its grip on American political and economic life. As reported by The New York Times, Sacks’ maneuvering within the Trump administration highlights a disturbing trend of policy manipulation for personal and peer enrichment, raising serious questions about national security and the integrity of governance.

The Silicon Valley Golden Boy

David Sacks, a South African venture capitalist, has ostensibly leveraged his position within the Trump administration to further the interests of his Silicon Valley circle, possibly including his own. Notably, Sacks operates as a special government employee, a status that conveniently exempts him from being financially compensated for his governmental role, perhaps offering a veneer of altruism to his activities. Yet, the outcomes of his recommendations tell a different story—a tale of significant financial boon for the tech elite.

The Deal that Set Off Alarms

One of Sacks’ standout maneuvers involved advocating for the U.S. to sell Nvidia chips abroad, culminating in a lucrative deal with the United Arab Emirates for 500,000 American-made artificial intelligence chips, predominantly from Nvidia. This deal, approved by Trump, ostensibly ignored the ringing alarms from White House officials concerned about national security implications, particularly the risk of China gaining access to advanced technology. Herein lies a stark illustration of the conflict between corporate gain and national interest—a conflict seemingly disregarded by those steering the ship.

Podcasts and Tequila: The Spoils of Influence

Sacks’ influence seems to extend beyond policy into personal branding and profit. His podcast, “All-In,” has seen a meteoric rise in popularity and profitability during his tenure in the Trump administration, with sales for its annual conference skyrocketing and the launch of a branded tequila priced at a staggering $1,200 a bottle. This blend of politics, personal brand elevation, and high-end entrepreneurship underscores a concerning trend of public office being used as a lever for private benefit.

Official Denials and the Conflict of Interest Narrative

The White House and representatives for Sacks have predictably fired back against the allegations, with White House spokesperson Liz Huston dubbing Sacks “an invaluable asset” and Sacks’ spokesperson dismissing the conflict of interest claims as “false.” However, the details reported paint a picture that’s difficult to dismiss—a scenario where Silicon Valley’s tech bros, with Sacks as a leading figure, wield disproportionate influence over national policy for dubious benefit to the public interest.

In Conclusion: A Road to Perdition?

Steve Bannon’s characterization of the situation as “the tech bros are out of control” and his warning of a path “down the road to perdition with this ascendant technocratic oligarchy” might come off as hyperbolic coming from a figure as controversial as Bannon himself. Yet, the essence of his critique hits uncomfortably close to home. The saga of David Sacks is emblematic of a broader crisis in American democracy, where the confluence of technology, capital, and politics threatens to erode the foundational principles of governance for the people, by the people.

As we navigate this complex and ever-evolving landscape, it is imperative that we remain vigilant, questioning, and critical of the forces that seek to shape our world under the guise of innovation and progress. The story of the crypto czar is not just a cautionary tale; it’s a call to action for transparency, accountability, and a reevaluation of whom our leaders truly serve.
