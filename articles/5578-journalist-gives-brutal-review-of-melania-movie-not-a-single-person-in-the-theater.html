A Journalist gave a brutal review of the new Melania documentary, which has been criticized by those who say it won’t make back the huge fees spent to make it, saying there was “not a single person in the theater.”

Waxman said Friday that she “wandered” into a Melania showing, and didn’t like what she found there.

“Friends, I wandered into a ‘Melania’ screening in Culver tonight after seeing ‘The Secret Agent.’ Not single person in the theater. Watched 10 minutes. I found myself with tears streaming down my face,” she wrote on X. But those weren’t tears from a moving documentary, she noted.

“Watching Trump take the oath to defend the Constitution, as he tears it down. The dissonance of Melania saying her husband would be a ‘unifier,’ and all the pomp and circumstance for someone so venal,” she wrote on X. “So beneath the great office - and wishing we could turn the clock back.”

Our Analysis:

The Melania Documentary: A Brutile Examination

The new Melania documentary, which has stirred a cauldron of criticism, particularly for its financial imprudence, recently came under the scathing scrutiny of journalist Sharon Waxman. The financial fiasco surrounding this documentary underscores a broader problem of misallocated resources in the entertainment industry, especially when such projects fail to resonate with the audience.

Empty Theaters and Unmet Expectations

Waxman’s experience at a Melania screening in Culver is a telling indicator of the documentary’s reception—or the stark lack thereof. Her account of an utterly deserted theater not only speaks volumes about the public’s disinterest but also highlights a glaring miscalculation by the producers. The imagery of an empty theater is a powerful testament to the documentary’s failure to captivate or even draw in an audience. This scenario begs the question: who exactly is the target audience for this documentary? Clearly, not even the novelty of a behind-the-scenes look at the former First Lady could entice viewers.

The Coercion Controversy

Adding a layer of controversy to the already troubled waters is the claim made by the Military Religious Freedom Foundation. The allegation that thousands of active-duty military personnel may have been coerced into attending screenings adds a disturbing dimension to the documentary’s saga. If true, it reflects a gross misuse of authority and an unethical attempt to inflate viewer numbers through coercion. This manipulation of service members, if substantiated, deserves unequivocal condemnation and demands accountability.

Waxman’s Emotional Turmoil

Waxman’s brief but poignant account of her experience watching the documentary sheds light on the emotional dissonance it evoked. Her tears were not of admiration or nostalgia but of frustration and sorrow. Watching a film that glorifies figures she perceives as having undermined the very essence of American democracy elicited a visceral response. The mention of tears streaming down her face as she watched a portrayal of Trump swearing to defend the Constitution—a moment she views as deeply ironic—captures the emotional conflict that many Americans feel about this period in the country’s history.

Melania’s Portrayal as a “Unifier”

The documentary’s attempt to paint Melania Trump as a unifying figure is, according to Waxman, laughably incongruent with reality. The former First Lady’s public image and her role in the Trump administration have been sources of division and controversy. This portrayal not only clashes with the public’s perception but also raises questions about the documentary’s objective and narrative integrity. It seems to be an exercise in revisionism, trying to cast a veneer of unity and positivity over a tenure marked by polarization.

Conclusion

The Melania documentary, as depicted through Waxman’s eyes, emerges as a misguided venture—a cinematic endeavor that neither finds its audience nor justifies its creation. The empty seats at its screening symbolize a broader disinterest and disconnection with the project’s subject matter. Meanwhile, the controversy of coerced military viewership, if verified, adds a layer of ethical breach to an already questionable project. In the end, the documentary serves as a cautionary tale about the pitfalls of misjudging public interest and the perils of attempting to whitewash complex legacies.
