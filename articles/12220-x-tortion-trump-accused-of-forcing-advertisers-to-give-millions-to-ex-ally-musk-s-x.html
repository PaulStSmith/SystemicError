'X-tortion': Trump accused of forcing advertisers to give millions to ex-ally Musk's X

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 31, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Elon Musk receives the key to the White House from U.S. President Donald Trump during a press conference in the Oval Office at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

The Trump administration is weaponizing federal agencies to prop up right-wing media in an unprecedented assault on free speech — with the Federal Trade Commission now forcing advertisers to return to Elon Musk's X platform through a controversial merger condition, an expert wrote Wednesday.

In a stunning break from traditional practice, the FTC announced it would only approve a massive ad agency merger if the combined company agrees to never "refuse to place ads on websites for political reasons," journalist Julia Angwin wrote for the New York Times.

The move represents a dramatic expansion of government power, transforming an agency focused on consumer protection into a tool for media manipulation.

"While the move would theoretically affect platforms of any political persuasion, there’s little doubt that it is a thinly veiled attempt to prop up X," Angwin wrote.

She claimed Trump was stepping in to save his former ally Elon Musk's company from an advertising death spiral. The platform, formerly Twitter, hemorrhaged advertisers after Musk's takeover unleashed a torrent of antisemitism and conspiracy theories. In 2023, dozens of major brands fled after watchdog groups exposed how their ads were appearing alongside pro-Nazi content.

X's response? Sue the messenger. The company launched legal attacks against Media Matters, the Center for Countering Digital Hate, and multiple advertisers, claiming they were "illegally boycotting" the platform.

Now Trump's FTC is investigating these same watchdog groups for alleged "conspiracy" — a chilling escalation that has Media Matters considering shuttering operations due to crushing legal costs.

"The agency's conditions for approving the ad merger are blatantly inconsistent with the First Amendment right of advertisers not to associate their brands with content or viewpoints that they know consumers find objectionable," Fordham University law professor Olivier Sylvain told reporters.

The implications are staggering. Advertising is a trillion-dollar industry that determines which media survives online. By controlling where ads flow, Trump is essentially controlling the media landscape itself. Marketing firm Forrester has already advised clients in an article titled "X-tortion: How Advertisers Are Losing Control of Media Choice" to buy X ads simply to avoid government retaliation.

This represents a fundamental threat to press freedom. The administration is simultaneously punishing mainstream outlets like the Associated Press, defunding public media, and filing defamation suits against critical coverage while forcing advertisers to fund conservative platforms.

The strategy could channel millions toward Trump's Truth Social and favored right-wing outlets while starving adversarial media of revenue. It's a sophisticated scheme to reshape America's information ecosystem through government coercion — a tactic more befitting authoritarian regimes than democratic societies.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Weaponization of Federal Agencies under Trump

The recent maneuver by the Trump administration to weaponize federal agencies in favor of right-wing media outlets marks a disturbing pivot away from democratic norms towards an overt consolidation of power through media manipulation. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC), traditionally a watchdog for consumer protection, is now being repurposed as a tool to force advertisers back onto Elon Musk's X platform, as detailed by journalist Julia Angwin. This move not only highlights a blatant misuse of government power but also underscores the administration's intent to undermine the principles of free speech and press freedom.

A Thinly Veiled Attempt to Prop Up X

The FTC's conditions to approve a massive ad agency merger—demanding the combined entity not refuse ad placements on websites for political reasons—strikes at the heart of the First Amendment. Ostensibly, this directive applies to all platforms regardless of political affiliation. However, as Angwin astutely points out, it's a clear lifeline to X (formerly Twitter), which has seen a mass exodus of advertisers due to its association with antisemitism and pro-Nazi content under Musk's leadership. By suing entities like Media Matters and the Center for Countering Digital Hate for exposing these affiliations, X's aggressive legal stance, backed by Trump's FTC, constitutes a direct assault on watchdog groups and the vital role they play in maintaining accountability.

The Chilling Implications of Government Overreach

The implications of the FTC's actions extend far beyond a mere regulatory decision; they represent a fundamental threat to press freedom and the right of advertisers to align their brands with their values. By forcing advertisers to support a platform that has become a hotbed for hate speech and conspiracy theories, the Trump administration is not just saving a failing ally but is also attempting to control the media landscape itself.

Fordham University law professor Olivier Sylvain's comments highlight the contradiction between the FTC's conditions and the First Amendment rights of advertisers. This move is not about ensuring a fair and open marketplace for ideas but about silencing opposition and bolstering supporters through financial coercion.

A Scheme to Reshape America's Information Ecosystem

The broader strategy of the Trump administration, to defund public media, file defamation suits against critics, and force financial support towards conservative outlets, is a sophisticated scheme to reshape the information ecosystem in America. The administration's actions parallel tactics employed by authoritarian regimes, using government power to control the flow of information and suppress dissent.

By advising clients to buy X ads to avoid government retaliation, marketing firm Forrester's article "X-tortion: How Advertisers Are Losing Control of Media Choice" encapsulates the dire situation for advertisers caught in this political crossfire. This not only undermines the autonomy of businesses to make ethical advertising choices but also sets a dangerous precedent for government intervention in the media and advertising industries.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's assault on free speech and press freedom through the weaponization of federal agencies is a clear and present danger to democracy. By coercing advertisers to support specific platforms and media outlets, the administration is attempting to control the narrative, suppress dissent, and bolster its allies. This tactic, more befitting authoritarian regimes, must be recognized and challenged to preserve the integrity of America's democratic institutions and the fundamental rights enshrined in the Constitution.
