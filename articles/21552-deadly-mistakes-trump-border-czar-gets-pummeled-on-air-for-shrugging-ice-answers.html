'Deadly mistakes': Trump border czar gets pummeled on-air for shrugging ICE answers

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A Czar Who Knows NothingThe Source of PowerMisdirection as MethodThe Lie of “Mistakes”ICE Cannot Be PolishedThe Pattern Behind the Pageant

The Violence Is the Policy

A Czar Who Knows Nothing

Tom Homan is not some outside commentator yelling at the edges of power. He is the White House border czar inside Trump’s deportation apparatus, the man meant to embody control. So when he cannot answer whether ICE agents in Texas and Maine were put on administrative leave after killing two fathers, the problem is not trivia. It is proof of what this operation is built to tolerate: force first, accountability later, if ever.

The Source of Power

The actual institutional power here sits with the Trump administration and the federal immigration machinery it commands. ICE agents carried out the shootings. Homan oversees the broader deportation project. That makes his ignorance politically meaningful, not accidental. An office that can move armed federal officers against civilians can also track what those officers did afterward. If it cannot or will not, that is not a gap in messaging. It is a governing choice.

Misdirection as Method

The article’s framing tries to turn deadly state violence into a question of individual competence: Did this agent pass vetting? Were they placed on leave? Were mistakes made? That narrows the frame in exactly the wrong direction. The public is being invited to look at paperwork failures and personal defects while the institution that produced two fatal shootings stays intact. A bad hire is not the story. An armed deportation system killing unarmed fathers is the story.

The Lie of “Mistakes”

Homan’s refusal to say “mistakes were made” is revealing. He is not protecting accuracy. He is protecting the state’s prerogative to commit harm without naming it. When one fatal shooting is tied to a disproved FBI drug claim and another to an agent with reported abuse allegations, the pattern is not confusion. It is an enforcement culture that treats vulnerable people as disposable and then scrambles for after-the-fact justification.

ICE Cannot Be Polished

This is not a training problem. It is not an oversight problem. It is not fixed by better vetting, more leave policies, or a new communications strategy. ICE exists to terrorize, detain, and expel under color of law, and these deaths are what that mission looks like when the camera catches the blood. The only honest democratic position is abolition. Anything less accepts the premise that a federal deportation force can keep killing and still be managed into respectability.

The Pattern Behind the Pageant

What this story really exposes is how authoritarian systems launder violence through bureaucracy. The uniforms stay crisp, the spokesperson shrugs, and the dead are reduced to administrative footnotes. Power is not absent here. It is fully present, speaking in evasions, counting on confusion, and betting that public outrage will settle for embarrassment instead of demanding the end of the machine itself.
