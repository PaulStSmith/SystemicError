Trump just put a 'spotlight' on his 'panic' that he'll lose 'death grip' on power: writer

Donald Trump just put a "spotlight" on his own fear that his empire is crumbling, according to a New York Times columnist.

NYT opinion columnist Michelle Cottle expressed the statement in a piece from three opinion writers "on Trump’s most recent power grabs."

Speaking to fellow NYT writers Jamelle Bouie and Steven Rattner, Cottle started out the conversation by saying, "The White House wrecking ball just keeps on swinging this summer as President Trump pursues his passion for undermining key American institutions."

"Just the past couple of weeks, we’ve seen the White House fire the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the agency responsible for providing unbiased info on the labor market, because the president was displeased with a jobs report," she added. "Meanwhile, and this is my personal obsession, Republican state lawmakers in Texas, at Trump’s command, redrew the state’s congressional map to give the G.O.P. five more House seats."

Cottle added, "Practically speaking, these moves don’t seem to have much to do with each other, but they both spotlight just how far this president will go to destroy public trust in vital institutions. And that is what I want to talk about today."

On Trump firing a jobs official after a disappointing report, Bouie said, "You can change the numbers they report to make you look better, but that doesn’t change the underlying reality of what’s happening in the economy or the underlying reality of what’s happening in anything if you’re going to just change the numbers — to juke the stats, if you will."

"And so the president can put pressure on the nation’s statisticians to make him look good, but if the underlying conditions are actually on the downturn, if things are actually getting worse for people, then the only thing he’s done is made it more difficult for his government to respond to whatever is bubbling up from the surface," Bouie then added.

That led Cottle to say, "That’s what I was going to ask you both: This move by Trump sort of spotlights his panic about what’s happening, certainly how it will impact his party’s fortunes and whether he keeps a death grip on the government going forward."

"I think our assumption has always been that no matter what the numbers say, if people start to feel some pain, it’s going to come back and then you will start to see some pushback," she added before asking, "Do you think that the tariffs and what we’ve got coming and the softening job numbers are the beginning of what Trump has been worried about? Or at least what his party has been worried about in terms of people actually being able to see what’s going on?"

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the "Spotlight" on Trump's Alleged Fear

In the labyrinth of American politics, former President Donald Trump's actions have once again ignited a firestorm of scrutiny. A recent New York Times opinion piece by Michelle Cottle, featuring insights from Jamelle Bouie and Steven Rattner, provides a compelling dissection of Trump's perceived desperation to maintain his crumbling empire. The article, while rich in its critique, stops just short of calling out the elephant in the room: the audacity of power grabs in broad daylight and the brazen undermining of American institutions.

The Wrecking Ball in the White House

Cottle's depiction of Trump as a "White House wrecking ball" is not just metaphorical flourish; it's a stark reality of the havoc wrought on key American institutions. The firing of the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics over displeasure with a jobs report is emblematic of Trump's disdain for inconvenient truths. This action is not just a personal vendetta—it's a strategic move to erode public trust in an institution that is foundational to economic transparency and accountability.

The Texas Redrawing: A Case Study in Manipulation

The redrawing of Texas's congressional map, as per Cottle's "personal obsession," is a glaring example of how Trump's influence extends beyond federal reach, infecting state politics with the virus of manipulation. Granting the GOP five more House seats is not just gerrymandering; it's a calculated assault on democratic principles. The silence on this issue is deafening, and Cottle's spotlight on it is both necessary and commendable.

The Illusion of Prosperity

Bouie's comments on the pressure Trump puts on statisticians to "juke the stats" reveals a disturbing trend of fabricating prosperity. Changing numbers to paint a rosy picture of the economy does nothing to address the underlying issues plaguing American society. It's a smokescreen, a dangerous game of deceit that has real consequences for governance and public policy.

The Underlying Panic

Cottle's interpretation of these actions as a "spotlight" on Trump's panic is insightful. The fear of losing grip on power, the dread of impending economic downturns, and the tariff-induced anxieties are not just Trump's personal nightmares—they are reflective of a broader Republican dread. The discussion around tariffs and softening job numbers opens up a Pandora's box of economic realities that Trump and his party are desperate to keep closed.

Conclusion: The Crumbling Empire

The article, while incisive, perhaps pulls its punches in the final analysis. Trump's efforts to undermine public trust and manipulate democratic institutions are not just signs of an empire in decline; they are symptoms of a deeper malaise within American politics—a malaise characterized by the erosion of truth, the commodification of democracy, and the naked pursuit of power at all costs.

In a political landscape marred by division and deceit, the spotlight on Trump's fear and desperation serves as a critical reminder of the stakes involved. The fight for the soul of American democracy is far from over, and articles like Cottle's are essential in keeping the public informed, engaged, and enraged. The audacity of Trump's power grabs must not be normalized; they must be challenged, critiqued, and condemned with all the ferocity and clarity at our disposal.
