Unholy War: Trump’s Religious Rhetoric as a Tool for Political Power

Weaponizing Faith for Political Gain

At a White House Easter event, Donald Trump’s spiritual advisor elevated him to a messianic status, equating his political challenges with the sufferings of Jesus Christ. This isn’t just poor theology; it’s a calculated move to harness religious fervor for political support. The conflation of spiritual salvation with Trump’s political agenda manipulates faith for authoritarian ends. This strategy isn’t merely about rallying the base—it’s about sanctifying the political narrative, making it unassailable by earthly standards of logic or ethics.

The Echoes of Religious War

The rhetoric surrounding the escalating tensions with Iran is alarmingly religious. Trump’s threats to destroy Iranian civilization and his Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s biblical framing of military operations are not just hyperbolic; they are dangerously irresponsible. By invoking divine sanction for political decisions, this administration blurs the lines between secular policy and holy war. The narrative being crafted isn’t just about national security—it’s about cosmic battle between good and evil, where dissent is not just unpatriotic, it’s sacrilege.

Institutional Cowardice and Complicity

The silence from many conservative Christians in the face of this blatant misuse of religious language is telling. It reveals a selective morality that is willing to overlook blasphemy and near-genocidal rhetoric for political expediency. This complicity is symptomatic of a broader political pattern where religious identity is exploited to justify or obscure authoritarian practices. This isn’t just hypocrisy; it’s a strategic silence that enables the continuation of dangerous policies.

The Consequences of a Crusade

Trump’s approach—declaring a civilization-scale war under the guise of divine mandate—is not only ethically reprehensible but also strategically disastrous. Historical precedents from the Crusades to modern sectarian conflicts show that wars fought under the banner of religion are especially brutal, protracted, and difficult to resolve. The administration’s stance risks turning a political conflict into a religious crusade, deepening hostilities and making any form of peaceful resolution more complex.

A Global Call for Reason

Pope Leo XIV’s denunciation of the war as a misappropriation of religious faith is a crucial counterpoint to the narrative coming from Washington. By declaring that no one can use Jesus to justify war, the Pope is not just critiquing a policy; he’s challenging a growing trend in global politics where leaders use religious language to mobilize support and demonize opponents. His warning underscores the broader consequences of this strategy—spiritual bankruptcy and a profound moral crisis.

The Risk of Spiritual Bankruptcy

As we navigate these tumultuous times, the misuse of religious rhetoric for political ends not only threatens global peace but also the integrity of faith itself. When leaders like Trump equate their political fortunes with divine will, they are not just rallying their base—they are gambling with the very soul of their nation. The risk, as Pope Leo XIV suggests, is a profound spiritual emptiness, where the sacred is so intertwined with the profane that the distinction becomes meaningless, leaving us in a moral and ethical void.

In conclusion, this isn’t just about the misuse of religious language or the escalation of a conflict; it’s about the soul of American politics and potentially, the future of global peace. When political leaders use faith as a weapon, they do so at a grave cost—not just to their political adversaries but to the very faith they claim to uphold.
