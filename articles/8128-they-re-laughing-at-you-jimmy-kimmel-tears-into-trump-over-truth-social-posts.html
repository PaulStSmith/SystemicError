'They're laughing at you': Jimmy Kimmel tears into Trump over Truth Social posts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 3, 20255 min read

Talk show host Jimmy Kimmel has ripped into a series of Truth Social posts made by Donald Trump.

The 58-year-old comedian would tear down a post made by the president which included his Thanksgiving message and a controversial attack on Minnesota Governor Tim Walz. Speaking in the opening address of Jimmy Kimmel Live, the host suggested one of Trump’s posts in particular had people laughing at him, not at the country.

Trump’s post reads, “A very Happy Thanksgiving salutation to all of our Great American Citizens and Patriots who have been so nice in allowing our Country to be divided, disrupted, carved up, murdered, beaten, mugged, and laughed at...”

Kimmel would take aim at the “laughed at” section of the post, using it as a segue into people mocking the president and his later comments on Truth Social. The talk show host said, “’Murdered, mugged, beaten, and laughed at?’ I hate to be the one to tell you, our country isn’t being laughed at, they’re laughing at you.”

Kimmel would then turn his attention to later in the Truth Social tirade where Trump had called Gov. Walz a leader who “does nothing, either through fear, incompetence, or both”. Kimmel joked this was Trump being “presidential when he wants to be.”

He continued, “I wonder why he didn’t get that Nobel Peace Prize. It makes no sense. Trump posted that little blessing on Thanksgiving and caused quite an uproar, even among some Republicans”. Trump would double down on his claim that Walz was “seriously retarded”, a statement he made as part of his Thanksgiving post.

The president would then tell Air Force One reporters he believed there is “something seriously wrong” with the Governor of Minnesota. Kimmel dubbed Trump “President of the Eighth Grade” following these comments on Walz.

Later in his address, Kimmel would joke the president “can’t read” when it comes to polling numbers. Trump claimed he was doing well in the polls but figures across the board are not on the president’s side.

It was reported earlier this week that Trump was losing ground not just in general polling but in Republican support too. Kimmel said, “According to every major poll, it’s [Trump’s approval rating] the lowest we’ve ever seen since we flushed him out of office the first time.”

“His negative rating is now at sixty percent. There are gas station bathrooms on Yelp with higher approval ratings than Donald Trump right now. We are not in an upwards trajectory.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Kimmel vs. Trump: A Battle of Wit and Wisdom

In the latest episode of the political circus, Jimmy Kimmel has taken the stage to dissect a series of Truth Social posts from none other than Donald Trump. The former president, known for his unorthodox and often controversial social media tirades, served up a Thanksgiving message that was anything but traditional. Kimmel, with his trademark blend of humor and sharp critique, wasted no time in skewering Trump’s post for its bizarre gratitude list that included thanking Americans for allowing the country to be “divided, disrupted, carved up, murdered, beaten, mugged, and laughed at.”

Trump’s “Thanksgiving Message”: A Study in Irony

Trump’s attempt at a Thanksgiving message, as highlighted by Kimmel, reads more like an indictment of the current state of affairs than a message of gratitude. The inclusion of “laughed at” in his list of grievances inadvertently turns the spotlight back on him, as Kimmel astutely points out. It’s hard not to see the irony in Trump’s lamentation about divisiveness and violence, given his own divisive rhetoric throughout his presidency and beyond.

Kimmel’s Counterattack: Wit Over Woe

Kimmel’s approach to Trump’s post—particularly his focus on the “laughed at” part—serves as a reminder of the power of humor to cut through political grandstanding. By suggesting that the laughter is directed not at the country but at Trump himself, Kimmel flips the script on Trump’s narrative of victimhood and division. This moment of levity highlights a significant truth about Trump’s presidency and post-presidency: the more outrageous the statement, the more fodder for comedic gold.

The Attack on Governor Walz

Moving beyond the Thanksgiving message, Kimmel’s analysis of Trump’s attack on Minnesota Governor Tim Walz is equally scathing. Trump’s choice of words—”seriously retarded”—is not only offensive but also indicative of his penchant for middle school insults over substantive political critique. Kimmel’s dubbing of Trump as the “President of the Eighth Grade” is a biting commentary on the maturity level of Trump’s discourse.

Polling Numbers: The Reality Check

Perhaps the most damning part of Kimmel’s critique comes when the attention turns to Trump’s polling numbers. Despite Trump’s claims of popularity and success, the polls tell a different story. Kimmel’s comparison of Trump’s approval ratings to the ratings of gas station bathrooms on Yelp is both hilarious and telling. It underscores the disconnect between Trump’s perception of his standing and the reality as reflected by public opinion.

Conclusion: The Laughter and the Truth

What this episode of Jimmy Kimmel Live illustrates is the ongoing tension between Trump’s portrayal of reality and the facts on the ground. Kimmel’s use of humor to dissect Trump’s posts is not just for laughs; it’s a method of uncovering the absurdity and danger of Trump’s rhetoric. By focusing on the “laughed at” aspect, Kimmel not only provides comic relief but also a pointed critique of how Trump’s leadership style and communication strategy have often led to ridicule rather than respect on the global stage.

In the end, Kimmel’s takedown of Trump’s Truth Social tirades is a stark reminder of the power dynamics at play. It’s a battle between wit and wisdom on one side, and a bewildering lack of self-awareness on the other. As the political landscape continues to evolve, one thing remains clear: when truth becomes stranger than fiction, satire becomes an essential tool for making sense of it all.
