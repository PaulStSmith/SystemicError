You Can Sit With Us — Unless You’re a Fascist

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 28, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Once upon a time, political disagreements were supposed to be about tax brackets, zoning laws, or trade policy. We lived under the illusion that all political ideologies, no matter how vile, were just “differences of opinion.” But that illusion shattered long ago. The people showing up to MAGA rallies aren’t there because of marginal tax rates — they’re there to scream for deportations, deny verified election results, wave Confederate flags, fantasize about civil war, and pledge allegiance to a convicted felon who tried to overthrow democracy. And yet, bafflingly, they still expect to be invited to brunch.

No. Absolutely not.

The viral Sidechat post making waves out of Harvard didn’t emerge from a vacuum, nor was it the hysterical cry of a snowflake. It was a manifesto — clear, deliberate, and necessary. It drew a line in the sand and dared others to acknowledge it. In essence, it said: if you’re going to support fascist ideology, then don’t expect to enjoy the social privileges that come from living in a pluralistic society. Don’t expect party invites, club memberships, dates, job referrals, or birthday wishes. There is no more pretending that we can “agree to disagree” when the disagreement is about who gets to exist safely in this country.

This isn’t cancel culture. It’s cultural immune response. It’s social hygiene — a community recognizing its pathogens and taking necessary action. We don’t share meals with people who want to strip others of their rights. We don’t entertain those who advocate for subjugation.

For generations, Americans were taught that “everyone is entitled to their opinion.” But too often, that slogan was used to protect opinions that were not just controversial — they were dehumanizing. Some opinions aren’t harmless perspectives; they are demands for the erasure of others. If you believe LGBTQ+ people are a threat, that Black lives don’t matter, that women should be controlled, or that immigrants are invaders — that’s not an opinion. That’s a declaration of war on your neighbors.

And guess what? We’re done negotiating with people who come to the table with a flamethrower.

What’s happening now — on campuses, in social circles, on dating apps, in coworking spaces — is not a moral panic. It’s a refusal. A refusal to legitimize fascist ideology through politeness. A refusal to tolerate intolerance. A refusal to keep treating hate like just another lifestyle choice. Students, once written off as coddled or naive, are now leading the charge with more moral clarity than many of their elders. They’ve watched their future be scorched by climate denial, gutted by gun violence, threatened by judicial regression, and mocked by bigots who believe cruelty is their birthright.

So when they say, “Don’t date fascists,” it’s not a joke. It’s survival. It’s ethics. It’s a way of saying: we won’t build a future with people who want to burn it down.

Naturally, the reactionaries are losing their minds. They cry censorship. They scream about being silenced. But no one is throwing them in jail. No one is banning their books. They still have podcasts, Fox News panels, AM radio rants, and social media echo chambers. They’re not being silenced — they’re just being ignored, unfollowed, unmatched, and uninvited. This is the free market they claimed to love: the market of social capital, and their stock is tanking.

Let’s be real: if your self-worth depends on supporting a wannabe authoritarian who brags about assaulting women, who incites violence, who revels in cruelty — then you don’t get to cry foul when people refuse to associate with you. You don’t get to call yourself a victim when your beliefs demand the victimization of others. If you want to make people disappear from society, don’t be shocked when society decides it can do without you.

Being socially iced out isn’t oppression. It’s a statement of community values. It’s saying, “We have standards. We don’t normalize hate.” It’s drawing a boundary and sticking to it. If your presence brings danger, division, or dehumanization, then yes — the group chat is full. The party’s at capacity. The brunch invite got lost in the mail.

This generation is not softer — they’re sharper. They’re not canceling you. They’re choosing not to subsidize your moral decay. They’re choosing not to pretend that white nationalism is just another hot take. They’re choosing dignity over decorum. And frankly, it’s about damn time.

So here’s the deal: You can sit with us — unless you’re a fascist. No fascists at brunch. No fascists in the friend group. No fascists in the future.
