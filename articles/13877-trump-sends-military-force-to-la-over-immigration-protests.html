Trump sends military force to LA over immigration protests

US President Donald Trump ordered National Guard troops to Los Angeles, a rare deployment expected Sunday against the state governor's wishes after sometimes-violent protests against immigration enforcement raids.

Trump took federal control of California's state military to push soldiers into the country's second-biggest city, a decision deemed "purposefully inflammatory" by California Governor Gavin Newsom and of a kind not seen for decades according to US media.

The development came after two days of confrontations during which federal agents fired flash-bang grenades and tear gas toward crowds angry at the arrests of dozens of migrants in a city with a large Latino population.

"It's up to us to stand up for our people," said a Los Angeles resident whose parents are immigrants, declining to give her name.

"Whether we get hurt, whether they gas us, whatever they're throwing at us. They're never going to stop us. All we have left is our voice," she told AFP as emergency services lights flashed in the distance.

An AFP photographer saw fires and fireworks light up the streets during clashes, while a protester holding a Mexican flag stood in front of a burnt-out car that had been sprayed with a slogan against the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agency.

"President Trump has signed a Presidential Memorandum deploying 2,000 National Guardsmen to address the lawlessness that has been allowed to fester," White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said, blaming what she called California's "feckless" Democratic leaders.

"The Trump Administration has a zero tolerance policy for criminal behavior and violence, especially when that violence is aimed at law enforcement officers trying to do their jobs."

Trump congratulated the National Guard for "a job well done" shortly before midnight on Saturday in a post on Truth Social.

However, Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass said on social media platform X the troops had not yet been deployed, while AFP journalists have so far not seen them on the ground.

Trump took a swipe at Bass and Newsom, saying in his post they were "unable to handle the task," drawing a comparison with deadly fires that hit the city in January.

- 'Purposefully inflammatory' -

The National Guard -- a reserve military -- is frequently used in natural disasters, such as in the aftermath of the LA fires, and occasionally in instances of civil unrest, but almost always with the consent of local politicians.

California's governor objected to the president's decision, saying it was "purposefully inflammatory and will only escalate tensions."

Federal authorities "want a spectacle. Don't give them one. Never use violence. Speak out peacefully," Newsom said on X.

Trump's Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth threatened to involve nearby regular military forces.

"If violence continues, active duty Marines at Camp Pendleton will also be mobilized -- they are on high alert," he wrote on social media.

Law professor Jessica Levinson said Hegseth's intervention appeared symbolic because of the general legal restriction on the use of the US military as a domestic policing force in the absence of an insurrection.

"The National Guard will be able to do (no) more than provide logistical (and) personnel support," she said.

- Arrests -

Trump has delivered on a promise to crack down hard on the entry and presence of undocumented migrants -- who he has likened to "monsters" and "animals" -- since taking office in January.

The Department for Homeland Security said ICE operations in Los Angeles this week had resulted in the arrest of "118 aliens, including five gang members."

Saturday's standoff took place in the suburb of Paramount, where demonstrators converged on a reported federal facility that the local mayor said was being used as a staging post by agents.

Masked and armed immigration agents carried out high-profile workplace raids in separate parts of Los Angeles on Friday, attracting angry crowds and setting off hours-long standoffs.

Fernando Delgado, a 24-year-old resident, said the raids were "injustices" and those detained were "human beings just like any."

"We're Spanish, we help the community, we help by doing the labor that people don't want to do," he told AFP.

Mayor Bass acknowledged that some city residents were "feeling fear" following the federal immigration enforcement actions.

"Everyone has the right to peacefully protest, but let me be clear: violence and destruction are unacceptable, and those responsible will be held accountable," she said on X.

FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino said multiple arrests had been made following Friday's clashes.

"Law and order will prevail," he said on X.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of Authoritarianism: Trump's Deployment of National Guard in Los Angeles

In an audacious move reminiscent of dark historical periods where government forces clashed with civilians on American streets, President Donald Trump has ordered the deployment of National Guard troops to Los Angeles. This decision, starkly against the wishes of California Governor Gavin Newsom, marks a chilling escalation in the Trump administration's aggressive stance on immigration enforcement. The pretext? A response to sometimes-violent protests against immigration raids in a city with a significant Latino population.

A Calculated Provocation

Trump's decision to take federal control of California's state military to push soldiers into the country's second-largest city is not only unprecedented in recent decades but is also, as Governor Newsom aptly described, "purposefully inflammatory." This action is a deliberate provocation, designed to escalate tensions and assert federal authority over state objections in a manner that is as reckless as it is dangerous.

The Spectacle of Force

The White House, through Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, blames California's "feckless" Democratic leaders for the so-called lawlessness, conveniently ignoring the systemic issues and the desperation that underpin the protests. Trump's administration, with its zero-tolerance policy for what it deems criminal behavior, fails to recognize the humanity and the voices of those protesting — individuals and communities standing against a draconian immigration policy that has been criticized for its brutality and inhumanity.

The Resistance on the Streets

The scenes described in Los Angeles — from the use of flash-bang grenades and tear gas by federal agents to the symbolic act of a protester holding a Mexican flag in front of a burnt-out car — speak volumes about the resistance from the city's residents. Their fight is not just against the immediate threat of immigration raids but also against a broader assault on their rights and dignity. The fact that people are willing to face harm to "stand up for our people," as one unnamed Los Angeles resident put it, underscores the depth of the community's resolve.

The Legal and Moral Quagmire

The deployment of the National Guard, and the threat to mobilize active duty Marines, represents a dangerous blurring of lines between military and law enforcement roles within the United States. Law professor Jessica Levinson's comments highlight the symbolic and legally dubious nature of such threats, reminding us of the general prohibition against using the military as a domestic policing force. This move by Trump and his Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth not only threatens to escalate tensions but also plunges the nation into a legal and moral quagmire.

The Human Cost of Trump's Policies

The Trump administration's hardline stance on immigration, characterized by derogatory language and punitive measures, has real human costs. The Department for Homeland Security's boast of arresting "118 aliens, including five gang members," fails to convey the terror and disruption inflicted on communities. Individuals like Fernando Delgado, who see the detained as "human beings just like any," highlight the fundamental disconnect between the administration's policies and the lived realities of those targeted.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Escalation

As Los Angeles braces for the arrival of National Guard troops, the city becomes a focal point in the struggle against an administration determined to enforce its will through intimidation and force. This episode is not just about immigration enforcement; it's about the broader battle for the soul of America — a battle between authoritarian impulses and the principles of justice and human dignity.

Mayor Karen Bass's call for peaceful protest is a reminder that, while violence and destruction are not the answer, silence in the face of authoritarian overreach is not an option either. The events in Los Angeles demand our attention and our unequivocal condemnation of tactics designed to suppress dissent and erode civil liberties. The deployment of the National Guard, against the backdrop of aggressive immigration raids and violent confrontations, is a stark reminder of the dangerous path the Trump administration is willing to tread — a path that leads away from the ideals of democracy and towards the shadows of tyranny.
