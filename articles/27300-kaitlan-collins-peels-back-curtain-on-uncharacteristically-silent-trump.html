Kaitlan Collins peels back curtain on 'uncharacteristically silent' Trump

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosNovember 16, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump remained publicly silent after a rare nationwide ground stop stranded thousands of travelers on Friday, even as he continued posting about Gavin Newsom, a New Orleans riverboat crash, the entertainment industry and Hannibal Lecter. His administration offered a theory for the absence—Trump was letting subordinates lead—but the reporting establishes no reason for why a president usually eager to dominate the news declined to address a disruption of the national aviation system.

CNN White House reporter Kaitlan Collins highlighted the contrast as airports dealt with the fallout and federal officials faced questions about the system failure. The White House then issued a travel photo lid for Saturday, signaling that no public appearance by Trump was expected.

The president posted, just not about the crisis

Trump was not entirely out of communication. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said he had spoken with the president by phone and in the Oval Office. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin also said he had talked with him.

That makes the administration’s predicament more revealing, not less. Trump was reportedly engaged enough to speak privately with Cabinet officials and active enough to publish on Truth Social, yet he did not use his public platform to explain what had happened, reassure stranded travelers or describe the federal response.

Collins called the silence “uncharacteristic,” an assessment supported by Trump’s well-established preference for placing himself at the center of major events. The New York Times likewise noted his absence as the aviation crisis unfolded. Former Republican Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene sharpened the criticism, writing on X that while people feared the country might be under attack, “Trump didn’t even bother to address the nation.”

The headline’s promise of a curtain being pulled back goes further than the available facts. Collins documented the president’s conspicuous absence and the subjects he chose to discuss instead. She did not establish why he stayed silent.

Delegation became the official explanation

Mullin portrayed Trump’s nonappearance as evidence of executive discipline.

“This is what President Trump does,” he said at Friday’s news conference. “He puts leaders in place, and he allows them to lead.”

That defense asks the public to treat presidential silence as a management philosophy. But the administration still relied on Trump’s presumed authority to strengthen its message: Duffy said the president wanted the full force of the federal government directed toward fixing the problem. Trump’s aides invoked his commitment while Trump avoided making it himself.

Delegating technical work to an agency secretary is ordinary governance. Leaving subordinates to account publicly for a nationwide emergency is a separate choice. Nothing about permitting Duffy to manage the response prevented Trump from addressing the people affected by it.

The arrangement also protected the president from immediate scrutiny. Duffy and Mullin stood before cameras, explained the administration’s actions and absorbed questions about the failure. Trump retained the power of the office while outsourcing its public accountability.

A funding demand without Republican ownership

Duffy called on Congress to provide another $50 billion to repair the nation’s aviation infrastructure. Reporters pressed him on an obvious political complication: Republicans had controlled Congress for the previous two years while a Republican occupied the White House.

The funding request may identify a real need, but it does not erase control of the institutions responsible for meeting it. An administration cannot plausibly present federal neglect as an external force when its own party has held the legislative and executive power needed to set priorities, appropriate money and oversee the system.

Trump’s silence helped keep that contradiction at the level of a Cabinet news conference. Duffy had to demand more money and answer for Republican stewardship; the president did neither.

A lower profile after electoral defeat

The Times connected Trump’s reduced visibility to Republicans’ significant losses in the 2026 midterm elections, reporting that he had maintained a somewhat lower profile since then. That context offers a possible political explanation, but not a confirmed account of his conduct during the ground stop.

What the episode does show is a selective retreat. Trump had not stopped broadcasting his interests or attacking political targets. He had stopped short of attaching himself publicly to a crisis that raised questions about federal capacity and Republican governance.

By the time AlterNet published its report, the president had found room in his public commentary for Hannibal Lecter but not for the thousands of Americans stranded by a nationwide aviation shutdown. His aides called that leadership through delegation. For the officials sent to face the cameras—and for the travelers waiting in airports—it looked more like authority without exposure.
