From Womb to Waste: The Cruel Hypocrisy of Forced Birth Politics

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 17, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Adriana Smith is dead.

She died in February 2025. She was a nurse. A mother. A daughter. A human being.

And yet, months after her brain shut down, her body is still being exploited—transformed into a gruesome monument to state overreach and ideological extremism. The state of Georgia, under its draconian “heartbeat” law, saw not a woman, not a corpse, not a tragedy, but an opportunity: a vessel that could be forcibly preserved and used to carry a pregnancy to term.

She was nine weeks pregnant when she was declared brain-dead. Her grieving family, already shattered by loss, begged for the humane thing—to let her go, to honor her life with dignity and allow mourning to begin. But Georgia had other ideas. It kept Adriana’s body hooked up to machines, overriding all consent and compassion, gambling that perhaps—if the body held out long enough—they could salvage a fetus.

This isn’t science. It isn’t medicine. It certainly isn’t ethics. It’s fanatical political theater performed on the cold stage of a woman’s dead body.

And it doesn’t stop with Adriana. Because in the twisted doctrine of forced birth politics, life only matters when it’s inside a womb. The moment that child is born—if it survives at all—it ceases to be a miracle and becomes a liability.

Let’s say the fetus survives. Let’s grant them that fantasy. After months of gestation in a corpse, the child takes its first breath. What next? Does the state rush in with diapers, formula, therapy, or security? Will there be guaranteed healthcare? Will someone offer counseling for the child born into grief and trauma?

Absolutely not.

The same Georgia legislature that commandeered Adriana’s dead body to serve their crusade will turn its back on the child it claims to have saved. No Medicaid expansion—because that would be socialism. No guaranteed parental leave—because corporations don’t like it. No childcare subsidies—because bootstraps. No housing security, food stability, or mental health support—because helping people costs money, and this state only spends on control.

The state that co-opted a corpse will leave a breathing child to fend for itself in a system designed to break them.

This is the hypocrisy of the pro-birth agenda: a fetus is sacred, but a child is expendable. A woman is revered as long as she’s pregnant—and irrelevant the moment she isn’t. And if she dies in service to their narrative? All the better. Martyrs can’t talk back.

Meanwhile, Georgia continues to rank among the worst states for maternal mortality—especially for Black women. It has a collapsing foster care system. It pushes punitive education policies. It criminalizes poverty while defunding assistance programs. It glorifies the unborn while abandoning the born.

And it does all this while cloaking itself in the sanctimony of religion. But this isn’t faith. This is rot. This is what happens when government and dogma fuse into something monstrous—when morality is stripped from governance and replaced with misogyny, control, and performative cruelty.

Adriana Smith was not a martyr. She was not a willing surrogate. She was a person. She deserved peace in death. Her family deserved closure. Instead, they were handed state-sponsored desecration dressed up as virtue.

Any child born of this nightmare will enter a world already hostile to their existence. They will grow up as a symbol, not a person. They will be praised before birth and ignored after. Their trauma will be legacy. Their care will be an afterthought. Their needs will be politicized, pathologized, or outright denied.

If you’re a fetus, the state will break all the rules to protect you. If you’re a child, good luck.

If you’re a woman? You’re just a container. And if you die, they’ll still find a way to use you.

This is not compassion. This is not morality. This is a grotesque necro-theocracy masquerading as governance—a system where the dead are exploited, the living are forgotten, and the unborn are weaponized.

Call it what it is: a death cult with a pulse fetish and no plan past the delivery room.
