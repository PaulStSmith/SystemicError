A new report Saturday shows one Donald Trump Cabinet official will be fired “really soon.”

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem won’t be around for long, according to the Bulwark’s reporting this weekend.

“For weeks, a rumor has been circulating in political circles that Noem may soon be on the outs. It briefly surfaced in a CNN report a few weeks back that listed her first among the cabinet officials who could be caught in a year-end turnover, noting that while Trump himself has been happy with Noem, top White House officials have grown frustrated with her tenure—specifically, her employment of her divisive and combustible chief adviser, Corey Lewandowski,” according to the report.

It continues:

“But in conversations with three former DHS officials—who served in both the Biden and Trump administrations and are still in touch with current staff—it was made clear that Trump is indeed considering moving on from Noem. Two of those officials said they believed such a move could happen ‘really soon,’ relaying that they’ve been told Noem has been taking on less of a role in directing department memos. But they and the third official also cautioned that the situation is fluid. Trump could decide to let Noem oversee the launch of new enforcement operations planned for January and February. And, as always, the president’s views are fickle and often heavily influenced by the latest person to grab his ear.”

According to journalist Adrian Carrasquillo, “Still, there are a few factors that make the possibility of a Noem exit likely.”

“The first is that Trump may want to begin the New Year with a clean slate, hoping that fresh blood could improve either the execution of his signature mass deportation policy or—more importantly—the public’s perception of it,” he wrote. “The second is that a prominent, if slightly faded, Republican governor may be soon on the job market, ready to burnish his MAGA credentials with a Trump appointment.”

Our Analysis:

The Impending Exit of Kristi Noem: A Reflection of Trump’s Chaotic Cabinet Carousel

In a news cycle that often feels like it’s spinning faster than a centrifuge, the report of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem’s looming dismissal is not just another blip on the political radar. It is a stark manifestation of the inherent instability and unpredictability that has characterized the Trump administration from its inception. The Bulwark’s latest scoop dives into this chaos, hinting at a shake-up that seems all but inevitable. Yet, as with all things under Trump’s tumultuous tenure, certainty remains as elusive as ever.

The Core of the Conundrum: Divisive Advisers and Fickle Leadership

At the heart of Noem’s precarious position is her association with Corey Lewandowski, described as a “divisive and combustible chief adviser.” This alone should raise eyebrows—not for the fact that Trump’s team is considering a change (that’s hardly news), but because it underscores a recurring theme of Trump’s leadership: the prioritization of loyalty and personal rapport over competence and stability. It’s an approach that has seen the revolving door of the White House spin unabated, with officials coming and going with bewildering frequency.

The report leans heavily on the insights of former DHS officials, painting a picture of a department caught in the crosshairs of presidential whimsy. Trump’s fickleness, as highlighted by these accounts, is not just a personality quirk—it’s a governance style that has profound implications for national policy and its execution. The suggestion that Noem’s fate could hinge on Trump’s latest mood swing or the last person he spoke to is alarming, to say the least.

The Bigger Picture: Strategy or Serendipity?

Adrian Carrasquillo’s analysis introduces two factors that could be driving the push for Noem’s exit: the desire for a “clean slate” in the New Year and the opportunity to bring a “prominent, if slightly faded, Republican governor” into the MAGA fold. These reasons, while plausible, seem almost too strategic to be true, given the administration’s track record of spur-of-the-moment decisions and policy pivots. One cannot help but wonder if there’s more serendipity at play here than strategy.

The prospect of using Noem’s departure as a means to “improve the public’s perception” of Trump’s controversial mass deportation policy speaks volumes about the administration’s priorities. It’s an admission, albeit indirect, that optics often trump (pun intended) efficacy and ethical considerations. The notion that a “fresh blood” could somehow sanitize a policy that has been widely criticized for its humanitarian implications is both naïve and cynical.

Conclusion: A Symptom of Systemic Error

The anticipated firing of Kristi Noem is not just a tale of one official’s fall from grace—it’s a symptom of the systemic error that plagues the Trump administration. This error is characterized by an ad-hoc approach to governance, where decisions are made on the fly, loyalty is valued above expertise, and the consequences are an afterthought.

While the Bulwark’s report provides a glimpse into the imminent reshuffle within the Department of Homeland Security, it also forces us to confront the larger, more disturbing picture of a government in perpetual disarray. As observers, we are left to wonder: if the leadership of the nation’s homeland security is subject to such whims, what does that say about the state of our union?

In the end, whether Noem stays or goes might be less significant than what her potential ousting represents—a system that is not only flawed but fundamentally broken.
