'Never seen anything like this': Hail Mary bid to fight Trump pick stuns MSNBC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 18, 20254 min read

NBC's Ryan Nobles weighed in Thursday on MSNBC over Senate Democrats' last-ditch move to walk out of the Judiciary Committee hearing to advance the nomination of Emil Bove, the massively controversial Trump Justice Department official up for an appellate court nomination, despite whistleblower allegations that he strategized on how to defy court orders blocking Trump's immigration policies.

The ploy is unlikely to stop him from being confirmed in the end, Nobles said, despite widespread opposition to Bove from respected legal experts around the country — but it's designed to make things as painful and politically costly for Republicans as possible.

"I'm not quite sure I've ever seen anything quite like what happened in the Judiciary Committee today," anchor Katy Tur said. "Am I wrong? Is this normal practice?"

"It's certainly not normal practice, Katy," said Nobles. "I'm not saying it's unprecedented, but it is certainly out of the norm to see an entire panel of a hearing break apart, because one side of the aisle decides to walk out en masse in protest."

Democrats' move here, he continued, "is really the only tool they have in their toolbox" given GOP control of the Senate, "and that's to try and grind the gears of this process down as slow as possible. And there's enough votes here for Emil Bove to win confirmation, ultimately, on the Senate floor and to get out of this committee. But what Democrats would like to do is try and put as much out there about this nominee so that perhaps, if there is a Republican that's worried or concerned about the nomination, that they would think twice before voting [for Bove]."

"What Democrats would argue here, Katy, is that this is a lifetime appointment, there should be as much vetting of this candidate as possible; we should hear from everyone who has insight into his work at the Department of Justice and in other places before they cast this vote," Nobles added. "And that's one of the reasons, among the things they're asking for more than anything, is the opportunity for this whistleblower to come forward and testify about their experience with Bove at the DOJ."

Our Analysis:

Senate Democrats' Last-Ditch Effort: A Bold but Likely Futile Stand

In an audacious move that has sent ripples through the political landscape, Senate Democrats executed a dramatic walkout during the Judiciary Committee hearing intended to advance the nomination of Emil Bove—a figure steeped in controversy due to his role in the Trump Justice Department and allegations of strategizing to defy court orders on immigration policies. While NBC's Ryan Nobles suggests this tactic is unlikely to derail Bove's confirmation, it's a clear attempt to increase the political cost for Republicans who support him. This act of defiance, though symbolic, underscores the depths of opposition Bove faces, not only from Senate Democrats but also from a chorus of legal experts nationwide.

The Unprecedented in the Judiciary Committee

Katy Tur of MSNBC encapsulated the shockwaves this move sent through the political sphere, questioning the normalcy of such an event. Nobles' response, highlighting the rarity of an entire panel walking out in protest, points to an extraordinary deviation from Senate norms. This isn't just a blip in the procedural radar—it's a loud, clear signal that business as usual is unacceptable when faced with a nominee accused of undermining the judiciary's authority.

A Desperate Strategy in a Polarized Arena

Democrats, according to Nobles, are scraping the bottom of their strategic barrel. Their walkout isn't just about blocking a controversial nomination; it's about slowing the machinery of confirmation to a crawl, hoping to spotlight Bove's alleged transgressions so brightly that even a single Republican might pause before casting a supportive vote. It’s an admission of their impotence in a GOP-controlled Senate, leveraging public spectacle since the balance of power offers them no recourse.

The Core of the Protest: A Demand for Thorough Vetting

At the heart of Democrats' theatrical exit is a plea for comprehensive vetting, particularly the testimony of a whistleblower with potentially damning insights into Bove's tenure at the DOJ. This isn't merely procedural nitpicking; it's a fundamental demand for accountability and transparency in the face of a lifetime appointment that could shape American jurisprudence for decades. Nobles underscores the gravity of this appointment, hinting at the broader implications of allowing such a controversial figure to ascend without exhaustive scrutiny.

Conclusion: A Futile Gesture or a Necessary Stand?

While Nobles and others may see the Democrats' walkout as ultimately futile in stopping Bove's confirmation, it's a testament to the lengths to which they're willing to go to contest what they view as a dangerous nomination. This isn't just political theater—it's a desperate bid to safeguard the integrity of the judiciary against what they perceive as a clear and present danger. Whether this will sway any Republican senators remains to be seen, but one thing is unmistakably clear: Senate Democrats are not willing to go quietly into the night on this issue, regardless of the likely outcome.
