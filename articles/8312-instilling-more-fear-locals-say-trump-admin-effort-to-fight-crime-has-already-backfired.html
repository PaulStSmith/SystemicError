'Instilling more fear': Locals say Trump admin effort to fight crime has already backfired

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Mismatch of PrioritiesThe Reality of Crime in D.C.A Misguided FocusConclusion: A Call for Reevaluation

Conservative reveals how GOP may abandon Trump if they lose big in the midterms

The GOP’s crime crackdown has been criticised by those living in communities the Trump administration targeted most of all.

According to an ANC commissioner for Washington’s Kingman Park area, Ebony Payne, the tough attitude towards crime from the president’s administration has caused more problems than it has solved. Speaking to NOTUS, Payne believes there is a desire from locals to live in a “safe community” but that the Trump administration has done very little to affect this positively.

She said, “The surge just felt more like making an example out of our city, really, than intending to genuinely help with crime. Neighbors definitely want to live in a safe community. Everybody wants that. … But it has felt more focused on immigration, which is kind of tearing families apart and just instilling more fear.”

“So, how much has it actually helped with driving down crime? You know, I’m not sure if it’s really had that much of an impact.” Worries over crime spikes come as Trump claimed on November 17 that there is “no crime in D.C. anymore,” and D.C. was a mess.”

Violet Jira, writing in NOTUS, refuted this claim. They explained, “On the same day [November 17], seven violent crimes and 132 property crimes were reported across seven of the city’s eight wards, according to Washington Metropolitan Police Department crime data.”

While the Trump administration has claimed a “clear victory” in tackling crime given there are “sharp declines” in overall lawlessness, data analytics expert Jeff Asher says it’s not as straightforward as the Trump administration claims.

He said, “We’re generally seeing sharp declines in crime, we’re seeing sharp declines in gun violence. We’re seeing sharp declines in murder, and we happen to be seeing those same trends in all the available D.C. data.”

“So it makes it very difficult, I think, to tease out whether or not a specific intervention in a specific city had any effect.” Payne believes the administration has failed to effectively crack down on crime, instead deploying ICE enforcement in various parts of the country to crack down on immigration.

Payne said, “There’s been a lot of frustration because there has been so much emphasis put on ICE enforcement, when that wasn’t ever really the type of crime that the neighbors cared about.”

“It took me a while to realize that what they really meant by cracking down on crime was really just cracking down on immigrants. What the city really needed was more attention on youth crime and solutions for youth.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Crime Crackdown: A Misguided Mission?

In a classic display of political misdirection and misplaced priorities, the GOP’s so-called “crime crackdown” under the Trump administration has come under fire, and rightly so. The criticisms, especially from those living in communities most targeted by these policies, like Washington’s Kingman Park area, reveal a stark disconnect between the administration’s actions and the actual needs of the communities they claim to be helping.

A Mismatch of Priorities

Ebony Payne, an ANC commissioner for Kingman Park, articulates a sentiment that resonates across many urban communities. There exists a universal desire for safety and security, yet the Trump administration’s approach has seemingly exacerbated problems rather than ameliorating them. Payne’s observations highlight a crucial flaw in the administration’s strategy: an obsession with immigration enforcement at the expense of addressing the real sources of insecurity and crime within communities.

“The surge just felt more like making an example out of our city, really, than intending to genuinely help with crime,” Payne notes. This statement cuts to the heart of the issue — the administration’s actions were more performative than practical, more about stoking fear and division than fostering safety and unity.

The Reality of Crime in D.C.

Contradicting Trump’s own claims of a crime-free D.C., the reality on the ground, as reported by NOTUS’ Violet Jira, paints a different picture. On the very day Trump boasted about the nonexistence of crime in D.C., the Washington Metropolitan Police Department reported several violent and property crimes across the city. This discrepancy between the administration’s narrative and the lived reality of D.C.’s residents is glaring.

Jeff Asher, a data analytics expert, further complicates the administration’s victory lap over crime reduction. While acknowledging declines in crime, Asher points out the difficulty in attributing these trends to any specific intervention by the Trump administration. This raises an important question: If the crime rates are falling due to broader trends, can the administration justifiably take credit for these declines?

A Misguided Focus

Payne’s insights reveal the crux of the frustration felt by many in targeted communities. The emphasis on ICE enforcement and the crackdown on immigration have diverted attention and resources from addressing the types of crime that genuinely concern residents. “It took me a while to realize that what they really meant by cracking down on crime was really just cracking down on immigrants,” Payne remarks, unveiling a troubling conflation of immigration with criminality in the administration’s rhetoric.

This misguided focus not only fails to address the actual issues of crime but also inflicts harm on communities by tearing families apart and instilling fear. The real need, as Payne suggests, is for solutions that tackle youth crime and provide constructive paths forward for younger generations.

Conclusion: A Call for Reevaluation

The GOP’s crime crackdown under the Trump administration is a textbook example of political optics triumphing over effective policy. By prioritizing a hardline stance on immigration over the nuanced needs of urban communities, the administration has missed the mark on genuinely enhancing public safety and security.

As we dissect the impact of these policies, it becomes evident that a reevaluation of priorities is desperately needed. Communities like Kingman Park deserve strategies that address the root causes of crime and insecurity, not ones that exacerbate fear and division. The path to a safer society lies not in targeting immigrants but in fostering inclusive, comprehensive approaches to crime prevention and community development.
