Trump rages at WSJ after new report: 'People don't explain to me, I explain to them!'

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts after disembarking Marine One at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., June 6, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump on Sunday raged against the Wall Street Journal, this time for a report about deliberations with his Cabinet members.

Trump has been recently dogged by the WSJ's blockbuster report on a purported birthday letter sent from Trump to disgraced financier and convicted pedophile Jeffrey Epstein. Trump has denied writing it.

He weighed in on a different story over the weekend.

"The Wall Street Journal ran a typically untruthful story today by saying that Secretary of the Treasury, Scott Bessent, explained to me that firing Jerome 'Too Late' Powell, the Worst Federal Reserve Chairman in History, would be bad for the Market," Trump wrote. "Nobody had to explain that to me."

Trump continued:

"I know better than anybody what’s good for the Market, and what’s good for the U.S.A. If it weren’t for me, the Market wouldn’t be at Record Highs right now, it probably would have CRASHED!"

He concluded, "So, get your information CORRECT. People don’t explain to me, I explain to them!"

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against the Wall Street Journal: A Critical Dissection

Donald Trump's latest outrage against the Wall Street Journal (WSJ) serves as yet another vivid illustration of his perennial war with the media. However, this episode is not just a simple spat over coverage; it's a revealing glimpse into Trump's modus operandi: denial, diversion, and self-aggrandizement.

The Alleged Untruths

Trump's claim that the WSJ's story on his discussion with Cabinet members, particularly with Secretary of the Treasury, Scott Bessent, is "typically untruthful" lacks substantiation in his response. Instead of addressing the specifics of the alleged inaccuracies, Trump deflects by invoking his supposed economic prowess. This tactic is classic Trump. It's not about the veracity of the report; it's about attacking the messenger and changing the narrative to something more flattering to him.

The Self-Declared Economic Savior

Trump's assertion, "I know better than anybody what’s good for the Market, and what’s good for the U.S.A.," is not just hyperbolic; it's dangerously misleading. This statement, devoid of any humility or acknowledgment of the complexities of economic management, posits Trump as the sole architect of economic success. It's a narrative that ignores the multifaceted factors influencing market performance, including global economic trends, corporate earnings, and, not insignificantly, the policies of the Federal Reserve, which operates independently of the White House.

Moreover, Trump's claim, "If it weren’t for me, the Market wouldn’t be at Record Highs right now, it probably would have CRASHED!" is a speculative assertion that's impossible to verify. It's a bold claim that seeks to rewrite history in his favor, positioning him as the economic messiah who single-handedly averted disaster. This kind of rhetoric is not just misleading; it's delusional.

The Arrogance of "Explaining" to Experts

Trump's conclusion, "People don’t explain to me, I explain to them!" epitomizes his well-documented disdain for expertise and his preference for his own counsel over that of seasoned professionals. This stance is not just arrogant; it's dangerously ignorant. It undermines the value of informed advice and the importance of diverse perspectives in effective decision-making.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is emblematic of Trump's broader pattern of engaging with the media and his governance style. It's a blend of denial, misinformation, and ego-driven assertions that serve more to feed his persona than to inform or lead. His attack on the WSJ over its coverage is not just about defending his actions; it's about crafting a narrative where he remains the infallible hero, regardless of the facts.

In conclusion, Trump's response to the WSJ's reporting is a microcosm of his tenure: a refusal to engage with criticism, a penchant for rewriting reality, and an unshakable belief in his own supreme wisdom. It's a dangerous cocktail for democratic discourse and an informed public. As ever, the onus is on us, the readers and voters, to sift through the bombast and bluster to uncover the truth.
