Abolish ICE: The Evidence Is In the Raids, the Fear, and the Ruin

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

For years, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has justified its existence under the guise of "protecting the homeland" by targeting violent criminals and dangerous elements within the undocumented population. That mask has now fully slipped. The last few weeks have made it grotesquely clear: ICE is no longer a law enforcement agency—it’s a political bludgeon and a machine of institutional cruelty. It’s time to abolish it.

The Machinery of Terror

Let’s start with Operation Safeguard, the Orwellian name given to ICE's aggressive, nationwide sweep to detain and deport undocumented individuals. Since January 2025, this operation has arrested over 23,000 people, deporting more than 18,000. This is not targeted enforcement. This is volume-based terror. This is bureaucratic purging by quota.

These aren’t “violent criminals” being surgically removed from society. Many of these individuals are simply workers—mothers, fathers, caretakers—snatched out of their lives in worksite raids designed for maximum visibility and fear. Consider the following:

  • In Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, ICE arrested 17 construction workers at the Five 10 Flats project. Their crime? Showing up to lay bricks and pour concrete without papers.

  • In Lake Charles, Louisiana, 84 people were rounded up at Delta Downs Racetrack. Again, not a den of criminal masterminds—just immigrants doing low-wage jobs nobody else wants.

  • In Baton Rouge, ICE stormed nine massage parlors and detained ten Chinese nationals. No mention of trafficking. No evidence of abuse. Just a photo-op sweep in an election year.

This is not justice. This is the state flexing its muscle on the vulnerable because it’s too cowardly to take on the powerful.

The Broadacres Closure: A Snapshot of Economic Sabotage

One of the most telling moments came on June 21, when Broadacres Marketplace in North Las Vegas—home to over 1,100 small businesses, predominantly Latino-owned—shut its doors indefinitely over fears of an ICE raid.

Think about that. A cultural and economic landmark, a vital commercial lifeline for hundreds of immigrant families, simply ceased to exist overnight. Not because of a raid. Not because of a legal order. But because of the threat of ICE.

ICE doesn't need to knock down your door with a battering ram to destroy your life. It only has to exist. Its presence alone warps the economy, instills panic, and leaves communities broken and silent. This is economic terrorism disguised as immigration enforcement.

The Lie of “Criminal Removal”

Yes, ICE does remove some violent offenders. Recently, they deported Joaquin Gallegos, a Mexican national wanted for homicide. Great. Let Interpol and Mexican authorities handle their fugitives. We don’t need an agency that arrests thousands of farmworkers and fry cooks just to get to one murderer.

This is the classic sleight-of-hand: highlight the handful of high-profile removals to distract from the thousands of nonviolent people being disappeared into detention centers.

This bait-and-switch isn’t just misleading—it’s deliberate policy. ICE inflates its success by lumping in misdemeanors, traffic infractions, and immigration violations as “criminal activity,” muddying the waters to justify its bloated budget and brutal tactics.

Militarization Against Protest

When ICE kicked off its latest raid campaign in Los Angeles on June 6, protests erupted almost immediately. The public—fed up, furious, and no longer willing to accept fear as normal—hit the streets. The federal response?

Two thousand National Guard troops were deployed to the city. Let that sink in: The federal government militarized a major U.S. city to protect ICE while it tore apart families in Home Depot parking lots and garment factories.

Is this what freedom looks like? Is this law and order—or authoritarianism with a press release?

ICE’s Assaults and the Manufactured “War”

The Department of Homeland Security has claimed a 500% increase in assaults on ICE agents. That sounds like a crisis—until you realize it's part of a broader effort to portray ICE agents as soldiers under siege. It’s the same rhetorical toolkit used to justify police militarization and suppress dissent: cast critics as violent radicals, then unleash the state.

But let’s entertain the premise. If the public is increasingly hostile to your agency, maybe—just maybe—the problem isn't the public. Maybe it’s you.

The Rot Goes Deeper Than Policy

ICE cannot be reformed. It cannot be recalibrated or retrained. It is functioning exactly as intended: a tool of racialized fear, weaponized bureaucracy, and creeping authoritarianism. This isn’t mission drift—it’s mission accomplished.

Its very existence corrodes our democracy. It turns neighborhoods into surveillance zones. It transforms employers into informants. It chills speech, fosters suspicion, and breeds despair. The agency answers not to the people, but to the political whims of whoever occupies the White House.

We must stop pretending ICE is a legitimate component of public safety. It is a state-sponsored predator.

What Comes Next?

Abolishing ICE doesn’t mean open borders. It means honest governance. It means immigration policy rooted in humanity, not humiliation. It means ending raids at workplaces and marketplaces. It means transferring genuine criminal investigations to the FBI or local law enforcement, where due process and oversight exist.

It means recognizing that tearing apart families to score political points is not law enforcement—it’s cruelty for sport.

Final Verdict

The evidence is overwhelming. The lies are exhausted. The pretense is dead.

Abolish ICE.

Not tomorrow. Not after “reform.” Now.
