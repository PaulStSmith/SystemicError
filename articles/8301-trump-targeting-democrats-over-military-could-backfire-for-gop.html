Trump targeting Democrats over military could 'backfire' for GOP

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Accusations: A Dangerous Game of Political Russian Roulette

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump’s decision to go after several Democrats could “backfire” according to a political commentator.

The president accused the six Democrats, including Arizona Senator Mark Kelly, of “sedition” and would go on to suggest they should be executed for calling on members of the military to refuse unlawful orders. Trump’s decision to go after the six Democrats, who appeared in a video encouraging military personnel to stand up against illegal orders, could backfire, according to Politico.

Nicholas Wu, Lisa Kashinsky, and Samuel Benson wrote the denouncement of the six Democrats could “backfire” as the six had been involved in delivering huge wins for the Democrats in swing states.

The trio wrote, “Most hail from swing states or districts, identify as centrists and are leveraging their national security backgrounds to argue that Democrats can retake the patriotic lane.”

Not only are these Democrats involved in the “undercutting” of GOP activities, but they’re helping to further new elects like New York City’s mayor elect Zohran Mamdani with the initiative to take a more vocal stand against the GOP.

The piece reads, “Trump is also boosting some potential 2028 presidential hopefuls, Arizona Sen. Mark Kelly and Michigan Sen. Elissa Slotkin, while further undercutting the GOP’s attempts to make New York progressives like Mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez the face of the Democratic Party.”

Republican strategists believe the change in tone comes from the level of visibility now afforded to the Democrats, which money can’t buy. Arizona-based strategist Barrett Marson said, “Pete Hegseth and Donald Trump are providing Mark Kelly with the kind of visibility that almost no amount of money could buy.”

Democrats like Mark Kelly were accused by Trump of “seditious” behaviour. Trump wrote in a Truth Social post, “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR AT THE HIGHEST LEVEL,” regarding the video Kelly and other Democrat representatives appeared in.

Kelly has since commented on the video and Trump’s response, when during an appearance on Jimmy Kimmel live he was asked what sedition is. He replied, “Well this is the investigation, because of what Donald Trump said. From what I can tell, and I don’t know Pete Hegseth well, I sit on the committee that ultimately had to confirm him, so I know a little bit about his background. He’s totally unqualified.

“From what I can tell, talking to some of my Republican colleagues, I mean he just wants to please the president. This is what he can do this week, he can go after me under the uniform code of military justice, which is a law in the military, which is kind of wild because we recited something in the uniform code of military justice and he’s going to prosecute me under the uniform code of military justice.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Accusations: A Dangerous Game of Political Russian Roulette

Donald Trump’s latest tirade against six Democrats, including notable figures like Arizona Senator Mark Kelly, teeters on the edge of political absurdity and sheer recklessness. His accusations of “sedition” against these individuals, coupled with the insinuation that they deserve execution for encouraging military personnel to defy unlawful orders, is not just a testament to Trump’s hyperbolic rhetoric but also underscores a perilous flirtation with authoritarianism. This move, according to a Politico analysis by Nicholas Wu, Lisa Kashinsky, and Samuel Benson, might just be the political equivalent of shooting oneself in the foot.

Bold Claims, Bolder Implications

Trump’s charges against the Democrats border on the ludicrous. The accused, who participated in a video advocating for the refusal of illegal military orders, were exercising a fundamental democratic right: the call for lawful conduct within the armed forces. To label this as “sedition” is not only a gross misinterpretation of the term but also a dangerous attempt to stifle democratic dissent.

The Politico piece astutely points out that these Democrats, primarily from swing states, have been instrumental in securing significant victories for their party. Their centrism and national security credentials have been pivotal in challenging the GOP’s monopoly on patriotism. By attacking them, Trump inadvertently shines a spotlight on their efforts and amplifies their voices, an outcome that is likely the antithesis of his intentions.

Strategically Misguided

Trump’s strategy, if one can call it that, appears to be a misguided attempt to divert attention from the GOP’s failures and to smear the Democratic Party by targeting its centrist figures. However, as Politico notes, this tactic might backfire spectacularly. The visibility and sympathy garnered by the likes of Senator Mark Kelly could translate into political capital that “no amount of money could buy,” as Arizona-based strategist Barrett Marson insightfully points out.

Furthermore, Trump’s attacks might be doing the Democratic Party a favor by highlighting potential presidential hopefuls for 2028, such as Kelly and Michigan Senator Elissa Slotkin. Instead of undercutting the Democrats, Trump seems to be inadvertently laying the groundwork for their future electoral successes.

Misguided Prosecutorial Fantasies

Senator Kelly’s response to Trump’s allegations, during an appearance on Jimmy Kimmel Live, paints a picture of a man bewildered by the absurdity of the situation. His reference to the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) and the ironic twist of being prosecuted under a law that champions lawful conduct encapsulates the surreal nature of Trump’s accusations. It also hints at a broader issue: the misuse of military and legal frameworks to settle political scores, a tactic reminiscent of authoritarian regimes.

A Cautionary Tale

In essence, Trump’s actions serve as a cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing the military and the legal system. His baseless accusations and threats against political opponents not only undermine the democratic process but also erode the foundational principles of law and order. It’s a high-stakes game of political Russian roulette, where the bullet seems increasingly likely to land on Trump’s own political prospects.

In conclusion, Trump’s decision to target these six Democrats reveals more about his own political vulnerabilities than it does about any alleged “seditious” behavior. The backfire anticipated by political commentators might not just be a minor setback for Trump but a significant blunder that cements his legacy as a divisive figure willing to sacrifice democratic norms for personal vendettas. The true sedition here lies not in the actions of those advocating for the refusal of unlawful orders but in the attempts to silence them through intimidation and baseless accusations.
