'Cowardice': Black GOP senator slammed for staying silent as Trump slashed pet project

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 23, 20255 min read

A federal government program that supports minority-owned businesses has been dismantled — and one of the GOP members who fought for years to keep it alive is staying silent, according to a new report from POLITICO.

Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott (R-SC) used to boost the Minority Business Development Agency (MBDA), which was under the direction of the Commerce Department.

But, according to the news outlet, not only has President Donald Trump’s administration dismantled MBDA “in a matter of weeks,” the Senator has been completely silent on the issue.

MBDA funds grants to business owners and provides technical assistance, support and mentorship. It dates back to the Nixon administration.

In March, Trump issued an executive order requiring the MBDA to cut its staff. Since his first term, Trump has been attempting to close or significantly reduce the agency in his budget proposals.

The Politico report noted, “[MBDA] had 100% of its staff, about 50 people, placed on administrative leave or redistributed within the Commerce Department, according to a Commerce employee and a Democratic staffer granted anonymity to discuss personnel matters.”

Another Commerce Department employee noted that the only members of MBDA who remain include a deputy secretary, an acting under secretary, and a DOGE employee.

Scott was once vying to be Trump’s vice president, even appearing on the campaign trail with him. The outlet wrote, “His silence underscores the fine line he and other Black conservatives have to walk between their own interests and loyalty to the party as the administration wages a broader war against the government’s diversity-focused initiatives.”

“They are watching this happen, and they are doing nothing. That’s cowardice. And it cuts especially deep when the people you once believed were your champions turn their backs in silence,” one of the two Commerce Department employees told the outlet.

In a statement, a spokesperson for Scott underscored for Politico his record of working with Trump to deliver “life-changing results for minority communities around the nation.”

This included the senator's efforts in Trump’s first term to secure permanent funding for historically Black colleges and universities and creating “opportunity zones” under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act.

The U.S. Department of Commerce, U.S. Department of Labor, and the Department of Government Efficiency did not respond to Politico's requests for comment.

Our Analysis:

Dismantling Progress: The Silent Betrayal of Minority Business Support

In a move that seems ripped straight from the playbook of 'How to Undermine Minority Empowerment 101', the Trump administration has effectively dismantled the Minority Business Development Agency (MBDA), a cornerstone in the support structure for minority-owned businesses. What's more baffling, or perhaps tragically predictable, is the eerie silence from Senator Tim Scott (R-SC), a figure who once championed the very agency now left in ruins. This scenario isn't just a policy shift; it's a glaring exposé on the precarious tightrope walked by Black conservatives within the GOP, particularly when the party's agenda sharply pivots away from diversity-focused initiatives.

The MBDA: A Brief Backstory

Established during the Nixon administration, the MBDA was tasked with a noble and crucial mission: to fund grants, provide technical assistance, and offer mentorship to minority business owners. Its existence was a recognition of the systemic barriers faced by minority entrepreneurs and an attempt to level the playing field. Fast forward to the present, and the Trump administration's decision to gut the agency speaks volumes about its stance on minority empowerment and diversity. According to POLITICO, the MBDA saw 100% of its staff, roughly 50 individuals, either placed on administrative leave or scattered across the Commerce Department, reducing the agency to a skeleton of its former self, manned only by a deputy secretary, an acting under secretary, and a DOGE employee.

Senator Tim Scott's Deafening Silence

Senator Tim Scott, once a vocal supporter of the MBDA and a figure who seemed to understand the importance of such an agency, has now taken a vow of silence as the agency is dismantled. This silence is not just disappointing; it's a stark betrayal of the minority business community he once professed to champion. Scott's previous endeavors, including securing permanent funding for historically Black colleges and universities and creating "opportunity zones" under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, now seem hollow in the face of his current inaction.

The GOP's War on Diversity

Scott's silence underscores a larger, more concerning trend within the GOP: a systematic rollback of diversity-focused initiatives. This isn't merely a policy disagreement; it's an ideological war against the very concept of governmental support for minority empowerment. The administration's actions, compounded by the silence of its Black conservative members, highlight a disturbing willingness to sacrifice minority interests on the altar of party loyalty.

The Cowardice of Inaction

The anonymous Commerce Department employees quoted in the POLITICO report hit the nail on the head: watching the dismantlement of the MBDA and doing nothing is cowardice. It's a betrayal that resonates not just within the halls of the Commerce Department but across the entire minority business community. The impact of this inaction extends beyond the immediate loss of support and mentorship for minority business owners; it sends a clear message about the value (or lack thereof) that the current administration, aided by silent complicit figures like Senator Tim Scott, places on minority empowerment and diversity.

Conclusion: A Call to Accountability

Senator Tim Scott, and indeed the entire GOP, must be held accountable for this silent betrayal. The dismantling of the MBDA is not just an administrative shuffle; it's a deliberate move to weaken the support structure for minority-owned businesses. This action, coupled with silence from those who could have made a difference, demands a response. It's time to call out this cowardice, demand better from our elected officials, and ensure that minority empowerment is not just a talking point but a cornerstone of policy-making. The fight for diversity and minority support is far from over, but it's a fight that requires voices, not silence, from all corners of the political spectrum.
