Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent hinted that the American people would never see the return of $175 billion in tariffs collected by the Trump administration after the Supreme Court struck down the president’s ability to impose them.

During an event at the Economic Club of Dallas on Friday, Ray Washburne noted that the administration had collected about $175 billion in tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) before the court ruled it unconstitutional.

“But is that going to be in dispute?” Washburne asked. “Like who gets it back?”

“Yeah, it’s in dispute,” Bessent confirmed. “The Supreme Court did not rule on that today. They pushed it back down to the International Tax and Trade Court. And my sense is that could be dragged out for weeks, months, years.”

“Well, that’s going to be a food fight going after the $175 billion,” Washburne observed.

“I got a feeling the American people won’t see it,” Bessent predicted.

Trump has vowed to continue imposing tariffs despite the court’s ruling on Friday.

Our Analysis:

Tariff Turmoil: A $175 Billion Dollar Dispute

In a revelation that should shock but not surprise, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has essentially waved goodbye to any hope the American public had of seeing the return of $175 billion in tariffs collected under the Trump administration. This sum, amassed through the application of tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), now sits in a legal limbo following a Supreme Court ruling that deemed the president’s ability to impose them as unconstitutional.

The Supreme Court’s Silence: A Path to Prolonged Dispute

The Supreme Court, in its infinite wisdom, decided to sidestep the issue of what happens to the collected tariffs, leaving it to the lesser-known International Tax and Trade Court. Bessent’s prediction that this could be “dragged out for weeks, months, years” is not just an offhand comment; it’s a forecast of an impending bureaucratic nightmare. The Supreme Court’s refusal to provide guidance on this matter is a glaring omission, essentially throwing the issue into the depths of the judicial system, where it is likely to be ensnared in endless debate and litigation.

A Food Fight Over Billions

Ray Washburne’s characterization of the scramble for the $175 billion as “a food fight” is apt yet understated. This isn’t merely a squabble over who gets the last piece of pie; it’s a full-blown battle royale over a substantial amount of money that was extracted from the American economy in the form of tariffs. These tariffs, which have been criticized for their impact on American consumers and businesses, now serve as a contentious pot of gold at the end of a very convoluted legal rainbow.

The American People: Unlikely Beneficiaries

Bessent’s bleak outlook, that “the American people won’t see it,” cuts to the core of the issue. This isn’t just about the legal entanglement of $175 billion; it’s a stark reminder of how distant the interests of the American public are from the decisions made in the corridors of power. The money, collected with the promise of protecting American interests, now seems destined to be swallowed by the black hole of governmental bureaucracy, legal fees, and possibly even redirected into purposes far removed from its original intent.

Trump’s Defiant Stance

In the midst of this legal and financial chaos, former President Trump’s vow to continue imposing tariffs, despite the Supreme Court’s ruling, is the cherry on top of this dysfunctional sundae. This defiance not only showcases a troubling disregard for the legal framework and checks and balances designed to curb executive overreach but also signals potential for further economic policy decisions that could ensnare the country in additional legal and financial quagmires.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

The saga of the $175 billion in tariffs is a glaring example of systemic failure. From the imposition of tariffs under questionable legal authority to the Supreme Court’s abdication of responsibility in resolving the ensuing dispute, every step of this process reflects a breakdown in the mechanisms supposed to protect American interests and uphold the rule of law.

The American people, once hopeful that such funds could be redirected towards public good or easing the burden tariffs placed on them, are now spectators in a frustrating display of legal and political brinkmanship. The reality, as Bessent so candidly puts it, is that the chances of these funds being returned to the public or used in a manner that directly benefits those impacted by the tariffs are slim to none.

In the end, this isn’t just a story about tariffs; it’s a cautionary tale about governance, accountability, and the often-overlooked consequences of political decisions on the everyday lives of the American people.
