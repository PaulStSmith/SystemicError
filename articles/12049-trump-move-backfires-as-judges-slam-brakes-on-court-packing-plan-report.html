Trump move backfires as judges slam brakes on court packing plan: report

Donald Trump, Emil Bove (Photo via Curtis Means for Reuters)

Donald Trump's decision to place one of his former personal lawyers on the Third Circuit Court of Appeals with a lifetime appointment is backfiring as judges postpone their own retirements to stop the president picking their replacements.

In May, Notre Dame Law Professor Derek T. Muller pointed to a slowing of bench retirements since Trump was re-elected, noting the "exceedingly slow pace of retirements in the second Trump administration, disproportionately low."

Over the weekend, former federal prosecutor Brendan Ballou wrote in The Atlantic that the president might have been better served if he didn't bully GOP lawmakers to ignore the wealth of whistleblower complaints and accusations against Bove during his brief tenure at the Department of Justice under fellow Trump appointee Attorney General Pam Bondi.

According to Ballou, the spectacle of Bove being elevated to a key appeals court seat despite credible accusations about his ethics did not go unnoticed among other judges.

"By appointing Bove—whose only apparent loyalty is to his own ambition, not to any particular legal philosophy—the GOP might have limited its own ability to appoint judges in the future," he wrote. "This is because the president typically gets to appoint new judges only when old ones die, retire, or move into the quasi-retirement position of 'senior status.'

" And some judges, even conservative ones who would otherwise be happy to let a Republican president pick their replacement, are likely to delay their retirement rather than hand Trump the opportunity to make more Bove-style appointments."

Adding, "Bove is not the kind of lawyer that a traditionally conservative judge would want to be replaced by," he continued, "For judges who care about the rule of law, even very conservative ones, Bove’s conduct offers a reason to reconsider retirement. Senate Republicans should keep that in mind the next time Trump nominates someone like him to the federal bench."

Our Analysis:

The Bove Backlash: Trump's Judicial Strategy Implodes

In what can only be described as a spectacular miscalculation, Donald Trump's decision to place a controversial former personal lawyer, whose primary credential seems to be unyielding loyalty to Trump himself, onto the Third Circuit Court of Appeals is unraveling in real-time. This maneuver, emblematic of Trump's broader approach to the judiciary, underscores a fundamental misunderstanding of the delicate balance of power and the respect for the rule of law that, at least theoretically, underpins the American judicial system.

The Slowdown of Judicial Retirements: A Silent Protest

Notre Dame Law Professor Derek T. Muller's observation of an "exceedingly slow pace of retirements" during Trump's second term is not merely an anomaly; it's a silent protest. Judges, it seems, are deliberately postponing their retirements, a clear rebuke of Trump's attempt to reshape the judiciary in his own image. This trend, highlighted by Brendan Ballou in The Atlantic, signals a growing concern among the judiciary about the caliber of judges Trump is intent on appointing.

The Ethical Quagmire of Bove's Appointment

The appointment of Trump's former personal lawyer to a lifetime position on a key appeals court is not just controversial—it's a case study in cronyism. The litany of whistleblower complaints and accusations of ethical breaches during his stint at the Department of Justice underlines a troubling disregard for the principles that should guide judicial appointments. Ballou's analysis hits the nail on the head: the spectacle of Bove's elevation, despite these credible accusations, has not gone unnoticed. It sends a chilling message about the value (or lack thereof) that the current administration places on integrity and legal philosophy.

The GOP's Judicial Dilemma

What's particularly striking about this saga is the strategic myopia it reveals within the GOP. By pushing through a figure like Bove—whose allegiance seems to lie more with personal ambition than with any discernible legal principles—the party has effectively shot itself in the foot. The reluctance of even conservative judges to retire under these conditions is a damning indictment of Trump's approach. It suggests a profound unease with the erosion of the rule of law, a cornerstone of conservative judicial philosophy.

The Rule of Law vs. Loyalty to Trump

Ballou's warning to Senate Republicans is as clear as it is urgent: the next time Trump nominates a candidate in the mold of Bove, they should think twice. The implication is that the long-term health of the American judiciary—and, by extension, American democracy—is at stake. Trump's penchant for loyalty over legality, for personal fealty over professional qualifications, threatens to undermine the very foundations of the judicial system.

In conclusion, the fallout from Trump's decision to appoint Bove to the Third Circuit Court of Appeals is a microcosm of a larger crisis. It's a crisis of confidence, of credibility, and of principle. As judges across the ideological spectrum quietly rebel by holding onto their gavels a little longer, the message is unmistakable: the rule of law cannot be trumped by unwavering loyalty to a single man. This unfolding drama is not just a political misstep; it's a systemic error with profound implications for the future of American jurisprudence.
