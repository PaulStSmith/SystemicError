‘Destructive and vindictive’ Trump battered for undermining UN ahead of address

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 21, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's UN Gambit: A Masterclass in Undermining Global Unity

NATIONAL HARBOR, MD, USA- February 24, 2024: Donald Trump speaks at CPAC (Photo: Jonah Elkowitz/Shutterstock)

President Donald Trump is set to address the United Nations General Assembly in New York on Tuesday, though critics are condemning the president for undermining the very intergovernmental organization the United States helped create.

“The Trump administration’s approach to the UN has been destructive and at times vindictive,” said Richard Gowan, who leads the International Crisis Group think tank as its director, speaking with the Wall Street Journal in a report published Saturday. “The administration seems immune to concerns about reputational damage.”

Under the second Trump administration, the United States slashed its funding to the UN, clawing back around $1 billion in previously-approved funding for the intergovernmental body, and has been without an UN ambassador for eight months until Friday when the Senate confirmed Mike Waltz for the position.

Trump has also regularly criticized the UN in a manner critics say have undermined the organization’s legitimacy and standing on the world stage.

“When do you see the United Nations solving problems?” Trump said back in late 2016. “They don’t. They cause problems. So, if it lives up to the potential, it’s a great thing. And if it doesn’t, it’s a waste of time and money.”

The Trump administration’s animosity toward the group, said Robert Anthony Wood, a former career ambassador who works for the UN, was not only undermining the organization’s legitimacy, but was counter to Trump’s stated goal of putting “America first.”

“The U.N. is a useful foreign policy tool,” Wood said, speaking with the Wall Street Journal. “What could be more ‘America First’ than advancing U.S. interests using a major foreign policy tool we actually first created?”

Our Analysis:

Trump's UN Gambit: A Masterclass in Undermining Global Unity

In a move that surprises no one yet disappoints many, President Donald Trump is gearing up to address the United Nations General Assembly in New York this Tuesday. But let's cut through the ceremonial niceties and look at the stark, uncomfortable truth: Trump's administration has been on a four-year-long crusade to undermine the very foundation of the United Nations—a body that, ironically, the United States once championed.

Destructive Diplomacy

Richard Gowan, the International Crisis Group's director, didn't mince words when he described Trump's approach to the UN as "destructive" and "at times vindictive." This isn't hyperbole. Under Trump's watch, the United States has yanked approximately $1 billion in funding previously pledged to the UN. This isn't just tightening the purse strings; it's a full-blown assault on international cooperation.

Empty Seats and Empty Promises

For eight excruciating months, the United States didn't even have a UN Ambassador, a vacancy that speaks volumes about Trump's disregard for global diplomacy. The recent confirmation of Mike Waltz does little to assuage concerns, given the administration's track record of international antagonism.

A Legacy of Criticism

Trump's critiques of the UN aren't new. Remember in late 2016 when he questioned the organization's problem-solving capabilities? His skepticism isn't entirely without merit. Yes, the UN has its flaws. Yet, Trump's relentless negativity only serves to erode the institution's legitimacy further, rather than constructively engaging with it to effect change.

Misunderstood American Interest

Perhaps most baffling is the administration's misunderstanding of how the UN can serve American interests. Robert Anthony Wood, a former career ambassador to the UN, pointed out the obvious: the UN is a "useful foreign policy tool." Advancing U.S. interests through a platform America helped build should be a no-brainer. Yet, Trump's "America First" rhetoric seems to translate into "America Alone."

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity

Trump's handling of the United Nations is a missed opportunity of historic proportions. At a time when global cooperation is more critical than ever—be it tackling climate change, managing pandemics, or addressing international conflicts—the United States under Trump has chosen to sideline itself, undermining both the UN and its own position on the world stage.

In essence, Trump's approach to the UN is emblematic of his administration's broader foreign policy stance: short-sighted, isolationist, and ultimately self-defeating. As he prepares to address the General Assembly, one can't help but wonder: will Trump seize the moment to change course, or will he continue to chip away at the very foundations of global diplomacy? History, as always, will be the judge.
