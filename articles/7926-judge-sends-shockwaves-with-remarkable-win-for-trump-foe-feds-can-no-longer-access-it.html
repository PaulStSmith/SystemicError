Judge sends shockwaves with 'remarkable win' for Trump foe: 'Feds can no longer access it'

A judge on Saturday hit Donald Trump’s DOJ with a negative ruling, giving some positive news to a well-known critic of the president.

As Politico reported, “A federal judge dealt a setback Saturday to the Justice Department’s effort to re-indict former FBI Director James Comey, blocking prosecutors’ access to key evidence from email accounts and a computer belonging to close Comey friend and attorney Daniel Richman.”

“U.S. District Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly granted a temporary restraining order sought by Richman’s lawyers, requiring that the evidence be sequestered pending a ruling on Richman’s claim that the government illegally retained his emails and other data,” the report states. “Kollar-Kotelly, a Clinton appointee based in Washington, issued the restraining order before getting a formal response from prosecutors to a petition Richman filed last week seeking return of his data. However, she said a lack of clear indication about who currently has the data and where it is stored supported her conclusion that a temporary order limiting access to the trove of records was warranted.”

Legal analyst Anna Bower said, “Judge grants Daniel Richman’s request to temporarily block DOJ’s access to evidence taken from his electronic devices. Richman was a key figure in the now-dismissed prosecution of former FBI director James Comey. Order could complicate DOJ’s effort to re-indict Comey.”

Josh Gerstein also added, “Judge complicates DOJ drive to re-indict Comey, orders his lawyer Dan Richman’s emails & computer off limits to prosecutors. Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly says likely Richman’s rights were violated by govt’s retention & use of the data.”

Roger Parloff wrote, “Remarkable win for Prof Dan Richman (and Comey), at least temporarily.”

“Judge Kollar-Kotelly orders USA not to access any info seized from Richman’s devices—which Mag Judge Fitzpatrick called the centerpiece of the case vs Comey—till she permits,” the legal analyst added Saturday.

Adam Klasfeld chimed in, “The evidence that another judge previously called central to the grand jury presentation leading to Comey’s indictment was likely obtained in violation of his lawyer’s Fourth Amendment rights. Feds can no longer access it, for now.

Our Analysis:

The Judiciary Strikes Back: A Blow to the DOJ’s Pursuit of Comey

In a stunning turn of events that reads like a political thriller rather than the dry docket of federal court proceedings, U.S. District Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly has effectively thrown a wrench into the Department of Justice’s (DOJ) machinery, which was churning its gears in an attempt to re-indict former FBI Director James Comey. The ruling in favor of Daniel Richman, a close confidante and attorney for Comey, is not just a temporary legal maneuver; it’s a significant blow to the DOJ’s efforts, and boy, does it reveal a deeper saga of political vendettas and questionable legal pursuits.

The Ruling: A Shield Against Prosecutorial Overreach

Judge Kollar-Kotelly, appointed by Clinton and often painted by critics as a figure of the liberal judiciary, didn’t just make a ruling; she essentially put up a bulwark against what appears to be a targeted prosecutorial overreach. By granting a temporary restraining order, she’s not only sided with Richman’s contention that the DOJ unlawfully retained his emails and other data, but she’s also signaled a troubling concern: the potential violation of Fourth Amendment rights.

The Implications: Beyond Comey and Richman

The legal analysts’ chorus—Anna Bower, Josh Gerstein, Roger Parloff, and Adam Klasfeld—sing a similar tune, emphasizing the ruling’s impact on the DOJ’s ambitions to re-indict Comey. But let’s read between the lines here. This isn’t just about Comey or Richman. It’s about a broader battle over the integrity of judicial proceedings and the sanctity of individual rights against governmental overreach. When evidence central to a case is potentially obtained in violation of constitutional rights, it’s not just a setback for a prosecution—it’s a red flag for the justice system itself.

The DOJ’s Quagmire: A Legal and Ethical Morass

The DOJ finds itself in a quagmire, not merely because of a temporary legal setback but because it’s now embroiled in a narrative of pursuing cases that hinge on questionable legal grounds. The pursuit of Comey, under these circumstances, isn’t just a legal battle; it’s morphing into an ethical and political morass that the DOJ might find increasingly difficult to navigate.

The Takeaway: A Victory for Judicial Oversight

In the grand scheme of things, Judge Kollar-Kotelly’s ruling is more than a temporary win for Richman and, by extension, Comey. It’s a testament to the power and necessity of judicial oversight in the face of governmental actions that may tread too close to the edge of constitutional boundaries. While some may argue about the political motivations of judges and their rulings, this case underscores the judiciary’s critical role as a guardian of rights and a bulwark against overreach.

In the coming days, as the DOJ scrambles to reassess its strategies, this episode will likely serve as a stark reminder of the delicate balance between pursuing justice and adhering to the principles of fairness and constitutional rights. For now, Comey and Richman can breathe a sigh of relief, but the overarching questions and concerns about prosecutorial power and individual rights remain as pertinent as ever.
