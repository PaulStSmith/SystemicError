'He finally learned his lesson': Mike Johnson pummeled by GOP colleague over Epstein delay

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) listens to other Republican leaders during a press conference focused on the ongoing partial shutdown of the U.S. federal government, at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 30, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) was dragged Sunday by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) over having dragged out efforts to force the Justice Department to release its files on Jeffrey Epstein, with the Kentucky Republican proclaiming Johnson to have “finally learned his lesson” after being forced to schedule a vote on the issue for next week.

“I think he finally learned his lesson. He should have brought this to the floor back in July or September,” Massie told The Washington Post in its report Sunday. “He drug this out. It’s caused nothing but political pain when he could have done the right thing politically, but also morally by bringing this to the floor immediately.”

Renewed calls for the DOJ to release its files on Epstein – the disgraced financier who died in 2019 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges – kicked off in July after the agency concluded that it could not identify any co-conspirators of Epstein’s, and that he had, in fact, died by suicide, contrary to speculation that he may have been killed.

The DOJ subsequently closed its investigation into Epstein and potential co-conspirators, a decision that outraged many MAGA supporters and Democratic voters alike. Johnson initially pushed back against legislative efforts to continue the investigation, including one launched by Massie through what’s known as a discharge petition, a tool that can circumvent legislative leadership and force a vote on a particular measure after receiving 218 signatures.

Johnson refused to schedule any bill for a vote that would have compelled the DOJ to release its files on Epstein, forcing Massie to pursue his discharge petition, which last week received enough signatures and forced Johnson to schedule a vote on the Epstein bill for this week.

President Donald Trump’s name appears thousands of times in the relatively small sampling of Epstein files that the House Oversight Committee has released in recent weeks, and Trump himself has launched an aggressive campaign to block the vote on the Epstein bill.

Massie, however, doesn’t believe that Trump is implicated in the files, and dismisses Trump’s opposition to seeing them released as an effort to protect his friends and donors from embarrassment.

“I think this is all about the president trying to protect his friends and his donors,” Massie told the Post.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files Saga: A Political Chess Game

In a spectacle that feels more akin to a late-night political thriller than the sober reality of American governance, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) finds himself embroiled in a contentious dispute with Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) over the prolonged release of the Department of Justice (DOJ) files on Jeffrey Epstein. This saga, riddled with delay, political maneuvering, and moral questioning, offers a glaring insight into the labyrinthine corridors of power and the complexities of accountability.

Dragging Feet and Political Pain

Johnson’s hesitance and subsequent delay in bringing the matter to the House floor until being cornered by Massie’s discharge petition—a legislative tool akin to a procedural sledgehammer—paints a picture of reluctance and political calculus over straightforward moral imperative. Massie’s scathing critique, “He drug this out. It’s caused nothing but political pain when he could have done the right thing politically, but also morally,” underscores a fundamental tension within the political establishment: the often gaping chasm between the expedient and the ethical.

The backdrop to this political theater is the murky and unsettling case of Jeffrey Epstein, a financier whose life and untimely death in prison under charges of sex trafficking have spawned more questions than answers. The DOJ’s decision to close its investigation into Epstein and potential co-conspirators, citing Epstein’s death by suicide and the inability to identify any co-conspirators, has only fueled public and political outrage. This shared sense of incredulity and demand for transparency transcends the usual partisan divides, uniting MAGA supporters and Democratic voters in their skepticism and calls for accountability.

The Trump Factor

Adding another layer of intrigue and complexity is the involvement of former President Donald Trump, a figure whose name is interwoven thousands of times within the Epstein files already released. Trump’s vehement campaign to block the vote on the Epstein bill is a classic example of political self-preservation, but as Massie suggests, it may also be an attempt to shield his circle from potential embarrassment or worse. This scenario is emblematic of the broader issue of political loyalty and the lengths to which individuals will go to protect their allies and benefactors, often at the expense of transparency and justice.

Conclusion: Morality vs. Expediency

At its core, the prolonged battle over the release of the Epstein files is a microcosm of a larger, systemic issue that plagues the halls of power: the constant balancing act between moral responsibility and political expediency. Johnson’s initial reluctance to schedule a vote, followed by his capitulation only in the face of overwhelming legislative pressure, is a stark reminder of the often reactive rather than proactive nature of political leadership when confronted with uncomfortable truths.

Massie’s maneuvering to force the issue onto the House floor, while certainly a victory for transparency, also raises questions about the mechanisms and motivations that drive our elected officials. Are they acting in the public’s interest, or are they guided by political survival and the protection of powerful interests?

In the end, the saga of the Epstein files is not just about the particulars of one man’s heinous crimes and the shadowy figures that may surround them. It’s about the broader struggle for accountability, the fight to shine a light in the darkest corners of power, and the ongoing challenge of aligning political actions with moral convictions. As this chapter unfolds, one can only hope that it moves us closer to the truth, however uncomfortable it may be.
