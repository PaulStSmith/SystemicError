Trump DOJ plan would let it indict lawmakers who opposed him with no oversight

According to a bombshell report from the Washington Post, the Department of Justice under Attorney General Pam Bondi is considering a plan that would let federal prosecutors investigate and indict members of Congress unfettered by traditional oversight designed to stop political persecution.

Traditionally, before such an investigation could proceed, a prosecutor would have their case reviewed by the lawyers in the Justice Department’s Public Integrity Section. But that would go away under the new proposal.

The report notes that, should the proposal go into effect, "a long-standing provision in the Justice Department’s manual that outlines how investigations of elected officials should be conducted" would be shunted aside and allow possible prosecutions based purely on politics.

Former Public Integrity Section attorney Dan Schwager sounded the alarm about the proposal by suggesting U.S. attorneys put in place by the president could use their power to pursue perceived enemies of the person who appointed them.

“The reason you have the section is exactly what this administration says they want, which is stop politicization,” he explained. “That requires a respect and ability to understand how the laws have been applied in similar situations in the past. The only way to ensure that public officials on both sides of the aisle are treated similarly is to have as much institutional knowledge and experience as possible.”

As it stands now, political corruption cases undergo special scrutiny near elections because they can severely impact election results even if nothing comes out of it and the investigation drops.

As the Post report notes, "The Justice Department manual says that PIN [Public Integrity Section] attorneys must approve — not just be consulted on — charges against members of Congress when the allegations are related to their public office or campaign activities. Still, the attorney general has final say on whether an indictment should be brought."

Our Analysis:

The Wolf Guarding the Henhouse: A Critical Analysis of the DOJ's Power Play

In what can only be described as a bombshell revelation by the Washington Post, we're witnessing the unfolding of a potentially catastrophic shift in the balance of power within the United States' Department of Justice (DOJ). Under the watch of Attorney General Pam Bondi, the DOJ is toying with a scheme that could dismantle the safeguards against political persecution, allowing federal prosecutors to target members of Congress with virtually no oversight. This isn't just a red flag; it's a blazing inferno signaling the erosion of democratic safeguards.

The Proposal: An Unfettered Power

The crux of this alarming proposal is to sideline a critical review process by the DOJ’s Public Integrity Section, a body traditionally tasked with evaluating cases against elected officials to prevent politically motivated prosecutions. If this proposal takes root, accountability and impartiality could become relics of the past, giving way to an era where prosecutions can be initiated based purely on political vendettas.

This isn't merely an adjustment of protocol; it’s an open invitation to abuse of power. The report frames it as removing a "long-standing provision" that ensures investigations into elected officials are conducted with due diligence and fairness. In other words, the DOJ is considering handing over a loaded weapon to prosecutors, with the safety off and no questions asked.

The Risks: Political Persecution Unleashed

Dan Schwager, a former Public Integrity Section attorney, articulates the core of the issue with alarming clarity. The very essence of the Public Integrity Section is to prevent the politicization of prosecutorial power. Schwager’s insights underscore a crucial point: the proposal doesn't just undermine the integrity of the DOJ; it annihilates it, paving the way for a partisan witch hunt that could target officials based on their political affiliations rather than any legitimate legal wrongdoing.

Moreover, the timing of such prosecutions – especially around elections – could have devastating impacts on the democratic process. Even baseless investigations can tarnish reputations and sway election outcomes, effectively allowing the DOJ to play kingmaker under the guise of law enforcement.

A Dangerous Precedent

The most harrowing aspect of this proposal is not just its immediate implications, but the precedent it sets. If the attorney general can unilaterally decide to bypass critical oversight mechanisms designed to ensure fairness and impartiality, what stops future administrations from further eroding the checks and balances that safeguard our democracy?

The Justice Department's manual, as it currently stands, mandates that the Public Integrity Section's attorneys must approve — not merely consult on — charges against members of Congress related to their official duties. This isn't bureaucratic red tape; it's a fundamental check on the abuse of power. Stripping away this provision doesn't streamline justice; it silences it, leaving it at the mercy of political winds.

Conclusion: A Call to Preserve Democratic Safeguards

The proposal under consideration by Attorney General Pam Bondi's DOJ represents a dire threat to the principles of justice and impartiality. It's a move that doesn't just blur the lines between justice and politics; it erases them entirely. As citizens, it's essential to recognize the gravity of this proposal and demand accountability from those in power.

The integrity of the justice system and the very foundations of democratic governance are at stake. This isn't just about the current administration or the next; it's about preserving the sanctity of our democratic institutions for generations to come. The DOJ's plan is not a step forward; it's a leap into an abyss from which there may be no return.
