Trump's 'indefensible' slur makes it 'tough' for MAGA to stand by him: GOP strategist

President Donald Trump’s use of a slur for intellectually disabled people to describe one of his political opponents may be costing him some MAGA support, according to one Republican strategist.

Mediaite reported Friday that during a segment on CNN, Joseph Moreno — a military veteran and former federal prosecutor — lamented Trump called Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz (D) “seriously retarded” on Thanksgiving Day. Trump insulted the Minnesota governor in the middle of a rant about Somali refugees in the Gopher State, as part of his comments in the wake of National Guard member Sarah Beckstrom being fatally shot in Washington D.C. this week.

When host Boris Sanchez quipped: “What happened to ‘Happy Thanksgiving,’” Moreno didn’t hold back.

“Boris, you’re putting your finger, honestly, on what’s so difficult to be a conservative-leaning citizen, who, my whole life has cherished vigorous, but respectful debate,” Moreno said. “And so when you see something like this, which is absolutely indefensible, and you’re sitting at the Thanksgiving table, and your family and friends say, ‘How can you possibly support an administration that comes out with messages like this?’ It’s not easy.”

“And you have to take the big picture view. Do you think that we’re better off as a country now than we would be under a Biden or Harris administration? And is it worth putting up with a president that puts out messaging like this?” He continued. “It’s a tough one, and I’m not gonna pretend I know the answer to that.”

Moreno wasn’t the only conservative who was out off by Trump’s comment. Earlier on Friday, Indiana state senator Michael Bohacek announced that he would not be supporting the president’s push for mid-decade redrawing of U.S. House district boundaries due to his “insulting and derogatory” remarks. Bohacek noted that he was particularly sensitive to the intellectually disabled community given that his daughter has Down Syndrome.

The Trump administration has announced it is halting all asylum applications and immigration from underdeveloped countries in the wake of the Washington D.C. shooting. 29 year-old Afghan national Rahmanullah Lakanwal was arrested and charged with carrying out the attack. Trump attributed Lakanwal’s alleged murder of Beckstrom to former President Joe Biden’s immigration and refugee policies, though the Trump administration’s Department of Homeland Security granted his asylum application earlier this year.

Our Analysis:

The Unabashed Irony of Trump’s Latest Rhetorical Misfire

In a display of rhetoric that can only be described as both crass and profoundly insensitive, President Donald Trump has once again ignited controversy. This time, by using a derogative term, “seriously retarded,” to describe Minnesota Governor Tim Walz. Trump’s comment, made amidst a rant about Somali refugees in Minnesota, came shockingly on Thanksgiving Day—a day traditionally reserved for gratitude and unity. This distasteful comment not only undermines the spirit of the holiday but also showcases a disturbing disregard for basic decency and the values of respect and empathy.

A Disheartening Tradition of Disrespect

It’s no secret that President Trump has, throughout his political career, frequently employed derogatory language and insults to demean his opponents. However, the use of a slur for intellectually disabled individuals marks a particularly low point. It’s not just an attack on a political adversary but also a slap in the face to individuals with intellectual disabilities and their families. The fact that this insult was hurled during a discussion about a tragic incident—the fatal shooting of National Guard member Sarah Beckstrom—only adds a layer of callousness to the act.

The Conservative Conundrum

Joseph Moreno, a Republican strategist who spoke on CNN, highlighted the moral quandary faced by conservative supporters of Trump. Moreno’s admission that supporting an administration that frequently indulges in such “absolutely indefensible” rhetoric is “not easy” sheds light on the internal conflict many Republicans feel. They are torn between their political beliefs and the increasingly indefensible actions and statements of their leader. Moreno’s struggle at the Thanksgiving table, attempting to reconcile his conservative leanings with Trump’s offensive messaging, epitomizes the broader discomfort within the party.

A Line Crossed

Indiana state senator Michael Bohacek’s decision to withdraw support for Trump’s redistricting push, citing the president’s “insulting and derogatory” remarks, underscores a critical point: there are lines of decency that should not be crossed, regardless of political affiliation. Bohacek, particularly sensitive to Trump’s latest slur due to his daughter’s Down Syndrome, represents a growing segment of the Republican base that is increasingly disillusioned with Trump’s abrasive style.

Policy Over People?

Trump’s response to the tragic shooting in Washington D.C.—halting all asylum applications and immigration from underdeveloped countries—reflects a broader pattern of exploiting individual crimes to justify sweeping and punitive policies. Blaming former President Joe Biden’s immigration policies for the actions of Rahmanullah Lakanwal, despite the Trump administration granting his asylum earlier this year, is not just hypocritical; it’s a deliberate obfuscation of facts to serve a political agenda.

Conclusion: The Cost of Indefensible Rhetoric

Trump’s Thanksgiving tirade and the subsequent fallout reveal a troubling disregard for the principles of respect, dignity, and empathy. As conservative figures like Moreno and Bohacek voice their discomfort and opposition, one has to wonder about the long-term consequences of Trump’s rhetoric on the fabric of the Republican Party. Will the party continue to accommodate, and thus normalize, such behavior, or will it finally draw a line in the sand? For the sake of basic decency, one hopes it’s the latter. However, given the track record, optimism seems a tall order.
