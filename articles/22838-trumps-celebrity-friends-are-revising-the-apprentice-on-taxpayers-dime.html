Trump’s celebrity friends are revising 'The Apprentice' on taxpayers' dime

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20263 min read

The SBA Is Not Funding Small Business. It Is Funding a Stage

The source story is straightforward on the surface: the Small Business Administration is putting on a live “Million Dollar Pitch Competition” in Washington, with a celebrity panel, a celebrity host, and taxpayer-backed prize money for five finalists. That is the context. The political meaning is simpler and uglier: a federal agency is being used as a prop in Trump’s ongoing conversion of government into performance.

Power Sits With The People Who Signed Off

The real institutional power here is not with the finalists, and not with the celebrity judges. It sits with the Trump administration, the SBA, and Kelly Loeffler, who are choosing to spend public resources on a made-for-TV event. Fox News can package it as celebration, but the state is the one underwriting it.

That matters because this is not some harmless branding exercise. A federal agency does not accidentally become a spectacle. Someone decided to make it one. Someone decided the SBA should stage a taxpayer-funded contest in Washington and wrap it in anniversary pageantry and celebrity culture. That is a political choice, not a misunderstanding.

Celebrity Is The Cover Story

Nicki Minaj, Grant Cardone, Joe Gebbia, Dean Cain: the names are there to create the illusion of legitimacy through fame. That is the old Trump method in a new federal costume. The point is not expertise. The point is attention, status, and the familiar Trumpian trick of making governance feel like entertainment so the audience stops asking who benefits.

The article treats the celebrity lineup as the hook. That framing is weak because it misdirects attention away from the actual abuse of institutional purpose. The problem is not that these personalities are controversial. The problem is that a public agency is being made dependent on celebrity theater to justify itself at all.

Small Business As Set Dressing

The pitch competition is sold as a tribute to “America’s legacy of free enterprise.” That language is doing ideological work. It turns a federal agency into a patriotic backdrop while obscuring the basic question: why is a taxpayer-funded office running a live contest with a $1 million prize pool and a celebrity panel in the first place?

The finalists are real businesses, and the prize money is real. But their presence does not redeem the spectacle. It gives the spectacle a human face. That is how political branding works: genuine participants are recruited to make a top-down decision look like opportunity instead of choreography.

Trump’s Favorite Medium Is Still Performance

This is not an isolated novelty. It fits Trump’s long habit of turning loss into grievance and governance into televised self-regard. The source notes that he repeatedly claimed cheating when award systems or elections did not reward him, and that he has already signaled he will do the same if the 2026 midterms go against him. That is the throughline.

Trump does not separate reality from entertainment; he collapses them. When he loses, he calls it theft. When he governs, he stages a contest. The machinery is the same. The purpose is to keep power flowing through his personal brand while reducing institutions to audience capture.

The Real Misdirection

The easy story is that this is just tacky. It is more than tacky. It is administrative degradation. A public agency is being asked to cosplay as a television franchise, and the press release language is there to disguise the fact that government capacity is being bent toward spectacle.

That is the pattern: deliberate political theater presented as normal civic life. The celebrity names are noise. The real story is institutional collapse into branding, where agencies stop acting like agencies and start acting like content platforms for the president’s ego.

Conclusion

What this story reveals is not a quirky SBA event. It reveals how authoritarian politics hollow out institutions from the inside while keeping the logo intact. The state still exists on paper, but its purpose is repurposed: not to govern, but to perform. Under Trump, that is not a side effect. It is the design.
