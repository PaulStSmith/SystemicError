The White House budget proposal requests a 20 percent across-the-board cut, giving some in the GOP headaches leading up to the Sept. 30 government shutdown deadline, Politico reports.

Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins (R-Maine) called the proposal “late” and lacking “key details,” and voiced “serious objections to the proposed freeze in our defense funding given the security challenges we face." She also opposed major slashing to non-military programs.

Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-Ky) described the president’s budget blueprint as fraught with Office of Management and Budget “accounting gimmicks” that fail to “counter the growing, coordinated challenges we face from China, Russia, Iran, North Korea and radical terrorists.”

“But they won’t fool Congress,” McConnell added.

Other pro-military Republicans like Sen. Roger Wicker (R-Miss.) said the White House’s request “a cut in real terms” for the U.S. military with an “intent to shred to the bone our military capabilities and our support to service members.”

Wicker’s state of Mississippi contains shipbuilder Huntington Ingalls, which drew down $1.6 billion in Department of Defense money in 2022. Second in line is DOD contractor ST Engineering, earning $651.8 million in defense contracting in a state with an economy valued only at $157.5 billion in 2024.

Aside from budget concerns, if Republicans agree with White House cuts, they risk losing Democratic support to pass a budget filibuster in Senate.

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of "Flat" Military Funding: A Closer Look at Trump's Budget Proposal

In the latest twist of fiscal fantasy, President Donald Trump has stirred the pot of congressional discontent with a budget proposal that ostensibly keeps military funding flat. However, in the convoluted logic that seems to pervade GOP circles, "flat" is synonymous with "cut." This is because the proposal glaringly omits the $3 trillion allegedly needed to fully support the military. This move has unsurprisingly ruffled the feathers of military-minded congressional Republicans, who view this as less of a strategic budgeting decision and more of a threat to national security.

Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins (R-Maine) did not mince words when she criticized the proposal for being “late” and scant on “key details.” Her concerns echo the broader sentiment that freezing defense funding amidst escalating security challenges is not just imprudent, but potentially dangerous. However, what is equally alarming, and perhaps even more telling of the administration's priorities, is the proposed 20 percent across-the-board cut to non-defense programs. This heavy-handed approach to budget trimming reveals a disturbing willingness to sacrifice essential services on the altar of military funding—even when that funding is ostensibly not being increased.

Senator Mitch McConnell (R-Ky), not exactly a stranger to political maneuvering, lambasted the budget blueprint for its reliance on what he terms “accounting gimmicks.” McConnell's critique, that the proposal fails to “counter the growing, coordinated challenges we face from China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, and radical terrorists,” points to a deeper issue within the GOP: a fracture over how to adequately fund defense without gutting the nation elsewhere.

Sen. Roger Wicker (R-Miss.), representing a state that significantly benefits from defense contracting, characterizes the White House’s request as “a cut in real terms” for the U.S. military, expressing concern over an “intent to shred to the bone our military capabilities and our support to service members.” This sentiment is not just hyperbolic fearmongering but highlights the real economic dependencies that states like Mississippi have on defense spending. With heavy hitters like Huntington Ingalls and ST Engineering pumping millions into the state's economy, the stakes are undeniably high.

The political tightrope that Republicans will have to walk if they choose to support these cuts is fraught with peril. Not only do they risk alienating a significant portion of their base that views military strength as sacrosanct, but they also face the potential loss of Democratic support necessary to bypass a budget filibuster in the Senate.

In essence, Trump’s budget proposal is a masterclass in political brinkmanship, proposing drastic cuts that seem more designed to provoke than to pragmatically address the nation's fiscal needs. The real question that emerges from this debacle is not just about the numbers on a page, but about the priorities and values that those numbers represent. As the Sept. 30 government shutdown deadline looms, one can only hope that reason, rather than rhetoric, will guide the final decisions. However, if history is any indicator, we might be in for a long and tumultuous ride.
