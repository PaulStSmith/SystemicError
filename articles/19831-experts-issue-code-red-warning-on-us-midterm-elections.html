Experts issue code red warning on US midterm elections

The United States is facing a major test of American democracy as experts warn that the Trump administration is dragging the nation into “some form of autocracy,” NPR reports.

The U.S. has already crossed the threshold and become an “electoral autocracy,” Staffan I. Lindberg, the director of Sweden’s V-Dem Institute, told NPR.

“I would argue that the United States in 2025-26 has slid into a mild form of competitive authoritarianism,” said Steven Levitsky, a professor of government at Harvard University and co-author of How Democracies Die. “I think it’s reversible, but this is authoritarianism.”

“Under competitive authoritarianism,” NPR explained, “countries still hold elections, but the ruling party uses various tactics — attacking the press, disenfranchising voters, weaponizing the justice system and threatening critics — to tilt the electoral playing field in its favor.”

Levitsky cited several critical points in September as examples, including the Trump administration’s threat against ABC parent company Disney following host Jimmy Kimmel’s remarks on the killing of Charlie Kirk.

“We can do this the easy way or the hard way,” Brendan Carr, the chairman of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), said.

He also cited Trump’s proposal to use American cities as “training grounds” for troops.

“We should use some of these dangerous cities as training grounds for our military, National Guard, but military,” Trump said, as the Military Times reported.

The president “told the commanders that defending the homeland was the military’s ‘most important priority’ and suggested the leaders in attendance could be tasked with assisting federal law enforcement interventions against an ‘invasion from within’ Democratic-led cities, such as Chicago and New York City.”

“No different than a foreign enemy,” Trump said, “but more difficult in many ways because they don’t wear uniforms.”

Levitsky, NPR reported, “said this is the kind of language dictators in South America used in the 1970s — leaders like Augusto Pinochet in Chile.”

NPR notes that the “next big test” could come during the midterms.

Kim Scheppele, a Princeton University sociologist who has studied the authoritarian tactics of Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, warned that in 2014 Orbán’s government “disenfranchised almost all the Hungarians in the U.K., most of whom were oppositional to Orbán,”

Dartmouth College professor of government Brendan Nyhan warned, “The way Election Day works in this country, there are no do-overs.”

Our Analysis:

The Slide into Autocracy: A Critique of Alarm Bells

The recent NPR report casts a disturbing yet unsurprising spotlight on the United States’ democratic backslide under the Trump administration, propelling the nation toward what experts describe as a “mild form of competitive authoritarianism.” This appraisal, far from hyperbolic, is grounded in the disturbing realities of today’s political landscape, characterized by brazen assaults on democratic norms and institutions.

Electoral Autocracy: The New American Reality?

Staffan I. Lindberg from Sweden’s V-Dem Institute doesn’t mince words, labeling the U.S. an “electoral autocracy.” This isn’t a term thrown around lightly; it’s a damning indictment of how far the United States has strayed from its democratic ideals. Steven Levitsky, the co-author of How Democracies Die, echoes this sentiment, arguing the nation has slipped into “competitive authoritarianism.” The critical takeaway here is not just the label but the implications: a democracy where elections occur, but the scales are so tipped that the concept of fair competition is laughable.

The Tactics of Tyrants

The Trump administration’s tactics, as outlined by NPR, read like a dictator’s playbook. Attacking the press, disenfranchising voters, weaponizing the justice system, and threatening critics are maneuvers straight out of the authoritarian handbook. The FCC’s veiled threat against Disney over Jimmy Kimmel’s comments is particularly chilling, reminiscent of how autocrats silence dissent. Trump’s proposition to use American cities as “training grounds” for the military not only blurs the line between the state and the military but also frames domestic dissent as akin to a foreign invasion. This is not just undemocratic; it’s a strategy designed to instill fear and compliance.

Historical Echoes and Future Threats

Levitsky’s comparison of Trump’s rhetoric to that of South American dictators in the 1970s is apt and alarming. The language of “invasion from within” and the othering of political opponents echoes the darkest periods of political repression in recent history. The mention of Chile’s Augusto Pinochet is particularly poignant, reminding us of the brutal lengths such regimes will go to maintain power.

The upcoming midterms loom large as a “next big test” of American democracy. The experience of Hungary under Viktor Orbán, as highlighted by Princeton University’s Kim Scheppele, serves as a cautionary tale. Orbán’s systematic disenfranchisement of oppositional voices abroad is a stark reminder of how electoral manipulation can entrench authoritarian leaders.

Brendan Nyhan’s warning about the irreversible nature of Election Day in the U.S. underscores the urgency of the situation. There are no “do-overs” in democracy; once eroded, the pillars of freedom and fairness are incredibly difficult to rebuild.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The NPR report is more than a wake-up call; it’s a siren blaring across the political landscape. The United States, long viewed as a beacon of democracy, is teetering on the edge of autocracy. This isn’t a future scenario; it’s the present reality. The path forward requires more than concern; it demands action. Vigilance, activism, and unwavering commitment to democratic principles are essential to navigate this crisis. The test is not just for American democracy but for those who believe in the ideals it represents. Will we rise to the challenge, or will we slide further into the abyss of authoritarianism? The choice, while still ours, is slipping away with each unchecked assault on our democratic institutions.
