Trump has built plan for international power after he leaves White House: Mike Huckabee

By Paulo Santos

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee indicated that President Donald Trump’s Board of Peace could become his primary post-presidency focus, giving him an indefinite position of power.

In an interview with the New York Post, Huckabee stated that Trump intends to remain influential following his departure from office in 2028. Huckabee said, “Anybody that knows him knows this is not a guy he’s ever going to retire. He’s not going to sit on a rocking chair on a front porch and just play golf once a week. He’s incapable of settling down like that.”

The Board of Peace, Trump claims, was built to assemble world leaders and international power brokers to address global conflicts. It requires a $1 billion contribution for membership, and was shunned by western leaders at an induction ceremony at the World Economic Forum in Davos last month.

But Huckabee said it could transition into a long-term personal initiative once Trump leaves the White House. Trump has already designated himself as the board’s indefinite chairman and indicated his preference for locating the organization’s headquarters in Washington.

Drawing on religious doctrine, Huckabee referenced biblical teachings about peacemaking. He stated: “There’s a scripture in the New Testament that says, ‘Blessed are the peacemakers.’ And unfortunately, a lot of people think that means, ‘Blessed are the peace-lovers.’ Well, everybody loves peace, but the people who love peace don’t necessarily make it.”

Huckabee continued, “You can give the peace sign, and you can wear symbols, and you get a tattoo on your arm saying, I love peace. That doesn’t make peace.”

“On the other hand, Trump wants to be a peacemaker.”

Huckabee credited Trump with resolving or reducing tensions in multiple international conflicts — though those claims have been doubted by multiple fact-checkers.

Our Analysis:

The “Board of Peace” or A Masterclass in Irony?

In an eye-opening revelation that seems to blur the lines between satire and reality, former US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee has served us a dish that’s hard to swallow without a hefty dose of skepticism. According to Huckabee, President Donald Trump, in a post-presidential pivot that could only be concocted in the most audacious of political laboratories, intends to morph into a global peacemaker via his “Board of Peace.” Yes, you read that right. Trump, a figure synonymous with division and controversy, is now fashioning himself as the architect of world peace. Let’s dissect this with the precision it demands.

The Billion Dollar Gatekeeper

First and foremost, the entry fee to this so-called peace council is a staggering $1 billion. This alone should raise eyebrows if not full-blown alarm bells. The notion that peace—a universal aspiration, free in its essence—has a price tag, and an exorbitant one at that, is ludicrous at best and deeply cynical at worst. It effectively turns peacemaking into an exclusive club for the ultra-wealthy and power brokers, sidelining the voices of those who are often most affected by conflict. The irony of establishing such a high barrier to entry in the name of peace is so thick you could slice it with a knife.

The Indefinite Chairman

Trump’s designation of himself as the indefinite chairman of the Board of Peace is as unsurprising as it is concerning. It screams of a desire for perpetual relevance and authority, wrapped in a veneer of altruism. By anchoring himself to this role, Trump seems to be seeking an unending tenure in the limelight, positioning himself as a central figure in global diplomacy without the inconvenience of democratic accountability. The fact that he’s eyeing Washington for its headquarters further cements his unwillingness to detach from the epicenter of power, despite no longer holding office.

The Misuse of Scripture

Huckabee’s invocation of New Testament scripture to bolster the legitimacy of this endeavor is a manipulative flourish that deserves scrutiny. The leap from “Blessed are the peacemakers” to the establishment of a billion-dollar, Trump-led peace initiative is a theological and logical gymnastics routine that should not go unchallenged. It’s a classic example of using religious sentiments as a shield for criticism, implying that any skepticism of Trump’s motives is akin to skepticism of the biblical endorsement of peace.

Fact-Checked to Oblivion

The claims of Trump’s success in resolving or reducing international conflicts, as touted by Huckabee, are, to put it mildly, contested. Multiple fact-checkers have thrown cold water on these assertions, highlighting the necessity of taking such statements with a grain of salt. The use of unsubstantiated claims to prop up Trump’s resume as a peacemaker does a disservice to the complex realities of international diplomacy and conflict resolution.

Conclusion

In the grand theatre of political narratives, the “Board of Peace” stands out as an audacious attempt to rebrand a deeply divisive figure as a beacon of global harmony. The contradictions inherent in its foundation—exorbitant membership fees, indefinite leadership, and the co-optation of religious teachings for political ends—are emblematic of a broader trend of exploiting noble ideals for questionable purposes.

As proponents of critical thinking and accountability, we must ask: who benefits from the establishment of such a board? Can peace truly be brokered in the shadows of power and wealth, or does this initiative risk becoming a mere extension of Trump’s quest for perpetual influence? The answers to these questions will undoubtedly shape the legacy of Trump’s post-presidency ambitions and the global perception of American diplomacy for years to come.
