Office Depot Stands Its Ground: Why Refusing to Print Charlie Kirk Propaganda Deserves Applause

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 13, 20257 min read

The Story That Sparked MAGA Outrage

On September 12, 2025, a seemingly mundane interaction at an Office Depot store in Portage, Michigan, became a flashpoint in America’s culture wars. Two employees refused to print posters for a vigil honoring Charlie Kirk, the far-right activist and founder of Turning Point USA, who had been fatally shot days earlier during a public event. Their reason was simple and blunt: “We don’t print propaganda.”

The request for the posters came from the Kalamazoo County Republican Party, which intended to distribute them for a Friday night prayer vigil. But when employees at Office Depot declined the order, the decision ignited immediate outrage across right-wing media ecosystems. Within hours, MAGA influencers, Republican politicians, and the professional outrage machine transformed this act of refusal into yet another rallying cry about “censorship” and “discrimination.”

But let’s strip away the MAGA hysteria and examine this for what it was: a rare and commendable act of conscience in a corporate retail environment that usually bows to customer pressure. Office Depot’s employees—and by extension the company—stood on principle. They refused to launder partisan propaganda under the false banner of a neutral “memorial.” For that, they deserve praise, not condemnation.

Propaganda by Any Other Name

The word “propaganda” has been so thoroughly diluted by political misuse that it’s worth defining clearly. Propaganda isn’t just false information; it’s communication designed to manipulate emotions and advance a political cause while disguising itself as something else. By that definition, Charlie Kirk’s vigil posters absolutely qualify.

Kirk was not a neutral public figure. He built his career attacking higher education, demonizing immigrants, undermining democracy, and trafficking in conspiracy theories. His Turning Point USA organization became infamous for pushing hard-right politics onto campuses under the guise of “free speech.” To pretend that a vigil in his honor was purely apolitical is to ignore the decades-long playbook of the right wing: weaponizing grief to sanctify their martyrs, and in doing so, recruiting more adherents to the cause.

When Office Depot employees said, “We don’t print propaganda,” they weren’t denying grief or mocking death. They were refusing to let their labor be co-opted into the political canonization of a man who thrived on division and disinformation. That distinction matters.

The MAGA Outrage Machine

Of course, refusing to comply with the demands of the MAGA machine guarantees one thing: instant vilification. Almost immediately, right-wing influencers and politicians descended like vultures.

  • Nick Sortor shrieked on X, “WTF?! Office Depot in Michigan is flat out REFUSING to print out Charlie Kirk tribute posters… These people need to be FIRED NOW.”

  • Libs of TikTok—a factory for online harassment campaigns—amplified the story, declaring “HOLY CRAP” at the refusal.

  • Sara Rose resorted to juvenile fat-shaming insults, calling the employees “disgusting fat libs.”

  • Matt Wallace exhorted his followers: “Internet, do your thing!”—a call to arms for harassment.

  • Even Rep. Bill Huizenga (R-MI) weighed in, labeling the refusal “absolutely shameful” and framing it as a customer rights violation.

Let’s pause here. These influencers and officials weren’t outraged at the murder of Charlie Kirk. They weren’t demanding stricter gun laws, better event security, or a culture less soaked in violent rhetoric. They were angry that two hourly retail workers exercised discretion over what they were willing to print. In other words, they redirected grief into grievance, weaponizing one man’s death to stoke their ongoing war against “woke corporations.”

This is the essence of the MAGA outrage machine: turn every inconvenience into persecution, every refusal into censorship, every act of conscience into an existential threat.

Corporate America Usually Caves—This Time, Not So Fast

Corporations, especially large chains, typically do one thing in the face of outrage: cave. The playbook is predictable. A controversy blows up on social media. Hashtags trend. Executives scramble into damage-control mode. An apology is issued. The employees involved are disciplined or fired. Case closed.

But Office Depot’s employees didn’t budge in the moment. And while the corporate office may eventually issue a bland statement to smooth things over, the fact remains: someone on the ground stood firm. That takes courage in today’s climate, where right-wing outrage can bring swarms of harassment, doxxing, and even threats of violence. It’s far easier to look the other way and print the damn posters.

Yet refusing to comply sent a message. It drew a boundary. It said: “Our workplace is not your propaganda factory.” That’s a stance worth defending.

The Right’s Hypocrisy on “Free Speech” and “Discrimination”

The irony of MAGA outrage is hard to miss. These are the same voices who, week after week, celebrate bakers refusing to make cakes for gay weddings, florists turning down LGBTQ+ customers, and businesses denying service on the grounds of “religious freedom.” The right has spent years arguing that businesses should not be compelled to provide services that conflict with their beliefs.

Yet when the shoe is on the other foot, suddenly the principle evaporates. When conservative politics are the product being rejected, it’s no longer “religious freedom” or “business rights.” It’s “censorship.” It’s “discrimination.” It’s “shameful.”

This hypocrisy reveals the truth: the right doesn’t care about free speech or freedom of association. They care about control. They demand a world where only their ideological allies can refuse service, while everyone else must comply. The Office Depot incident exposes this double standard in stark relief.

Office Depot Employees as Unsung Heroes

The refusal by these employees may seem small in the grand scheme of things. But in today’s political climate, it’s anything but trivial. It represents:

  1. Courage in the Workplace: Saying “no” to a partisan demand in a retail setting is rare and risky. These workers risked backlash, job security, and personal safety.

  2. Refusal to Normalize: Printing vigil posters for Charlie Kirk would normalize his legacy as something beyond politics, as though his career wasn’t defined by vitriol. Refusing to print is a refusal to sanitize.

  3. Solidarity with Reality: By calling it “propaganda,” the employees named the truth plainly. They rejected euphemisms and called a spade a spade.

This deserves applause. These employees may never be recognized formally, but they have already etched their names into the long line of small, everyday acts of resistance that matter more than grand gestures.

Why This Matters Beyond One Store

Some might argue: so what? What difference does refusing to print a poster really make? But it’s precisely these small acts that push back against the creep of authoritarian normalization. Fascism doesn’t arrive all at once; it advances by inches, disguised as ordinary requests, framed as harmless traditions. Printing a “tribute poster” here, endorsing a “patriotic rally” there, until suddenly the machinery of everyday life is in service to the propaganda state.

By refusing to print, those employees slowed the machinery. They denied the normalizing of Kirk as a martyr whose death demands political reverence. They disrupted the seamless blending of grief and recruitment that the right counts on in moments like this.

A Call to Action: Support Office Depot

If MAGA world can swarm Office Depot with hate, the rest of us can balance the scales with support. Here’s how:

  • Public Praise: Post on social media congratulating Office Depot employees for standing firm. Make sure the story of their courage circulates beyond MAGA outrage bubbles.

  • Customer Loyalty: If you have an Office Depot or OfficeMax nearby, consider directing some of your business their way. Support matters when corporations are under pressure.

  • Pressure Corporate HQ: Just as MAGA influencers pressure corporations to cave, progressives should pressure them to stand by their employees. Send emails, make calls, demand that no one be punished for refusing to print propaganda.

The right has mastered the art of outrage mobilization. The left needs to master the art of defense and solidarity.

Conclusion: Applause Where It’s Due

In the end, this isn’t just a story about a store refusing to print a poster. It’s about the creeping normalization of propaganda, the weaponization of grief, and the willingness of ordinary people to resist being co-opted into that machinery. Office Depot’s employees drew a line in the sand and said: not here, not today.

For that, they deserve our thanks and congratulations. They stood up to the outrage machine and, in doing so, reminded us that conscience still has a place—even in the aisles of a big-box store.

So here’s to Office Depot. In a week dominated by the grim spectacle of Charlie Kirk’s death and the cynical exploitation of it by his allies, two employees reminded us that resistance is possible. And sometimes, resistance looks as simple—and as powerful—as saying: “We don’t print propaganda.”
