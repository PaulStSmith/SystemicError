'Junk poll!' GOP panics as Trump's popularity with essential voting bloc plummets

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump’s favorability among a key voter demographic has plummeted according to new polling acquired by Politico and reported on Monday, leading to Republicans lashing out at the findings as “junk” as the 2026 midterm elections draw closer.

According to new polling conducted by a Latino voter group between Aug. 26 and Sept. 4, Trump’s favorability among Hispanic voters has fallen significantly since he took office, dropping from a net-negative 12 points in February to net-negative 20 in September.

Support from Latino voters was credited as being vital to Trump's November election victory.

Mike Marinella, the national press secretary for the National Republican Congressional Committee, called the findings a “junk poll,” and told Politico that it proved “Democrats have no message, no leader, and no plan to stop Hispanic voters from moving toward Republicans.”

He went on to flatly reject the poll’s findings, even though the results closely mirrored a CNN survey last week showing similar declines in Trump’s approval among Hispanic voters.

“Democrats are desperate to spin a false narrative about Hispanic voters, but the reality is simple: Republicans continue earning their trust while Democrats have been losing it for years,” Marinella told Politico.

While Trump’s favorability with all Hispanic voters dropped significantly during his first six months in office, among key demographics within the Hispanic community the drop was even greater. For young Hispanic voters, Trump’s favorability fell from 43% in February to 33% in September, and with Hispanic men, from 52% to 47%.

And it’s this drop in support among Hispanic voters, argued Melissa Morales, president of Somos Votantes, the Latino voter group that conducted the new polling, that could open a “huge opportunity” for Democratic candidates come next year.

“What began earlier this year with independents and women has really intensified and spread to basically every demographic subset of the Latino electorate, including groups that once leaned toward him like Latino men,” Morales said, speaking with Politico.

“This won’t automatically translate to support for Democrats, but there is a huge opportunity to turn what was a liability into a positive. There is an opportunity here for Democrats to show an alternative.”

The dramatic shift in favorability for Trump among Hispanic voters could also bode poorly for all Republican candidates in 2026, Morales added.

“Voters are really equating Republicans and Trump as one in the same,” she said.

Our Analysis:

The Political Pendulum Swings: Trump's Hispanic Voter Dilemma

In a recent revelation that has sent shockwaves through the Republican echelons, President Donald Trump’s favorability amongst Hispanic voters has taken a nosedive. This is not just a minor hiccup but a plummeting descent from a net-negative 12 points in February to net-negative 20 in September, according to polling conducted by a Latino voter group and reported by Politico. This demographic, once a bastion of vital support for Trump's November election victory, now seems to be retracting their endorsement at an alarming rate.

The GOP's Predictable Denial

In what can only be described as a textbook case of denial, the National Republican Congressional Committee's response, spearheaded by Mike Marinella, was to decry the findings as “junk.” This desperate attempt to discredit the polling is as predictable as it is flawed. Marinella's assertion that "Democrats have no message, no leader, and no plan" is a glaring attempt to deflect from the core issue - the significant erosion of Hispanic support for Trump. The irony of dismissing the poll’s findings while they align closely with a CNN survey from the previous week showcasing similar declines is palpable. Republicans' knee-jerk reaction to discredit unfavorable news rather than addressing the underlying issues is a dangerous gambit as the 2026 midterm elections loom on the horizon.

The Great Hispanic Voter Shift

The detailed breakdown of the polling reveals even more concerning trends for Trump and, by extension, the Republican Party. Among young Hispanic voters and Hispanic men, there's been a significant drop in Trump’s favorability, from 43% to 33% and from 52% to 47%, respectively. These figures aren't just numbers; they represent a seismic shift in voter sentiment within a key demographic that was once considered a stronghold for Trump.

Melissa Morales, president of Somos Votantes, the Latino voter group behind the new polling, posits that this shift presents a "huge opportunity" for Democratic candidates. Morales' analysis cuts to the heart of the matter – the decline in Hispanic support for Trump is symptomatic of a broader disenchantment that, if leveraged correctly by Democrats, could turn a significant liability into a formidable asset.

The Conflation of Trump and the GOP

Perhaps the most damning indictment from the polling is the suggestion that voters are increasingly equating Republicans with Trump. This conflation is a double-edged sword. While it may have bolstered the GOP's fortunes while Trump's star was ascendant, it now threatens to drag down the party's prospects across the board.

Final Thoughts

The dramatic shift in favorability among Hispanic voters for Trump - and by extension, the GOP - is not just a blip on the political radar. It is a clarion call for the Republican Party to reassess its strategies and messaging. The GOP's dismissal of the polling as "junk" and the insistence on a narrative of Democrats lacking a message or a plan is a misguided smoke screen. The real story here is the GOP's failure to maintain the trust and support of a key voter demographic. As the 2026 midterm elections approach, this could spell trouble not just for Trump but for Republican candidates across the board. The political pendulum is swinging, and it waits for no one. Will the GOP heed the warning, or will they continue to bury their heads in the sand? Only time will tell, but as things stand, the future looks increasingly uncertain for Trump’s Republican Party.
