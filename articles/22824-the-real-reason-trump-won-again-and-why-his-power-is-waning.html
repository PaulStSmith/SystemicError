The real reason Trump won again —and why his power is waning

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 31, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What the Source SaysWho Actually Holds PowerThe Scam Is the StoryThe Democratic Failure Is Not the CenterpieceCruelty With a BrandThe Larger Pattern

Trump Is the Symptom. The Theft Came First.

What the Source Says

The piece contrasts a figure of civic generosity and democratic idealism with Trump’s documented vanity and cruelty, then argues that his return is not an isolated accident but the product of decades of wealth transfer upward, economic betrayal, and organized scapegoating. It also notes his AI-generated White House fantasy, his boast on Glenn Beck’s show, and the coming election as a chance to contain the damage.

Who Actually Holds Power

The article is right to place Trump inside a larger structure, but it still risks making him look like the whole machine. He is not. He is the loudest frontman for a coalition of money, grievance, and institutional cowardice that has been building for decades.

The source’s own numbers point to the real story: roughly $80 trillion moved from the bottom 90 percent to the richest 1 percent since the late 1970s. That is not a mood swing. That is a governing project. Trump did not invent it. He weaponized it.

The Scam Is the Story

The core political trick is simple: rob people, then train them to blame one another for being robbed.

Undocumented workers, transgender people, Black people, Latinos, Muslims, “the deep state,” “communists,” Democrats, even Canada, all get cycled through the same script of resentment. The target changes. The function does not. Scapegoating keeps attention pointed downward and sideways while power moves upward and stays there.

That is why Trump remains useful to wealth even when he looks chaotic. His incompetence is not a bug in the system. It is part of the cover. A reckless strongman can do enormous damage while making the damage seem like personality, not policy.

The Democratic Failure Is Not the Centerpiece

The source gestures at Biden, a mini-primary, Harris, and campaign strategy. Those are real questions, but they are not the main explanation. The deeper failure is that Democrats still too often behave as if the problem is messaging rather than material betrayal.

People do not need another sermon about American ideals. They need proof that government can reverse theft, punish predation, and widen security. When democratic politics is reduced to candidate management and procedural caution, it leaves room for demagogues to pose as truth-tellers while serving the same wealthy interests.

The source is strongest when it names the theft. It is weaker when it lets political process absorb too much blame and too little responsibility.

Cruelty With a Brand

Trump’s AI fantasy image of himself arm in arm with a dead public figure is not a quirky media anecdote. It is a perfect expression of his politics: appropriation without consent, image without reality, domination dressed up as familiarity.

His radio boast that she “really liked” him fits the same pattern. He treats memory as raw material for self-promotion. He treats public life as a mirror. That is how authoritarian politics works at the level of culture: it colonizes symbols, empties them of meaning, and uses the wreckage to sell grievance back to the public.

The source calls this cruelty and incompetence. That is accurate. It should also be called political vandalism.

The Larger Pattern

The story is not really about one man, or one election, or even one era. It is about what happens when concentrated wealth destroys the social bargain, then funds a politics of resentment to keep people from looking up.

That is the recurring American pattern: extraction, scapegoating, and spectacle. Trump is simply the most brazen salesman for it. The democratic response cannot be nostalgia for a cleaner past that never fully existed. It has to be a direct fight over power, redistribution, and whose lives the state is built to protect.

If the country wants out of this cycle, it will not come from better vibes or nicer rhetoric. It will come from breaking the coalition that profits from making ordinary people hate each other while the theft continues above them.
