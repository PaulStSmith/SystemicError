A Golden Throne for a Starving Nation

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

While 42 million Americans brace for hunger, Donald Trump is busy showcasing his latest bathroom renovations. The timing would be comedic if it weren’t a grotesque reflection of everything wrong with the American political order: a billionaire demagogue parading his gilded toilet while millions wonder how they’ll eat tomorrow. This isn’t irony. It’s indictment.

Halloween was supposed to be the night of manufactured fear — ghosts, ghouls, and overpriced candy. But the real horror came the next morning, November 1st, when federal SNAP benefits were set to run out of funding due to the government shutdown. Forty-two million people — children, veterans, the working poor — left staring into the abyss of an empty pantry, while the self-proclaimed champion of the forgotten man bragged about marble finishes and toilet placement in the Lincoln bathroom.

Trump’s True Social feed became a grotesque gallery of priorities: photo after photo of opulence, posted the very day before food assistance was to expire. It was as if Marie Antoinette had returned, armed with Wi-Fi and a selfie stick, declaring, “Let them admire my fixtures.”

Meanwhile, Republicans pretended helplessness, claiming their hands were tied. Somehow, the same hands that write trillion-dollar tax cuts for the wealthy and bailouts for corporations suddenly couldn’t hold a pen to feed the poor. The excuses flowed faster than champagne at Mar-a-Lago: “We can’t use emergency funds now,” they bleated, “because what if there’s an emergency later?”

The absurdity would be laughable if it weren’t lethal. America’s emergency is now. It’s families choosing between groceries and rent. It’s single mothers watering down milk for their kids. It’s seniors skipping meals so their prescriptions will last. Yet to this administration, hunger is not an emergency — but a parade, a jet retrofit, or a $350 million ballroom somehow is.

And then, as if by cosmic satire, two federal judges ruled that SNAP funding must continue. Relief for millions, yes — but also a convenient political escape hatch for Republicans who can now claim victory without admitting defeat. “The judges made us do it,” they can say, as though basic humanity required judicial authorization.

When confronted with this ruling, they didn’t celebrate. They filed appeals. Because nothing says ‘party of family values’ like suing for the right to let families starve.

This is the GOP’s moral universe: a world where compassion is a liability, greed is governance, and empathy is replaced by spreadsheet arithmetic. They wring their hands about fiscal responsibility as they torch billions on military toys and corporate subsidies. They talk about “helping those truly in need” as if hunger were a moral failing to be audited, not a national disgrace to be remedied.

Meanwhile, Trump remains the patron saint of tone-deafness, the man who would’ve tweeted about his soft landing on 9/11. He’s turned narcissism into a political ideology — an entire movement built on the theology of self-worship. While the poor go hungry, he posts about tiles. While democracy starves, he feeds his ego.

There’s something almost mythic about it — not in grandeur but in grotesquery. The golden toilet has become America’s new national emblem: opulent, ornamental, and utterly useless to those who can’t afford to flush.

His enablers cheer from the sidelines, praising “elegance” and “beauty,” mistaking decay for design. Carolyn Leavitt gushes over his “beautiful transformation,” as if that toilet were the Ark of the Covenant. In truth, it’s the perfect symbol of his presidency: a monument to waste built on the suffering of others.

And so, on the eve of hunger, Trump flushed empathy down the drain. The Republicans stood guard at the door, moral plumbers of the apocalypse, making sure no crumbs of decency clogged the system.

The courts may have bought the nation a reprieve. But make no mistake: the hunger remains — not just for food, but for justice, for leadership, for a country that measures greatness not in gold leaf but in how it treats its most vulnerable.

The next time you hear a politician talk about “budget priorities,” remember this Halloween farce. Remember the golden bathroom. Remember the excuses. Remember the lawsuits to stop feeding the poor. Because in this America, hunger isn’t an accident — it’s policy. And luxury isn’t a reward — it’s the point.

When history writes its verdict, it won’t ask about the marble veins in Trump’s bathroom. It will ask why a nation let millions starve while worshiping the man who turned cruelty into interior design.

America’s real crisis isn’t fiscal. It’s moral. And the stench doesn’t come from the drain — it comes from the throne.
