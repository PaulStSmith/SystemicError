MAHA’s treatments for autism: Camel’s milk, stem cell injections — and spelling therapy

The Controversy of Control: How Autism Treatment Debates Reflect Deeper Sociopolitical Struggles

Power in Professional Hands

Elizabeth Bonker’s push for governmental support of assisted spelling—a controversial communication method for nonverbal people with autism—highlights a significant clash between institutional medical authority and advocacy groups. The primary power holders here are the established professional organizations, such as those representing autism science, psychologists, and speech pathologists, which wield significant influence over what therapies receive endorsement and funding. These groups base their authority on research and widely accepted scientific methods, positioning themselves as the gatekeepers of legitimate medical practices.

Decisions Framed as Science vs. Pseudoscience

The decision to support or reject assisted spelling as a valid therapeutic method is ostensibly based on scientific validity and patient safety. Professional organizations cite a lack of evidence and potential for harm, deeming the practice akin to pseudoscience. However, this framing oversimplifies and somewhat misdirects the issue by not fully addressing the nuanced needs and varied responses of individuals with autism to different treatments. This approach also marginalizes the voices of those who report personal success with the method, like Bonker and others, by treating their experiences as anecdotal or irrelevant.

Misdirection and Erasure of Patient Agency

The debate often misdirects public understanding by framing the controversy solely in terms of scientific legitimacy, overshadowing important discussions about autonomy, patient rights, and the diversity of patient needs. Critics argue that assisted spelling doesn’t allow for autonomous communication, suggesting that facilitators could be influencing the communication unduly. This concern is valid but also removes agency from the nonverbal individuals who might choose this method as their preferred way to communicate, as long as it is genuinely their choice.

A Broader Pattern of Dismissal

This situation is emblematic of a broader pattern where medical and governmental institutions often dismiss patient and caregiver experiences that deviate from the established norm. The controversy surrounding assisted spelling is not just about the method itself but also about who gets to decide which treatments are valid and how those with disabilities are allowed to interact with the world. It reflects a paternalistic approach to medicine and disability rights, where ‘expert’ opinions are valued over the lived experiences of those directly affected.

Conclusion: Beyond the Binary of Right and Wrong

The assisted spelling controversy should not be seen merely as a debate between right and wrong but as a complex interaction of power, scientific authority, and the struggle for autonomy and recognition. It serves as a reminder that in the realm of treatment and care, especially concerning conditions as diverse as autism, a one-size-fits-all approach is neither practical nor respectful. Moving forward, it is crucial to foster an environment where multiple voices, including those of the patients and their families, are heard and considered in shaping practices that deeply affect their lives and identities. Policies and practices must evolve to reflect a balance between professional guidance and patient autonomy, ensuring that all individuals have the right to choose their paths to communication and interaction.
