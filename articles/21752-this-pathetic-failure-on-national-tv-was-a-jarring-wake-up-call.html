Todd Blanche and the Senate’s Theater of Surrender

A nominee that should have ended the hearing

Todd Blanche arrived at the Senate Judiciary Committee with a record that should have made confirmation politically impossible: a documented role in burying Epstein files, emails showing him running Trump’s retribution operation, and a recent federal ruling that sent his conduct toward state bar scrutiny. That is not a normal résumé for attorney general. It is disqualifying conduct wearing a suit.

Instead, the hearing became a demonstration of how little accountability survives when power decides it does not need to be embarrassed.

Who actually held power

The central fact of this story is simple: Blanche was not the weak actor in the room. He was the person under scrutiny, but he was there because Donald Trump wanted him there, and because Senate Republicans were prepared to clear the path. That is where the institutional power sat. Not with the senators asking questions. Not with the survivors whose names were exposed in the Epstein redactions. Not with the former Justice Department employees who signed their names to a warning. Power sat with the president, the chairman, and the committee majority that treated this as a procedural obstacle rather than a moral emergency.

Blanche did not need to win the argument. He only needed Democrats to fail to make his confirmation toxic.

Democrats brought fragments, not a case

The most damning part of the hearing is not that Blanche lied or dodged. That was predictable. The failure was that Democrats never forced him to answer as a single, coherent defendant in an abuse-of-power case.

There were isolated moments of pressure. Cory Booker seemed to understand the stakes. Adam Schiff pressed on the split between Blanche’s old prosecutor image and his current role as Trump’s shield. Dick Durbin, Chris Coons, Peter Welch, Sheldon Whitehouse each found something to say. None of it added up to a disciplined prosecution. They took turns. They did not build momentum.

That matters. A scandal only becomes a political consequence when the target is cornered, not merely interrupted.

The misdirection worked

Blanche was supposed to answer for three things: the Epstein redactions, the anti-weaponization fund, and the pardons of January 6th criminals and extremists. Instead, he got to retreat into familiar evasions. “Mistakes were made” becomes the language of bureaucratic violence when nobody forces the question of who made them, who approved them, and who benefited.

The hearing also drifted into side quests that served Blanche’s interests. Mel Gibson. Kash Patel. Gun rights. None of that was the point. The point was whether a man who has spent his recent career protecting Trump can credibly run the Justice Department as something other than Trump’s personal legal apparatus. Every minute spent elsewhere was a minute Blanche did not have to explain his allegiance.

Republicans understood the assignment

Republicans were not confused. They were organized. Chairman Grassley opened by praising Blanche as “well qualified,” then pivoted to attacking Democrats for calling the department a disgrace. That is the oldest trick in the playbook: shift attention from the misconduct to the people naming it.

Even the sharper Republican questioning did not amount to resistance. Cornyn and Tillis pressed Blanche on the anti-weaponization fund, but the larger arc still pointed toward advancement, not rejection. The majority did what majorities do when they have decided the answer in advance: they created the appearance of scrutiny while protecting the outcome.

What this hearing exposed

This was not just a bad confirmation hearing. It was a demonstration of institutional cowardice. Blanche is being positioned to become attorney general while still behaving like Trump’s lawyer, not the nation’s top law officer. That is the real scandal: the deliberate conversion of law enforcement into an instrument of personal loyalty.

The deeper pattern is not simply that Democrats failed to land a blow. It is that the Senate keeps normalizing authoritarian conduct by processing it as if it were routine personnel business. The country is not being ruined by one man alone. It is being hollowed out by the officials who know exactly what he is and confirm him anyway.
