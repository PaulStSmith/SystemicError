'We don’t have much time': George Conway issues dire warning about Donald Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 4, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Urgency of Impeachment: A Critical Examination

George Conway (MSNBC)

Republican never-Trump attorney and critic turned Democratic congressional candidate George Conway issued a dire warning on Monday about President Donald Trump and his “megalomania.”

“The way things are going in America, it should be clear we don’t have much time,” Conway wrote on social media. “We certainly don’t have three years. We need to help ourselves by pushing for impeachment and removal as hard as we can and carrying it out as soon as humanly possible.”

Reiterating that he sees this as “a race against time,” Conway asked, “How quickly does the megalomaniac lose strength versus how quickly he destroy[s] everything around him. The one thing you can depend on is that the megalomaniac gets more destructive and dangerous over time before he’s done.”

Conway kicked off his social media thread with a New York Times opinion piece by history professor Dr. Ruth Ben-Ghiat, a scholar on fascism and authoritarian leaders.

He quoted Dr. Ben-Ghiat, who wrote: “I have seen this brand of strongman megalomania and the adverse effects it can ultimately have on leaders and their governments. I call it autocratic backfire.”

“As autocrats surround themselves with loyalists who praise them and party functionaries who repeat their lies, leaders can start to believe their own hype,” the excerpt continued. “As they cut themselves off from expert advice and objective feedback, they start to promulgate unscrutinized policies that fail. Rather than course correct, such leaders often double down and engage in even riskier behavior — starting wars or escalating involvement in military conflicts that eventually reveal the human and financial tolls of their corruption and incompetence. The result: a disillusioned population that loses faith in the leader and elites who begin to rethink their support.”

Conway added that the word “megalomania” is “essentially a synonym for narcissistic sociopathy or malignant narcissism.”

“All three terms accurately describe Trump,” he charged.

He offered some “good news,” saying that, as Ben-Ghiat pointed out, “megalomaniacal leaders ultimately blow themselves up politically or militarily. The bad news is that the longer they survive, the bigger the figurative blast radius.”

Conway ended the social media thread by saying this is why he is running for Congress and posted a link to his campaign website.

Our Analysis:

The Urgency of Impeachment: A Critical Examination

George Conway’s transition from a Republican never-Trump attorney to a Democratic congressional candidate is a journey that, in itself, speaks volumes about the current political landscape. His recent dire warnings about President Donald Trump’s “megalomania” are not just political rhetoric; they are a clarion call to action underscored by a deep concern for the trajectory of American democracy.

Conway’s Warning: A Race Against Time

Conway’s assertion that “we don’t have much time” and the push for impeachment should be carried out “as soon as humanly possible” could not be more stark. He frames the situation as a “race against time,” a battle between how quickly Trump’s megalomania escalates and how swiftly he can be restrained by constitutional measures. This is not merely a political stunt; it is a dire warning from someone who has closely observed Trump’s rise and behavior. The use of the term “megalomania” is particularly telling. It’s not everyday political jargon but a serious diagnosis that suggests a dangerous level of self-obsession and power lust that threatens the fabric of American governance.

The Scholarly Backing: Dr. Ruth Ben-Ghiat’s Analysis

Conway’s argument gains substantial weight from his reference to Dr. Ruth Ben-Ghiat, a noted historian on fascism and authoritarian leaders. Ben-Ghiat’s analysis of “autocratic backfire,” where leaders become victims of their own hubris, is a historical pattern that seems to be repeating itself. The description of autocrats cutting themselves off from expert advice, engaging in risky behavior, and eventually facing a disillusioned population, mirrors the trajectory of Trump’s presidency. This scholarly backing adds a layer of gravitas to Conway’s warnings, grounding them in historical precedent rather than mere partisan critique.

Megalomania and Trump: A Dangerous Combination

Conway’s bold claim that “megalomania” is synonymous with “narcissistic sociopathy or malignant narcissism” and that all three terms accurately describe Trump is a scathing indictment. Such psychological terms are not wielded lightly, indicating a profound concern for Trump’s mental fitness and his capacity for rational decision-making. This is a significant charge, suggesting that Trump’s presidency is not just politically detrimental but is fundamentally dangerous.

The Silver Lining and the Ultimate Warning

Interestingly, Conway offers a glimmer of hope, citing Ben-Ghiat’s observation that megalomaniacal leaders often “blow themselves up politically or militarily.” However, he is quick to temper this with the “bad news” - the longer these leaders remain in power, the more extensive the damage. This is a critical insight, highlighting the importance of timely action to mitigate the fallout of Trump’s presidency.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

In concluding his argument with a link to his campaign website, Conway makes it clear that his critique is not just an academic exercise but a call to arms. His decision to run for Congress is presented as a direct response to the threat posed by Trump’s continued influence, suggesting that political engagement and representation are the antidotes to megalomania in power.

In essence, George Conway’s warnings are a potent mix of political urgency, scholarly insight, and personal conviction. They serve as a stark reminder of the stakes involved in American democracy and the need for vigilant, immediate action. Whether one agrees with his political transition or not, the clarity and intensity of his message are undeniable. It’s a call that, regardless of political affiliation, demands serious consideration.
