WSJ editorial warns new Trump 'assault' will haunt America: 'Terribly damaging'

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he attends the National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC) dinner at the National Building Museum in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 8, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump is playing with fire by escalating his war against Harvard, the conservative Wall Street Journal editorial board warned in an analysis published on Friday evening.

The president has done everything in his power to try to bring Harvard to heel after the prestigious Boston-area Ivy League university refused to kowtow to his demands for eliminating diversity policies and cracking down on student protests, or to turn over confidential records of foreign students. He revoked billions of dollars in scientific research funding at Harvard and moved to revoke the university's tax-exempt status, and this week, he upped the ante further by revoking Harvard's ability to accept international students altogether — a measure already blocked in federal court.

It's that last step in particular, the board warned, that will sabotage America's future viability as a place for scientific innovation.

"That’s 6,800 students, or a quarter of Harvard’s student body, whose futures are suddenly in disarray. It’s also a short-sighted attack on one of America’s great competitive strengths: Its ability to attract the world’s best and brightest," wrote the board. It's likely Harvard will prevail in its lawsuit, they continued, but regardless, "This will be terribly damaging to America’s ability to attract talented young people who bring their enterprise and intellectual capital to the U.S. Non-citizens accounted for more than half of doctoral degrees in AI-related fields in 2022. Many have gone to work at U.S. companies like Nvidia or started their own."

"The National Foundation for American Policy finds that 'immigrants have founded or cofounded nearly two-thirds (65% or 28 of 43) of the top AI companies in the United States, and 70% of full-time graduate students in fields related to artificial intelligence are international students,'" wrote the board. "Immigrants have also started more than half of America’s privately-held startups valued at $1 billion or more."

And Trump's attack on Harvard signals to future international students that they should choose other countries instead. This "assault on immigrant talent," the board continued, simply isn't worth the pain it will cause.

"Like most of U.S. higher education, Harvard needed a jolt to return to its mission of educating open minds," the board concluded. "But that requires reform. The Trump Administration seems to think it needs to destroy Harvard to save it. This is the opposite of making America great."

Our Analysis:

Trump's War on Harvard: A Misguided Assault on America's Intellectual Future

President Donald Trump's latest maneuvers against Harvard University are not just a personal vendetta; they are emblematic of a broader, more insidious agenda to undermine the very foundations of America's global intellectual prestige. The Wall Street Journal's editorial board, hardly a bastion of progressive thought, has rightly sounded the alarm on Trump's escalating conflict with one of the nation's most esteemed academic institutions. But let's dissect this with the precision it deserves, because the implications are far-reaching and the strategy, if one can call it that, is profoundly misguided.

The Attack on Diversity and International Talent

Trump's efforts to bully Harvard into submission by revoking billions in scientific research funding and threatening its tax-exempt status are alarming enough. But his decision to revoke the university's ability to accept international students is a new low. This move, thankfully blocked in federal court, would have stripped Harvard of 6,800 students, or a quarter of its student body, throwing their futures into disarray. It's a stark reminder of the administration's disdain for diversity and global engagement.

But why does this matter? It matters because America's competitive edge on the global stage has always been bolstered by its ability to attract the world's best and brightest. The Wall Street Journal editorial board points out, "Non-citizens accounted for more than half of doctoral degrees in AI-related fields in 2022," highlighting the essential role international talent plays in the U.S.'s technological and scientific advancement. Immigrants are not just participants in America's educational system; they are leaders in innovation, founding or co-founding nearly two-thirds of the top AI companies in the United States.

The Short-sightedness of Trump's Assault

Trump's attack on Harvard is emblematic of a broader assault on immigrant talent and, by extension, on America's ability to lead in the global innovation economy. The message sent to future international students and innovators is clear and chilling: look elsewhere. This policy doesn't just undermine Harvard; it undermines the entire U.S. higher education system and, by extension, the country's economic and intellectual future.

The Wall Street Journal editorial concludes, "Like most of U.S. higher education, Harvard needed a jolt to return to its mission of educating open minds. But that requires reform." Indeed, reform is necessary in many aspects of American higher education, from addressing ballooning tuition costs to ensuring that campuses remain venues for free exchange of ideas. However, Trump's approach is not reformative; it's destructive. It seeks not to bolster the strengths of American higher education but to dismantle them.

Conclusion: The Misguided Mission to "Save" Harvard

The irony of Trump's mission to "save" Harvard by destroying it cannot be overstated. The misguided belief that America can maintain its global standing by isolating itself from international talent and stifling diversity is a recipe for decline. This strategy, if unchecked, will not only diminish Harvard but also erode America's position as a beacon of innovation and free thought.

The administration's actions reflect a profound misunderstanding of what makes America great. It is not uniformity or insularity that has propelled the United States to the forefront of scientific, technological, and economic leadership; it is diversity, openness, and the ability to attract and nurture talent from around the world. Trump's war on Harvard is, in essence, a war on these values. It is a policy path that leads not to greatness, but to mediocrity.

In the final analysis, the Wall Street Journal's warning should be heeded not just by those who care about the future of Harvard, but by all who value America's role as a leader in global innovation. Trump's approach isn't just playing with fire; it's threatening to burn down the very institutions and principles that have made America a beacon of hope and progress for the world.
