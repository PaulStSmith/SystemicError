Judges who rule against Trump face ominous threats: 'Somebody is going to get killed'

The families of at least 11 judges who have ruled against Donald Trump's administration have been targeted with threats and harassment, including sinister pizza deliveries, continuing an alarming trend related to the president and his legal troubles.

Tech mogul Elon Musk painted a target on the back of U.S. District judge James Boasberg's daughter after the judge ruled last month that administration officials could face criminal contempt charges for deporting migrants in defiance of a court order, and Trump supporters lashed out, reported Reuters.

"The president’s supporters unleashed a wave of threats and menacing posts," the outlet reported. "And they didn’t just target the judge. Some attacked Boasberg’s brother. Others blasted his daughter. Some demanded the family’s arrest – or execution."

The same thing happened to U.S. District judge John McConnell’s family after he ruled that Trump had overstepped his authority by freezing federal grants for education and other services, and far-right influencer Laura Loomer tweeted a photo of his daughter, who had worked as a policy adviser for the U.S. Education Department, and Musk amplified that post to his 219 million X followers.

"Loomer continued her attacks with nine more posts in the ensuing days – and more than 600 calls and emails flooded McConnell’s Rhode Island courthouse, including death threats and menacing messages taunting his family, according to a court clerk and another person familiar with the communications."

At least 60 judges or appeals courts have paused or blocked some of the Trump administration's actions in his first 100 days back in office, and Reuters spoke to a dozen federal judges who expressed concern about the safety of their families or others involved in Trump-related cases.

"[These threats] are ultimately threats to constitutional government – it’s as simple as that,'" said U.S. Circuit judge Richard Sullivan, who chairs a security committee for the federal judiciary’s policymaking arm.

U.S. Marshals have been investigating a string of ominous Domino's Pizza deliveries to the homes of U.S. Supreme Court justice Amy Coney Barrett, after she ruled against the administration's freeze of foreign aid, and several other judges who have ruled against the administration.

"Reuters identified at least a dozen judges who, after ruling against the Trump administration, have had pizzas delivered to their homes by anonymous senders – a tactic law enforcement characterized as a form of intimidation meant to convey that a target’s address is known," the outlet reported.

The pizza deliveries were ordered using the name Daniel Anderl, the late son of U.S. District judge Esther Salas who was killed by a disgruntled lawyer from a case heard by his mother.

The assailant had posed as a pizza delivery driver and fatally shot the 20-year-old and wounded the judge's husband before killing himself.

"Another federal judge told Reuters that someone using his spouse’s name and email address arranged a pizza delivery to his home after he ruled against a Trump administration action in March," the outlet reported. "His spouse was 'alarmed and chilled' that a stranger knew her personal information, the judge said."

Marshals have reviewed security precautions at the spouse's workplace and alerted local police, and the judges who spoke to Reuters say the president's inflammatory rhetoric and highly personal attacks on the judiciary would eventually lead to violence.

“Somebody is going to get killed,” said the judge whose wife was targeted with a pizza delivery. “That’s where it all leads eventually, and I think that’s what we’re all terrified about.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Threats Against Judges in the Wake of Trump Administration Rulings

In an age where the boundaries between political dissent and outright harassment blur, the recent targeting of judges and their families—who have ruled unfavorably against the Trump administration—marks a dangerous escalation in the tactics employed by some of the president's most fervent supporters. This concerted effort to intimidate and harass members of the judiciary through threats, menacing messages, and bizarrely, sinister pizza deliveries, is not just disturbing but a stark indicator of how the tenor of political discourse has shifted under Trump's aegis.

The Weaponization of Information by High-Profile Figures

The involvement of figures like Elon Musk and Laura Loomer in amplifying attacks against judges and their families is particularly egregious. Musk's targeting of U.S. District Judge James Boasberg's daughter and Loomer's harassment of Judge John McConnell’s family underline a troubling trend where influential personalities use their platforms to encourage or partake in personal attacks. This is not mere criticism or political activism; it is an affront to the principles of democracy and the sanctity of individual privacy. The fact that Musk amplified a post to his 219 million X followers is a testament to the reckless disregard for the consequences of such actions.

The Broader Implications for Judicial Independence

The harassment faced by these judges and their families is emblematic of a larger assault on the judiciary's independence. When judges are targeted for simply performing their duties—interpreting and applying the law without fear or favor—it sends a chilling message not only to current members of the bench but also to anyone who might consider a judicial career. This is not merely about the individuals involved; it's an attack on the very foundation of the constitutional order.

The Role of the President's Rhetoric

The article points to the inflammatory rhetoric and personal attacks by President Trump as a catalyst for these threats. The connection between the president's words and the subsequent actions of his supporters cannot be overstated. When the leader of a country engages in or condones personal attacks against judges, it legitimizes such behavior among the populace. The judges' concern—that this could eventually lead to violence—is not unfounded. The mention of Daniel Anderl, the late son of U.S. District Judge Esther Salas, is a harrowing reminder of the real-world consequences of such targeted harassment.

The Use of Intimidation Tactics

The peculiar tactic of ordering pizzas to judges' homes, using the names of their family members or victims of related violence, is a macabre form of intimidation. It's not just about the inconvenience or the cost; it's a message that the perpetrators know where they live and are willing to invade their personal lives. Such tactics are designed to instill fear and uncertainty, making it clear that their professional decisions have personal repercussions.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Protection

This situation demands a robust response, not only from law enforcement agencies but also from political leaders across the spectrum. The U.S. Marshals' investigation and the judicial system's examination of security measures are steps in the right direction, but more needs to be done to protect judges and their families from harassment and threats. Moreover, figures like Musk and Loomer, who use their platforms to amplify these attacks, must be held accountable for their actions. Their influence is not a license to endanger others.

In the final analysis, the harassment of judges over their rulings against the Trump administration is a symptom of a deeper malaise affecting American political culture. It is a test of the nation's commitment to the rule of law and the independence of the judiciary. How the country responds to this challenge will say much about the health of its democracy and the values it purports to uphold.
