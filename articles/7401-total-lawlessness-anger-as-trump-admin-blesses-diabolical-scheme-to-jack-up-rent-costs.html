But Trump assistant attorney general Abigail Slater announced that her antitrust division at the department of justice had settled the lawsuit with a sweet deal for Realpage that exacted no penalties and extracted no admission of wrongdoing from the company.

“The cost of living is top of mind as we continue to do our important work at the antitrust division. Thank you for your attention to this matter and thanksgiving wishes to you all — even the haters,” said Slater, while agreeing to a “settlement” that Realpage company attorney Stephen Weissman described as a “blessing.”

Meanwhile, MS NOW anchor Ali Velshi said nine states that were part of that lawsuit against Realpage’s rent-hiking practices have rebelled against the settlement and refused to sign on to it.

“In a healthy democracy, antitrust enforcers are supposed to protect consumers like you. In reality, it looks like they’re working against you,” reported Velshi. “That this is all happening while millions of people struggle to pay for rent, groceries and health insurance is shameful. That President Trump openly mocks the affordability crisis while his antitrust people help fuel it is diabolical.”

Former federal trade commissioner Lina Khan agreed, calling the settlement “too weak to describe as a slap on the wrist.”

“This is a scheme that systematically hiked rent for people,” said Khan, who Trump replaced when he returned to the White House for his second term. “By one estimate, algorithmic price fixing accounted for Americans overpaying by $3 billion. The Arizona attorney general, when she brought her lawsuit against Realpage, found that residents in Phoenix had been overpaying [their rent] by 30 percent, which is, for most families, one of the largest expenses that they face month to month.”

“This was inflating prices left and right all across the country because this corporation had devised a new way using algorithms to facilitate price fixing and collusion,” Khan continued. “… Realpage is paying $0. There’s no admission of wrongdoing. And, in fact, Realpage is now using this DOJ settlement to fight state laws that are trying to ban algorithmic price fixing. The governor of New York recently signed a bill that would prohibit algorithmic price fixing, and Realpage has sued, saying, ‘this justice department settlement actually says that our business practices are totally fair game.’”

Our Analysis:

The Great Antitrust Setback: A Closer Look at the RealPage Settlement

In a move that could only be described as a coup de grâce to consumer protection, President Donald Trump’s Antitrust Chief, Gail Slater, has effectively neutered the Biden DOJ’s antitrust case against alleged software rent-fixing conspirator, RealPage. This settlement not only marks a regressive step in antitrust enforcement but also highlights a troubling trend of governmental leniency towards corporate malfeasance.

The Settlement: A “Blessing” for RealPage

Let’s dissect the so-called “settlement” that has been reached. In what universe does a settlement that exacts no penalties and extracts no admission of wrongdoing from a company accused of colluding to hike rents across the nation count as justice? It is nothing short of a farce. Stephen Weissman, RealPage’s company attorney, called it a “blessing.” Of course, it’s a blessing for RealPage – it’s a get-out-of-jail-free card! For the millions struggling with inflated rents? Not so much.

Antitrust Division’s Dubious Priorities

Abigail Slater’s statement that “The cost of living is top of mind as we continue to do our important work at the antitrust division” would be laughable if it weren’t so tragically ironic. What part of allowing a company to walk away scot-free from allegations of rent-fixing suggests a concern for the cost of living? This statement is a slap in the face to every American struggling to keep a roof over their head.

The Real Victims: Consumers

The core of this issue revolves around the victims, the countless individuals and families whose financial burdens have been exacerbated by RealPage’s alleged rent-fixing schemes. According to former federal trade commissioner Lina Khan, algorithmic price fixing could account for Americans overpaying by a staggering $3 billion. In Phoenix alone, residents were found to have been overpaying their rent by 30 percent. These aren’t just numbers; they’re stark reminders of the human cost of corporate greed and regulatory failure.

Mockery of Antitrust Laws

This settlement isn’t merely a failure; it’s a mockery of the very essence of antitrust laws. These laws are designed to protect consumers and ensure fair competition – yet here we are, witnessing a scenario where a company not only escapes accountability but is also emboldened to challenge new laws aimed at preventing such egregious practices in the future. RealPage’s lawsuit against the state of New York, which seeks to ban algorithmic price fixing, is the ultimate insult, suggesting that their questionable business practices are “totally fair game.”

A Call to Action

This situation calls for more than just condemnation; it demands action. The refusal of nine states to sign onto the settlement is a heartening sign of resistance, but it’s not enough. We need a systemic overhaul of how antitrust cases are handled, ensuring that corporations cannot simply buy their way out of accountability. Moreover, this case should serve as a rallying cry for stricter laws and regulations against algorithmic price-fixing and other forms of digital collusion.

In closing, the RealPage settlement is not just a failure of the Trump administration’s antitrust division; it’s a symptom of a broader systemic disease – one where corporate interests are routinely prioritized over consumer welfare. It’s high time we demand more from our elected officials and regulatory bodies. If we don’t, we’re all complicit in the erosion of consumer rights and the fair market principles that are supposed to define our economy.
