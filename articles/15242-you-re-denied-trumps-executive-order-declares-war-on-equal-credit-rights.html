You're Denied: Trump’s Executive Order Declares War on Equal Credit Rights

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 5, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What the EO Actually DoesThe Return of Legalized DiscriminationEquality ≠ FavoritismProject 2025: The Blueprint for RegressionThe EndgameIf You’re Not Outraged, You’re Not Paying Attention

“This administration’s commitment to equality of opportunity requires ending the use of disparate-impact liability in federal civil rights enforcement.”
— Executive Order: Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy, April 23, 2025

So this is how it starts.

Not with a jackboot kicking down your door — but with the soft thud of a pen, signing away your rights under the pretense of “meritocracy.”

Donald Trump’s latest executive order, laughably titled “Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy”, is a direct assault on decades of civil rights progress. With one sweeping stroke, it guts enforcement of the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) — a law passed in 1974 to ensure that your ability to get a loan isn’t determined by your race, sex, marital status, or the fact that you receive public assistance.

The ECOA was one of the most consequential civil rights protections in modern finance. It not only barred explicit discrimination but required creditors to justify their denials. If you were turned down for credit, they had to tell you why. That level of transparency became a powerful shield for people — especially women — who had for decades been treated as second-class financial citizens.

And now? That protection is a ghost. A brittle, easily-ignored shadow of its former self.

What the EO Actually Does

Let’s be clear: the order doesn’t change the ECOA itself — only Congress can do that.

What it does instead is far more insidious: it tells federal agencies to stop enforcing it. Specifically, it:

“Directs the Attorney General to initiate rulemaking to repeal regulations that recognize disparate-impact liability under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and similar statutes.”

And more alarmingly:

“Orders the heads of all federal agencies to evaluate ongoing enforcement actions, including under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, and to withdraw or amend those that rely on disparate-impact theories.”

Translation? If a lender doesn’t explicitly say “we’re denying you because you’re a woman” — but still somehow keeps denying women — the government will look the other way. Because, according to this EO, unless there is intentional discrimination, there is no problem.

That is the legal equivalent of saying, “Unless you confess to the crime, there is no crime.”

The Return of Legalized Discrimination

The ECOA was a response to a grotesque financial reality: before 1974, banks could — and did — deny loans to women unless they had a male co-signer. They could ask about your marital plans, your intent to have children, and decide you were just too much of a risk because you had ovaries.

This executive order wants us to “reconsider” that. It seeks to reframe a history of abuse as a story of “neutral policies.” It erases the reasons these laws existed in the first place.

“Agencies shall ensure enforcement of civil rights statutes promotes equality under the law and does not penalize individuals or entities for neutral policies that do not intentionally discriminate.”

This is the rhetorical sleight of hand we’ve come to expect: “neutral policies” that, surprise, overwhelmingly favor white, male, straight, wealthy applicants — but hey, since they didn’t say they were being racist or sexist, it’s all good.

What makes this especially devious is that it erodes the concept of impact. Disparate impact has been a cornerstone of civil rights enforcement for decades because it recognizes that bias isn't always overt. Sometimes discrimination hides in data, in patterns, in the quiet click of an algorithm that always finds a reason to say "no" to the wrong people.

Equality ≠ Favoritism

The EO sells itself as a defense of “color-blind” fairness. It wants you to believe that efforts to close the racial and gender gaps in finance, education, housing, and employment are somehow reverse discrimination.

Let’s say it plainly: that’s a lie.

“Other groups having equal rights to white straight men isn’t favoritism. It’s equality.”

There is nothing fair about pretending we all start at the same line when some people were held back for generations. There is nothing meritocratic about pretending structural inequality doesn’t exist. Meritocracy becomes a hollow myth when you dismantle the mechanisms meant to guarantee fair play.

This isn’t about leveling the field. It’s about ripping up the leveler and calling the crater "freedom."

Project 2025: The Blueprint for Regression

This EO didn’t materialize out of thin air. It’s ripped straight from the playbook of Project 2025 — the far-right Heritage Foundation plan to dismantle the administrative state and drag society back to the 1950s, barefoot and pregnant.

“Project 2025 has been clear: giving women financial freedom was a mistake.”

This movement isn’t just ideological. It’s strategic, tactical, and incredibly well-funded. Its goal is to reassert patriarchal dominance by removing the tools women used to claw their way into economic stability.

They want employers to fire pregnant women. They want to pay women less. They want to ensure that women can’t afford to leave abusive homes. They want you economically trapped — because if you can’t survive without a man, guess what?

You’ll stay.

And if you’re poor, queer, Black, brown, or disabled? Multiply that vulnerability by ten.

The Endgame

This isn’t a glitch. This is the design.

The EO’s very existence is a permission slip for lenders to return to redlining, to gendered denial, to quietly rerouting resources away from those deemed “undeserving” by the invisible hand of systemic bias.

It’s the regulatory version of the old country club rule: we don’t have to put up a sign saying "No Women or Minorities Allowed" — we just make sure the rules work out that way.

And the kicker?

“Federal enforcement shall not require statistical parity or proportionality in outcomes.”

In other words: we don’t care if the numbers scream discrimination — as long as no one says it out loud.

We’re meant to believe this is fairness. But it’s a stacked deck. It’s a rigged game. It’s the illusion of equality served with a smirk and a shrug.

If You’re Not Outraged, You’re Not Paying Attention

You think we can’t go backward?

Take a look at the women of Afghanistan in the 1970s — in universities, working jobs, living independently. Then look again today. Covered, silenced, confined.

That future isn’t as distant as you think.

Because if this executive order stands — if we let this slide under the banner of “meritocracy” — we aren’t going backward.

We’re already there.

And unless we call it out, fight it, and refuse to let it normalize, we’ll stay there.

This isn’t policy. It’s regression disguised as reform. It’s prejudice in a pinstripe suit.

And we should be screaming from every rooftop until it’s repealed.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
