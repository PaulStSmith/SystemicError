FBI agents who reviewed investigative files in the criminal case against Jeffrey Epstein were instructed to "flag" any information involving President Donald Trump, according to Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL).

Durbin sent letters Friday to the Department of Justice and the FBI seeking an explanation for allegations about possible mishandling of the Epstein files and findings from a July 7 DOJ memorandum and instructions given to FBI personnel about the matter.

“According to information my office received, the FBI was pressured to put approximately 1,000 personnel in its Information Management Division (IMD) … on 24-hour shifts to review approximately 100,000 Epstein-related records in order to produce more documents that could then be released on an arbitrarily short deadline,” Durbin wrote to Attorney General Pam Bondi, FBI Director Kash Patel, and Deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino.

“This effort, which reportedly took place from March 14 through the end of March, was haphazardly supplemented by hundreds of FBI New York Field Office personnel, many of whom lacked the expertise to identify statutorily-protected information regarding child victims and child witnesses or properly handle FOIA requests,” the letter continued.

“My office was told that these personnel were instructed to ‘flag’ any records in which President Trump was mentioned," Durbin added.

Durbin, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Judiciary Committee, pointedly asked Bondi to explain why she had claimed on Feb. 21 that Epstein had compiled a "client list" that she had sitting on her desk, when the DOJ released records just six days later saying such a list did not exist.

"Aside from the negative backlash you received over the February 27 record release, what was the purpose of placing almost 1,000 FBI IMD personnel on 24-hour shifts to review Epstein-related records over the course of a two-week period in March?" the senator asked. "Who made the decision to reassign hundreds of New York Field Office personnel to this March review of Epstein-related records?"

"Why were personnel told to flag records in which President Trump was mentioned?" Durbin added.

The Wall Street Journal reported Thursday night that Trump had signed a bawdy letter to Epstein, his longtime friend, in 2003 for his 50th birthday, which the president denies writing, and he threatened to sue the newspaper as questions continue to swirl around his relationship with the late sex trafficker.

The DOJ and FBI kicked off a firestorm in the MAGA movement by publicly disputing conspiracy theories about the client list and Epstein's 2019 suicide in jail as he awaited trial on additional charges.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Tangled Web of Allegations and Political Intrigue

The recent revelations surrounding the FBI's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein criminal case files, as outlined by Senator Dick Durbin (D-IL), raise a multitude of alarming questions about the integrity of the investigation, potential political interference, and the mishandling of sensitive information. At the heart of this controversy is the instruction to FBI agents to specifically "flag" any information involving President Donald Trump, a directive that not only reeks of political bias but also undermines the very foundation of impartial justice.

The Hasty and Haphazard FBI Review

Senator Durbin's letter to key figures at the Department of Justice (DOJ) and the FBI paints a picture of a frenzied, ill-conceived effort to sift through approximately 100,000 Epstein-related records. The decision to put nearly 1,000 personnel from the FBI's Information Management Division (IMD) on 24-hour shifts for a two-week period in March, supplemented by hundreds from the New York Field Office, is baffling at best and recklessly negligent at worst.

The lack of expertise among these personnel, particularly in identifying protected information regarding child victims and witnesses, is a grave concern. This chaotic scramble not only jeopardizes the confidentiality and dignity of the victims but also raises questions about the true motives behind this massive document review.

The Trump Connection: A Red Flag?

The instruction to flag any records mentioning President Trump adds an insidious layer to this already convoluted scenario. Why was there a specific interest in Trump-related information? Was this an attempt to politicize the investigation, or was there genuine concern about potential evidence or connections that required further scrutiny? Either way, the focus on Trump amidst a sea of potential leads and evidence suggests a diversion from the objective pursuit of justice for Epstein's victims.

Doubts and Discrepancies

Senator Durbin's interrogation of the DOJ's claims, particularly regarding the alleged "client list" that Attorney General Pam Bondi mentioned, only to be contradicted by later DOJ records, adds another layer of doubt to the government's handling of the Epstein case. The inconsistency in the DOJ's narrative not only undermines its credibility but also fuels speculation and conspiracy theories, further complicating an already opaque investigation.

The Wall Street Journal's report about Trump's supposed letter to Epstein adds to the intrigue, especially given Trump's vehement denial and threat of legal action. This development, while tangential, feeds into the broader narrative of mystery and controversy surrounding Epstein's relationships and the ongoing investigation.

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Accountability

The mishandling of the Epstein investigation files, as alleged by Senator Durbin, is a stark reminder of the challenges in maintaining integrity and impartiality in politically sensitive cases. The specific instructions to flag Trump-related information, the rushed and poorly managed review of records, and the discrepancies in the DOJ's public statements all point to a deeply flawed process marred by potential political interference.

As this saga unfolds, it is imperative that there be a renewed commitment to transparency, accountability, and the unwavering pursuit of justice, free from political bias or influence. The victims of Epstein's crimes and the public deserve nothing less than a thorough, impartial investigation that seeks the truth, wherever it may lead.
