'Hell no': House Republican among critics of major Senate GOP change to megabill

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump and U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) walk on the day of a closed House Republican Conference meeting on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 20, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

Critics are trashing a new idea from Republicans on the Senate Finance Committee for President Donald Trump's 2026 budget bill.

Politico reported that Republicans plan to brief their colleagues Monday night on lowering the "Medicaid provider tax to 3.5 percent." The measure would be a "huge" change from what the House passed, the report said about Trump's megabill. It "would put a moratorium on states’ ability to raise their provider tax beyond the current 6%."

One economic analyst said the provision would disproportionately harm rural hospitals.

"Senate's Medicaid provider tax cuts will devastate rural hospitals already on life support," wrote Tahra Hoops, the director of economic analysis at the Chamber of Progress. "Seven hundred rural hospitals risk closure; this move would accelerate that crisis. Rural communities thus lose jobs, healthcare access, + economic stability all to fund tax cuts for the wealthy!"

Others pointed out the political impact for the 2026 election.

"This cut to Medicaid is basically a message to New York and California House Republicans that the Senate sees their seats as expendable," The Atlantic's Jordan Weissmann said on X.

Politico reporter Meridth Lee Hill spoke to one moderate House Republican who is against the measure. "Hell no," they said.

It's "already triggering a major Medicaid fight," she assessed.

Radio host Mat Smith wondered if "those folks in the sticks" will ultimately blame repercussions on former President Joe Biden when "their local hospital closes down, putting them, in some cases, hours away from the next nearest medical facility."

Another individual called it, "Losing your hospital to own the libs."

Sen. Mike Crapo (R-ID) plans to brief other GOP senators on the provision Monday night.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the GOP's Controversial Medicaid Provider Tax Proposal

In an audacious move that reeks of political calculus over public welfare, the Republicans on the Senate Finance Committee have unveiled a plan within President Donald Trump's 2026 budget that could drastically alter the landscape of healthcare in the United States, particularly for those in rural areas. According to Politico, the proposed measure seeks to reduce the Medicaid provider tax to 3.5 percent, a stark departure from the current 6% cap. This isn't just a minor tweak in numbers; it's a seismic shift that could have catastrophic consequences for rural hospitals teetering on the brink of financial viability.

The Stark Reality for Rural Hospitals

Tahra Hoops, the director of economic analysis at the Chamber of Progress, doesn't mince words about the potential fallout, stating that 700 rural hospitals risk closure as a direct result of this policy change. The domino effect here is chilling: as hospitals close, rural communities would face a devastating blow, losing not only critical healthcare access but also jobs and economic stability. The rationale? Funding tax cuts for the wealthy. It's a classic case of robbing Peter to pay Paul, except in this scenario, "Peter" is the lifeline of rural America.

Political Consequences and the 2026 Election

The political implications of this proposal are equally troubling. Jordan Weissmann of The Atlantic highlights a cynical strategy at play, suggesting that by targeting Medicaid, the Senate is effectively signaling to House Republicans from states like New York and California that their seats are "expendable." This is divisive politics at its worst, potentially sacrificing the health and wellbeing of millions on the altar of electoral strategy.

The opposition within the GOP, as noted by Politico's Meridth Lee Hill, points to a brewing storm. A moderate House Republican's vehement "Hell no" response to the measure underscores the internal conflict and the broader implications for Medicaid's future. The question posed by radio host Mat Smith is particularly pointed: Will those affected in rural areas blame former President Joe Biden for the loss of their local hospitals? This insinuates a broader strategy of misdirection and blame-shifting that could further muddle public understanding and debate on the issue.

The derogatory sentiment of "Losing your hospital to own the libs" encapsulates the absurdity and tragedy of the situation. It's a stark reminder of the human cost of political gamesmanship.

Conclusion

As Sen. Mike Crapo (R-ID) gears up to brief his GOP colleagues, one must wonder about the ethical and moral considerations at play. Are we witnessing a deliberate undermining of the healthcare infrastructure in rural America for short-term political gain and tax cuts for the affluent? The very fabric of rural communities is at stake here, with healthcare access, economic stability, and lives hanging in the balance.

This proposal isn't just a policy shift; it's a litmus test for the values and priorities of our elected officials. The message being sent is clear: the health of rural Americans is negotiable, expendable in the calculus of political strategy and fiscal conservatism. It's a dangerous precedent, and one that voters, regardless of their political leanings, should scrutinize with fierce criticality.

In a nation already riven by deep divisions, policies like these only serve to widen the chasm, sacrificing the most vulnerable on the altar of political expediency. It's not just a matter of fiscal policy; it's a question of our collective humanity.
