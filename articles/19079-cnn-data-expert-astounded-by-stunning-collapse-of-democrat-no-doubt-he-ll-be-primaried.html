CNN’s Harry Enten zeroed in on a Democratic senator who will “no doubt” face a primary challenge, and history suggests he will likely lose.

Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) has seen a stunning reversal in his support from his own party’s voters since he was first elected to the U.S. Senate in 2023, and Enten told “CNN News Central” that he has never seen an elected official lose backing so dramatically.

“I would just say that John Fetterman is doing as well with Pennsylvania Democrats as the New York Giants are as liked in the state of Pennsylvania, the commonwealth of Pennsylvania,” Enten said. “I mean, just look at this: Among Pennsylvania Democrats and that approval of Fetterman, back in 2023, he was a Democrat, liberal darling. He was at plus-68 points. Look at how low he has fallen, down to minus-40 points. He’s down there with the Titanic among Democrats in the commonwealth of Pennsylvania, and, you know, put a comparison point on it.”

“You know, we always talk about how [Senate Minority Leader] Chuck Schumer is not well liked by the Democratic base nationwide,” Enten added. “Chuck Schumer has a net popularity rating of about minus-two points. He is 38 points more popular than John Fetterman is with Pennsylvania Democrats, and I was also looking at Kyrsten Sinema, who, of course, ended up leaving the Democratic Party in Arizona. She was considerably more popular just before she shifted over than John Fetterman is, at minus-40 points.”

Anchor John Berman marveled at Fetterman’s drop. “This is 108-point swing,” he said, and asked Enten what bearing that might have on his fortunes when he faces re-election in two years.

“Wait a minute, 108-point swing, you never see anything like that,” Enten said. “But my goodness gracious. I mean, just take a look here, okay, 2000s senators on party net ratings about when they lost a primary. Bob Smith plus-15, Arlen Specter plus-13 – of course, he switched parties to the Democrats. It didn’t work for him as a Republican. Plus-six Joe Lieberman, Dick Lugar was at zero points. Lisa Murkowski was at minus-15 points. All of these were considerably more popular than John Fetterman is right now at minus-40 points. He is below the lowest, the ones who actually got beat in a primary.”

Murkowski lost Alaska’s Republican primary in 2010 but was re-elected as a write-in candidate, and she continues to serve in the Senate.

“There is no historical analog to this,” Enten said. “That is how unpopular John Fetterman is with Pennsylvania Democrats. There is basically no doubt in my mind that Fetterman decides to run for re-election as a Democrat, he will face a primary challenge and will be a very competitive one.”

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of John Fetterman’s Popularity: A Deeper Dive

In the swirling vortex of American politics, the rapid descent of Senator John Fetterman’s approval ratings among Pennsylvania Democrats, as highlighted by CNN’s Harry Enten, demands a nuanced analysis. The narrative presented suggests an unprecedented fall from grace for Fetterman, positioning him as a political pariah within his own party. However, to fully grasp the implications of this situation, we must dissect the underlying power dynamics, decision-making processes, and potential misdirections in the framing of Fetterman’s predicament.

Institutional Power and Decision Making

At the heart of any political analysis is the question of who holds the power. In the case of Fetterman’s declining popularity, the immediate power appears to reside with the Democratic voters of Pennsylvania. Their collective approval or disapproval shapes the senator’s political fate. Yet, this is an oversimplified view. The more nuanced power dynamics involve party leadership, influential donors, and the media, all of which can significantly sway public opinion.

Decision-making authority in this context pertains to Fetterman’s actions and policy positions that may have led to his fall in popularity. However, the article does not specify these decisions, leaving a critical gap in understanding the cause of his declining approval. Without this information, attributing the outcome solely to Fetterman’s decisions is premature.

Misdirection and Framing

The article’s comparison of Fetterman to other politicians like Chuck Schumer and Kyrsten Sinema introduces a framing bias, suggesting a broader issue of unpopularity among Democratic senators. However, it fails to account for the unique political landscapes and circumstances each senator faces. This comparison may misdirect responsibility toward Fetterman as an individual, without considering external factors such as economic conditions, local issues in Pennsylvania, or national party dynamics that may have influenced his approval ratings.

Moreover, the focus on approval ratings as a sole indicator of political viability simplifies the complex nature of electoral politics. Historical examples of senators winning re-election despite low approval ratings (e.g., Lisa Murkowski’s write-in campaign victory) demonstrate that factors such as campaign strategy, opponent weaknesses, and voter turnout dynamics can defy conventional expectations.

The Verdict

False Equivalence and Scapegoating: By comparing Fetterman to Schumer and Sinema without context, the article leans into a false equivalency that obscures the unique challenges and circumstances each senator faces. Additionally, it implicitly scapegoats Fetterman for his plummeting approval ratings without a thorough examination of the contributing factors.

Lazy Narratives: The dramatic portrayal of Fetterman’s decline as unprecedented and indicative of inevitable electoral defeat subscribes to a lazy narrative that overlooks the complexities of political survival and the potential for recovery.

Conclusion: While Senator John Fetterman’s declining popularity among Pennsylvania Democrats is cause for concern for his re-election prospects, a deeper dive reveals that the narrative is more nuanced than presented. The real story requires an understanding of the broader political, economic, and social dynamics at play, which are not fully addressed in the article. As observers, it is crucial to question simplistic narratives and consider the multifaceted nature of political fortunes.
