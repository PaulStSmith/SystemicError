In the wake of the deadly shooting targeting National Guard members in Washington, D.C. on Wednesday, President Donald Trump made a stunning announcement that one legal expert says is about to spark a wave of chaos and confusion for thousands.

On Thursday, Trump made the unprecedented announcement that he had ordered U.S. Customs and Immigration Enforcement to review every Green Card holder “from every country of concern,” a list of 19 countries his administration named in June.

With around 12.7 million Green Card holders in the United States, immigration attorney and legal expert Curtis Morrison warned that with the announcement, things could soon spiral out of control.

“My fellow immigration attorney colleagues and me are about to receive thousands of panicked emails from good people freaking out on behalf of their friends and families,” Morrison wrote late Thursday in a social media post on X. “Let’s take a deep breath. We will get through this.”

Among those countries of concern is Afghanistan, also where the suspected shooter lawfully immigrated from in the wake of the United States’ 2021 withdrawal from the country under the Biden administration.

There are more than 190,000 Afghans alone who had immigrated to the United States since 2021, and while there lacks comprehensive data on the amount of Green Card holders from the remaining countries of concern, data does suggest the number to be in the hundreds of thousands, or potentially more than a million, all of whom will now be subject to having their immigration status reviewed or revoked.

The deadly shooting Wednesday saw two National Guard members shot, with the suspected perpetrator being a 29-year-old Afghan migrant, and one who was confirmed to haveworked for the CIAamid the United States’ occupation of Afghanistan. Army Specialist Sarah Beckstrom, 20, ultimately died from her injuries, and the other shooting victim, Air Force Staff Sgt. Andrew Wolfe, 24, remains in critical condition.

Controversial Trump ally Laura Loomer had a simple response to the warning from Morrison:

“We are done caring about immigrants,” she wrote on Friday

Our Analysis:

The Chaos Catalyst: Trump’s Immigration Review Directive

In a move that can only be described as both breathtakingly audacious and profoundly troubling, President Donald Trump has ignited what promises to be a firestorm of confusion, fear, and legal entanglements with his latest decree. With the ink barely dry on reports of a tragic shooting targeting National Guard members in Washington, D.C., Trump has leveraged this incident to justify a sweeping review of Green Card holders from a so-called list of “countries of concern,” a maneuver that reeks not of sound policy but of xenophobic opportunism.

A Cloak of Security, Woven from Threads of Fear

Trump’s directive, instructing U.S. Customs and Immigration Enforcement (ICE) to scrutinize the status of approximately 12.7 million Green Card holders, is a masterclass in using national security as a guise for draconian immigration measures. The list, comprising 19 countries, ostensibly targets nationals based on security concerns. However, the broad sweep of this review, affecting millions, is less a scalpel and more a sledgehammer, causing panic among communities and overwhelming legal experts and immigration attorneys like Curtis Morrison. Morrison’s prediction of a “wave of chaos and confusion” is not hyperbolic but a grounded expectation of the turmoil to come.

The Real Victims: Immigrants and Rational Discourse

The real casualties of Trump’s announcement will not be the nebulous threats he purports to combat but the countless immigrants who sought the U.S. for refuge, opportunity, and a promise of a better life. The suspected shooter’s background as an Afghan migrant and a former CIA associate during the U.S. occupation underscores the complexity and nuance missing from Trump’s broad-brush policy approach. To link the actions of one individual to an entire demographic is not only intellectually lazy but morally bankrupt.

Laura Loomer: A Voice of Divisiveness

In a display of rhetoric that is as predictable as it is disheartening, controversial Trump ally Laura Loomer’s dismissal of concerns about immigrants encapsulates the heartless stance of Trump’s most ardent supporters. “We are done caring about immigrants,” she declares, a statement that strips away any pretense of compassion or understanding from the immigration debate. It’s a chilling reminder of the ethos underpinning Trump’s immigration policies: an ethos marked not by a thoughtful consideration of security and humanitarian concerns but by fearmongering and exclusion.

A Path Forward, Paved with Reason and Empathy

The fallout from Trump’s announcement will undoubtedly be vast, affecting millions who now face an uncertain future in the U.S. But beyond the immediate logistical and legal nightmares lies a deeper, more insidious threat: the erosion of the principles of refuge, opportunity, and compassion that have long defined the United States. In this moment of crisis, the response must be one of resolute opposition—not just to a policy that is as impractical as it is unjust but to the underlying currents of xenophobia and fear that propel such measures.

As we navigate the chaos sown by this latest directive, let us remember that the strength of a nation lies not in its ability to exclude, but in its capacity to embrace. The path forward must be paved with reason, empathy, and an unwavering commitment to the very ideals that Trump and his allies seem so eager to abandon.
