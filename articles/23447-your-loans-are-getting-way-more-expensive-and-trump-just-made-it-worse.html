Your loans are getting way more expensive —and Trump just made it worse

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20263 min read

The Bond Market Is Pricing Trump’s Disorder

The Damage Has an Author

Treasury yields are climbing, borrowing costs are rising, and the pain will travel directly into mortgages, car loans, student debt, and federal finances. The immediate backdrop described in the reporting is not mysterious: Donald Trump’s war in Iran has pushed oil above $107 a barrel, his tariffs have compounded inflation fears, and his promise of $5,000 checks to 270 million adults has advertised another potential $1.3 trillion shock.

Bessent Manages the Symptom

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent tried to suppress long-term yields by repurchasing $6 billion in government bonds. Markets answered by driving yields higher, with the 10-year reaching 4.85 percent and longer maturities hitting 5.3 percent.

That failure matters, but it should not obscure the chain of command. Bessent did not start the war, impose the tariffs, or promise the checks. Trump did. The Treasury secretary is attempting a technical repair while the president keeps manufacturing the underlying risk.

A bond buyback cannot persuade investors to disregard oil shocks, inflationary trade policy, or fiscal improvisation. Markets are not subordinate agencies. They cannot be ordered to treat instability as competence.

Complicity in a Suit

Bessent’s lack of control does not absolve him. His institutional power lies in advising the president, shaping Treasury policy, and defending the government’s credibility before global markets. According to the source, he has offered no visible resistance to the policies driving inflation fears. Instead, he used a Republican event to rant about a “socialist hellscape” and invoke the Soviet bloc, Caracas, and Havana.

That is not economic stewardship. It is partisan propaganda delivered under the seal of an office whose authority depends heavily on credibility. Bessent may be unable to overrule Trump, but he can choose whether to lend Treasury’s prestige to Trump’s political theater. He chose compliance.

The Convenient Socialist Decoy

Blaming “the radical left” performs a specific political function: it redirects attention from officials currently exercising federal power. Progressives did not order the military escalation described in the source. They did not initiate Trump’s trade war with Canada. They did not float an election-season transfer whose stated price would exceed a trillion dollars.

The socialist panic is therefore not merely ridiculous rhetoric. It is scapegoating. While the administration’s own decisions unsettle markets and raise household borrowing costs, Bessent points toward enemies who do not control the White House or Treasury. The weaker actor becomes the villain; the governing actor disappears from the indictment.

The article recognizes Bessent’s degradation of his office, but its fixation on whether he is sufficiently “adult” softens the reality. This is not a child wandering into economic policy. It is a powerful official making deliberate choices about whom to serve, what to excuse, and whom to blame.

Credibility Cannot Be Repurchased

The broader pattern is government by provocation followed by frantic institutional cleanup. The president creates economic risk through war, tariffs, and extravagant promises. Subordinates then deploy limited administrative tools to contain the consequences without confronting their source. When those tools fail, the same officials intensify the propaganda.

That arrangement preserves Trump’s freedom of action while distributing the costs elsewhere: to borrowers, consumers, and future budgets. It also turns institutions such as Treasury from constraints on executive recklessness into instruments for disguising it.

The market’s reaction exposes what the administration’s rhetoric tries to conceal. Credibility is not a communications problem, and stability is not something Treasury can manufacture with a $6 billion intervention. When policy is organized around one man’s impulses and senior officials treat submission as professionalism, disorder is not an accident. It is the governing system.
