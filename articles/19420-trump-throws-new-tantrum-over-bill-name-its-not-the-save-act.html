Trump throws new tantrum over bill name: ‘It’s not the SAVE Act!’

For the second time, President Donald Trump took to social media Sunday to air his grievances with lawmakers for apparently not using his preferred name for a major voter requirements bill.

That bill is the SAVE Act, an abbreviation for the Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act, which would require voters to produce proof of citizenship to register to vote. The bill is a major priority of Trump’s, who earlier on Sunday vowed to not sign any new bills into law until the SAVEE Act reaches his desk.

“It’s not the Save Act, it’s The Save America Act!” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social. “A MUCH better, and more important, name!!! President DJT”

The bill’s official name is not the “SAVE America Act,” though it’s been referenced as such casually by some, according to text of the bill on Congress’ website.

Trump had a similar outburst last month when he demanded that Republicans “must start using the term,” calling his preferred name for the bill “much more potent and descriptive.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Quixotic Quest for the “SAVE America Act”

In a display of what can only be described as a bizarre fixation on semantics, President Donald Trump once again took to his social media platform, Truth Social, to vent his frustration over the naming of the Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act (SAVE Act). Trump’s insistence on calling it the “SAVE America Act” underlines a deeper, more troubling agenda cloaked in the guise of patriotism and voter integrity.

It’s Not Just About a Name

At first glance, Trump’s tantrum over the bill’s name might seem like just another one of his eccentricities. However, a closer look reveals a strategic manipulation of language to further his political objectives. By demanding the bill be called the “SAVE America Act,” Trump is not just seeking a catchier name; he’s attempting to frame this legislation as a heroic, almost messianic effort to ‘save’ America, rather than what it actually is—a potentially restrictive voting measure.

The Misleading Mantra of Voter Fraud

Trump’s fixation on this legislation is rooted in his longstanding, yet largely debunked, claims of widespread voter fraud—a mantra he has been chanting since losing the 2020 election. The SAVE Act, requiring voters to produce proof of citizenship to register, appears to be his latest attempt to institutionalize these claims under the guise of protecting voter integrity. However, critics rightly point out the inherent challenges this bill could pose to a significant portion of the electorate.

Disenfranchisement by Design?

Let’s dissect the numbers. Critics of the SAVE Act highlight a crucial concern: 52% of registered voters do not possess an unexpired passport, and 11% lack access to their birth certificate. These statistics are not just numbers; they represent millions of American citizens who could potentially be disenfranchised by this legislation. It’s essential to question the motive behind a bill that could systematically exclude a substantial chunk of the electorate. Is the goal to ‘safeguard’ voter eligibility, or to suppress voter turnout among demographics perceived to be unfavorable to Trump and his allies?

A Call to Action

Trump’s social media outbursts might provide momentary entertainment or frustration, depending on one’s political leanings, but the implications of the legislation he champions are far from trivial. The SAVE Act, or the “SAVE America Act” as Trump prefers, is a clear indication of his ongoing efforts to reshape the American electoral landscape to his advantage.

As engaged citizens, it is crucial to see beyond the semantics and understand the potential impact of such legislation. The right to vote is the cornerstone of democracy, and any attempt to undermine it under the guise of protecting it should be met with informed resistance and critical scrutiny. The battle for the soul of America’s democracy is not just about a name; it’s about ensuring every eligible citizen maintains their right to vote without undue barriers.
