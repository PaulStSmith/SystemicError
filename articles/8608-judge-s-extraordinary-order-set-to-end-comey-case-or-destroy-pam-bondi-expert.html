Judge's 'extraordinary' order set to end Comey case — or 'destroy' Pam Bondi : expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

A former federal prosecutor predicted during a podcast interview on Sunday that the Department of Justice’s “extraordinary” case against former FBI Director James Comey will either fall apart — or Attorney General Pam Bondi will get “completely destroyed” by the court’s findings.

Last week, Trump-appointed attorney Lindsey Halligan, who is currently prosecuting Comey on two counts of obstruction of justice and lying to Congress, was ordered to turn over tapes of her interactions with grand jury members. The court’s order happened at a time when experts expressed skepticism about the legality of Halligan’s appointment.

Former federal prosecutors Kevin Flynn and Glenn Kirschner discussed Halligan’s case against Comey on a new episode of the podcast, “All The King’s” Men.”

“Through 35 years of my experience...we never had a judge order us to turn over these tapes to begin with,” Flynn said. “It was a showing of some type that there was abuse in the grand jury process that a judge would order the tapes of what the prosecutors actually said to the grand jury to be turned over.”

“So, we already have a judge who has crossed the Rubicon there, and has already made a determination that this material needs to be turned over in the first place, which is extraordinary,” Flynn continued. “And then to have the materials include missing recordings is even more extraordinary.”

“I predicted in a private conversation with you that this is going so far south that the Department of Justice is going to end up dismissing the case against Comey as opposed to having Lindsey Halligan and Pam Bondi completely get destroyed by findings that this judge is going to make, and by testimony that they themselves are going to be compelled to provide,” he added.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the “Extraordinary” Case Against James Comey

In a recent episode of the podcast “All The King’s Men,” revelations surrounding the Department of Justice’s actions against former FBI Director James Comey have sparked significant controversy and criticism. This case, spearheaded by Trump-appointed attorney Lindsey Halligan and overseen by Attorney General Pam Bondi, accuses Comey of obstruction of justice and lying to Congress. The directives and outcomes emerging from this legal battle offer a fertile ground for a critical examination, especially in light of a judge’s order demanding Halligan turn over tapes of her interactions with grand jury members.

The Judge’s Unprecedented Order

The order to release tapes of the prosecutors’ grand jury interactions signals an unusual judicial intervention, as highlighted by former federal prosecutor Kevin Flynn. With 35 years under his belt, Flynn’s astonishment at the judge’s demand underscores the rarity and gravity of the situation. This isn’t standard procedure; it’s a red flag indicating potential misconduct or abuse within the grand jury process. The fact that there are missing recordings only thickens the plot, suggesting that the case against Comey might not just be weak, but potentially marred by procedural missteps or even deliberate obfuscation.

Predictions of a Case Falling Apart

The analysis provided by Flynn and Glenn Kirschner paints a grim picture for the Department of Justice’s pursuit against Comey. The prediction that the DOJ might dismiss the case to avoid further embarrassment or exposure is telling. It reflects a lack of confidence in the case’s foundation and the legal strategies employed by Halligan and Bondi. This scenario posits that the case against Comey is not just flimsy but could potentially unravel, highlighting the prosecutors’ vulnerabilities rather than Comey’s alleged wrongdoing.

The Implications of Legal Skepticism

The skepticism surrounding Lindsey Halligan’s appointment and the legality of her actions is a critical point of analysis. When legal experts question the integrity of the case’s foundation, it underscores the potential politicization of legal processes. The DOJ’s actions, through the lens of these events, appear less about upholding justice and more about settling political scores. This not only tarnishes the reputation of the involved legal professionals but also undermines public trust in the justice system.

Conclusion: A Case of Systemic Error?

As we dissect the unfolding events of the DOJ’s case against James Comey, it’s impossible not to see the shadows of a deeper, systemic error at play. The extraordinary judicial interventions, the missing recordings, and the skepticism around the legal proceedings paint a picture of a justice system that might be veering dangerously off course. Instead of a beacon of impartiality and integrity, we’re presented with a scenario that smacks of bias, procedural missteps, and potentially, a miscarriage of justice.

In this context, the case against Comey transcends the individual; it becomes a litmus test for the health of American legal and political systems. If the predictions hold true and the case collapses or leads to a devastating critique of Bondi and Halligan, it won’t just be a personal defeat for them but a damning indictment of the processes that allowed it to get this far. The Department of Justice, and by extension, the administration under which it operates, must take a long, hard look at the implications of this case—not just for Comey, but for the integrity of justice in America.
