‘Not a fact-based human’: Trump busted by ex-Atlanta mayor over crime claims

As President Donald Trump continues to tease the idea of deploying federal officers and National Guard members to Chicago, Illinois to crack down on crime, Kasim Reed, the former mayor of Atlanta, Georgia, is calling out the president on what he characterized as his distorted inflation of crime data.

Trump teased a major announcement regarding a federal takeover of Chicago on Thursday, a tease made amid the ongoing federal takeover in Washington, D.C. Some Chicago residents, many highlighted by the White House, have said they welcome such a takeover, but to those residents, Reed said it was merely demonstrative of the “corrosive impact of Trump not being a fact-based human anymore.”

“The fact of the matter is, the crime statistics – when they were high and they were reported – no one disputed them when they were record numbers,” Reed said, speaking on CNN’s “First of All with Victor Blackwell” on Saturday.

“The other part is, the crime data is reported to the FBI – the FBI is part of the Trump Justice Department. If there's crime data that's false or inaccurate, then have the FBI say so. You won't do that because if you analyze Chicago's crime statistics, then all of the crime statistics for your friendly states are now going to be subject to the same issue.”

Trump first teased a federal takeover of Chicago shortly after launching the takeover of the nation’s capital, with thousands of National Guard members and federal officers currently patrolling the city’s streets, some of them tasked with picking up garbage.

Trump has also publicly feuded with Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker in recent months, with Trump having labelled Pritzker as “probably the worst (governor) in the country,” and Pritzker, calling the president a “wannabe dictator.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Political Theatre: Trump’s Chicago Takeover Tease

In what can only be described as a disturbing blend of reality TV antics and heavy-handed governance, President Donald Trump’s flirtation with the idea of deploying federal officers and National Guard members to Chicago, Illinois, ostensibly to crack down on crime, underscores a deeply troubling approach to leadership. The former mayor of Atlanta, Georgia, Kasim Reed, has stepped into the fray, offering a scathing critique of Trump’s manipulation of crime data to justify his actions.

Trump’s Misleading Narrative

Trump’s narrative on crime in Chicago is as misleading as it is dangerous. His penchant for distorting facts to serve his agenda is not new, but in the context of federal intervention in city governance, it takes on an even more sinister hue. Reed’s pointed observation that Trump is no longer “a fact-based human” is a stark reminder of the president’s departure from reality and reason.

Trump’s claim of a forthcoming “major announcement” regarding Chicago, made in the shadow of an ongoing federal takeover in Washington, D.C., is not about addressing crime. It’s about showcasing power. The reality is, crime statistics are complex and cannot be boiled down to simplistic narratives that serve political ends. Yet, Trump’s strategy leans heavily on fear-mongering, painting Chicago as a city in desperate need of his brand of law and order.

The Irony of Trump’s Critique of Pritzker

The feud between Trump and Illinois Governor JB Pritzker adds another layer of irony to this political theater. Trump’s labeling of Pritzker as “probably the worst (governor) in the country” is rich, coming from a president whose leadership has been marked by chaos, division, and a stunning disregard for facts. Pritzker’s retort, calling Trump a “wannabe dictator,” is not just a witty comeback; it’s a chillingly accurate description of Trump’s authoritarian tendencies.

The Role of the FBI and Crime Data

Reed’s challenge to have the FBI, a part of the Trump Justice Department, dispute the crime data if it's false, highlights a critical aspect of this debate. The FBI’s crime data is a complex aggregation that requires nuanced interpretation. Trump’s selective use of this data to justify a federal takeover is not just intellectually dishonest; it’s a dangerous precedent. The implication that Trump would not dare to subject crime statistics from states friendly to him to the same scrutiny he applies to Chicago is a telling admission of his political motivations.

Conclusion: A Call for Fact-Based Governance

This entire episode is a glaring example of the corrosive impact Trump’s leadership has on the fabric of our democracy. The use of federal forces as a tool for political grandstanding, the manipulation of crime statistics to foster fear, and the attack on state leaders who dare to challenge him are all indicative of a presidency that has strayed far from the principles of fact-based governance and respect for state autonomy.

As citizens, it is imperative to see through these political maneuvers and demand accountability and truth from our leaders. The situation in Chicago, and indeed in any city grappling with crime, requires thoughtful, community-based solutions, not the heavy hand of a federal takeover that serves only the interests of one man’s bruised ego.
