Our Analysis:

The Perpetuation of Sexist Stereotypes Under the Guise of Perceptiveness

In a recent interaction that reads like a script from a bygone era, Vice President JD Vance lauded President Donald Trump’s so-called perceptiveness for critiquing CNN anchor Kaitlan Collins on her lack of smiling during a press interaction. This exchange, recounted by Vance during an interview with Megyn Kelly, is not just a throwback to outdated and sexist expectations placed upon women in the workplace but also a glaring illustration of how these attitudes are normalized and even praised within certain echelons of power.

Trump’s comment, “Why don’t you ever smile?” directed at Collins, is emblematic of a broader societal issue where women are often expected to adhere to a certain demeanor that is pleasing, approachable, and non-threatening, irrespective of the professional context. Vance’s endorsement of Trump’s observation as “perceptive” not only misses the mark but also underscores a fundamental misunderstanding (or perhaps a willful ignorance) of the power dynamics and gender biases at play.

The Misguided Notion of Antagonism vs. Professionalism

Vance’s rhetorical question, “even if you’re asking a tough question, even if you take your job very seriously, like why does it always have to be so antagonistic?” is a bewildering conflation of professionalism with antagonism. There is an insinuation here that the absence of a smile, particularly in the context of a woman performing her professional duties, is inherently antagonistic. This notion is not just flawed; it’s dangerous. It perpetuates the stereotype that women must be perpetually congenial and that any deviation from this norm is a direct affront.

Megyn Kelly’s Dubious Support

Megyn Kelly’s response to Vance, highlighting her own critique of Collins for not smiling and invoking Roger Ailes’ advice that “Every once in a while, you have to smile,” adds another layer of irony and sadness to this discourse. Roger Ailes, a man whose legacy is marred by allegations of sexual harassment, is perhaps the last person one should quote when discussing appropriate behavior towards women in the workplace. Kelly’s alignment with this sentiment not only undermines the progress women have fought hard to achieve in professional settings but also highlights the insidious nature of internalized sexism.

The Bottom Line

The interaction between Vance, Trump, and Kelly, wrapped in a veneer of camaraderie and shared insights, is a stark reminder of the systemic issues that persist in our society. The expectation for women to smile, the quick dismissal of critiques as sexism without a deeper understanding of the issues at hand, and the citing of discredited figures as moral compasses are all indicative of a broader problem.

It’s crucial to challenge and critique these narratives, not just for the sake of political correctness, but to ensure that the professional landscape becomes more equitable and free from archaic stereotypes. Women, like Kaitlan Collins, should be valued for their professional contributions, their ability to ask tough questions, and their commitment to their roles, not reduced to whether or not they choose to smile while doing so.
