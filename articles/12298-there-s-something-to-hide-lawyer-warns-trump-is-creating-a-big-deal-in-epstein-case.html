'There's something to hide': Lawyer warns Trump is creating a 'big deal' in Epstein case

By Paulo Santos

CNN Screenshot

President Donald Trump's actions surrounding the Jeffrey Epstein case have become a problem largely of the president's own making, according to one lawyer.

CNN legal analyst Michael Moore, who previously served as a prosecutor for the Middle District of Georgia, said on Sunday that Trump has made several curious moves related to the Epstein case that seem to be making matters worse for him. For instance, he sent Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche to meet with Epstein's former accomplice, Ghislaine Maxwell. He's also attempted to deflect questions about the case despite numerous media reports suggesting that Epstein and Trump were once close friends.

"Typically, when somebody’s trying to cover something up or hide something, that means that there’s something to hide," Moore said. "We don’t know what it is yet, but it’s there. So that can be a problem for him legally.”

Over the last several weeks, the Trump administration has sought to control the Epstein files narrative to almost no avail. For example, Trump has lashed out at reporters for asking him about the files and attacked members of his base who continue to call for him to release the files, as he promised to do while on the campaign trail.

The lack of concern about breaking that promise to his voters has led several analysts to conclude that Trump is trying to cover up something. Sending Blanche to meet with Maxwell while questions swirl about whether Trump will pardon her has reinforced that point, Moore argued.

"It's a big deal politically, but also from a legal standpoint," Moore said.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Trump-Epstein Nexus: A Cloaked Dilemma?

In the vortex of American politics, few names conjure as much controversy and speculation as Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein. The latest entanglement, as dissected by CNN legal analyst Michael Moore, provides a convoluted narrative that speaks volumes about Trump's curious maneuvers in the shadows of the Epstein case. Moore, with his background as a prosecutor, cuts through the political fog, highlighting actions by Trump that not only exacerbate his predicament but also deepen the mystery of his connection with Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell.

Sending Mixed Signals

Trump's decision to send Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche to meet with Ghislaine Maxwell, Epstein's former accomplice, is nothing short of bewildering. This move, ostensibly an attempt to navigate the legal labyrinth surrounding the Epstein case, reeks of desperation or, worse, complicity. It's a machiavellian play that raises more questions than it answers, especially when coupled with Trump's overt attempts to deflect pertinent questions about his past relationship with Epstein.

A Promise Unkept

Trump's reactions to inquiries about the Epstein files are telling. His aggressive stance against reporters and his own base, who demand the release of said files, illuminates a stark reality. The breach of his campaign promise to unveil the files morphs from a mere political faux pas into a potential cover-up. This breach is not just a failure to uphold a promise but a deliberate effort to keep the curtains drawn on whatever lurks behind them.

The Legal Quagmire

Moore aptly points out the dual threat this situation poses: legally and politically. The legal ramifications of Trump's actions—or inactions—regarding the Epstein case could be significant. The act of sending Blanche to meet with Maxwell, coupled with the cloud of a potential pardon, paints a picture of a political figure seemingly entwined with questionable characters and possibly dubious activities.

Conclusion: A Web of Intrigue

The Trump-Epstein connection, as dissected by Michael Moore, is a labyrinth of power plays, broken promises, and potential legal entanglements. Trump's maneuvers, far from dispelling the cloud of suspicion, have only thickened the fog. The question is not just about the nature of Trump's relationship with Epstein but also about what truths lie buried beneath layers of political maneuvering and legal gymnastics.

In the grand chessboard of American politics, Trump's moves in relation to the Epstein case could be his most perilous yet. The implications of these actions, both legally and politically, are profound. As Moore succinctly puts it, the attempt to bury or deflect from the Epstein saga only serves to highlight that there is indeed "something to hide."

In the end, the true casualty may well be the public's trust in its leaders to uphold not just the law but the moral and ethical standards we expect of them. The Trump-Epstein saga is a stark reminder that in the murky waters of politics, transparency is often the first victim.
