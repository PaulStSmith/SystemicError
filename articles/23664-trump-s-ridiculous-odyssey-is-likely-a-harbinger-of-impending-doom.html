Trump's ridiculous odyssey is likely a harbinger of impending doom

Based on an article by Jason Miciak

The Presidency as a Profit Center

A Public Office, a Private Course

Donald Trump plans to attend the Irish Open at his own golf resort in County Clare. The presidency supplies the spectacle, security, diplomatic choreography, and global attention; Trump’s business receives the customers and publicity. That is the essential fact. Everything else is decoration.

The Decision Belongs to Trump

This is not an unavoidable presidential duty. Trump chose the destination, the event, and the commercially useful setting. Meetings with Irish President Catherine Connolly and Taoiseach Micheál Martin may give the itinerary an official component, but they do not erase the financial interest underneath it.

The relevant power is concentrated in one person: the president can direct an enormous security apparatus toward a venue he owns. The Secret Service must survey the grounds, secure escape routes, and protect expansive outdoor space because Trump has decided that his private property should become a presidential destination.

Taxpayers Absorb the Costs

Trump’s company stands to benefit from an “avalanche of cash,” as an Irish Times writer quoted in the source described it. The public, meanwhile, carries the protection and transportation costs created by his attendance.

That arrangement is the political scandal. Profit remains private while expense and risk become public. Camp David already exists as a secure presidential retreat. Trump’s resort offers something Camp David cannot: revenue and promotion for Trump.

Diplomacy as Commercial Camouflage

The official meetings provide institutional cover for a journey whose defining feature is a presidential appearance at a Trump-branded property. The article is right to reject the suggestion that adding diplomacy automatically transforms the trip into public service. Scheduling government business around a private commercial event does not cleanse the conflict; it recruits diplomacy to disguise it.

This is how corruption is normalized without requiring a secret payment. The office itself becomes the valuable asset: motorcades confer prestige, security restrictions manufacture exclusivity, and presidential attention turns a business venue into an international stage.

The Distraction Is Trump’s Taste

The source spends too much time on folklore, aircraft décor, personal gluttony, and humiliation. Those details make Trump look ridiculous, but ridicule can misdirect attention from the machinery doing the damage.

The problem is not that a president enjoys gold interiors or golf. It is that executive power can be deployed to increase the value of his property while the institutions responsible for restraining him treat the arrangement as another eccentric weekend. Personality is the spectacle. Institutional submission is the story.

Corruption Through Accommodation

Trump can attempt this because presidential conflict-of-interest standards depend heavily on restraint, convention, and enforcement by other powerful actors. The Republican Party, executive officials, security agencies, and diplomatic personnel may prefer a different political image, but preference without resistance is acquiescence.

The larger pattern is government converted into personal infrastructure. Public agencies protect the venue. Diplomats legitimize the itinerary. Taxpayers finance the operation. Party officials absorb the embarrassment. The owner collects the benefit. When institutions repeatedly accommodate self-dealing, corruption stops looking like a breach of the system and starts revealing what the system has agreed to permit.
