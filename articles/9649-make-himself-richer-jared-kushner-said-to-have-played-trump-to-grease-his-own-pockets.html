'Make himself richer': Jared Kushner said to have 'played' Trump to grease his own pockets

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump’s son-in-law just “played the president,” according to a controversial writer.

Michael Wolff, a journalist who has written four books about Trump, claimed on a recent episode of the podcast “Inside Trump’s Head” that Jared Kushner may have recently “played” the president in connection with their efforts to secure a Middle Eastern peace deal.

In a piece called “How Jared Played Trump to Grease Own Pocket: Wolff,” The Daily Beast quotes the writer in asserting “Kushner’s business connections and Trump manipulation may have cleared the way for a Gaza peace deal.”

The outlet further notes, “Donald Trump’s (so-far) successful plan to end the conflict in Gaza was orchestrated by Jared Kushner in a bid to make himself richer, according to Trump biographer Michael Wolff. Speaking on the Inside Trump’s Head podcast, Wolff outlined how Kushner, Donald Trump’s son-in-law, played Qatar and the president in order to further his own business interests.”

The article quotes Wolff as saying Kushner “craves influence in the Middle East. He craves business opportunities in the Middle East. He craves further, deeper relationships with the powerful people in the Middle East, all of which is helped by peace. So peace becomes a byproduct of business.”

The Beast continues:

“Wolff believes Kushner, along with real estate developer and US Special Envoy to the Middle East Steve Witkoff, pressed their business connections with Middle Eastern royal families to broker the Israel and Hamas deal. On Friday, The New York Times reported on the extent of the pair’s involvement, which earned bipartisan praise.”

“The Qataris basically say... we will come down hard on Hamas,” added Wolff. “And remember, Israel attacked the Hamas negotiators, essentially the top Hamas leadership in Qatar. So they were completely freaked out about this. And I think they realized, this is not in our interest.”

Wolff himself has also been the source of some controversy. High-profile people like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity have denied quotes published by Wolff in his books.

Our Analysis:

The Puppeteer Behind the Peace: Kushner’s Quest for Power and Profit

In a recent revelation that reads more like a plot from a political thriller than reality, Michael Wolff, the controversial chronicler of the Trump presidency, has once again stirred the pot with claims that would have Machiavelli nodding in approval. This time, the spotlight isn’t directly on Donald Trump, but rather on his son-in-law, Jared Kushner, who, according to Wolff, has “played” the president to grease his own pockets, all under the guise of orchestrating peace in the Middle East.

The Alleged Master Manipulator

Wolff, who has carved out a niche for himself by peeling back the layers of the Trump administration’s inner workings through his books, made these assertions on the “Inside Trump’s Head” podcast. He painted Kushner not just as a political adviser but as a shrewd businessman who saw an opportunity to enrich himself and seized it with both hands. The claim that Kushner leveraged his position to push for a Gaza peace deal, not primarily for the noble goal of ending conflict but to bolster his own business interests, is a grave indictment.

The Mechanics of Manipulation

The mechanism of this alleged manipulation, as outlined by Wolff, revolves around Kushner’s deep-rooted business connections with Middle Eastern royalty. By pressing these connections, Kushner, alongside real estate developer and U.S. Special Envoy to the Middle East Steve Witkoff, reportedly played a pivotal role in brokering the Israel-Hamas deal. This narrative suggests that Kushner’s motivations were not purely diplomatic but deeply intertwined with personal gain.

Peace as a Byproduct

Wolff’s assertion that “peace becomes a byproduct of business” in Kushner’s strategy is particularly damning. It implies a cynical exploitation of geopolitical tensions, where the end goal of peace is secondary to the allure of business opportunities in the region. This perspective paints a troubling picture of Kushner’s priorities and his influence on U.S. foreign policy.

The Controversy Surrounding Wolff

It’s important to note, however, that Wolff himself is a figure of contention. His work has often been met with skepticism and outright denial by some of his subjects, including high-profile personalities like Tony Blair and Sean Hannity. This backdrop of controversy serves to muddy the waters further, raising questions about the veracity of his claims.

Conclusion: Between Fact and Fiction

The allegations, if true, reveal a stark manipulation of power for personal benefit at the highest levels of American politics. Kushner’s alleged actions, as described by Wolff, underscore a disturbing confluence of business and politics, where personal profit can drive foreign policy decisions. However, given Wolff’s contentious history, these claims must be approached with caution.

What remains clear is that the intersection of politics, personal gain, and peace processes is fraught with ethical quandaries and potential conflicts of interest. As the dust settles on Wolff’s latest bombshell, the discerning observer must navigate these murky waters with a critical eye, aware that the truth may be as elusive as peace in the Middle East itself.
