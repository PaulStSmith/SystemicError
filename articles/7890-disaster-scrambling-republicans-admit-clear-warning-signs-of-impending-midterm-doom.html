'Disaster': Scrambling Republicans admit 'clear warning signs' of impending midterm doom

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (R-LA) attends a press conference with other House Republicans, more than a month into the ongoing U.S. government shutdown on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 4, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

The Republican Party faces a brewing storm of economic discontent that threatens to undermine its midterm strategy, according to party insiders.

Top Republicans acknowledge they haven’t done enough to sell the “one big, beautiful bill” as cost-of-living concerns continue to rise across the political spectrum, and GOP pollsters are sounding the alarm to lawmakers as they struggle to address health care and other affordability issues, reported Politico.

“While many in the GOP — including Trump — continue to lay blame for their economic problems with former President Joe Biden, there are clear warning signs for Republicans,” the outlet reported. “Forty-six percent of respondents in a recent Politico poll said the cost of living is the worst they can remember it being. That includes 37 percent of those who voted for Trump in 2024, and about a quarter of Trump voters say he is either fully or mainly responsible for the current state of the economy.”

Republicans are struggling to unite around a health care plan to prevent Obamacare premium spikes set to kick in next month, and efforts to alleviate Trump’s tariffs have foundered in the House, and the administration’s proposal to send out $2,000 rebate checks wasn’t greeted with enthusiasm on Capitol Hill.

“The cost of living is a legitimate issue — I think it was one of the main reasons President Trump was elected. I think it’s still an issue,” said Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA).

Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) insisted “Republicans are dialed in like a laser” on the cost of living, but economic benefits from the megabill Republicans promised have not yet materialized after GOP lawmakers have repeatedly gotten bogged down by internal conflicts, including a lengthy government shutdown and debates over the Jeffrey Epstein files, further eroding public confidence.

Trump himself has complicated matters by dismissing affordability concerns as a “hoax,” a stance that further alienates voters feeling economic pressure. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) pointedly criticized this approach last week.

“They are in a bubble from Donald Trump on down,” Schumer said. “Donald Trump says there’s no affordability crisis — what kind of world is he living in?”

The Republican Study Committee is pushing for additional legislative measures, but party leaders like Johnson and Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) remain hesitant. A potential second reconciliation bill faces significant obstacles, with the party divided on policy specifics and the midterm elections approaching, Politico reported.

“Tony Fabrizio, a top Trump pollster, also urged members of the RSC last week to tackle high prices for prescription drugs and housing — warning members in a closed-door meeting that affordability concerns were a key reason a House special election in Tennessee was so close,” Politico reported.

GOP leaders in both the House and Senate are scrambling to cobble together a health care overhaul to present, but so far no proposals have garnered much support among the competing factions inside the party.

“It’s a disaster,” said Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO). “Health care, as it currently is, is too expensive for everybody.”

Our Analysis:

GOP’s Economic Discontent: A Brewing Storm

The Republican Party is currently navigating through turbulent waters, with economic discontent among voters posing a significant threat to their midterm election strategy. This issue is not just a whisper in the corridors of power but a loud alarm that’s been ringing, albeit seemingly ignored by some within the party until now. Let’s dissect the layers of this brewing storm and the GOP’s floundering attempts to address it.

The Unconvincing “Big, Beautiful Bill”

At the heart of the Republican Party’s dilemma is their failure to effectively promote what they’ve termed the “one big, beautiful bill.” Despite their efforts, the ghosts of cost-of-living concerns continue to haunt the political landscape, affecting voters across the spectrum. It’s a classic case of political disconnect - promising grand solutions without demonstrating tangible benefits to the electorate. This scenario is not just disappointing; it’s a stark misreading of the room at a time when many Americans are struggling to keep up with rising expenses.

The Blame Game and Reality Check

The GOP’s strategy of laying the blame for economic woes at the feet of former President Joe Biden is not only tired but increasingly ineffective. According to a recent Politico poll, a significant portion of the electorate, including 37 percent of Trump voters from 2024, perceives the cost of living as the worst they’ve experienced. Alarmingly, about a quarter of Trump voters attribute the current economic state either fully or mainly to him. This stark reality check should be a wake-up call for the Republican Party. Ignoring or dismissing these concerns risks alienating a core part of their base.

Internal Conflicts and Public Confidence

The Republican Party’s internal squabbles have done little to inspire confidence among voters. From government shutdowns to debates over the Jeffrey Epstein files, these distractions have overshadowed the party’s economic agenda. The failure to unite around a healthcare plan or address Trump’s tariffs only adds to the image of a party in disarray. Speaker Mike Johnson’s claim that Republicans are “dialed in like a laser” on cost of living seems almost ironic in this context, highlighting a disconnect between rhetoric and reality.

Trump’s “Hoax” and the Affordability Crisis

Donald Trump’s dismissal of affordability concerns as a “hoax” is perhaps the most glaring example of the GOP’s misalignment with the economic realities facing voters. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer’s critique that the party is “in a bubble” is spot on. Dismissing genuine economic pressures as fabrications is not just out of touch; it’s political malpractice. It demonstrates a troubling insensitivity to the hardships of everyday Americans.

The Road Ahead: Legislative Stalemates and Factionalism

The Republican Study Committee’s push for legislative measures to address affordability issues shows some recognition of the problem. However, the party’s leadership remains hesitant, caught between the demands of upcoming midterm elections and the challenges of uniting a divided party. With significant obstacles facing a potential second reconciliation bill, the GOP’s path forward seems fraught with difficulties.

In conclusion, the Republican Party’s economic discontent is not just a storm brewing on the horizon but a tempest that’s already here. The party’s inability to address cost-of-living concerns effectively, combined with internal conflicts and a disconnect with voter realities, paints a troubling picture for their midterm election strategy. As Senator Josh Hawley succinctly puts it, the situation is a “disaster.” Without a coherent and unified approach to healthcare and the broader economic challenges facing Americans, the GOP risks not just electoral losses but a deeper erosion of public trust and confidence.
