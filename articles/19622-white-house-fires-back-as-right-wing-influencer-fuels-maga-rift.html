White House fires back as right wing influencer fuels MAGA rift

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 3, 20266 min read

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt reacts while she takes questions from the media during a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 10, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

The White House was forced to fire back after a prominent conservative influencer and podcaster criticized President Donald Trump‘s various and rapidly-shifting reasons for attacking Iran in a massive and ongoing military exercise that the president and defense chief have called “war.”

Matt Walsh, who hosts his right-wing podcast on The Daily Wire and has four million followers on X, on Monday expressed his confusion with the administration’s talking points.

“So far we’ve heard that although we killed the whole Iranian regime, this was not a regime change war,” he began. “And although we obliterated their nuclear program, we had to do this because of their nuclear program. And although Iran was not planning any attacks on the US, they also might have been, depending on who you ask. And although we are not fighting this war to free the Iranian people, they are now free, or might be, depending on who seizes power, and we have no idea who that will be.”

“The messaging on this thing is,” he said, “to put it mildly, confused.”

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt responded to Walsh just hours later, saying that Trump on Saturday had “released a statement laying out clear objectives to the American people for Operation Epic Fury.”

According to Leavitt, they include destroying Iran’s missiles and Navy, ensuring Iran’s proxies cannot destabilize the region or the world, stopping them from making and using IEDs, guaranteeing Iran can never have a nuclear weapon, and preventing the Iranian regime from threatening America.

“Simply put,” she wrote, “the terrorist Iranian regime would not say yes to peace.”

“For 47 years, the Iranian regime has actively and intentionally facilitated the killing of Americans while chanting ‘death to America’ and funding other bloodthirsty terrorists seeking to destroy the United States and all of Western Civilization. Prior American leaders were too weak and cowardly to do anything about it. Now, President Donald J. But Politico’s White House bureau chief Dasha Burns noted that Walsh “is among many right wing voices questioning the administration’s actions in Iran.”

“I have heard repeated warnings from Republican sources that the WH needs to do more to get MAGA on side,” she added.

Sean Davis, co-founder of the right-wing website The Federalist, reposted Walsh’s remarks and shared similar ones of his own.

“Is the goal to eliminate the Iranian regime or free the Iranian people or degrade their nuclear capability or degrade the conventional weapons capability or eliminate their regional hegemony or to cut off their oil supply to China or to help Israel or what?” Davis asked. “The lack of any coherent message seems to suggest the lack of any coherent objective.”

Former Trump ally and former U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who months ago broke with Trump, wrote: “And just like that we are no longer a nation divided by left and right, we are now a nation divided be those who want to fight wars for Israel and those who just want peace and to be able to afford their bills and health insurance.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Confusion: Trump’s Iran Policy

The recent article detailing the Trump administration’s policy (or lack thereof) towards Iran reads less like a strategic military briefing and more like a script from a political satire, were it not so terrifyingly real. The White House was put in a position to defend its stance after Matt Walsh, a conservative influencer, pointed out the glaring inconsistencies in the administration’s reasons for its military actions against Iran. The situation has escalated to what the president and his defense chief are calling “war,” yet the objectives and justifications seem as clear as mud.

Walsh’s critique lays bare the contradictions: the U.S. has apparently managed to both eliminate the Iranian regime without aiming for regime change, destroy their nuclear capabilities while attacking because of those very capabilities, and liberate the Iranian people without intending to or knowing who will take power next. The administration’s message is, to borrow Walsh’s understatement, “confused.”

In response, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt claims President Trump has laid out “clear objectives” for Operation Epic Fury. These objectives include a smorgasbord of goals from destroying Iran’s military capabilities to ensuring regional stability, all under the guise of stopping Iran from threatening America and its allies. Leavitt’s rhetoric, painting the Iranian regime as a universally acknowledged “terrorist” entity, echoes the administration’s justifications for its actions. Yet, this justification comes off as a thinly veiled attempt to retrofit a rationale onto a series of potentially impulsive military decisions.

The article points out that Walsh is not alone in his criticism. Other right-wing voices, including Sean Davis and even former Trump ally Marjorie Taylor Greene, express confusion or outright disagreement with the administration’s approach. Davis questions the ultimate goal of the U.S.’s actions, while Greene highlights a growing divide not along traditional political lines but between warmongers and those desiring peace.

The Lack of Coherent Strategy: A Dangerous Game

The administration’s inability to communicate a coherent strategy is alarming. The objectives as stated by Leavitt – from destroying military assets to ensuring regional stability – are broad and, in some respects, mutually exclusive. For instance, how does one ensure the freedom of the Iranian people by launching massive military strikes that inevitably result in civilian casualties?

Furthermore, the attempt to cast previous administrations as “too weak and cowardly” is a tired trope that oversimplifies complex international relations and the painstaking efforts to avoid outright conflict. It’s a dangerous game of bravado that risks not only American lives but also regional and global stability.

A Nation Divided: Beyond Left and Right

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s comment reveals a deeper, perhaps more significant shift in the American political landscape. The division is no longer merely between conservatives and liberals but between those advocating for endless wars to serve external interests and those who prioritize domestic welfare and peace. This realignment suggests a growing wariness of interventionist policies, irrespective of one’s political leanings.

In Conclusion: A Strategy of Confusion?

The Trump administration’s policy on Iran, as evidenced by the chaotic messaging and lack of clear objectives, seems less a well-thought-out strategy and more a series of reactions. Whether this confusion is by design or due to internal disarray, the consequences are real and potentially catastrophic. It’s a scenario where the line between strategic ambiguity and outright confusion is blurred, leaving both allies and adversaries guessing.

As observers and stakeholders in the outcome of these policies, it’s crucial to demand clarity, coherence, and, most importantly, a strategy that prioritizes peace and stability over geopolitical posturing. The current approach, as highlighted by the critiques within the president’s own ideological camp, suggests a policy not just confused but fundamentally flawed in its conception and execution.
