Republicans are now deathly afraid of Trump — but just as scared of dumping him

Following the returns coming in, it was exhilarating, an experience that helped counter what we felt in 2024.

So yes, on a personal, psychological level, this will fuel us for the enormous work ahead. And this built on No Kings, which again points to the importance of organizing.

But we also surely needed this politically, to counter Trump and the GOP—and the corporate media, which is always pandering to the GOP.

Mikie Sherrill, winning by double digits in the New Jersey race for governor, killed the narrative of New Jersey as drifting toward the GOP. The pollsters and the pundits had a big upset, telling us it was going to be single digits and MAGA Jack Ciatterelli could win. Black and Latino voters, who the media told us had strayed to Trump, voted in big percentages for Sherrill.

In the Virginia gubernatorial race, the spread for Abigail Spanberger’s trouncing was also bigger than predicted, and the GOP harping on a texting scandal didn’t stop Jay Jones from becoming attorney general. The House of Delegates is seeing double-digit pickups for Democrats. Virginia, a true bellwether, is a massive blow to the GOP.

Californian organized fiercely within a matter of two months, led by Governor Gavin Newsom, and voted to fight Trump and Texas by redistricting their congressional map. It was a stunning turnaround—so rapidly—and an understanding of the stakes.

Newsom gave a powerful speech, letting Trump know we will not accept his authoritarian rule. And Newsom implored other Democratic states to follow suit with redistricting — and I think they will.

And in New York, Zorhan Mamdani brings a whole new generation to City Hall, as voters decided it was no more business as usual with the same old corrupt politicians. He ran on affordability, as the other Democrats did, hitting the GOP and Trump for only continuing to enrich the billionaires. Mamdani is also a model for Democrats across the country in how to engage with voters, and he sent a message to Democratic leadership: It’s time for change. Much will be written on that.

Mamdani’s acceptance speech, rather than being conciliatory, was fiery and defiant—the right stance for this moment—as he spoke directly to Trump, telling him to “turn up the volume” to listen, and then letting him know he will have a big fight on his hands if he comes for us. Pitch perfect.

So, let’s celebrate these big wins, and the fact that two women elected as governor, and an immigrant—the first Muslim and South Asian elected to be New York’s mayor—won the marquee races.

We needed it. And the GOP, Trump and the media needed to see it. Our opponents are now on the defensive. Republicans will fret, deathly afraid of what Trump will do to their chances—but also deathly afraid of what he’ll do if they pull away from him. That’s exactly where we want them moving into the 2026 midterm elections.

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of Election Night Revelations

The recent article provides a vivid narrative of the political landscape post-election night, painting a picture of triumph and strategic victory for the Democrats over the GOP, Trump, and the so-called complicit corporate media. However, beneath the surface of this seemingly straightforward victory tale lies a complex web of implications, strategies, and political maneuverings that deserve a closer, more critical examination.

The Illusion of a Singular Victory

The article jubilantly proclaims the electoral victories as a much-needed psychological and political counter to the GOP and Trump’s influence, highlighting significant wins in New Jersey, Virginia, California, and New York. While these victories indeed signal a momentary shift in political momentum, the narrative dangerously skirts the edge of complacency. It heralds these wins as if they alone could dismantle the entrenched systems of power and influence that have long governed American political dynamics.

The Misleading Narrative of Unwavering Support

A particularly troubling aspect of the article is its portrayal of Black and Latino voters. It simplifies their electoral behavior, suggesting a monolithic voting bloc that the Democrats have successfully recaptured from the GOP’s clutches. This oversimplification not only erases the nuanced motivations behind individual voting patterns but also dangerously assumes that these communities will remain loyal to a party that has historically taken their support for granted. It’s a stark reminder of the Democratic Party’s failure to consistently address the needs and concerns of these vital constituencies beyond election cycles.

The Double-Edged Sword of Redistricting

Governor Gavin Newsom’s redistricting maneuver in California is hailed as a masterstroke in the battle against Trump and the GOP. While redistricting can indeed serve as a tool to ensure fairer representation, the article glosses over the inherently undemocratic nature of gerrymandering, regardless of who wields it. This celebration of redistricting as a tactic overlooks the long-term implications for democratic governance and the potential for such strategies to be weaponized by any party in power, further eroding public trust in electoral processes.

The New Faces of Political Leadership

The victories of Mikie Sherrill, Abigail Spanberger, and Zorhan Mamdani are rightly celebrated for breaking molds and introducing new perspectives into the political arena. However, the real test lies not in their elections but in their actions moving forward. The article’s focus on Mamdani’s fiery rhetoric underscores the importance of holding these new leaders accountable to their campaign promises, particularly in addressing systemic issues like affordability and corruption that have long plagued American politics.

The Perpetual GOP Dilemma

Finally, the article touches on the GOP’s existential crisis in the face of Trump’s looming presence. It’s a schadenfreude-laden observation that fails to recognize the broader implications for the American political system. The GOP’s internal conflict is not just a spectacle for Democrats to relish but a symptom of the deep ideological divides that threaten the stability and functionality of the country’s political institutions.

Conclusion

While the article captures the immediate euphoria of electoral victories, it largely misses the forest for the trees. The real work lies not in celebrating these victories as endpoints but in leveraging them as opportunities to address the systemic flaws and inequalities that pervade American politics. As we look toward the 2026 midterm elections and beyond, the focus must shift from mere political gains to the enactment of substantive, systemic change. Only then can we begin to dismantle the entrenched power structures that continue to hinder true democratic progress.
