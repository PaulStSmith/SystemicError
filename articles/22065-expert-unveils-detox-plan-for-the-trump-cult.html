Expert unveils detox plan for the Trump cult

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Trump’s Cult Is Not the Story. The Power Network Is.

The Source, Briefly

The interview argues that Trump’s movement behaves like a cult: loyalty overrides policy, lies survive obvious contradiction, and media ecosystems, religious networks, and algorithms keep followers sealed inside a hostile information environment. That is the frame. It is not wrong, but it is incomplete.

Start With Power

The central mistake in a lot of Trump coverage is treating followers as the main event. They are not. The people with actual institutional power are Trump, the media operators who amplify him, the billionaire ecosystem that launders his image, and the party apparatus that keeps surrendering to him. The source names some of that machinery outright: Fox News, Joe Rogan, Telegram, WhatsApp, Elon Musk, Peter Thiel, and New Apostolic Reformation prophets and apostles. That is not a cult in the abstract. That is an influence stack.

The article reaches for cult psychology because it is easier to explain than political capture. But cult language can still soften the hard fact: this is organized power, not merely mass confusion.

Followers Are Not the Institution

The interview asks why ordinary Trump supporters ignore broken promises, obvious corruption, and documented misconduct. That is a fair question. But the sharper question is why so many institutions keep making that avoidance possible. Trump does not need every supporter to understand the contradictions. He needs enough people, enough of the time, plus enough platforms willing to repeat the message until reality becomes optional.

That is why the “people voting against their self-interest” framing is too neat. It places responsibility almost entirely on the voter while leaving the machinery of manipulation in the background. The source does identify manipulation, but it still leans on psychology where political economy belongs. If prices rise, if wages stagnate, if grievance is redirected toward scapegoats, that is not a misunderstanding. It is strategy.

Misdirection as Method

The most important pattern in the piece is not devotion. It is deflection. Trump’s movement survives by turning material harm into identity performance. Forever wars become “strength.” Price pressure becomes someone else’s fault. Sexual misconduct becomes a smear campaign. Election lies become patriotism. The source gives the evidence, but the deeper political meaning is that MAGA depends on laundering consequence through resentment.

That is why the cult frame is useful only if it points back to power. A cult is not just a set of delusions. It is a system that trains people to stop trusting external reality so the leader can keep acting without restraint. In this case, the leader is backed by institutions that benefit from the damage: media outlets chasing attention, donors chasing deregulation and impunity, and political actors too cowardly to enforce limits.

The Democratic Failure Inside the Story

The piece is right to insist that Trump’s movement is not a normal political coalition. It is also right to reject the fantasy that facts alone will dismantle it. But there is a gap where democratic politics should be. If the response ends at “reality-test more carefully,” that is too thin for the scale of the problem.

Democrats are the necessary institutional vehicle against authoritarian politics in the United States, which means the task is not to politely out-argue the cult. It is to build durable counter-power: voter registration, media literacy, organized labor, disciplined messaging, and plain language about who is doing what to whom. The source gestures at mobilization through 2028. That is closer to the mark than therapeutic commentary, but it still understates the need for material political organization.

The Larger Pattern

This story is not mainly about Trump supporters being unusually gullible. It is about how modern authoritarian politics works in plain sight: billionaire amplification, platform fragmentation, religious absolutism, anti-intellectual grievance, and a party structure willing to trade governance for domination. The cult is the surface. The system beneath it is the point.

Trump did not just persuade people. He revealed how much of American political life can be bent once institutions stop defending reality and start profiting from breakdown.
