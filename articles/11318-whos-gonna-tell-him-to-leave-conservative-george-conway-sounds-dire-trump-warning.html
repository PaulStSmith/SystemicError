The president has already executed a takeover of the Washington, D.C. Metropolitan Police Department, and has deployed thousands of National Guard troops and FBI, ATF, DEA, DHS, and ICE agents to patrol the streets of the nation’s capital, despite massive opposition from the city’s residents. Trump declared a “crime emergency” on Aug. 11, when, according to experts, there was none.

In his overnight statement, Trump threatened D.C. Mayor Muriel Bowser, declaring she “must immediately stop giving false and highly inaccurate crime figures, or bad things will happen, including a complete and total Federal takeover of the City!”

Speaking to MeidasTouch News in a video published on Thursday (excerpt below), Conway warned that President Trump might never leave the White House if he had complete control of the city’s police force and National Guard.

“If Donald Trump gets control of policing in the capital to the extent that he seems to desire, which is he wants complete control, because that’s just the way he is, he wants complete control of anything he deems important to him,” Conway began.

“Think about the consequences of that, because what would have happened if he had had that kind of control on January 6, when he didn’t call out the National Guard to defend the Capitol?” Conway posited. “What if he actually did have control of the Metropolitan Police and the National Guard in a way in the way that he wants?”

When asked, “What would have happened on January 20th?” Conway replied that Trump “would have remained in office because there would not have been the election of Joe Biden on January 6th, and he would have declared an emergency.”

“And, lo and behold, what do you do when you have an emergency? You call in the National Guard,” Conway continued. “He would have never left.

“Some people were saying that what they’re doing in D.C. is going to be a model for other cities. But I think they’re just cutting straight to the chase, because if you want to have a coup against the constitutional order, you want to control the capital city, and if he has control of the policing in the city of Washington, you know, there’s no —how do you stop him?”

“Who’s gonna tell him to leave the White House?”

Others appear to have arrived at the same conclusion.

Journalist Wajahat Ali separately on Friday afternoon warned: “Donald Trump isn’t going to leave willingly or peacefully. Republicans are gearing up for martial law. You all aren’t paying attention.”

Our Analysis:

Anatomy of a Power Grab: The D.C. Warning Signal

In an era where political rhetoric frequently blurs the line between reality and dystopian fantasy, the recent developments in Washington, D.C., as reported, serve as a chilling reminder of how fragile democratic institutions can be under the strain of autocratic ambitions. George Conway's warnings about President Donald Trump's apparent quest for "complete control" over the policing in the nation's capital aren't just the anxious musings of a concerned observer. They are a dire prognosis for the health of American democracy.

Trump's Power Play: More Than Just Rhetoric

Let's dissect the situation with the precision it deserves. President Trump's declaration of a "crime emergency" in Washington, D.C., on August 11, despite expert consensus suggesting otherwise, is not merely an overzealous law and order campaign. It is a calculated move to consolidate power by exerting control over the city’s Metropolitan Police Department and deploying an overwhelming force of National Guard troops and federal agents. This maneuver isn't just an overreach; it's a blatant power grab dressed in the guise of public safety.

Trump's threat of a "complete and total" occupation of Washington, D.C., coupled with his ominous warning to Mayor Muriel Bowser, reveals a troubling inclination towards authoritarianism. This isn't governance; it's strong-arm tactics that would make mob bosses nod in appreciation. The reference to "bad things will happen" if Bowser doesn't comply is nothing short of mobspeak, a language of coercion and implied threats that has no place in the lexicon of a democratic leader.

The Conway Perspective: A Coup in Slow Motion?

George Conway's analysis brings to the forefront a scenario that reads like a page out of a political thriller, yet is terrifyingly plausible. The notion that Trump, with unchecked control over D.C.'s policing forces, could essentially barricade himself in the White House and defy the outcome of an election isn't far-fetched. It's a potential reality that should have every American on high alert. Conway's hypotheticals, grounded in the actions and tendencies of the current administration, paint a picture of a leader unbound by the checks and balances that define a healthy democracy.

This isn't just about staying in office beyond one's term; it's about systematically dismantling the mechanisms of accountability and oversight that prevent the emergence of a dictatorship. If the capital city’s policing is under the thumb of the President, who indeed will have the authority or the power to challenge him?

The Bigger Picture: A Model for Autocracy?

The implications of Trump's actions in D.C. extend far beyond the city's borders. They set a dangerous precedent for the erosion of civil liberties and the expansion of executive power. If Washington, D.C., can be transformed into a pseudo-police state at the President's whim, what stops other cities from falling under similar control? The specter of martial law, once a fringe conspiracy theory, suddenly looms as a tangible threat under the guise of restoring order and combating crime.

Conclusion: Democracy on the Defensive

The article not only highlights the immediate crisis unfolding in Washington, D.C., but also serves as a clarion call for vigilance and action. The United States stands at a precipice, facing down the barrel of potential autocracy. It's a scenario where democratic institutions are not eroded overnight but chipped away gradually under the guise of national security and public order.

In the face of such threats, complacency is complicity. The American public, and indeed its representatives, must remain ever vigilant, challenging overreach and defending the principles of democracy with unwavering resolve. The warning signs are flashing bright and clear; the time to act is now.
