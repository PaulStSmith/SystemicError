Eyes roll as musician and noncitizen Nicki Minaj urges Americans to ‘vote red’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosSeptember 28, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedThe power was in the broadcastA weak frame aimed downwardGuilt by family associationThe transaction deserves scrutinyOutrage is not accountability

What happened

RawStory reports that musician Nicki Minaj urged Americans to “vote red” during a Saturday Fox News interview with Lara Trump. Minaj briefly laughed and offered a joking “nah,” but Trump embraced the endorsement and called it a sales pitch to American voters.

Minaj also encouraged viewers to research local candidates before voting. According to the report, she cannot currently vote because she is not a U.S. citizen; although she has claimed she received a Trump Gold Card, she said her citizenship paperwork was still being finalized.

The power was in the broadcast

Minaj supplied the celebrity endorsement, but Fox News and Lara Trump controlled the political use made of it. Trump immediately converted an ambiguous, jokingly qualified remark into a clean partisan appeal. The network provided the audience and the legitimacy. Minaj’s citizenship status did not prevent her from expressing a political view, and it did not diminish the platform’s power to circulate that view.

The relevant institutional story is therefore not that a noncitizen spoke about an election. It is that a major political media platform turned celebrity access into partisan messaging.

A weak frame aimed downward

RawStory’s headline centers eye-rolling over Minaj’s inability to vote. That is a cheap substitute for examining the people and institutions that can shape elections: party operatives, broadcasters, candidates and wealthy political patrons.

Noncitizens are allowed to hold opinions and encourage lawful voters to act. Minaj’s lack of a ballot may make the endorsement ironic, but it does not make her the principal power holder. Treating citizenship as the decisive rebuttal also echoes a familiar political misdirection: scrutinize the immigrant’s standing while giving the powerful host and network supporting roles.

Guilt by family association

The report then pivots to the criminal histories of Minaj’s husband and brother. Their convictions are serious: RawStory cites reporting that Kenneth Petty was convicted of attempted rape and later pleaded guilty to manslaughter, while Jelani Maraj was convicted of sexually assaulting an 11-year-old girl. But those facts do not establish anything about the merits of Minaj’s electoral message.

Introducing relatives’ crimes as partisan evidence is guilt by association. Quoting an activist who uses those convictions to declare that Minaj belongs with the MAGA movement does not repair the logic; it merely outsources the smear. If the argument against Republican candidates depends on crimes committed by a celebrity supporter’s relatives, the coverage has abandoned institutional accountability for social-media contempt.

The transaction deserves scrutiny

The more politically consequential detail is Minaj’s claim that she received a Trump Gold Card while still finalizing citizenship paperwork. RawStory describes the initiative as offering a pathway to citizenship for $1 million, but the article does not establish the terms of Minaj’s purported gift, who provided it or whether it changed her legal status.

That unresolved relationship—celebrity endorsement beside claimed access to a costly immigration pathway—warrants careful reporting. The supplied account does not prove a quid pro quo, and it would be irresponsible to allege one. It does, however, raise a sharper question than whether Minaj can vote: how political leaders distribute access, prestige and immigration benefits around influential supporters.

Outrage is not accountability

This episode shows how partisan media and reactive coverage can reinforce each other. One side packages celebrity speech as voter persuasion; the other answers with mockery about immigration status and attacks involving family members. The institutions exercising power escape sustained examination.

The consequence is coverage that personalizes politics precisely where it should follow authority. Lara Trump made the endorsement useful. Fox News amplified it. The reported Gold Card claim raises unanswered questions about elite access. Those are matters of agency and power. Everything else is spectacle arranged around them.
