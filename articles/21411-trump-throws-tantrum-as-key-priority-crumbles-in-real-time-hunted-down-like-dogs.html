Trump throws tantrum as key priority crumbles in real time: ‘Hunted down like dogs’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Public Money for Political Loyalty

Power, Plainly Stated

Donald Trump spent Friday raging about a stalled $1.7 billion “anti-weaponization fund” meant to pay Jan. 6 participants he says were “hunted down like dogs.” That is the basic fact pattern. The larger fact is more important: the president is using the machinery of government to seek public reimbursement for people who tried to overturn an election and then to frame that payout as justice.

This is not a muddled dispute over process. It is a deliberate demand that the state underwrite political violence after the fact.

A Fund for Impunity

Trump’s language tries to launder consequence into victimhood. The people at issue were not abstract casualties of “weaponization.” They were participants in a violent attack on the Capitol, and the proposed fund would direct taxpayer money toward their reward. That is the political meaning of the proposal, no matter how much sentimental fog Trump throws around it.

The decision-maker here is Trump. He is not accidentally describing an abuse of power; he is openly defending one. He calls the fund a correction, but the actual correction would be the opposite: paying people for trying to intimidate democracy into submission.

Cornyn Sees the Leverage

Sen. John Cornyn has put a hard condition on Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche’s confirmation: eliminate the fund. That is not moral clarity so much as institutional leverage, and it matters because it shows where the pressure point is. Cornyn is not stopping the president’s broader agenda. He is using one confirmation to extract a concession on one especially ugly scheme.

That does not make him the hero of the story. It does show that the system is being forced to confront a naked proposition: should federal power be used to compensate the people who attacked federal power? Trump’s fury is the clearest evidence that Cornyn has found a real point of friction.

The Blame Flip

Trump’s rant also performs a classic authoritarian maneuver: reverse the roles. The aggressors become “victims,” the victims of aggression become the villains, and accountability gets renamed persecution. He invokes the Biden administration, Mar-a-Lago, and “weaponization” as if piling grievances together can hide the basic structure of the issue.

This is misdirection, not confusion. Trump is not unclear about who has power. He knows exactly who does, and he is asking that power to finance revenge. The complaint about his own exposure, including the Mar-a-Lago raid, is folded into the same story so that every legal consequence becomes proof of martyrdom. That is how impunity talks when it wants to sound injured.

The Real Political Pattern

The pattern is simple: political violence, institutional capture, and the recasting of accountability as persecution. Trump is not merely protecting allies. He is trying to establish a precedent in which the state pays off loyalists who participated in an assault on constitutional order.

That is the deeper scandal in the reporting. The issue is not that one man is angry on Truth Social. It is that a president is still attempting to convert public authority into private protection for an anti-democratic faction. The fund is the policy expression of that project. The rhetoric is just the camouflage.
