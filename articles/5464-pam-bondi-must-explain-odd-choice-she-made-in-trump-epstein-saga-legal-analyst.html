Pam Bondi must explain 'odd choice' she made in Trump-Epstein saga: legal analyst

Attorney General Pam Bondi will appear Wednesday before the House Judiciary Committee, where she will no doubt be grilled about her handling of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, but a legal analyst pinpointed one specific question she must answer about that matter.

Bondi is overseeing the delayed release of investigative files to comply with the Epstein Files Transparency Act passed last fall by Congress, but MS NOW legal analyst Jordan Rubin said he’s most interested in a decision she made months before.

“Why was Todd Blanche the one to conduct a proffer of Ghislaine Maxwell?” Rubin wrote.

The deputy attorney general – a former defense lawyer for President Donald Trump – said Bondi directed him to visit the convicted Epstein co-conspirator in prison to interview her, but Rubin is puzzled why the attorney general would have selected the second highest-ranking Justice Department official for that task.

“On top of Blanche’s enduring allegiance to Trump and the clear conflict it creates, he was an odd choice to question Maxwell about any actionable Epstein-related information she might have — that is, if the point was to properly collect and analyze such information,” Rubin wrote.

Proffer sessions are typically conducted by prosecutors who’ve actually worked on the case at hand, so they would know which questions to ask and how to assess the value of the defendant’s answers, but that’s not the case with Blanche, who Rubin said was “doomed to fail.”

“Unsurprisingly, the published transcripts and audio of Blanche’s conversations with Maxwell showed that at least one of his priorities — perhaps his sole priority — was to try to exonerate Trump,” Rubin wrote. “Indeed, there seemed to be an unspoken understanding of that purpose between Blanche and Maxwell.”

The convicted child sex trafficker told Blanche that she never saw Trump in any “inappropriate setting” and insisted he was always “a gentleman in all respects” in her experience, and shortly after the proffer session she was transferred without explanation to a minimum-security facility, and her attorney has said Maxwell would testify if granted clemency by the president.

“The truth does matter,” Rubin wrote. “That’s why Blanche was the wrong one to purportedly seek it in this situation.”

“And that’s what makes the question of why Bondi sent him to do so a challenging one for her to answer honestly,” the legal analyst added. “She either tasked Blanche with a political mission to please their boss at the expense of doing the job of a prosecutor, or she didn’t understand the job of a prosecutor. Either answer would reflect that she’s in the wrong job herself.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misdirection: The Curious Case of Pam Bondi and the Epstein Investigation

In what reads like a script ripped from the most convoluted of political thrillers, Attorney General Pam Bondi steps into the spotlight, not for her prowess in upholding justice but for her controversial handling of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation. The stage for this spectacle? The House Judiciary Committee, where Bondi is slated to face a grilling that promises more heat than light, particularly regarding the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Yet, amidst the anticipated barrage of questions, one pierces through the fog with the precision of a well-aimed arrow: Why was Todd Blanche, of all people, sent to conduct a proffer of Ghislaine Maxwell?

The Unlikely Profferer: Todd Blanche’s Curious Role

Todd Blanche, a figure whose past as a defense lawyer for President Donald Trump casts a long and dubious shadow over his appointment, was chosen by Bondi to interview convicted Epstein co-conspirator Ghislaine Maxwell. This decision is bewildering at best and egregiously misguided at worst. Blanche’s “enduring allegiance to Trump” and the glaring conflict of interest it presents is just the tip of the iceberg.

Proffer sessions, as any legal novice knows, are the domain of seasoned prosecutors deeply entrenched in the case at hand. Their purpose? To extract actionable information—a task for which Blanche was spectacularly ill-equipped. As MS NOW legal analyst Jordan Rubin aptly puts it, Blanche was “doomed to fail.” But fail at what, exactly? If the objective was to gather and analyze crucial information related to Epstein, then the mission was a bust from the get-go. However, if the goal was to shield Trump from potential fallout, then perhaps Blanche was not such an odd choice after all.

The Unspoken Agenda: Exonerating Trump

The transcripts and audio from Blanche’s conversations with Maxwell do not contain the probing questions of a thorough investigator. Instead, they reveal a singular, perhaps obsessive, priority: to exonerate Trump. Maxwell’s portrayal of Trump as a paragon of gentlemanly behavior only adds a layer of absurdity to the proceedings. Following this farcical exchange, Maxwell’s mysterious transfer to a minimum-security facility further muddies the waters, hinting at a quid pro quo that would make even the most cynical observer blush.

The Core Question: Competence or Complicity?

Rubin’s analysis leaves us grappling with a disturbing dichotomy: Was Bondi’s decision rooted in incompetence or complicity? Both options paint a damning picture of an Attorney General woefully out of her depth or, worse, willfully subverting the course of justice for political gain. The implications of either scenario are dire, not just for the individuals involved but for the very foundations of the justice system.

In the end, the Epstein investigation, under Bondi’s stewardship, emerges not as a beacon of truth-seeking but as a cautionary tale of political maneuvering and systemic failure. The question of why Blanche was chosen to conduct the proffer session with Maxwell is more than a mere procedural query; it is a spotlight on the murky intersections of power, justice, and accountability.

As the House Judiciary Committee convenes, one can only hope that amidst the political theater, the pursuit of truth is not entirely forgotten. For in the shadow of such flagrant misdirection and manipulation, the quest for justice remains an uphill battle—one that demands not only keen oversight but a relentless commitment to integrity and transparency.
