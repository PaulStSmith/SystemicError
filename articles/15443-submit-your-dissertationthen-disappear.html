Submit Your Dissertation—Then Disappear?

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 30, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

This Isn’t Satire AnymoreThe Academic American Dream, ShatteredThe Real Dissertation Defense

What a darkly funny comic reveals about an even darker reality in America.

There’s an XKCD comic that starts like every Ph.D. horror story: meet your advisor, write your proposal, survive your qualifying exams, and labor for years on your dissertation.

But then, right as you're ready to submit and defend your work—BAM!—you're grabbed off the street by masked agents, never identified, never read your rights, and whisked away before a judge even knows you’re gone.

It should be absurd.

But it’s not.

This Isn’t Satire Anymore

This isn’t a joke about academic stress. This is the real, present-day threat faced by international students, immigrants, and increasingly, anyone the state deems inconvenient.

We’ve already seen it: not in some distant past, but right now. In 2025, a Ph.D. student—legally residing in the U.S.—was abducted in broad daylight by so-called ICE agents who refused to identify themselves, show badges, or follow due process. No warrant. No Miranda rights. Just a black SUV, a snatch, and silence.

This is the timeline now.

And it’s not limited to student visa holders. The same rhetoric that justified ripping people from sidewalks is now being aimed squarely at natural-born citizens, with Donald Trump vowing to round up "enemies" and use military force within U.S. borders. He’s openly floated plans for mass detentions, new camps, and legally dubious executive powers.

The infrastructure for authoritarianism isn’t being built.

It’s already operational.

The Academic American Dream, Shattered

For decades, U.S. academia sold itself as a beacon of free thought and meritocratic opportunity. But what does that mean if writing a dissenting thesis can land you in an unmarked van? What good is a degree when your visa—and your personhood—can be revoked without warning?

International scholars, especially those from marginalized backgrounds, now carry a second invisible workload: navigating a legal and political minefield while trying to publish, teach, and survive.

And the stress isn’t just hypothetical. It’s embedded in every email from ICE, every visa renewal delay, every protest you second-guess attending, every glance over your shoulder walking home.

The Real Dissertation Defense

The dark brilliance of the XKCD comic lies in how seamlessly it transitions from academic absurdity to political horror—because the two are now intertwined.

Today, defending your dissertation may be the easy part. The real challenge?

Defending your rights in a country increasingly hostile to facts, to dissent, and to the very people it once invited in pursuit of knowledge.
