Political observers and analysts were stunned on Wednesday after the Wall Street Journal released a bombshell new report about President Donald Trump’s activity on his personal social media network, Truth Social.

The Journal reported that Trump meant to send his September 20 post telling Attorney General Pam Bondi that the administration was “losing credibility” by not prosecuting former FBI Director James Comey as a direct message. Instead, Trump posted it publicly, which caused Bondi to move forward with prosecuting Comey, according to the report.

The report led observers and analysts to conclude that Trump may be conducting state business on his social media network, which may violate federal law and be used to evade accountability for illicit acts.

“I know everyone on this site cares passionately about the information security habits of top Government officials, but the President of the United States communicating with his cabinet via Truth Social direct message is both insane and a violation of the Presidential Records Act,” former Obama administration staffer Dan Pfeiffer posted on X.

“The president is using direct messages on a private social media app (that he owns) to communicate with his attorney general,” journalist Scott Nover posted on X.

“There’s zero chance that this administration is preserving any of their communications,” attorney Kevin Baum posted on X.

“Mostly i just can’t believe he uses truth social to DM people,” former Obama staffer Tommy Vietor posted on X. “Must be convenient for all the foreign intel services.”

“Pundit accountability: I said it was just Trump being weird and not an errant DM,” ex-GOP strategist Tim Miller posted on X. “He’s even stupider than a TDS inflicted podcaster realizes. We regret the error.”

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Reality of Trump’s Social Media Shenanigans

In an era where political absurdity seems to have hit its peak, the latest revelation about President Donald Trump and his misuse of his personal social media network, Truth Social, serves as a stark reminder that we are far from the bottom. According to a Wall Street Journal report that left political observers and analysts with their jaws on the floor, Trump’s mishandling of a post intended as a direct message to Attorney General Pam Bondi has opened a Pandora’s box of legal and ethical questions.

The Blunder Heard ‘Round the World

Trump’s accidental public post on September 20, which was meant to nudge Bondi towards prosecuting former FBI Director James Comey, inadvertently revealed a disturbing practice. The President appears to be conducting state business over a social media platform he owns, which is not just reckless but likely illegal. This isn’t a mere faux pas; it’s a glaring spotlight on the potential for evading accountability and violating federal law, specifically the Presidential Records Act.

Critics Weigh In

Critics from various corners have been quick to highlight the gravity of this issue. Dan Pfeiffer, a former Obama administration staffer, pointed out the insanity and illegality of a President communicating with his cabinet via Truth Social direct messages. Journalist Scott Nover and attorney Kevin Baum underscored the impossibility of preserving these communications, which is a fundamental requirement for transparency and accountability in government.

Even Tommy Vietor, another former Obama staffer, couldn’t help but express his disbelief at the President’s choice of communication, noting how convenient it must be for foreign intelligence services. This sentiment was echoed by Tim Miller, an ex-GOP strategist, who admitted to underestimating the extent of Trump’s recklessness.

The Larger Implications

This incident is not just about a misfired direct message. It’s a glaring example of the casual disregard for the mechanisms of accountability that underpin the American political system. When a President chooses to conduct official business on a platform that he owns, it raises profound questions about conflicts of interest, security, and the preservation of official records.

Moreover, the reaction from both sides of the political spectrum indicates a rare consensus on the foolhardiness of Trump’s actions. It serves as a reminder that, despite our polarized political climate, the principles of transparency, accountability, and legal compliance still hold significant weight among the public and political commentators alike.

In Conclusion

The Wall Street Journal’s report is more than just a bombshell; it’s a clarion call for a serious examination of how modern technology intersects with the age-old principles of governance. Trump’s blunder on Truth Social isn’t just an embarrassing slip-up; it’s a symptom of a broader systemic problem that threatens the very fabric of our democracy.

As we move forward, it’s crucial that we demand better from our leaders. The use of personal social media platforms for conducting official business is not just inappropriate; it’s a dangerous precedent. It’s time for a serious conversation about the boundaries of technology in politics, and perhaps, a reevaluation of the laws that are supposed to keep our leaders in check.
