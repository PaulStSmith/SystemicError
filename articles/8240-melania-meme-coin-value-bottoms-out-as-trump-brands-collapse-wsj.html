Melania 'meme coin' value bottoms out as Trump brands collapse: WSJ

U.S. first lady Melania Trump relays a reporter’s question to President Donald Trump. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Supporters of Donald Trump, who believed everything he touches turns to gold, are finding out the hard way that investing in the president has turned into a bad bet.

According to a Saturday report from the Wall Street Journal, since Trump was inaugurated in January, shares in the parent company behind the president’s Truth Social platform have spiraled downward 75 percent and the numbers for Trump-branded “meme coins” have suffered a worse fate as of late.

With the Journal reporting that the market has “soured” on Trump brands, reporter Jack Pitcher wrote, “Digital ‘meme coins’ named for Trump and first lady Melania Trump are down 86% and 99% since inauguration day, respectively. And one of the Trump family’s crypto ventures, a token called World Liberty Financial, has dropped roughly 40% since its September launch.”

Noting the market for such investments have also seen slumps, the Journal is reporting, “The turnabout has been particularly stark with stocks and digital tokens with direct associations to the president and his family. They have been hit hard by a broader slump affecting the priciest and most-speculative corners of the market.”

According to the Journal, the president’s stunning second term win led of a flood of financial activity with regard to anything Trump which now has some investors wondering if they need to pack it in and take a loss.

As the Journal’s Pitcher is reporting, “DJT, for example, trades at 1,240 times its annual revenues, according to FactSet. And the Trump family’s various crypto businesses, including World Liberty Financial and American Bitcoin, have been swept up in a recent drop in cryptocurrencies. One of many users discussing the stock on Reddit investing forums last month self-identified as a ‘DJT bag holder’ who bought at $46 a share. The stock is now trading at $11.07. ‘When do I give up on this and move on?’ user SimpleMindHatter asked.”

Our Analysis:

The Midas Touch That Wasn’t: The Fall of Trump-Branded Investments

In a revelatory piece that cuts through the golden facade of former President Donald Trump’s investment allure, the Wall Street Journal has laid bare the stark reality facing his ardent supporters. It appears the Midas touch attributed to Trump by his followers has lost its luster, turning investments into lead rather than gold.

A Financial Fiasco

The report highlights a grim picture for investors in Trump’s ventures since his inauguration. Shares in the parent company of his Truth Social platform have plummeted a staggering 75 percent. However, the nosedive doesn’t end there. Trump-branded “meme coins” have faced an even grimmer fate, with values plummeting 86% and 99% for coins named after Trump and Melania Trump, respectively, since inauguration day. The family’s foray into cryptocurrency with World Liberty Financial has also seen a significant drop of 40% since its September launch.

A Market Soured

What we witness here is not just the failure of specific investments but a broader market’s growing disillusionment with the Trump brand. The Journal’s insight into the market’s reaction is telling; investments associated with Trump and his family, once seen as lucrative bets, are now languishing due to a broader market slump targeting speculative ventures.

The Investors’ Dilemma

The article sheds light on the quandary facing Trump’s investors. Following his unexpected second term win, there was a surge in financial activity around Trump-associated ventures. Yet, now, investors are left pondering whether to cut their losses and move on. The mention of a Reddit user, a self-proclaimed “DJT bag holder,” contemplating abandoning his investment at a significant loss encapsulates the sentiment of disillusionment.

Analysis: The Emperor’s New Clothes

The narrative unfolding around Trump’s economic ventures is reminiscent of the classic tale, “The Emperor’s New Clothes.” Investors, swayed by the allure of Trump’s golden touch, are now confronted with the reality that the investments might have been nothing more than elaborate illusions of prosperity.

The staggering valuation of DJT at 1,240 times its annual revenues is a testament to the speculative frenzy that often accompanies celebrity-branded ventures. However, as the markets correct themselves, these inflated valuations are coming back down to earth, leaving investors in a precarious position.

The Broader Implications

This financial debacle is not just a lesson on the perils of speculative investment; it’s a cautionary tale about the dangers of intertwining political loyalty with financial decisions. The market’s souring on Trump-branded ventures could reflect a broader skepticism towards the sustainability of political figures as financial prophets.

Conclusion: A Reality Check

The Wall Street Journal’s report serves as a reality check for those who believed investing in Trump was a guaranteed path to prosperity. As the dust settles and the losses mount, the narrative of Trump’s Midas touch is being rewritten to one of caution and skepticism.

In the end, the saga of Trump-branded investments is a stark reminder that in the volatile world of finance, not all that glitters is gold.
