'A bit confused': Soccer stars baffled as Trump hogs victory limelight

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Show: A Masterclass in Limelight Hogging

Soccer Football - FIFA Club World Cup - Final - Chelsea v Paris St Germain - MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, New Jersey, U.S. - July 13, 2025 Chelsea’s Reece James and teammates celebrate with the trophy alongside U.S. President Donald Trump after winning the FIFA Club World Cup REUTERS/Hannah Mckay

President Donald Trump left soccer stars baffled as he presented them with the Club World Cup trophy Sunday — then stayed to hog the limelight of their victory.

Trump took to the stage after Chelsea shocked Paris Saint-Germain with an unexpected 3-0 win Sunday.

But, instead of handing over the prize and leaving the team to their celebrations, the president lingered — despite clear indications that he should leave.

"I was a bit confused, yes," match-winner Cole Palmer, who scored twice, told PA Media.

“I knew he was going to be here but I didn’t know he was going to be on the stand when we lifted the trophy."

After presenting the trophy alongside FIFA president Gianni Infantino, Trump was clearly instructed to step aside but simply wouldn't budge.

"They told me that he was going to present the trophy and then exit the stage, and I thought that he was going to exit the stage, but he wanted to stay," Chelsea captain Reece James said afterward.

The bizarre scene left players visibly bemused as they attempted to celebrate their £90 million jackpot victory while Trump remained planted center stage amid the fireworks and confetti.

Even FIFA president Infantino had to personally invite Trump to move aside — a request the president ignored, PA Media reported.

During the match, Trump had gushed about the "tremendous sport" and even joked about using executive orders to rename soccer to football in America, telling DAZN: "I think we could do that, I think I could do that."

Earlier in the game, Trump had been booed by many of the 81,000 soccer fans at the MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Show: A Masterclass in Limelight Hogging

In what can only be described as a staggering display of ego over etiquette, President Donald Trump transformed the Club World Cup trophy presentation into the latest episode of his ongoing reality show. The event, which should have been a spotlight moment for Chelsea following their unexpected 3-0 triumph over Paris Saint-Germain, instead became a bizarre spectacle of one man's refusal to cede the stage.

Trump's insistence on remaining center stage—even in the face of clear signals to exit—left players, officials, and the global audience somewhere between bafflement and disbelief. Cole Palmer's admission of confusion and Reece James's expectation of a swift exit that never came underline a fundamental misunderstanding or, more likely, a willful disregard for the norms of sportsmanship and respect.

The audacity of remaining planted amid the fireworks and confetti, effectively photobombing Chelsea's victory celebration, speaks volumes about Trump's character. It's a vivid illustration of his well-documented desire to be at the center of attention, regardless of the occasion's appropriateness or the discomfort it may cause others. FIFA president Gianni Infantino's futile attempt to nudge Trump offstage only adds to the farcical nature of the situation, highlighting a disturbing willingness to ignore social cues and professional advice.

Moreover, Trump's earlier comments during the match, where he toyed with the idea of using executive orders to rename soccer to football in America, further demonstrate his penchant for blending whimsy with authority in ways that often skirt the line between jest and earnest. The mixed reaction from the crowd at MetLife Stadium—ranging from boos to, presumably, cheers—mirrors the polarized reception Trump continues to elicit from the American public and the world at large.

What should have been a moment of pure celebration for Chelsea and the sport of soccer was instead overshadowed by a single individual's inability to step aside. The incident is emblematic of Trump's broader impact on the political and social fabric of the United States and beyond: a relentless pursuit of the spotlight, with little regard for tradition, protocol, or, in this case, the achievements of others.

In the end, this episode will likely be remembered not for Chelsea's remarkable victory but for the unwelcome sideshow provided by Trump. It serves as a stark reminder of the challenges posed by leaders who prioritize personal aggrandizement over the collective joy and achievements of others. Trump's behavior at the Club World Cup is not an anomaly but a consistent pattern, one that continues to draw criticism, incredulity, and, from some quarters, admiration. But for those who value the integrity of sport and the importance of grace in defeat and humility in victory, it's a cautionary tale of what happens when the lines between leadership and showmanship become irrevocably blurred.
