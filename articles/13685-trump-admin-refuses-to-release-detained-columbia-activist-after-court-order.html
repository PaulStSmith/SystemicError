Trump admin refuses to release detained Columbia activist after court order

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

People demonstrate outside Thurgood Marshall United States Courthouse, on the day of a hearing on the detention of Palestinian activist and Columbia University graduate student Mahmoud Khalil, in New York City, U.S., March 12, 2025. REUTERS/Shannon Stapleton

A federal judge ruled against the Trump administration's detention of Palestinian freedom activist Mahmoud Khalil this week — but the administration has said it has no plans to release him in response to that ruling, Newsweek reported Friday.

According to the report, the Trump administration's reasoning is that Judge Michael Farbiarz did not actually order Khalil's unconditional release, but simply determined his detention cannot be solely on the basis of Secretary of State Marco Rubio's determination that Khalil's political activism is against U.S. foreign policy.

"In a letter to Farbiarz, the DOJ's civil division said the detained Columbia University graduate can still be held on separate grounds, alleging that he misrepresented his personal information on his application for a green card," said the report.

Khalil, one of the leaders of protests at the university against U.S. support for Israel in the invasion of Gaza, was arrested by federal agents earlier this year and transported across the country to an infamous detention facility network in Louisiana.

The administration has argued that Khalil has spread support for the terrorist group Hamas, whose Oct. 7 attack against Israel triggered the current conflict in Gaza.

But Farbiarz, who serves in the District of New Jersey, found the administration was punishing Khalil for protected political speech.

"The Respondents have not contested the evidence put forward by the Petitioner, and in light of that the Court finds as a matter of fact that the Petitioner’s career and reputation are being damaged and his speech is being chilled — and this adds up to irreparable harm," said his ruling.

The Trump administration has sought to deport international students involved in protests against Israel. Last month, a judge ordered officials to release Rümeysa Öztürk, a Tufts University academic who had also been arrested over anti-Israel activism.

Our Analysis:

A Stark Reminder of the Tenuous Balance Between National Security and Individual Rights

In a recent ruling that should have been a straightforward affirmation of the American values of free expression and due process, a federal judge found himself at odds with the Trump administration over the detention of Palestinian freedom activist Mahmoud Khalil. This case, as reported by Newsweek, encapsulates the ongoing struggle between national security imperatives and the safeguarding of individual liberties—a battle that seems to skew more Kafkaesque with each passing day.

The Heart of the Matter

At the core of this legal debacle is the Trump administration's detention of Mahmoud Khalil, a Columbia University graduate and a vocal critic of U.S. support for Israel, particularly in the context of the invasion of Gaza. Khalil's arrest and subsequent transport to a notorious detention facility in Louisiana mark a chilling escalation in the administration's crackdown on political dissent.

Judge Michael Farbiarz's ruling against the sole basis of Khalil's detention—namely, Secretary of State Marco Rubio's assertion that Khalil's activism was contra to U.S. foreign policy—should have been a beacon of judicial sanity. Yet, in a move that reeks of authoritarian overreach, the administration has doubled down, refusing Khalil's release under the pretext of alleged misrepresentations on his green card application.

A Dangerous Precedent

This case sets a dangerous precedent, not merely for the rights of non-citizens within the United States but for the very concept of political freedom. The administration's argument—that Khalil's support for Hamas, deemed a terrorist organization by the U.S., justifies his detention—bypasses a crucial legal and moral distinction between actual support for terrorism and the exercise of free speech, including criticism of U.S. or Israeli policies.

Judge Farbiarz's finding, that the administration's actions were causing "irreparable harm" to Khalil's career, reputation, and right to free speech, underscores a disturbing trend toward using national security as a blanket justification for the suppression of dissent. The refusal to release Khalil even after the judicial rebuke is a stark reminder of the executive branch's increasing willingness to flout judicial authority.

The Echoes of History

It is worth noting that this is not an isolated incident. The Trump administration's efforts to deport international students involved in protests against Israeli policies, as exemplified by the case of Rümeysa Öztürk, a Tufts University academic, represent a broader assault on academic freedom and the rights of non-citizens.

Conclusion

The case of Mahmoud Khalil is a litmus test for the resilience of American democracy and its foundational freedoms. In its handling of this case, the Trump administration has shown a flagrant disregard for the principles of free speech and due process—principles that are supposed to distinguish the United States on the world stage. As this saga unfolds, it serves as a grim reminder of the ease with which political expediency can trump (pun intended) the most basic human rights.
