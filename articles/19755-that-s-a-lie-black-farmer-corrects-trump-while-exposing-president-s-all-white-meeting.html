'That's a lie!' Black farmer corrects Trump while exposing president's 'all-white' meeting

By Paulo Santos

A Black farming leader erupted Sunday to correct a Trump “lie,” all while exposing a purported “all-white” meeting held by the president.

John Boyd Jr., president of the National Black Farmers Association, appeared on MS NOW over the weekend to talk about the Supreme Court’s decision striking down Trump’s tariff powers.

Trump has raged at the high court’s justices since the decision was handed down.

But Boyd, who previously said Trump had “sold out” farmers, was focused on the president “lying” about $12 billion being paid out to farmers.

“He went down to Iowa and said that there was $12 billion that was paid out. People. That is a lie!” he exclaimed. “The money has not been paid out, not $0.01 of it. And I just recently got an email, I think, Friday that said that sign up period has now moved to April. The president said we’re going to get the money in November. Then he said it’s going to get the money in December and then January and then February. And now I just got an email saying that the sign up period is now April. The president just keeps making false statements and he’s not being held accountable. This president has a problem with Black leaders that speak up or anyone that opposes anything that he says.”

Boyd went on to expose what he said was an “all-white farm meeting with an all-white audience.”

“And when he announced that $12 billion... he had a roundtable for farmers and excluded me from the meeting. And nobody believed that he excluded me from the meeting until it happened to a black governor, who they disinvited and didn’t want him at the table. That’s this president’s M.O. and he says it’s not race. They said, you know, we’re moving away from DEI. We’re moving away from the Black farmer issue, the small farmer issue. And that’s the kind of coding they put in there. But in all reality, he had an all-white farm meeting with the all-white audience that excluded Black farmers.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Facade: Trump’s Farming Fiasco

In a blatant display of misinformation and exclusion, former President Trump’s actions and words come under fire, exposing a narrative rife with deceit and discrimination. John Boyd Jr., a figurehead in the agricultural community and president of the National Black Farmers Association, pierces through the smoke screens with factual precision and warranted indignation.

Trump’s Misinformation Campaign

The core of Boyd’s outrage centers around Trump’s blatant lie regarding the distribution of $12 billion in aid to farmers. Trump’s itinerary of promised payment dates shifts like sand, with deadlines fluttering from November to a seemingly arbitrary extension to April. The inconsistency and failure to deliver promised funds highlight a troubling pattern of deception, where the truth is as malleable as it is elusive.

The Exclusionary Tactics

Beyond the misinformation lies a more sinister facet of Trump’s presidency: systematic exclusion. Boyd’s revelations about an “all-white farm meeting” are not merely about the absence of diversity; they represent a deliberate sidelining of Black voices and concerns within the agricultural sector. This move, described by Boyd as a snub not only to himself but also to a Black governor, is emblematic of a broader strategy to marginalize minority perspectives.

The DEI Backtrack

The article touches upon a significant pivot away from Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) initiatives, framing it as a maneuver to further sideline the issues facing Black and small farmers. By dismissing DEI, the administration not only abandons a commitment to fairness and representation but also codifies its indifference toward the systemic challenges these groups face.

Conclusion: A Pattern of Prejudice and Deception

Boyd’s confrontation of Trump’s lies and the exposure of an exclusionary, “all-white” meeting spotlights a disturbing pattern within the Trump administration. The tactics of misinformation paired with discriminatory practices serve not just to mislead the public but to entrench a system of inequality within one of America’s foundational sectors.

In this scenario, the actions and policies of Trump are not mere oversights or missteps; they are calculated moves in a game of power that prizes deception and exclusion. The agricultural sector, with its deep roots in the American identity, becomes a battleground for broader issues of race, representation, and truth.

John Boyd Jr. emerges not just as a critic but as a beacon of truth in an era where such qualities are in dire shortage. His refusal to stay silent in the face of falsehoods and exclusion underscores a resolute stand against the systemic errors plaguing our political and agricultural landscapes.
