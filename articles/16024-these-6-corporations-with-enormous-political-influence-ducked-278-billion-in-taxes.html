These 6 corporations with 'enormous political influence' ducked $278 billion in taxes

Analysts accuse big U.S. tech firms of paying almost $278 billion less corporate income tax in the past decade compared with the statutory rate for U.S. companies making the same profits.

The Guardian reveals Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, Netflix, Apple and Microsoft —the “Silicon Six”— generated a combined $11 trillion in revenue and $2.5 trillion in profits over the last decade. But they paid an average of just 18.8% in combined national and federal corporation taxes, compared with an average 29.7% in the U.S., according to the Fair Tax Foundation (FTF).

“Our analysis would indicate that tax avoidance continues to be hardwired into corporate structures,” Fair Tax Foundation Chief Executive Paul Monaghan told The Guardian. “The Silicon Six’s corporate income tax contributions are, in percentage terms, way below what sectors such as banking and energy are paying in many parts of the world.”

Analysis at FTF also discovered the companies had inflated their stated tax payments by $82 billion over the same period by including contingencies for tax they did not expect to pay. Monaghan added that these companies have "enormous political influence as well as economic power," in that they all spend hundreds of millions of dollars on lobbying government officials on key tax-writing committees to have more say in how the tax system works.

The Guardian reports that Netflix had the lowest rate of tax actually paid compared to profit at 14.7%, while Microsoft paid 20.4%. Amazon, meanwhile, had the worst tax conduct based on factors such as the total amount of tax paid, much if it through “obvious profit shifting” such as booking a sizeable portion of its U.K. income in low-tax Luxembourg. But Amazon’s corporate tax rate was still 19.6% ahead of Netflix and Meta (15.4%) and Apple (18.4%).

The report comes as leaders of many of these same tech companies’ influence genuflect to President Donald Trump. Amazon’s Jeff Bezos, Apple’s Tim Cook and Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg all attended Donald Trump’s second inauguration.

Sparky's Analysis:

The Silicon Six: A Masterclass in Tax Avoidance

In a revelation that should shock no one yet somehow still manages to appall, The Guardian has shed light on the egregious tax practices of the so-called "Silicon Six" - Amazon, Meta (formerly Facebook), Alphabet (Google's parent company), Netflix, Apple, and Microsoft. According to an analysis by the Fair Tax Foundation (FTF), these behemoths of the tech industry have managed to pay a staggering $278 billion less in corporate income tax over the past decade than what they should have, given the statutory rate for U.S. companies.

Let's break down the audacity of this financial maneuvering, shall we?

The Numbers Don't Lie

The Silicon Six have generated a combined revenue of $11 trillion, with profits amounting to $2.5 trillion over the last decade. Yet, these titans of technology have paid an average tax rate of merely 18.8%, a far cry from the U.S. average of 29.7%. This isn't just a loophole; it's a black hole in our tax system that these companies are gleefully diving into.

Tax Avoidance: A Feature, Not a Bug

The Fair Tax Foundation's Chief Executive, Paul Monaghan, didn't mince words, stating, “Our analysis would indicate that tax avoidance continues to be hardwired into corporate structures.” This isn't an accident. It's a deliberate design, with tax avoidance strategies being as much a part of these companies' business models as their actual products and services.

Comparative Misconduct

The report goes further, illustrating how these companies' tax contributions are laughably low compared to other sectors, like banking and energy. Netflix, the streaming giant with a penchant for churning out endless content, takes the crown for the lowest tax rate relative to profit at 14.7%. Meanwhile, Microsoft appears to be the "best" of the worst, with a rate of 20.4%.

Amazon's tactics receive special mention for their brazenness, engaging in “obvious profit shifting” by booking considerable portions of its U.K. income in the tax haven of Luxembourg. Despite this, Amazon's corporate tax rate hovered around 19.6%, still below the average but higher than Netflix and Meta.

Political Puppetry

To add insult to injury, the Fair Tax Foundation highlights the "enormous political influence" wielded by these companies. They pour hundreds of millions into lobbying, ensuring that the tax system is contorted to fit their needs, not the public's. Their influence extends beyond the financial, with leaders like Jeff Bezos, Tim Cook, and Mark Zuckerberg bending the knee to figures like Donald Trump, indicating a worrisome blend of economic power and political capitulation.

The Bottom Line

What we're witnessing is not just tax avoidance; it's a systemic exploitation of the tax code, facilitated by a combination of corporate greed, political influence, and legal loopholes. The Silicon Six's practices underscore a broader issue within our economic and political systems, where the wealthiest companies exploit their power to widen the gap between themselves and the rest of society.

This report from The Guardian isn't just a wake-up call; it's a rallying cry for substantial reform. As these tech giants continue to grow in power and influence, the need for stringent regulations and a fair tax system has never been more apparent. The question now is, will we heed this call, or will we continue to let the Silicon Six — and others like them — rewrite the rules in their favor?
