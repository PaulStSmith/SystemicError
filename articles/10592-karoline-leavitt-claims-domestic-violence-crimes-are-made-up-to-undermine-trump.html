Leavitt was asked, “What crimes was the president referring to?” when Trump said: “If a man has a little fight with the wife, they say, ‘This was a crime,’ see?”

She responded that the President “wasn’t referring to crimes.”

“That’s exactly the point he was making,” she continued, “but the president is saying, and that is that these crimes will be made up and reported as a crime to undermine the great work that the federal task force is doing to reduce crime in Washington, D.C.”

“I think the president has every reason to believe that, given the efforts of many reporters in this room, who actively seek to undermine the president and what he’s doing in our nation’s capital,” Leavitt claimed.

“We all know that deep inside, you all agree with this,” she added, apparently referring to federal troops occupying Washington, D.C., "because you all live here, and I’m sure you are very grateful for the administration’s efforts to make the city, which we all reside in, much safer for ourselves and our families.”

READ MORE: ‘Unconstitutional Coercion’: Trump’s New Prayer Push Sparks Backlash

Trump’s full remarks included this statement:

“Things that take place in the home, they call crime, you know, they’ll do anything they can to find something,” he said on Monday at a meeting of his Religious Liberty Commission. “If a man has a little fight with the wife, they say, ‘This was a crime,’ see?”

Calling Trump’s comment “alarming,” HuffPost on Tuesday reported that Trump was “suggesting that officials in the city were unfairly manipulating crime statistics to make him look bad.”

On Monday, former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance, now a professor of law and MSNBC/NBC News legal analyst, responded to the President’s comment:

“Domestic abuse is a crime. Marital assault and marital rape are both criminal conduct and anyone who commits them should be prosecuted. Full stop.”

Our Analysis:

A Dangerous Disregard for Domestic Violence

In what can only be described as a chilling display of indifference, the White House Press Secretary, Karoline Leavitt, echoed President Donald Trump's alarming remarks about domestic violence, essentially dismissing them as non-crimes and fabrications meant to tarnish his administration's efforts. This assertion not only trivializes the gravity of domestic violence but also perpetuates a dangerous narrative that threatens to undermine the progress made in addressing such a critical issue.

Trump's Controversial Comments

Donald Trump's comments, suggesting that domestic altercations should not be considered crimes, are not just controversial; they are outright dangerous. His remarks, "If a man has a little fight with the wife, they say, ‘This was a crime,’ see?” blatantly minimize the severity of domestic violence. By referring to domestic violence as "a little fight," Trump grossly underestimates the often severe and sometimes fatal consequences of such actions.

Leavitt's Defense: A Disturbing Echo

Karoline Leavitt's defense of Trump's comments does not come as a surprise but is disturbing nonetheless. By stating that these alleged crimes are "made-up" statistics aimed at undermining Trump's work, Leavitt not only supports Trump's dangerous rhetoric but also contributes to a broader culture of disbelief towards victims of domestic violence. This stance is particularly troubling coming from an administration that should be at the forefront of protecting all citizens, especially the most vulnerable.

The Dismissal of Domestic Violence as a Political Tool

What's most alarming about Leavitt's remarks is the implication that domestic violence can be dismissed as a non-issue when it's politically expedient to do so. The suggestion that concerns about domestic violence are somehow a tactic used by reporters to undermine the president is not only baseless but also offensive. It reflects a disturbing trend of politicizing critical social issues at the expense of those affected.

The Reality: Domestic Violence as a Grave Crime

Contrary to the claims made by Trump and Leavitt, domestic violence is a grave crime, one that affects millions of individuals regardless of their political affiliations. The response from Joyce Vance, a former U.S. Attorney and legal analyst, underscores the legal and moral consensus: domestic abuse is criminal conduct that should be prosecuted without exception. Vance's statement serves as a necessary reminder of the legal realities that Trump and his administration seem keen to ignore.

Conclusion: A Call for Responsibility

In light of these comments from Trump and Leavitt, it's crucial to reaffirm the reality of domestic violence as a significant social issue that demands serious attention and action. Dismissing or minimizing domestic violence not only endangers victims but also emboldens perpetrators. It's incumbent upon those in positions of power to lead with empathy and responsibility, recognizing the impact of their words and the reality of domestic violence. The recent remarks from the White House serve as a stark reminder of the ongoing battle against domestic violence and the dangerous rhetoric that seeks to undermine it.
