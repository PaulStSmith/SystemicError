Trump accuses right-wing org's leader of 'rigging' courts: 'Very dangerous mouth!'

President Donald Trump escalated his attacks on one of his former most powerful allies in a rant on his Truth Social platform Tuesday afternoon.

“Sleazebag ‘influencer’ and lawyer Leonard Leo, who has legal issues of his own, goes around telling everyone how he has the Legal System RIGGED,” Trump wrote. “I hope that’s not true and, if it is, it should be thoroughly looked into. Leonard has a big and very dangerous mouth!”

Leonard Leo is the longtime leader of the Federalist Society, a right-wing network of lawyers and legal scholars who infamously make up a sort of “pipeline” to help conservative law students to win clerkships with conservative judges, then judgeships themselves under Republican presidents up to and including the Supreme Court.

He had a hand in advising Trump’s first administration on judicial appointments to transform the federal bench in the GOP’s favor, and was the architect of a right-wing takeover of the courts that allowed for decisions like the overturn of Roe v. Wade. In 2024, he pledged $1 billion to “crush liberal dominance” in the media and corporate boards.

But in his second term, Trump has soured on Leo, largely out of suspicion that Leo had influenced some of the judges who ruled against him on key issues like his tariff scheme.

Last year, he proclaimed, “I was new to Washington, and it was suggested that I use The Federalist Society as a recommending source on Judges. I did so, openly and freely, but then realized that they were under the thumb of a real ‘sleazebag’ named Leonard Leo, a bad person who, in his own way, probably hates America, and obviously has his own separate ambitions.”

This comes amid reporting that Trump is hitting significant roadblocks appointing judges at the same pace he did in his last term, partly because of his falling out with groups like the Federalist Society, and partly because former President Joe Biden filled as many vacancies on the way out as possible to leave him with far fewer chances to make new appointments.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tirade: A Deep Dive into the Fallout with Leonard Leo

In a recent eruption on his Truth Social platform, Donald Trump has launched a scathing attack against Leonard Leo, a figure once deemed an ally in the conservative reshaping of the American judiciary. This assault is not just a mere spat in the political playground; it’s a window into the fractious nature of power, loyalty, and ideology within the conservative movement. Let’s dissect the implications and the undercurrents of this public fallout.

The Target: Leonard Leo

Leonard Leo, a name synonymous with the Federalist Society, has been a linchpin in the conservative legal community, instrumental in steering the judiciary to the right. His efforts have been pivotal in landmark decisions, notably the overturning of Roe v. Wade. Trump’s description of Leo as a “sleazebag ‘influencer’ and lawyer” with a “big and very dangerous mouth” is not just a personal attack but a strategic disavowal of a once-valuable ally.

The Accusations

Trump’s allegation that Leo has the legal system “RIGGED” and his hope that it’s not true (yet suggesting it should be investigated) is a classic Trumpian move. It’s an attempt to sow doubt without providing concrete evidence, a tactic Trump has employed against adversaries and now, it seems, against former confidants.

The Irony of Trump’s Discontent

It’s profoundly ironic that Trump’s disillusionment stems from the belief that Leo influenced judges who later ruled against him, especially considering Trump’s previous reliance on the Federalist Society for judicial recommendations. Trump’s lamentation that he was “new to Washington” and misguided into using the Federalist Society’s recommendations underscores a broader theme in his political narrative: a betrayal by the establishment he sought to manipulate.

The Bigger Picture

Trump’s tirade against Leo is emblematic of a larger battle within the conservative movement—a struggle over the control and direction of its legal and ideological goals. The mention of Leo pledging $1 billion to “crush liberal dominance” juxtaposed with Trump’s attacks illuminates a rift not in objectives but in tactics and leadership.

The Fallout

This public fallout has tangible consequences for Trump, notably in his diminished capacity to appoint judges at the pace he managed in his first term. The combination of his estrangement from influential groups like the Federalist Society and Biden’s proactive filling of judicial vacancies has left Trump grappling with a constrained ability to shape the judiciary.

Conclusion

Trump’s onslaught against Leonard Leo is not merely a personal vendetta; it’s a reflection of the tumultuous and often transactional nature of political alliances. As Trump navigates the challenges of reasserting his influence, the conservative movement finds itself at a crossroads, with its leading figures clashing over the path forward. This discord is not just a narrative of personal ambition but a crucial pivot point that could redefine the conservative agenda and its strategies in the years to come.

In essence, Trump’s tirade is a stark reminder of the fragile alliances within the political sphere, where yesterday’s architect of conservative legal strategy becomes today’s “sleazebag” adversary. As the conservative movement reckons with these internal divisions, the broader implications for the American legal and political landscape remain to be seen.
