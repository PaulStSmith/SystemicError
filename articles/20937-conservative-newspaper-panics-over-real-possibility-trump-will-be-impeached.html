Conservative newspaper panics over 'real possibility' Trump will be impeached

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20262 min read

The GOP’s Majority Is a Shell, and This Article Proves It

A Coalition Held Together by Fear

The Washington Examiner thinks it is warning Republicans about a rough electoral season. What it actually does is admit the obvious: the GOP’s congressional control is fragile, Trump’s approval is weak, and the party’s governing project is thin enough to fit in a footnote. A majority that can be lost by “a literal handful of seats” is not a mandate. It is a precarious hold on institutional power.

That matters because the paper keeps treating Trump’s vulnerability as the story. It isn’t. The story is that Republicans built a government around one man, then handed him a party so hollow it can barely defend itself.

Who Has the Power

The relevant power is not some abstract “polarization” or the public mood. It is the House, the Senate, and the narrow margins that decide whether Trump can continue to use them as shields. The Examiner is right that if Democrats flip the House, they can block his agenda and potentially impeach him again. If they also take the Senate, removal becomes possible.

That is the actual political structure here: institutions still matter, and partisan control still determines whether democratic accountability exists in practice. The paper presents this as a threat to Trump. It is also a reminder that power can be taken away from men who rely on captured institutions and weak opposition.

The Frame Is Already Doing Damage Control

The Examiner’s language tries to convert accountability into partisan misbehavior. A Democratic House impeaching Trump is framed as something Democrats would do to “kill” his agenda, as if the problem were obstruction rather than the president’s conduct and the party’s defense of it.

That is misdirection. Impeachment is not a scandal if the target is a president who has already been impeached twice and continues to govern through extremism, grievance, and institutional degradation. The rhetorical trick is to make democratic restraint look radical and executive abuse look normal.

Trump’s “Agenda” Is Just Power

The paper also slips in a revealing detail: beyond the SAVE America Act, Trump “doesn’t really have a legislative agenda to kill.” That is the tell. This is not a governing coalition with a durable program. It is a power project searching for policy camouflage.

When a presidency has almost no legislative substance, the actual agenda is simpler and uglier: consolidate authority, dominate attention, punish opponents, and survive accountability. Everything else is branding.

What the Story Accidentally Admits

The Examiner wants Republican readers to brace for “trying years.” Fine. The trying years would be a consequence of a party that narrowed itself into dependence on Trump, normalized his conduct, and now has to live with the institutional math it created.

The deeper pattern is not election volatility. It is the fragility of authoritarian politics when democracy still retains some machinery of resistance. A party can stack itself around a strongman, but it cannot repeal the fact that Congress is elected, majorities can change, and power built on grievance eventually has to answer to votes.
