Firestorm ignites over Trump’s golf club ‘mingling’ as ‘zero IQ war’ claims American lives

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Went Golfing While the Strike Machine Kept Running

Trump was reportedly at his Bedminster golf club on Saturday while the U.S. escalated attacks on Iran and the White House delayed its press pool call. At least two U.S. service members had already been killed. That is the context. The rest is damage control.

Power Has a Name

This is not a story about “controversy” over optics. It is a story about presidential power being exercised badly and then dressed up as inevitability. Trump holds the office. His administration is directing the escalation. Central Command is announcing the strikes. The chain of responsibility is not mysterious, and it does not dissolve because the president is photographed among wealthy club patrons while the war machine keeps moving.

The people posting outrage on X are not the ones ordering strikes, delaying briefings, or deciding what the public is allowed to know when U.S. personnel are killed. Their anger may be justified, but it is not the center of the story. Power is.

Damage Control Is Not Confusion

The White House “repeatedly delayed” the press pool call, which the reporting itself says suggested damage control behind the scenes. That is the important phrase. Damage control is what institutions do when they know the facts are politically toxic and want to manage the sequence in which the public sees them.

This is how power launders itself: not by denying reality outright, but by slowing access to it, fragmenting it, and letting the spectacle of the president’s leisure absorb the attention that should be on the decision to keep escalating.

The War Is Being Framed to Hide Its Authors

Central Command says the new strikes were meant to “punish” IRGC forces after attacks on American service members in Jordan. That language is built for moral cover. It turns escalation into discipline, retaliation into necessity, and policy choice into automatic response.

But the source also notes that Trump had previously claimed Iran’s military had been “destroyed,” while U.S. forces were still being targeted by ballistic missiles and drones. That gap matters. It exposes the political function of inflated claims: declare victory first, then let the facts struggle to catch up. When the facts do not cooperate, the administration does not correct itself. It escalates again.

The Golf Club Is Not the Story

The golf club matters only because it reveals contempt for responsibility. A president can be at a luxury club while service members are dying because the office has been reduced to branding and performance. The setting is not incidental. It is part of the hierarchy. Wealth, insulation, and impunity sit above consequence.

That is why the outrage lands. Not because a president is forbidden from being photographed off-duty, but because the image captures the moral architecture of the moment: ordinary people absorb the costs, while the people with power circulate through private comforts and public denials.

What This Pattern Reveals

This is the standard operating procedure of American militarized politics: make the decision at the top, diffuse the consequences downward, and outsource accountability to patriotic language and bureaucratic delay. The press gets stalled. The public gets slogans. The dead get folded into a narrative about resolve.

That is the larger pattern here. When leaders can launch violence, obscure responsibility, and then treat the fallout as a communications problem, the system is not malfunctioning. It is performing exactly as designed.
