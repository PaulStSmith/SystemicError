Fox News admitted a major blunder Saturday after it reported on videos of Americans “complaining” about federal food assistance being disrupted amid the government shutdown, videos it originally failed to identify as being generated with artificial intelligence.

“Wow – Fox News fell for a racist AI video about [Supplemental Nutrition Food Assistance Program] recipients with ‘7 baby daddies’… then when called out changed the story to be about AI videos going viral,” wrote The Tennessee Holler, a progressive news outlet, in a social media post on X Saturday.

Fox News published its original report on Friday, titled “SNAP beneficiaries threaten to ransack stores over government shutdown,” which included several videos of supposed SNAP recipients voicing outrage at the program’s disruption. After some of the included videos were flagged as being AI-generated, however, the article’s headline was changed to “AI videos of SNAP beneficiaries complaining about cuts go viral.”

Additionally, an editor’s note appeared at the bottom of the revised story to note the following: “This article previously reported on some videos that appear to have been generated by AI without noting that.”

Unsurprisingly, Fox News was swiftly pounced on by critics for its error.

“Not sure if I’ve seen anything like this before – Fox [fell] for an AI video and basically rewrote their whole story when called out,” wrote CNN senior politics reporter Andrew Kaczynski in a social media post on X Saturday.

Tim Miller, an MSNBC analyst and host of The Bulwark Podcast, sarcastically quipped at Fox News’ “interesting news judgement,” particularly in quoting a SNAP recipient who said that “it is the taxpayer’s responsibility to take care of my kids,” which the updated Fox News article now notes “appears to be generated by AI.

“Fox News ran an article about a woman who posted on TikTok about how she has 7 baby daddies and needs her SNAPs,” Miller wrote in a social media post on X. “Interesting news judgment.”

Our Analysis:

Fox News’ AI Blunder: A Deep Dive into Misinformation and Bias

In what can only be described as a spectacular failure of journalistic integrity, Fox News recently served its audience a heaping dish of misinformation, garnished with a side of racism, only to perform a hasty backtrack when their source was revealed to be as artificial as the intelligence that generated it. This incident is not just about a major news outlet getting duped by AI; it’s a glaring spotlight on the underbelly of media bias and the dangers of unchecked technology in news reporting.

The Original Sin: Fox News and the AI-Generated Racist Trope

Fox News published a report on Friday, under the alarmist headline “SNAP beneficiaries threaten to ransack stores over government shutdown,” which featured videos of purported SNAP recipients expressing outrage over disruptions in their benefits. The narrative: chaos, ungratefulness, and the age-old stereotype of the welfare queen with “7 baby daddies.” It’s a storyline that would fit neatly into the conservative media’s playbook, except for one minuscule detail – the videos were fake, generated by artificial intelligence.

Caught Red-handed: The Shift to Damage Control

After the fabricated nature of the videos was flagged, Fox News scrambled to revise the story, now headlined “AI videos of SNAP beneficiaries complaining about cuts go viral.” An editor’s note was tacked onto the bottom, a meek admission of the oversight in failing to recognize the videos as AI-generated. This swift pivot raises a critical question: Where was the due diligence? In the rush to paint a narrative, Fox News exposed not just a lapse in fact-checking but a willingness to disseminate harmful stereotypes without verification.

The Backlash: A Chorus of Criticism

The fallout was immediate and fierce. Critics, including CNN senior politics reporter Andrew Kaczynski and MSNBC analyst Tim Miller, lambasted Fox News for its gullibility and questionable news judgment. The incident was not merely an “oops” moment but a stark illustration of how easily AI can be weaponized to spread misinformation, especially when it plays into pre-existing biases.

The Bigger Picture: AI, Media, and Responsibility

This debacle serves as a watershed moment for the intersection of AI and media. As AI-generated content becomes increasingly sophisticated, the potential for misuse skyrockets. News outlets, particularly those with significant influence like Fox News, bear the responsibility of rigorous fact-checking before broadcasting information to the public. The dissemination of AI-generated falsehoods, particularly those that reinforce racist tropes, is not just irresponsible; it’s dangerous, fueling divisions and undermining trust in media.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Vigilance

The Fox News blunder is not just a cautionary tale about the pitfalls of AI in journalism; it’s a revealing glimpse into the biases that can drive media narratives. As we venture further into the digital age, the need for media literacy and journalistic integrity has never been more critical. News outlets must prioritize accuracy over sensationalism, and audiences must remain vigilant, questioning the sources and motives behind the information they consume. In the battle against misinformation, both media and the public have pivotal roles to play.
