Trump would be 'hanged' if he went before Nuremberg trials: top political scholar

By Paulo Santos

The Real Story Behind the Scandal

In recent discussions about the legality of U.S. and Israeli military actions in Iran, stark accusations resonate with historical gravity. Professor John Mearsheimer, a prominent figure in political science, has made a chilling comparison: if subjected to Nuremberg-style trials, key leaders such as President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu would face severe consequences for their decisions. This assertion, made during an interview on a podcast hosted by former judge Andrew Napolitano, underlines a critical analysis of how these leaders and their administrations have approached international law.

Decoding the Allegations

Mearsheimer argues that the military actions undertaken by the U.S. and Israel in Iran lack a legal pretext. He states unequivocally, “We didn’t even make any attempt to argue that the Iranians had done something militarily to precipitate our attack.” This statement is crucial as it highlights the absence of provocation, potentially classifying the attack as an unprovoked aggression—a serious breach of international norms and laws.

Furthermore, Mearsheimer brings to light other severe allegations, including the assassination of world leaders and the situation in Gaza, which he describes as genocide under an apartheid state. These are heavy accusations, implicating the U.S. and Israeli governments in actions forbidden under international law, specifically referencing their role in exacerbating the situation in Gaza.

The Nuremberg Echo

The reference to the Nuremberg trials is particularly poignant. These trials, held after World War II, set a precedent in international law, holding leaders accountable for war crimes, including genocide and waging aggressive war, deemed “the supreme international crime.” By invoking Nuremberg, Mearsheimer isn’t just making a legal argument; he’s making a moral one, suggesting that the actions of Trump and Netanyahu should be scrutinized under these established international standards.

Current Actions and International Law

Since the initiation of hostilities against Iran, accusations against Trump include threatening and committing war crimes. A recent example cited involves Trump’s threat to destroy Iranian civilian infrastructure, such as “Electric Generating Plants, Oil Wells, and Kharg Island.” The targeting of civilian infrastructure is a clear violation of the laws of war, classified as a war crime. The White House’s response, as articulated by the Press Secretary, underscores a dismissive attitude toward international legal norms, emphasizing a deal under threats rather than diplomacy.

Institutional Power and Decision Making

It is crucial to identify who holds the institutional power in this scenario and who made the decisions that led to these outcomes. The actions taken by the U.S. and Israel can be directly linked to decisions made at the highest levels of government by President Trump and Prime Minister Netanyahu. By recognizing this, we avoid misdirecting responsibility toward actors with limited or no control over these military strategies.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The chilling analysis provided by Mearsheimer calls for a broader reflection on the accountability of those in power. The allegations of war crimes and violations of international law require serious consideration and an unbiased investigation. If history has taught us anything, it is that the consequences of unchecked power can lead to grave injustices. Thus, the international community must ponder the implications of these actions and consider the mechanisms available to ensure that those who wield power are also bound by the rules of the global order.
