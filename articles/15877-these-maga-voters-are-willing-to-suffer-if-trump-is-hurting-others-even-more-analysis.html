But hardcore MAGA voters are expressing no regrets. And CNN's Harry Enten believes that "very few" of the 2024 MAGA voters now "regret" voting for Trump.

In a biting opinion column published on April 18, The Guardian's Arwa Mahdawi argues that many MAGA voters are suffering because of Trump's policies — but are willing to suffer if others are suffering even more.

"The stock market is plunging, prices are rising, federal workers are getting laid off, students are being snatched off the street by immigration agents," Mahdawi observes. "The U.S. is many things at the moment, but stable is not one of them. So, amid all this turmoil, how are all the Donald Trump voters feeling? Has buyer's remorse set in?.... Not according to the polls. Rather, the U.S. appears to be a nation of Édith Piafs: they regret rien."

Mahdawi is referring to "No, Je Ne Regrette Rien" ("No, I Regret Nothing"), a song famously recorded by the iconic French singer Édith Piaf in 1960.

The Guardian columnist stresses that staunch MAGA voters are still loyal to Trump even though his policies are "hurting" them, and they don't regret their 2024 vote at all.

"Everyone is feeling some pain right now," Mahdawi writes. "In a hyper-polarized country, however, what seems to really matter to many voters isn't how much pain they're feeling themselves, but whether the other side is suffering more…. Right now, however, Trump is hurting the sort of people many of his voters seem to be interested in seeing get hurt. He's an avenging angel, wreaking vengeance on the elite institutions, scapegoats and bogeymen that the Republican Party has spent years blaming for the state of the U.S. He's cut funding to all the Ivy League universities he's called 'woke' and declared out of touch with American values."

Mahdawi continues, "He's gone after transgender people. And he has rounded up immigrants and protesters, just as he promised he would do. Trump isn't just doing every vindictive thing he told his supporters he was going to do: he's trolling his detractors via nasty memes. He's rubbing salt in their wounds."

Mahdawi argues that although "Trump may be hurting all the people he said he'd be hurting, there's plenty of pain to go around." And that "pain," she warns, could get worse in the months ahead.

"With his tariff plan," the Guardian columnist says, "he is just getting started.… When the price of basic goods keep rising, there's only so long you can keep feeding people obvious lies. Perhaps the Trumpers won't get quite so much of a dopamine rush from 'owning the libs' when they can't afford to own anything else."

Our Analysis:

The Masochism of MAGA Loyalty: A Deep Dive

In a recent dissection of the current political climate, Arwa Mahdawi of The Guardian shines a harsh light on the paradox of support for Donald Trump among his base, even as his policies actively undermine their economic stability and social welfare. This analysis begs a deeper examination, not just of the what, but the why, behind this unwavering loyalty that seems to thrive on a diet of spite and self-harm.

Swing Voters' Buyer's Remorse vs. MAGA's Unwavering Support

Firstly, the divide between swing voters and hardcore MAGA supporters is stark. Swing voters who flipped from Joe Biden in 2020 to Donald Trump in 2024 are starting to feel the sting of their choices. Policies like steep tariffs and layoffs at the U.S. Social Security Administration (SSA) are beginning to bite, proving that economic policy isn’t just rhetoric—it has real-life consequences. Yet, for the MAGA crowd, these detrimental effects seem to pale in comparison to the satisfaction derived from seeing their perceived adversaries suffer even more. This dichotomy is not just fascinating; it's a startling revelation of how deeply polarization has infected the American psyche.

The Economics of Vengeance

Mahdawi's observation that Trump's policies are causing widespread pain, yet failing to dent his support among his base, underscores a disturbing trend: the prioritization of vengeance over personal and national well-being. Trump’s actions, from cutting funding to Ivy League universities to targeting transgender individuals and immigrants, are less about policy and more about punishment. Trump is not just a president; in the eyes of his supporters, he’s an avenging angel tasked with exacting retribution on the "elite institutions" and marginalized communities.

This approach is not just vindictive; it’s economically suicidal. Tariffs, for example, are not a one-way ticket to economic superiority; they are a burden on consumers, a tax that hits the wallets of everyday Americans, not the foreign powers they’re intended to punish. When the cost of basic goods rises, no amount of schadenfreude can fill the stomach or pay the rent. The "dopamine rush" of "owning the libs" is a poor substitute for economic stability and prosperity.

The Cult of Personality vs. Economic Reality

What this boils down to is a classic case of a cult of personality triumphing over economic reality. Trump's base appears to be so deeply invested in the persona of Trump as an outsider and fighter against a corrupt elite that they are willing to suffer, so long as they believe he is still fighting their war. This is not just misguided; it's masochistic. The real tragedy here is that the policies and actions that make them cheer are the very ones that will ensure their continued economic and social disenfranchisement.

Conclusion: The High Cost of Vindictiveness

In closing, Mahdawi’s column is not just a critique of Trump’s policies; it's an indictment of a political culture that values retribution over progress, division over unity, and personal loyalty over the common good. The real question is not whether Trump's supporters will ever feel buyer's remorse, but whether they will recognize the cost of their vindictiveness before it's too late. As the economic pain deepens, one wonders how long this loyalty will last once the reality of Trump's policies hits home. Will the MAGA base continue to sing "No, I Regret Nothing" as their economic prospects dwindle? Only time will tell, but history is not on their side.
