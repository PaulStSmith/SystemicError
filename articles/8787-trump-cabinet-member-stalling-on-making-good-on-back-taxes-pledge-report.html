Trump Cabinet member stalling on making good on back-taxes pledge: report

Now that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is securely ensconced within Donald Trump’s administration, he is dragging his feet over a confirmation pledge that he would set aside funds to pay almost $1 million in taxes he should have paid into Medicare.

According to a report from the New York Times’ Andrew Duehren on Wednesday morning, Bessent, who has become the face of Donald Trump’s economic policies, has been using a tax dodge for his personal income by setting up an investment firm in such a way that he can avoid paying into Social Security and Medicare.

As the Times is reporting, objections have been raised about the loophole, which the IRS agreed was a problem and, when confronted with the issue when he was being confirmed, the Wall Street hedge funder said he would not follow the IRS guidance, but would “create “a reserve fund to address any contingency related to this issue.”

However, as he has doubled as the head of the IRS since August, the agency has been dragging its feet when it comes to definitive regulations allowing him to avoid making any payment at all as he disputes the amount.

The Times is reporting, “Mr. Bessent’s hedge fund, Key Square Capital Management, was set up as a limited partnership. Through that structure, Mr. Bessent avoided paying roughly $910,000 in Medicare taxes on money he made running his hedge fund in 2021, 2022 and 2023, according to a memorandum prepared by Democratic Senate staff for Mr. Bessent’s confirmation hearing in January.”

The Times’ Duehren observed, “Mr. Bessent’s decision to not pay the additional tax has now put him in the unusual spot of personally opposing — and having a personal stake in — how the I.R.S. interprets tax law. And since he took office, the Treasury and I.R.S. have backed away from developing regulations to address it.”

Noting that Bessent is up to date on Social Security taxes now, the report adds, “Under the Biden administration, the I.R.S. and Treasury made it a goal to curb the ability of firm owners to avoid paying self-employment taxes like Mr. Bessent did. In 2023 and 2024, they included the issue on a public list of priorities for further guidance or regulation.”

Our Analysis:

The Brazen Hypocrisy of Scott Bessent: A Closer Look

In an administration that has become synonymous with the flouting of norms and ethical boundaries, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s actions, as detailed in a recent New York Times report by Andrew Duehren, are not just a slap in the face to the American taxpayer but a textbook example of the audacity that seems to be a prerequisite for office within Donald Trump’s cabinet.

Bessent, who has positioned himself as the poster child for Trump’s economic strategies, has been caught utilizing a tax avoidance scheme that screams of both privilege and disdain for the obligations of citizenship. By manipulating the structure of his investment firm, Key Square Capital Management, Bessent has shirked approximately $910,000 in Medicare taxes—a sum that would make most Americans’ eyes water, considering it’s more than many will earn in a lifetime.

The Heart of the Matter:

Let’s break down the egregiousness of Bessent’s actions:

  1. Tax Dodging with a Side of Irony: By setting up his hedge fund as a limited partnership, Bessent has danced around a significant financial obligation to Medicare, a program that provides healthcare to millions of Americans over 65. It’s particularly ironic, given that as head of the IRS since August, he’s in a prime position to influence tax policy.

  2. A Pledge Ignored: During his confirmation, Bessent promised to set aside funds to cover this tax discrepancy. Yet, here we are, witnessing his blatant disregard for that commitment. It’s a classic move in the political playbook: promise accountability to secure confirmation, then backtrack once the position is secured.

  3. Opposing IRS Guidance: Despite the IRS identifying the loophole as problematic, Bessent has asserted that he will not follow the agency’s guidance. It’s a bold stance, especially considering that, under his watch, the Treasury and IRS have seemingly lost their appetite for closing this loophole.

  4. The Consequences of Inaction: While Bessent has reportedly caught up on his Social Security taxes, the Medicare tax evasion issue remains unresolved. Under the Biden administration, there was momentum to address such tax avoidance schemes, but with Bessent at the helm, progress appears to have stalled.

The Bottom Line:

What we have here is a glaring example of the systemic error that permeates this administration. Bessent’s case is not just about tax evasion; it’s a narrative thread in the larger tapestry of an administration marked by ethical flexibility and a disconcerting willingness to prioritize personal gain over public good.

The audacity of Bessent to oppose and delay IRS tax law interpretations that would affect him personally is not just unethical; it’s a stark reminder of the power dynamics at play. It begs the question: Who benefits from such policies, and at what cost to the American public?

In an era where the divide between the haves and have-nots is increasingly pronounced, Bessent’s actions—and the administration’s apparent complicity—underscore a disturbing trend of governance that is, at its core, antithetical to the principles of equality and justice.

It’s time for a reckoning, not just for Bessent but for all who maneuver through loopholes at the expense of the many. Accountability must be more than a buzzword trotted out during confirmation hearings; it must be the cornerstone of public service. Until then, we remain trapped in a cycle of systemic error, doomed to repeat the mistakes of the past unless we demand and enact change.
