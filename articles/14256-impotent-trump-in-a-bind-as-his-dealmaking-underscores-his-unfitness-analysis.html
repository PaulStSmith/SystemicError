'Impotent' Trump in a bind as his dealmaking 'underscores his unfitness': analysis

New Republic columnist Greg Sargent spoke with Bulwark writer Cathy Young on the ‘Daily Blast’ podcast about how President Donald Trump may ultimately handle the ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine based upon his own self-perception of weakness.

Trump recently revealed his mounting frustration with Russian leader Vladimir Putin in a series of Truth Social posts and public statements. Trump lashed out at Putin for ramping up drone attacks on civilian territory in defiance of Trump’s stated goal for peace. Trump called Putin “crazy,” and admitted he has no idea what Putin is thinking. The Kremlin recently responded by calling the U.S. president 'emotional.'

Sargent said part of Trump’s frustration with the Russian leader is that he can’t conceive of anybody acting independently of Trump’s own needs.

“What strikes us about all this is that Trump only seems capable of understanding Putin’s continuing warmaking as an affront to him personally, which really underscores his unfitness for this moment,” said Sargent. “He seems to be incapable of grasping that Putin has a set of goals and a worldview independent of Trump. … Trump is not capable of asking ‘how does Vladimir see the world.’”

Young said she believed Trump is either “entirely indifferent to the state of Ukraine or actively rooting for Russia,” but she was confident Trump could not openly hand the nation over to its larger invading neighbor without personally looking “weak.”

“It would seem, given one of Trump’s chief concerns is to project an image of his own strength and not be seen as impotent, you’d think he would get onboard with tough sanctions, as some Republicans are proposing,” Young said. “… He understands he can’t afford to … tell Russia to go ahead and take what you want, because that’s really not going to make him look great.So, I think he’s in a bind, and part of the reason he’s raging is because he’s flailing trying to decide which course of action will make him look weak—because that’s his preoccupation.”

She added that much of what Trump decides to do appears to be dictated by who’s influencing him at that moment.

“Trump is really susceptible to the last person he speaks to,” she said, adding jokingly that this is probably why Zelensky should “learn how to play golf and spend a lot of time at Mar-a-Lago.”

Someone may ultimately talk Trump into imposing new Russian sanctions, she said, but she feared the war would drone on with no significant movement on either side without dramatic intervention of some sort. The Ukrainian people are more emboldened than ever as Putin bombs schools, playgrounds and bomb shelters, while Putin has “caught a tiger’s tail” and can’t surrender the invasion without creating a bunch of jaded ex-soldiers demanding, ‘what was it all for?’”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Self-Perception and Global Politics: A Scathing Analysis

In a recent episode of the ‘Daily Blast’ podcast, Greg Sargent of New Republic and Cathy Young of Bulwark dissected the convoluted psyche of Donald Trump, particularly in relation to the ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine. The analysis provided is as revealing as it is troubling, painting a picture of a former leader whose actions—or lack thereof—are driven by personal vanity rather than geopolitical strategy or humanitarian concern.

Trump’s recent outbursts against Vladimir Putin, labeling the Russian leader as “crazy” and expressing bafflement at his strategies, are not rooted in a sudden epiphany about the horrors of war or a principled stand against aggression. Rather, they stem from a petulant sense of betrayal and a narcissistic inability to understand actions not revolving around him. The Kremlin’s dismissal of Trump as 'emotional' is the icing on this cake of international diplomacy turned farce.

Trump's Worldview: Ego Above All

The key takeaway from Sargent and Young’s discussion is the alarming revelation that Trump perceives global events through the narrow lens of his own ego. The notion that Putin’s aggression in Ukraine could be motivated by anything other than a personal affront to Trump is, apparently, unfathomable to the former U.S. president. This is narcissism, not leadership.

"What strikes us about all this is that Trump only seems capable of understanding Putin’s continuing warmaking as an affront to him personally, which really underscores his unfitness for this moment," Sargent rightly points out. This ego-centric view of world affairs is dangerous, not just for the United States, but for global stability.

The Dilemma of Appearing Weak

Cathy Young’s insights into Trump’s fear of appearing "weak" are particularly telling. It’s a classic Trumpian bind: on one hand, his affinity for strongmen like Putin makes it difficult for him to condemn Russia’s actions outrightly; on the other, outright support for the invasion would tarnish his self-crafted image of strength. His solution? Lash out in confusion and frustration, a child lost in a game too complex for his understanding.

Trump's indecisiveness and susceptibility to the last person he speaks to, as Young jests, suggest a man easily swayed by the winds of convenience rather than the compass of principle. The suggestion that Zelensky should "learn how to play golf" to gain Trump's favor is a darkly humorous indictment of Trump's superficial approach to international relations.

The Human Cost

Lost in Trump’s egocentric drama is the real, human cost of the conflict in Ukraine. As Putin bombs schools, playgrounds, and bomb shelters, the resilience of the Ukrainian people shines as a beacon of courage against tyranny. Yet, Trump’s preoccupation with his own image blinds him to the tragedy unfolding and the strategic importance of a robust, principled response to Russian aggression.

Conclusion: Leadership or Lack Thereof

The discussion on the ‘Daily Blast’ podcast is a stark reminder of the perils of leadership that prioritizes personal ego over genuine statesmanship. Trump’s handling—or mishandling—of the Russian invasion of Ukraine is a case study in how not to conduct foreign policy.

As the world watches Ukraine's struggle, the need for leaders who understand the complexity of global affairs, empathize with the suffering of the innocent, and act decisively for the greater good has never been clearer. In this context, Trump’s actions and attitudes are not just disappointing; they are a systemic failure of character and duty.
