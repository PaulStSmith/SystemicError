'Don't want what's best for them': Trump reveals he disagreed with slain Kirk on key issue

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks onstage during a memorial service for slain conservative commentator Charlie Kirk at State Farm Stadium, in Glendale, Arizona, U.S., September 21, 2025. REUTERS/Caitlin O’Hara

President Donald Trump revealed on Sunday that he disagreed with slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk on a key issue during Kirk's memorial service in Glendale, Arizona.

Trump praised Kirk's skill for "uniting" the MAGA movement and approach to debating his opponents during his memorial speech. However, Trump said he disagreed with Kirk wishing his political opponents well.

Trump made the comments during a story he told about a conversation Kirk and a staffer had on the day he was assassintated. Trump said Kirk told the staffer that he wanted to "lead them into the great way of life in our country."

"In that private moment on his dying day, we find everything we need to know about who Charlie Kirk was," Trump said. "He was a missionary with a noble spirit and a great, great purpose. He did not hate his opponents; he wanted the best for them."

"That's where I disagreed with Charlie," Trump said. "I hate my opponent and I don't want the best for them. I'm sorry. I am sorry, Erika [Kirk]."

"But now maybe Erika can talk to me and the whole group and convince me that's not right," Trump continued. "But right now I can't stand my opponent."

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of Trump's Eulogy: Ideology Over Empathy

In a moment that could have been dedicated entirely to the memory of a fallen conservative activist, former President Donald Trump chose the memorial service of Charlie Kirk to underscore a fundamental divide not just within the conservative movement, but within the very fabric of American political discourse. During his eulogy in Glendale, Arizona, Trump made it clear: his worldview is starkly at odds with the notion of wishing political opponents well, a sentiment attributed to Kirk in his final hours.

Trump's Praise and Pivot

Trump began with praise for Charlie Kirk, lauding his ability to unite the MAGA movement and his adeptness in debate—a commendation fitting for a memorial. Yet, the tone took a sharp turn as Trump recounted a tale that was meant to highlight Kirk's character. Kirk, on the day he was assassinated, apparently expressed a desire for his political opponents to be led "into the great way of life in our country." This revelation was poised to paint Kirk as a figure of noble spirit and great purpose, one who harbored no hate for his adversaries.

The Disagreement

However, it was here that Trump diverged, stating plainly, "That's where I disagreed with Charlie." Trump's admission—"I hate my opponent and I don't want the best for them"—was not just a disagreement over tactics or policy but a fundamental clash of ideologies. It was a moment of stark honesty, revealing a bitterness and division that transcends political rivalry and enters the realm of personal animosity.

The Implications

This admission is telling for several reasons:

  • Ideological Rifts: It highlights a deep ideological rift within conservative circles and the broader political arena. Kirk's approach, as recounted by Trump, suggests a belief in political opposition as a healthy part of democracy, one where adversaries are still seen as fellow citizens. Trump's stance, conversely, views opposition as fundamentally adversarial, not just on policy but on personal grounds.

  • Political Discourse: Trump's comments at a memorial service, intended to honor Kirk, instead served as a platform for airing personal grievances and political philosophy. This choice underscores a broader trend where political figures leverage every opportunity to push their agenda, even those that might traditionally be apolitical or solemn.

  • Empathy vs. Animosity: At the heart of Trump's statement is a rejection of empathy for opponents, a stance that could further polarize an already divided nation. It suggests a political landscape where compromise is impossible because the opposition is not just wrong but unworthy of well-wishes.

Conclusion

In his eulogy for Charlie Kirk, Donald Trump inadvertently laid bare the fault lines not only within the conservative movement but in American political life at large. By choosing division over unity, even in death, Trump's remarks serve as a grim reminder of the challenges facing American democracy. It's a stance that doesn't just reject the notion of wishing well to political opponents but undermines the very principles of civility and mutual respect necessary for a functioning democracy.

As we reflect on Trump's words and the legacy of Charlie Kirk, it's crucial to ask ourselves what kind of political culture we wish to cultivate. Is it one marked by animosity and division, or one that, despite profound disagreements, sees the humanity in our opponents? The answer to this question will shape not just the future of political discourse but the very soul of the nation.
