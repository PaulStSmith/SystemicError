'Bad optics every day': Lawyer slams Trump DOJ for 'turning the clock back to mob rule'

CNN released footage showing Ed Martin, a controversial Trump appointee to the DOJ, taking pictures outside of New York Attorney General Letitia James' home. Abbe Lowell, who currently represents James in a mortgage fraud lawsuit the DOJ filed against her, joined CNN's Brianna Keilar on "The Source" Tuesday night to discuss the department's actions.

"This Justice Department creates new, bad optics every day," Lowell said.

On Sunday, Martin joined Fox News host Maria Bartiromo and said that he would play out some of his investigations in the court of public opinion. Lowell said that the move is "extraordinary."

"If we're at the point at which prosecutors, be they federal or state, believe their job is to try people in the court of public opinion, then we're basically turning the clock back to mob rule," Lowell said.

Lawell said these, and other actions, suggest Martin has no interest in being an "ethical lawyer, or someone who follows the law."

For instance, Lowell pointed out that Martin has not asked him for information about the case against James since he was appointed. Lowell also noted that Martin asked James to resign twice in his initial correspondence. Martin said in the letter that he would see James' resignation as an "act of good faith."

"If people needed better evidence that this is not about what she did, but is about what President Trump has ordered the Justice Department to do, nothing better than his letter demanding those two things," Lowell said.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of Justice: A Critique of the DOJ's Latest Antics

In a recent development that reads more like a script from a political thriller than the sober proceedings expected of the Department of Justice (DOJ), Ed Martin, a controversial appointee of the Trump administration, was caught on camera by CNN taking photographs outside the home of New York Attorney General Letitia James. This incident, emblematic of a broader pattern of questionable tactics, has drawn fierce criticism from Abbe Lowell, a high-profile Washington, D.C. lawyer representing James in a mortgage fraud lawsuit filed by the DOJ against her.

The Spectacle of Justice

Lowell's comments during his appearance on CNN's "The Source" with Brianna Keilar highlight a concerning trend within the DOJ under Trump's influence. He described the department's actions as creating "new, bad optics every day," a statement that barely scratches the surface of the issue. The real crux of the problem is not just the optics, but the apparent shift in the DOJ's approach to justice—a shift towards playing out cases in the court of public opinion, as Ed Martin explicitly stated on his Fox News appearance with Maria Bartiromo.

Lowell rightly points out the dangers of such an approach, equating it to "mob rule," a regression to a state where public sentiment, manipulated by sensationalism and misinformation, dictates the outcome of legal proceedings rather than the facts and the law. This is a dangerous precedent, one that undermines the very foundation of the judicial system and threatens the principles of fairness and impartiality.

Ethical Quandaries and Political Machinations

The refusal of Martin to engage with Lowell for information about the case against James, coupled with his audacious demands for James' resignation as some form of "act of good faith," speaks volumes about the motivations driving these legal actions. It's not about the law or justice; it's about political retribution, orchestrated by those in power to target perceived adversaries. Lowell's assessment that this is not about what James did, but rather what Trump has ordered the DOJ to do, hits the nail on the head. It exposes the DOJ's actions as not just unethical, but fundamentally anti-democratic.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This incident, and the broader implications it carries, should alarm anyone who cares about the integrity of the United States' legal system. It is a stark reminder that the tools of justice can be twisted into weapons of political warfare, undermining the rule of law and eroding public trust in vital institutions.

The actions of Ed Martin and the DOJ, as directed by the Trump administration in this case, represent a systemic error that needs urgent correction. It is imperative for the legal community, the media, and the public to remain vigilant, to question and critique the motivations behind such legal actions, and to demand accountability from those who pervert the course of justice for political gain.

In a democracy, the court of public opinion should be informed by the outcomes of fair and lawful judicial proceedings, not the other way around. The spectacle at the DOJ serves as a cautionary tale, highlighting the fragile balance between justice and the abuse of power. It's a balance that must be fiercely protected, now more than ever.
