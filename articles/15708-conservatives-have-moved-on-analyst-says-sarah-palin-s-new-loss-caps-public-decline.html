'Conservatives have moved on': Analyst says Sarah Palin's new loss caps 'public decline'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 24, 20255 min read

FILE PHOTO: Republican candidate for the U.S. House of Representatives Sarah Palin attends a campaign event ahead of mid-term elections in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S. October 9, 2022. REUTERS/Kerry Tasker/File Photo

Sarah Palin's court loss against The New York Times is the final nail in the coffin of a public career that has been sliding inexorably toward oblivion, Alex Sammon wrote in a blistering analysis for Slate published on Tuesday evening.

Palin's defamation suit against The Times, the third such suit of the decade after previous verdicts were thrown out by an appeals court for procedural issues, ended with the jury finding, after just three hours of deliberation, that the paper could not be held legally liable for wrongly implying, in a 2017 article, that Palin may have incited the 2011 Tucson mass shooting that severely injured former Rep. Gabby Giffords.

But it caps off a long string of humiliations — and a "steady public decline" — for the former Alaska governor and vice presidential candidate once seen as a rising star in the Republican Party, wrote Sammon.

"A guilty verdict in the long-shot defamation case may have resolidified Palin’s reputation in Republican politics," Sammon wrote. "Conservatives have long sought an opportunity to overturn New York Times v. Sullivan, the landmark Supreme Court case that strongly limited the ability of a public official to sue for defamation. But in the three years since the first go-around of the Palin trial, conservatives have moved on. More recently, billionaire Trump donor and casino magnate Steve Wynn filed a libel lawsuit against the Associated Press, which seemed more promising than Palin’s effort. But that, too, failed: The Supreme Court declined to hear it in March."

Notably, he wrote, this comes after Palin suffered a resounding humiliation while trying to get back into elected office: "Her 2022 run for Alaska’s at-large congressional seat was also a multi-try failure; despite an endorsement from President Donald Trump, she lost in a special election and again in a general election in 2022 to Democrat Mary Peltola. With her finally on the sidelines in 2024, Republicans won the seat back."

The defamation suit, Sammon continued, was one of her final shots at breaking through the cycle for Republicans — and that, too, failed.

And in a final humiliation, he concluded, "Palin has said publicly that she 'would love to' take a position in the Trump administration. But as of February, according to News Nation, she 'hasn’t been contacted.'"

The Unraveling of Sarah Palin: A Critical Examination

In a blistering analysis for Slate, Alex Sammon delivers a scathing overview of Sarah Palin's public career trajectory, culminating in a court loss against The New York Times - a defeat that seems to symbolize the final act of Palin’s political relevance. This article not only sheds light on Palin's recent legal defeat but also encapsulates her steady decline from a once-promising figure in the Republican Party to an individual struggling to maintain her political significance.

The Defamation Suit: A Failed Attempt at Vindication

Palin's defamation suit against The New York Times, concerning a 2017 article that implied her potential incitement of the 2011 Tucson mass shooting, ended in a verdict that the paper could not be held legally liable. The jury's decision came after a mere three hours of deliberation, highlighting the weakness of Palin's legal challenge. This was not Palin's first rodeo in the legal arena, yet it marks a significant blow to her credibility and her quest for a political resurgence. It’s important to note the broader context here: conservatives have been itching for a case to challenge New York Times v. Sullivan, a landmark Supreme Court decision that makes it exceedingly difficult for public officials to sue for defamation. Palin's case, however, fell flat, signifying not just a personal defeat but a missed opportunity for her political camp.

A Series of Public Humiliations

The article meticulously chronicles Palin's "steady public decline," a narrative filled with failed attempts to claw back into political relevance. Her 2022 bid for Alaska’s at-large congressional seat, despite the backing of President Donald Trump, ended in a dismal failure against Democrat Mary Peltola - not once, but twice. This sequence of defeats underscores a broader trend of rejection and signifies a disconnect between Palin’s political ambitions and the electorate's appetite.

The Final Insult

In what can only be described as a final humiliation, Palin’s publicly expressed desire to take a position in the Trump administration has been met with deafening silence. This detail, though small, speaks volumes about Palin's current standing within the Republican Party. Once a vice-presidential candidate, Palin now finds herself sidelined, her aspirations for a comeback unfulfilled and seemingly ignored by the very administration she seeks to join.

Conclusion: The Palin Paradox

Palin’s journey is emblematic of a particular brand of political celebrity - one that burns bright but fades quickly. Her fall from grace, punctuated by legal, electoral, and personal setbacks, raises critical questions about the nature of political longevity and the volatile dynamics of American conservatism. While Sammon’s analysis might be seen by some as a harsh postmortem, it serves as a poignant reminder of the transient nature of political influence and the unforgiving landscape of public life.

In dissecting Palin's decline, we are prompted to ponder the mechanisms of political redemption and the ever-changing contours of party loyalty. Her story is not just one of personal downfall but a mirror reflecting the broader challenges and shifts within the Republican Party. The narrative of Sarah Palin, as outlined by Sammon, is a cautionary tale of ambition, adversity, and the relentless scrutiny of the public eye.
