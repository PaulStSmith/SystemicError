'Unlawfully appointed': Alina Habba 'disqualified' as US attorney by Republican judge

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20254 min read

Alina Habba (Photo by Evelyn Hockstein for Reuters)

U.S. District Judge Matthew Brann ruled that Alina Habba, President Donald Trump's acting U.S. attorney in New Jersey, could not continue in her role.

In a 77-page ruling on Thursday, Brann noted that the Trump administration had used "a novel series of legal and personnel moves" to keep Habba in her position after a panel of judges effectively ousted her. Two defendants, Julien Giraud Jr. and Cesar Pina, argued that Habba could not oversee their prosecutions.

"Faced with the question of whether Ms. Habba is lawfully performing the functions and duties of the office of the United States Attorney for the District of New Jersey, I conclude that she is not," Brann wrote. "And because she is not currently qualified to exercise the functions and duties of the office in an acting capacity, she must be disqualified from participating in any ongoing cases."

Brann, a Republican though he was appointed by President Barack Obama, voided Pina's indictment because he said Habba took the action after being "unlawfully appointed" by Trump.

"I disqualify Ms. Habba from engaging in the prosecutions of the Girauds and Mr. Pina, and from supervising the same," the judge wrote. "Any Assistant United States Attorney who prosecutes the Girauds or Mr. Pina under the supervision or authority of Ms. Habba in violation of my Order is similarly subject to disqualification."

The Trump administration was expected to appeal the ruling.

The Unraveling of Legal Decorum: Alina Habba's Disqualification

In a striking blow to the remnants of the Trump administration's legal maneuverings, U.S. District Judge Matthew Brann delivered a scathing 77-page verdict that reads less like a legal ruling and more like a narrative on how not to play legal chess. The central figure of this judicial rebuke? This is a polite way of stating that the Trump administration engaged in legal gymnastics to keep Habba in power, a move that has now spectacularly backfired.

The Legal Labyrinth

The case against Habba's legitimacy wasn't pulled from thin air but argued on solid legal grounds by defendants Julien Giraud Jr. and Cesar Pina. Their argument? Habba was not legally qualified to oversee their prosecutions. Judge Brann, in his ruling, didn't just agree; he underscored the point with the legal equivalent of a neon sign. "Faced with the question of whether Ms. Habba is lawfully performing the functions and duties of the office of the United States Attorney for the District of New Jersey, I conclude that she is not," Brann stated. This wasn't just a rebuke; it was a dismantling of the Trump administration's attempt to circumvent legal norms and procedures.

The Irony of Appointment

It's worth noting the delicious irony that Judge Brann, a Republican appointed by President Barack Obama, is the one to issue this ruling. This underlines a critical point often glossed over in the partisan squabbles of our times: legal integrity and the rule of law often transcend political affiliations. Brann's decision to void Pina's indictment on the grounds that Habba was "unlawfully appointed" by Trump isn't just a procedural footnote. It's a monumental statement on the importance of lawful appointments and the dangers of administrative overreach.

The Fallout

Brann's ruling isn't just a personal blow to Habba; it's a systemic shock to any remaining notions that the Trump administration's legal stratagems were all above board. By disqualifying Habba from engaging in or supervising the prosecutions of the Girauds and Mr. Pina, Brann has effectively put a stop to any immediate legal actions taken under her purview. Moreover, the warning issued to any Assistant United States Attorney who dares to prosecute under Habba's supervision echoes like a shot across the bow to any who would follow in her footsteps.

The Expected Appeal

The Trump administration's expected appeal of the ruling is as predictable as it is futile. This expected appeal is not just a legal maneuver; it's a political statement, clinging to the vestiges of power through the courts.

In the end, the law—not the whims of any administration—has the final say.
