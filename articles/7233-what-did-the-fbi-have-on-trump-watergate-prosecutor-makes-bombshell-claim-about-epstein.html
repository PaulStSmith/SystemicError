Donald Trump has been trying to stall the release of the Epstein files because of the bombshell revelation the documents are hiding, according to Watergate lawyer Nick Ackerman.

Ackerman, who has often commented on Trump’s legal matters, on Sunday flagged “a key email from Jeffrey Epstein’s estate,” in addition to “statements by Trump ally House Speaker Mike Johnson,” which together purportedly show “it is highly likely that Trump was a confidential FBI informant in the first sex trafficking investigation into Epstein and his partner in crime Ghislaine Maxwell.”

“The press has totally overlooked the significance of this email,” Ackerman said.

The former prosecutor went on to highlight the email from Epstein in which he suggests he’s 75% sure Trump is an informant.

“i want you to realize that that dog that hasn’t barked is trump,” according to Epstein.

“Let’s connect the dots to what else we now know,” Ackerman wrote. “Epstein authored this email after the conclusion of the investigations by the State of Florida and the FBI into his conduct with underage girls, and after Epstein had served his overly lenient sentence. The second federal investigation had not yet begun, but victims began filing civil lawsuits against him, and Epstein was a registered sex offender.”

After analyzing the emails in their chronological context, he noted that the next clue came from Mike Johnson.

“What nails it is the September 5, 2025, statement by House Speaker Mike Johnson to reporters that what Epstein did was an ‘unspeakable evil’ and that Donald Trump ‘was an FBI informant to try to take this stuff down,’ referring to Epstein’s criminal activities,” Ackerman wrote. “Johnson said that he and Trump had ‘spoken about this many times’ ‘as recently as twenty-four hours ago.’”

Ackerman added, “Within days, Johnson, who is in regular contact with Trump, backtracked from his statement without a coherent or valid explanation. His excuse was that he might not have used the ‘right word.’ Really!! Unsurprisingly, the White House denied that Trump was an FBI informant in the Epstein case, saying Johnson’s original description was not correct.”

Then Ackerman tied it all together, including potential motives.

“Clearly, Trump does not want it publicly known that he was an FBI informant. From my experience as a prosecutor, the principal way a person becomes a confidential informant is when the FBI uses a person’s involvement in criminal activity to turn the individual into an informant to avoid prosecution,” according to Ackerman. “In the case of Trump, that does not necessarily mean the criminal leverage was Trump’s involvement with Epstein’s sex trafficking. It could have been something else. If so, what did the FBI have on Trump? The big question — will future productions of DOJ’s Epstein files reveal Trump’s involvement as an FBI informant against Epstein? It certainly should.”

Our Analysis:

Unraveling the Epstein-Trump Saga: A Critical Analysis

Nick Ackerman’s latest bombshell assertions surrounding Donald Trump’s efforts to suppress the release of the so-called Epstein files are stirring the pot with the intensity of a hurricane approaching a fragile coastline. Ackerman, a veteran of the Watergate scandal and no stranger to the intricacies of legal warfare, suggests that within these cloaked documents lies evidence potent enough to redefine Trump’s historical narrative. But are we witnessing a genuine revelation or a speculative tempest? Let’s dissect the claims with our characteristic skepticism and demand for hard evidence.

The Alleged Email: A Smoking Gun or Smoke and Mirrors?

Ackerman centers his argument around an email purportedly from Jeffrey Epstein, hinting at Trump’s role as a confidential FBI informant. The claim is as explosive as it is bewildering, suggesting that Trump, a figure entwined with Epstein in public speculation for years, might have been playing a double game. Yet, the evidence presented hinges on an email in which Epstein himself was “75% sure” about Trump’s informant status. The language here is inherently speculative - since when did criminal confidence become a metric of truth?

Mike Johnson’s Retraction: A Slip or Strategic Retreat?

The narrative takes a curious turn with the involvement of House Speaker Mike Johnson, whose alleged admission and subsequent retraction adds a layer of political intrigue. Johnson’s statement, as quoted by Ackerman, that Trump was an informant against Epstein’s “unspeakable evil” is a narrative bombshell. However, the retraction muddies the waters significantly. Johnson’s backtrack, dismissed by Ackerman as lacking a “coherent or valid explanation,” raises questions about the veracity of his initial claim. Was it a slip of the tongue, a miscommunication, or a glimpse into a hidden truth? The lack of clarity here does little to bolster Ackerman’s case.

The White House Denial: Standard Procedure or Something More?

Unsurprisingly, the White House’s denial of Trump’s alleged role as an informant follows the expected script of political damage control. Yet, in the absence of concrete evidence, this denial stands as much a part of the narrative as Johnson’s retraction. It’s a predictable move, yet without more substantial evidence, it neither confirms nor denies the truth of the matter.

The Motive: A Tangled Web of Speculation

Ackerman’s closing musings on Trump’s motives and potential blackmail by the FBI are intriguing yet remain speculative. The suggestion that Trump’s informant status could have been leveraged from unrelated criminal activities opens a Pandora’s box of possibilities. However, speculation without solid evidence remains just that - speculation. The true nature of Trump’s involvement, if any, with Epstein’s activities, and how the FBI might have played into this, is a story yet to be fully uncovered.

Conclusion: A Demand for Transparency and Truth

Nick Ackerman’s analysis, while grounded in his prosecutorial experience, leaves us in a labyrinth of conjecture and half-lit truths. The implications of Trump being an FBI informant in the Epstein saga are monumental, yet the evidence provided is frustratingly nebulous. As we navigate these murky waters, the need for rigorous investigation and undeniable proof has never been more critical. The public deserves more than speculative leaps; it demands, and rightfully so, a clear and unambiguous unveiling of the facts. Until such evidence is presented, the narrative remains a captivating, yet incomplete, puzzle.

In this high-stakes game of shadows and secrets, one thing is clear: the truth, however elusive, must be pursued with relentless determination and unwavering scrutiny. The saga of Epstein, Trump, and the tangled webs they wove continues to unravel, demanding our vigilant attention and critical analysis.
