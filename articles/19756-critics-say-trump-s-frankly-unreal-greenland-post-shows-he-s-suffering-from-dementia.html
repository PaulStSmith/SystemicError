Critics say Trump's 'frankly unreal' Greenland post shows he's 'suffering from dementia'

President Donald Trump made the bizarre announcement Saturday that he would be sending a hospital ship to Greenland on an apparent humanitarian mission, and with no explanation for what prompted the expedition – but critics have zeroed in on what they say is “almost certainly” the cause for the president’s confusing statement.

There is no documented health crisis in Greenland, a territory of Denmark, with all of its citizens entitled to universal health care that’s free at the point of service, as it is in most moderately wealthy nations other than the United States. Nevertheless, Trump suggested that he would be deploying the USNS Mercy to the arctic island, and that the hospital ship was already “on the way.”

As noted by critics, the peculiar announcement came within hours of an incident in which an American service member aboard a U.S. submarine off the coast of Greenland was evacuated by Danish forces and taken to a hospital after falling ill.

“I’ve now seen a number of comments on this mysterious Truth Social post from Donald Trump,” wrote Lars Christensen, a Danish economist, to their more than 32,000 followers on X.

“It demonstrates with absolute clarity that the man is suffering from dementia and cannot grasp the most basic connections. Why do I say this? Because what his post is almost certainly prompted by is the fact that the Danish navy evacuated a sick crew member from an American submarine near Greenland within the last few days.”

Trump’s announcement comes amid his ongoing and increasingly threatening campaign to acquire Greenland for the United States, which has included the deployment of a submarine off the coast of the arctic island.

Whether the USNS Mercy can even depart to Greenland is another open question, with maritime expert Mike Schuler noting in a report Saturday that the hospital ship is currently sitting in a dry dock in Alabama undergoing maintenance, maintenance that appears scheduled to continue through July.

“This Trump post is frankly unreal in its cynicism and gaslighting. He posted this because a US nuclear submarine – whose very presence near Greenland is already deeply provocative given the context – was just rescued by Danish military forces after one of its sailors fell ill,” wrote Arnaud Bertrand, an entrepreneur and frequent political commentator, in a social media post on X to their more than 380,000 followers.

“Denmark graciously rescued the sailor and evacuated him to treat him in a hospital in Greenland. The cherry on the cake is that there’s no ‘great hospital boat’ coming to Greenland because both U.S. hospital ships – the USNS Mercy and the USNS Comfort – are currently incapacitated, undergoing maintenance. So ‘It’s on the way!!!’ is simply a bold face lie.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump’s Greenland Hospital Ship Fiasco: A Masterclass in Misdirection

In what can only be described as a bewildering move, President Donald Trump announced his intention to dispatch the USNS Mercy, a hospital ship, to Greenland—an announcement as perplexing as it is baseless. At face value, this gesture might seem like an act of humanitarian goodwill, if not for the glaring absence of any health crisis in Greenland, a Danish territory known for its robust universal healthcare system. This move, devoid of context or necessity, reeks of political theatrics, underscoring Trump’s penchant for grandstanding over governance.

The Illusion of Humanitarian Aid

Greenland has no health crisis to speak of. This is a fact, plain and simple. The residents of this Arctic territory enjoy comprehensive healthcare, a privilege that seems to elude vast swathes of the American populace. Trump’s proposal to send a hospital ship to an island that doesn’t need one is not just bizarre; it’s a slap in the face to the under and uninsured Americans back home.

A Convenient Distraction

The timing of Trump’s announcement is suspiciously convenient, coming hot on the heels of an incident where a U.S. servicemember was evacuated by Danish forces from a submarine near Greenland due to illness. Critics, including Danish economist Lars Christensen and political commentator Arnaud Bertrand, have been quick to point out the absurdity and opportunism of Trump’s statement. It seems almost farcical that the president would deploy a hospital ship in response to a single servicemember’s illness—especially when the ship in question, the USNS Mercy, is currently incapacitated in dry dock.

The Real Agenda

Trump’s fixation on Greenland is hardly a secret, with his administration making previous overtures to acquire the territory. The deployment of a U.S. submarine near Greenland’s coast, viewed in isolation, is already a provocative act. In this context, Trump’s hospital ship announcement can be seen as nothing short of cynical misdirection—a feeble attempt to cloak expansionist ambitions under the guise of humanitarian aid.

The Verdict: A Bold Face Lie

Let’s call it what it is: a bold face lie. Both of the U.S. Navy’s hospital ships, the USNS Mercy and the USNS Comfort, are out of commission, undergoing maintenance. This fact alone renders Trump’s announcement not just misleading, but a fabrication designed to deceive. In a world already beset by misinformation, such deceit from the highest office is not just irresponsible; it’s dangerous.

In Conclusion

Trump’s announcement to send a hospital ship to Greenland is a masterclass in misdirection, a ploy aimed at diverting attention from his geopolitical maneuvers and bolstering his image. It’s a stark reminder of the lengths to which he will go to fabricate success and benevolence where there is none. As observers and critics dissect this latest episode, it serves as a cautionary tale about the depths of deceit in the political arena, urging us to remain vigilant, critical, and informed.
