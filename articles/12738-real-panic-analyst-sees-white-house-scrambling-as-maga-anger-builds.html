The president directed his followers to stop discussing the notorious sex trafficker in a Truth Social post over the weekend, but MSNBC's Jonathan Lemire said on the show "Morning Joe" that his demands were met with anger, confusion and derision — and the MAGA base has turned on some key Trump officials.

"For years, less Trump himself, but certainly people around Trump, have been talking about this [Epstein client] list and that it was going to implicate powerful people, powerful institutions, largely on the left – the Clintons, the Obamas, others, and they would all go down for it, and now we have seen since president Trump took office, again, a backing away from that," Lemire said.

"There was a statement a month back or so from the FBI, you mentioned it, and now from the Department of Justice and Pam Bondi, the attorney general this past week, saying, well, there's there's no list, even though we have heard from her earlier this term there was a list."

"We've heard from Kash Patel, we've heard from Dan Bongino there was a list – they had access to it, they'd release it," Lemire added. "There was that much ballyhooed stunt earlier this year where they gave a bunch of MAGA influencers some Epstein documents, and that turned out to be sort of a bust, all just publicly available material, and that that kicked up some backlash on the right, and now a real fury here in the last few days, and this is what's interesting about this. Do we know what the actual Epstein list is, or even if there is a list?

"No, we do not, but what we do know is this is a rare moment, a very rare moment where there is a real fracture in the president's base and some of his most ardent supporters, ... but there are other social media influencers, like Benny Johnson and the like, who took to Twitter over the weekend, really trashing President Trump, which we never see before, accusing him of hiding things, accusing him of not being honest about this list and Trump's efforts to try to quell this storm."

Trump's lengthy post Saturday on Truth Social in which he tried to control the backlash seems to have backfired, Lemire said.

"His Truth Social post, nearly a thousand words, over the weekend did exactly the opposite," he said. "I mean, Truth Social, let's remember this is a Donald Trump-created and owned platform. This is the first time I've ever seen him be ratioed, meaning he got more comments, in this case all overwhelmingly negative comments, than he got likes and stamps of approval for that post. There's real, real anger here.

" ... He was talking out of both sides of his mouth, and I'm told, back to you guys here, there's some real panic in the White House right now," Lemire added. "A lot of people have gone quiet, a lot of people have gone dark, but they recognize those that are talking, recognize right now this is a problem. It's the first time they've had a problem like this, and they don't think it's going to go away anytime soon."

The Epstein Scandal: A Tumultuous Chapter in Trump's Second Presidency

The Jeffrey Epstein scandal has erupted into a formidable storm, one that Donald Trump's administration appears ill-equipped to weather. In an era defined by controversy and polarized politics, the uproar surrounding Epstein has cut through the noise with a particularly sharp edge. Here, we dissect the unfolding drama, the White House's floundering response, and the implications of a divided MAGA base.

The Epstein List: A Promised Revelation That Never Came

For years, the specter of the so-called Epstein list has loomed large over American politics. Trump's allies have long insinuated that this list would expose a network of powerful figures, particularly targeting the left, including the Clintons and the Obamas. The narrative promised a seismic shift, yet, as recent developments reveal, it has crumbled under the weight of its own promises.

The FBI and the Department of Justice, along with Trump's own Attorney General Pam Bondi, have now stated that there is, in fact, no list. This stark reversal is not just an admission of the absence of evidence; it's a glaring indication of the administration's willingness to peddle baseless claims for political gain. The mention of notable figures like Kash Patel and Dan Bongino in the propagation of these claims only adds layers to the deceit, showcasing a reckless disregard for truth in the pursuit of political vendettas.

The Backlash: A MAGA Base Betrayed

Trump's attempt to quash discussion of Epstein via a Truth Social post backfired spectacularly. The platform, a bastion for Trump's unfiltered proclamations, became the ground zero for discontent among his most ardent supporters. The backlash was not just a rejection of Trump's plea for silence but a condemnation of his perceived duplicity. High-profile social media influencers, previously staunch Trump defenders, publicly accused the president of obfuscation and dishonesty.

This fracture within the MAGA base is unprecedented. It signals a rare moment of vulnerability for Trump, whose teflon persona has weathered countless scandals. The reaction from his base — ranging from anger to outright denunciation — underscores a profound sense of betrayal. It reveals cracks in the facade of unwavering loyalty that Trump has long enjoyed.

The White House Panic: An Administration Adrift

The turmoil has reportedly sent shockwaves through the White House, plunging an administration already accustomed to controversy into a state of panic. The silence from Trump's officials speaks volumes, betraying a lack of strategy for navigating the fallout. This disarray is not just symptomatic of a singular scandal but indicative of a broader dysfunction within Trump's presidency.

Conclusion: The Fallout of False Promises

The Epstein scandal, and Trump's administration's handling of it, underscores a critical flaw in the political machinations of the current White House. The reliance on conspiracy theories and false promises as political tools has backfired, exposing the administration to a crisis of its own making. As the MAGA base grapples with disillusionment, the implications for Trump's political future are profound. The erosion of trust among his most loyal supporters could very well signal a turning point, one that challenges the foundation of his political strength.

In the final analysis, the Epstein debacle is not just a scandal; it's a stark reminder of the dangers inherent in manipulating truths and manufacturing enemies. The fallout is a self-inflicted wound, one that reveals the fragility of a presidency built on the precarious ground of deception and divisiveness.
