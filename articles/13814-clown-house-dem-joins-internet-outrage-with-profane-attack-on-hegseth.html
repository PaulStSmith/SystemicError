'Clown': House Dem joins internet outrage with profane attack on Hegseth

Protesters have swarmed Los Angeles in response to a series of federal immigration enforcement actions, specifically large-scale raids from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement that resulted in the arrest of more than 100 immigrants last week.

The protests intensified following the arrest of David Huerta, president of the Service Employees International Union, or SEIU, of California, during a Friday demonstration. Many protesters have opposed the deployment of the National Guard, which Trump ordered over the weekend despite opposition from Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom.

On Monday evening, Hegseth confirmed reports that hundreds of Marines had been mobilized.

"Due to increased threats to federal law enforcement officers and federal buildings, approximately 700 active-duty U.S. Marines from Camp Pendleton are being deployed to Los Angeles to restore order," Hegseth wrote on the platform X. "We have an obligation to defend federal law enforcement officers - even if Gavin Newsom will not."

Newsom called out Trump for sending troops and giving them no fuel, food, water or even a place to sleep.

"Here they are — being forced to sleep on the floor, piled on top of one another. If anyone is treating our troops disrespectfully, it is you @realDonaldTrump," wrote Newsom.

Others soon piled on and attacked Hegseth, including Rep. Pat Ryan (D-NY).

"This is a bulls---, bad-faith argument from Hegseth. A total amateur clown who's in way over his head. Every soldier knows it is wrong – AND FUNDAMENTALLY UNAMERICAN – to deploy active duty US troops against US citizens," Ryan railed on X.

Sen. Andy Kim (D-NJ) echoed Ryan's sentiments on X.

"Deploying 700 Marines on U.S. soil against the will of local leaders and law enforcement is a dangerous abuse of power. Our military is a sacred trust meant to protect the American people, not be used by President Trump and Secretary Hegseth to strike fear into Americans. A strong and trusted United States military must remain apolitical. Trump’s escalation today not only weakens Americans’ trust in their own freedoms of speech and protest but trust in what our military stands for," he wrote.

S.V. Dáte, White House correspondent at HuffPost, noted that when Trump "wanted his defense secretary to order soldiers to shoot Americans back in 2020, Mark Esper told him no."

"This time, Trump has a solid 'yes' man in that job in Pete Hegseth," wrote Dáte.

Rep. Mikie Sherrill (D-NJ), a member of the House Armed Services Committee, meanwhile, told MeidasTouch Network that to "have someone like Hegseth say that he’s going to put the Marines on the streets of America, that’s really dangerous, it’s dangerous for our civilization population, it’s not good for the Marines."

She added: "I think Governor Newsom going to court is exactly he should be doing and to actually try to contain the situation, try to keep people safe as Trump is as usual doing nothing but what he thinks works for himself politically and actually putting a lot of people in danger."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Militarization of American Streets

In a move that has set the internet ablaze with controversy, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth confirmed the deployment of approximately 700 active-duty Marines to Los Angeles. This decision comes in the wake of escalating protests against a wave of federal immigration enforcement actions, notably large-scale raids by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), leading to over 100 arrests. The spark that seemed to ignite a powder keg was the arrest of David Huerta, president of the Service Employees International Union (SEIU) of California, during a protest.

A Disproportionate Response?

The heart of the outrage lies not just in the deployment itself but in the implicit message it sends. Deploying the military on American soil against American citizens is a drastic measure that should alarm anyone with a vested interest in civil liberties and the sanctity of democratic protest. The optics are undeniably grim: active-duty Marines positioned against civilians voicing their opposition to government policies.

Political Chess at the Expense of Civil Liberties

The deployment, ordered amidst opposition from Democratic Governor Gavin Newsom and despite widespread public dissent, smacks of political theater at its most cynical. Hegseth's justification, citing "increased threats to federal law enforcement officers and federal buildings," fails to address the core issue at hand—the right of the people to protest governmental actions perceived as unjust. Instead, it frames the narrative as a defense of law enforcement at the cost of civilian dissent.

Governor Newsom's response highlights a disregard for the well-being and dignity of the troops involved, underscoring the administration's willingness to sacrifice military welfare for political gain. The condition in which these troops are expected to operate, as described by Newsom, is not only inadequate but dehumanizing.

Voices of Opposition

Critics, including Rep. Pat Ryan and Sen. Andy Kim, have lambasted the move as fundamentally un-American and a dangerous abuse of power. Their condemnation focuses on the sacred trust between the American military and the populace it is sworn to protect—a trust severely undermined by such deployments.

S.V. Dáte's comparison to a previous incident where former Defense Secretary Mark Esper refused a similar request from Trump illuminates a worrying trend towards compliance with authoritarian impulses within the highest ranks of military decision-making. Pete Hegseth's acquiescence to Trump's demands marks a stark departure from the principle that the military should remain apolitical.

Rep. Mikie Sherrill's comments underscore the broader implications of Hegseth's actions—putting civilians and service members at risk, undermining the foundation of civil-military relations, and prioritizing political maneuvering over public safety and constitutional rights.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precipice

The deployment of Marines in Los Angeles is not just a response to protests; it is a statement of intent from the Trump administration. It indicates a willingness to use military force to quell dissent and intimidate the populace, setting a dangerous precedent for the use of armed forces in domestic affairs.

At its core, this situation raises fundamental questions about the balance between security and liberty, the role of the military in civil society, and the lengths to which a government will go to maintain power. The use of military personnel to confront civic unrest over immigration policies is a chilling step towards the militarization of American streets, one that should concern anyone who values democratic principles and the rights enshrined in the Constitution.

In this pivotal moment, the need for vigilance and advocacy for civil liberties and democratic norms has never been greater. The deployment of Marines against American citizens is a symptom of a larger systemic ailment—a willingness to compromise the very ideals upon which the country was founded in the name of political expediency and power consolidation.
