Trump bolted from pope's funeral so he could get back to his New Jersey golf club: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Photo by Vincent Carchietta for Reuters)

After attending the Rome funeral for Pope Francis on Saturday, Donald Trump and his wife Melania posed for a few pictures with other foreign dignitaries then headed to Air Force One immediately because the president wanted to get back to New Jersey.

According to a report from the New York Times, the president and the first lady were only in Rome for 14 hours, with the Times' David Sanger and Motoko Rich writing it was, "... a startlingly fast turnaround for the first overseas trip of a new president, and left no time for discussion of his tariffs on the European Union, his turn toward normalizing relations with Russia or his insistence that Europeans must take far larger responsibility for their own defense."

The report notes that the swift departure was the result of Trump telling his aides, "he wanted to make it back to his [Bedminster] golf resort in New Jersey before the end of the day."

The report goes on to note Trump's brief but highly publicized meeting with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, adding that an aide representing the president of Ukraine attempted to delay Trump's "speedy departure" for more talks but it did not happen due to "very tight schedules of the presidents.”

According to the Independent, upon their return to the U.S., Trump and his wife "parted ways" after stepping off Air Force One despite the fact that it was the first lady's 55th birthday.

Our Analysis:

A Whirlwind of Priorities: Trump's Rome Dash

In an episode that could be mistaken for a satirical sketch rather than a slice of reality, Donald Trump's brief sojourn in Rome for Pope Francis' funeral encapsulates the former president's notorious approach to international diplomacy and personal priorities. The New York Times report shines a light on what can only be described as a farcical escapade under the guise of a presidential duty. Trump, with Melania in tow, spent a mere 14 hours in Rome, an astonishingly brief period that underscores an alarming disinterest in leveraging international events for substantive diplomatic engagement.

A Startlingly Swift Turnaround

Described by David Sanger and Motoko Rich as a "startlingly fast turnaround for the first overseas trip of a new president," Trump's fleeting visit was devoid of any discussions on critical issues such as tariffs on the European Union, the warming relations with Russia, or the pressing need for Europe to assume a greater role in its defense. This oversight is not just a missed opportunity but a blaring siren highlighting Trump's indifference to matters of global significance. It's as if the world stage is merely a backdrop for photo ops rather than a platform for meaningful dialogue and policy advancement.

The Bedminster Golf Resort Beckons

The report's revelation that Trump's hasty departure was fueled by a desire to return to his Bedminster golf resort is both laughable and lamentable. It paints a picture of a leader for whom personal leisure trumps (pun intended) the responsibilities of office. This prioritization is a slap in the face to the intricate dance of diplomacy and the seriousness required when representing a nation on the global stage. The fact that this retreat to comfort came at the expense of potentially significant diplomatic engagements is a testament to Trump's myopic leadership style.

A Missed Opportunity with Ukraine

The brief but noteworthy meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy adds a layer of gravity to Trump's hurried exit. An aide's attempt to facilitate further discussions, thwarted by the "very tight schedules of the presidents," is a microcosm of the broader issue at hand: the squandering of diplomatic opportunities. In a world where U.S.-Ukraine relations are of paramount importance, Trump's inability to carve out time for deeper engagement is not just a missed opportunity but a dereliction of duty.

A Parting of Ways

Adding a personal twist to the narrative, Trump and Melania's swift separation upon returning to the U.S.—on the first lady's 55th birthday, no less—serves as a fitting metaphor for the entire trip: a fleeting, perfunctory engagement followed by a rapid return to personal pursuits. This personal anecdote, while seemingly trivial, underscores the transactional nature of Trump's approach to both his public duties and private relationships.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Missed Opportunities

Trump's Rome rendezvous is emblematic of a presidency plagued by missed opportunities and a glaring disregard for the nuances of international diplomacy. As the leader of one of the world's most powerful nations, the expectation is for a president to engage deeply and thoughtfully with global counterparts. Instead, what we witnessed was a leader more concerned with the comfort of his golf resort than the complexities of the world stage—a troubling indictment of Trump's tenure.

In sum, this episode is not just a quirky footnote in the annals of presidential history but a stark reminder of the consequences of electing leaders who place personal convenience over national and global welfare.
