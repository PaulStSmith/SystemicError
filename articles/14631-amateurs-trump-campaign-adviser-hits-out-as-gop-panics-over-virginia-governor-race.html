'Amateurs': Trump campaign adviser hits out as GOP panics over Virginia governor race

Republican Lieutenant Governor Winsome Earle-Sears is not kicking off her campaign in the best way.

“A lot of us are looking at it and saying, ‘I’d do it differently,’” said a senior GOP staffer that Politico granted anonymity. “She’s a good person, she’d make a good governor, and she’s just not as strong of a candidate as we’d like to have.”

The outlet paraphrased nearly a dozen Republican strategists and officials in the state who are worried that the candidate's “sluggish fundraising,” repeated comments where she compares “Diversity, Equity and Inclusion programs to slavery,” and “frosty relationship with MAGA rising star John Reid,” could cost the GOP the governorship.

Reid is running for lieutenant governor this year. The state's current Governor Glenn Youngkin (R) is barred from serving consecutive terms, according to Virginia law.

“With the demographics of Richmond, in an off year with the Republican White House, it’s going to be tough,” said longtime Virginia Republican strategist Jimmy Keady. “To be a Republican to win in Virginia, you have to run a very good campaign. You’ve got to have [tailwinds] and the Democratic candidate’s got to make a mistake.”

The Democratic candidate this year is Abigail Spanberger who has a record of winning competitive races.

According to Politico, “Chris LaCivita, a former senior adviser to President Donald Trump’s 2024 campaign who has worked for a number of high-profile Virginia Republicans, called Earle-Sears’ campaign staff ‘amateurs.’”

It’s not just her slow campaign that has the GOP concerned. It's also Earle-Sears' written opposition to bills and constitutional amendments that she is constitutionally required to sign.”

“There are those kinds of glitches, [when] you see them, you manage them, you get through them, and you go to the next thing,” Kay Coles James told Politico. Coles James is the former head of the conservative Heritage Foundation and has deep ties to Virginia GOP circles. “I am not in a panic mode at this point.”

Our Analysis:

Virginia Republicans: A Preemptive Eulogy for a Governor's Race?

The recent Politico report paints a rather bleak picture for the Virginia Republicans as they brace themselves for what appears to be an impending loss in the Virginia Governor's race. Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the unforgiving clarity of a critic.

Winsome Earle-Sears: A Rocky Start

Republican Lieutenant Governor Winsome Earle-Sears is evidently not off to the races in what one might call a formidable manner. When your own party insiders are quoted saying, “A lot of us are looking at it and saying, ‘I’d do it differently,’” you know there's a storm brewing, and not the kind you can easily weather with a sturdy umbrella and a can-do attitude.

The critique doesn't stop at strategy disagreements. Oh no, it plunges deeper into the abyss of "sluggish fundraising" and what could only be described as controversial comments that liken Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion programs to slavery. If that's not enough to raise eyebrows, her “frosty relationship with MAGA rising star John Reid,” adds another layer of intra-party tension that you'd expect in a daytime drama, not a gubernatorial race.

The Elephant in the Room: Strategy and Demographics

Longtime Virginia Republican strategist Jimmy Keady hits the nail on the head: to win as a Republican in Virginia, you need more than just a campaign; you need a near-perfect alignment of stars, winds, and perhaps a sprinkle of luck. This isn't just about running a good campaign; it's about navigating the shifting demographics of places like Richmond and understanding the nuanced dance of appealing to both the base and the undecided middle.

The Opposition: Abigail Spanberger

On the flip side, we have Abigail Spanberger, described as having "a record of winning competitive races." This isn't her first rodeo, and if the GOP was hoping for a Democratic candidate to fumble, they might be waiting for a while.

Campaign Woes and Internal Critiques

Chris LaCivita's commentary labeling Earle-Sears' campaign staff as “amateurs” is the kind of internal critique that can either spur a dramatic overhaul or sink a ship faster than you can say "Titanic." It's not just about the slow start; it's about the fundamental aspects of governance and legal obligations, like Earle-Sears' opposition to certain bills and constitutional amendments she's constitutionally required to sign.

Kay Coles James, with her calm demeanor, says she's “not in a panic mode at this point.” But one can't help but wonder if that's the sound of someone blissfully unaware of the iceberg ahead or a seasoned captain confident in navigating treacherous waters.

Conclusion: A Foregone Conclusion or a Wake-Up Call?

As we stand back and look at the unfolding drama, it's clear that the Virginia Republicans are at a crossroads. This Politico report doesn't just signal potential defeat; it screams a desperate need for introspection, strategy overhaul, and perhaps a bit of humility.

Will this be the wake-up call the GOP needs, or will it serve as a prelude to a post-mortem analysis of what could have been? Only time will tell, but one thing is certain: in the unforgiving arena of politics, it's adapt or perish. And right now, Virginia Republicans seem to be on the brink.
