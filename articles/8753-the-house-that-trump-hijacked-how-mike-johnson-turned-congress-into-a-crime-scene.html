The House That Trump Hijacked: How Mike Johnson Turned Congress Into a Crime Scene

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 14, 20255 min read

In the video, the situation is laid out with all the subtlety of a car alarm going off at 3 a.m.—but behind the sensationalism lies a truth just as loud: the United States House of Representatives is no longer functioning as a legislative body. It’s a hostage, gagged and blindfolded, in the basement of the Trumpist apparatus that now calls itself a political party.

The mechanics of the latest debacle are by now sickeningly familiar. First, you engineer a crisis. Then, you let it fester until even your own voters start asking why the government they supposedly own looks like a condemned building. Finally, you yank the cord, ‘heroically’ reopening the mess you created and declaring victory while everyone’s still coughing from the smoke.

Except this time something else happened. Something Trump and Speaker Mike Johnson absolutely did not want: a newly sworn-in Democratic representative joined a discharge petition that would force Congress to vote on releasing the Epstein files—files that, according to the “video,” include explicit mentions of Donald Trump. And that’s when the circus spilled out of the tent.

A Government Run at the Whim of One Man

The House was closed for forty-three days. Forty-three. Longer than many countries allow for an election season. Longer than some wars. But that wasn’t an accident—it was a strategy. The Republican-led House skipped town after passing a resolution designed to corner the Senate, then lounged on a fifty-three-day vacation while the government crumbled.

The moment they returned, they opened shop for eight hours—eight—and then slammed the doors again. Why? Because Adelita Grajalva, freshly sworn in, signed the discharge petition demanding the release of the Epstein documents. Those documents include emails in which Jeffrey Epstein refers to Trump as “the dog that hasn’t barked.” Translation: the ghost everyone pretended not to see is suddenly rattling its chains again.

Mike Johnson couldn’t risk even the possibility of a vote. So he shut Congress down—again. Not to save the country, not to negotiate, not to govern. To protect Trump.

This isn’t leadership. It’s servitude.

Trump Gives the Orders, Johnson Fetches the Stick

Let’s dispense with the fiction that Mike Johnson is Speaker of the House. The Speaker of the House is Donald Trump. Johnson is the uniformed valet holding the umbrella.

The “video” recounts scene after scene confirming what Democrats have been warning for years: Trump issues orders on social media, and Congress jumps. Trump demands the filibuster be nuked—Republicans salute. Trump decides when the House adjourns—Republicans pack their bags. Trump frowns, and a caucus of elected officials contorts itself like a yoga class in a collapsing building.

Representative Hank Johnson put it plainly: Congress is no longer an Article I institution. It’s a decorative appendage of Article II—the executive—being weaponized by a man who already attempted a coup.

That’s not hyperbole. That’s constitutional fact.

Mike Johnson, a self-described constitutional scholar, is willfully surrendering the very powers his office was created to uphold. He’s not just ignoring separation of powers—he’s burning the doctrine for warmth as Trump’s shadow grows.

The Epstein Files: The Ghost Trump Fears Most

The panic over the discharge petition wasn’t about governance. It wasn’t about the budget. And it certainly wasn’t about the American people, whose daily lives have been throttled by Republican negligence.

It was about fear.

The newly released emails—cited in the “video”—reference Trump by name in Epstein’s correspondence, specifically identifying him as the unnamed “dog that hasn’t barked.” Epstein points out that the media and public have scrutinized everyone except Trump, and Ghislaine Maxwell responds, “I’ve been thinking about that.”

If you’re wondering why a single House vote scares Trump more than a courtroom, this is why. Court cases are about the law. Discharge petitions are about exposure. Filthy laundry hung on a national clothesline.

And that’s why Johnson shut the doors.

What’s the Cost of a Captured Congress?

While Johnson bends the knee, the rest of the country takes the hit:

  • 53 days of vacation during a crisis that their own party manufactured.

  • 43 days of shutdown, the longest in U.S. history.

  • Only four legislative weeks left in the year.

  • Another shutdown deadline in January, already ticking.

  • Critical work abandoned, even as Republicans cheer themselves for their “aggressive calendar.”

And then there’s the petty cruelty: locking Capitol doors, blocking staff from entering, clogging hallways with needless delays—treating the legislative branch like a tourist attraction temporarily closed for fumigation.

The United States government—once a symbol of global stability—now operates like a cheap roadside motel run by a con man who keeps stealing the room keys.

The Raw Story Interviews Expose the Rot

Democrats interviewed in the “video” are exhausted, frustrated, and furious. Not just because Republicans are refusing to govern, but because they’re actively dismantling democracy while smiling for the cameras.

They describe Johnson as “the most disappointing member of Congress,” someone who lies in daily press conferences, who spouts extremist rhetoric, who manages the House with pettiness instead of principle, and who has handed operational control to Trump as casually as someone passing the TV remote.

Representative Danny Davis did not mince words: Republicans are “humping to please” Trump. Every decision is made to keep him happy, no matter the cost to the republic.

A Country on the Edge While the House Sleeps

The theme running through every Democratic comment is the same: this shutdown isn’t just incompetence. It’s sabotage.

By engineering chaos, Republicans strengthen the narrative that only Trump can fix what Trump’s own party destroyed. It’s a political arson job with a built-in alibi.

And yet, there’s a silver lining. The shutdown galvanized Democrats and their base. People are waking up. They see the scam clearly now. They’re connecting the dots between GOP dysfunction and their own economic pain—tariffs, billionaire tax cuts, and a government that lurches from crisis to crisis because one man demands loyalty over law.

Representative Hayes said it best: the goal isn’t to fix this in the midterms. It’s to keep fighting, every day, even if all you accomplish is trying.

Trump’s America: A Government in Service of One Man’s Fear

In the closing moments of the “video,” the truth lands with force: the United States now functions according to Trump’s whims. The legislative branch is an accessory. The judicial branch is being hollowed out by the Supreme Court’s own abdication. And the executive branch functions like a fortress protecting its king.

Congress didn’t close because of policy. It closed because transparency is dangerous to Trump. It closed because a representative dared to sign the wrong piece of paper. It closed because the Speaker refuses to speak for the people.

This is what authoritarian drift looks like—not in theory, but in practice, in real time, inside the walls of the Capitol.

America has spent 249 years trying to prove that self-governance can work. Trump and his loyalists are now testing how quickly they can unravel that experiment.

The House isn’t closed for business.

It’s closed for obedience.

And as long as Mike Johnson remains Speaker, it will stay that way.
