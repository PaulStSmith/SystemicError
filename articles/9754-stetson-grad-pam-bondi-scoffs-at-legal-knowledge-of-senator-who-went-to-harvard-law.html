Stetson grad Pam Bondi scoffs at legal knowledge of senator — who went to Harvard Law

Attorney General Pam Bondi tried to put down a U.S. senator Tuesday as she refused to answer multiple questions — begging to know of Adam Schiff (D-CA), “Do you have a law degree?”

Unfortunately for Bondi, her intended sleight failed as he does — from Harvard Law School.

The law school is ranked sixth in the country, according to U.S. News and World Report.

Stetson University, from which Bondi graduated, is ranked 99th.

Bondi testified in front of the Senate Judiciary Committee hearing Tuesday and was asked by Schiff and several other senators about a reported 2024 bribery scheme involving White House Border Czar Tom Homan receiving a bag of cash with $50,000. It launched an FBI probe — though Homan has not been charged — and senators were asking Bondi about what happened to the money.

“So I’m asking you the question, did he take the money?” Schiff asked.

Bondi said she had already answered the question. Schiff pushed back and said he didn’t think she had responded to it. Then, she said the investigation was prior to her confirmation as attorney general.

“All I know is that Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director [Kash] Patel said there was no case. And Caroline Leavitt is one of the most trustworthy human beings I know,” Bondi said.

Then she attacked Schiff, saying she would have fired him if he worked for her.

Schiff didn’t hold back, saying “You can stipulate to all your personal attacks on the Democratic members of the committee.”

Bondi was visibly upset and responded, “Personal attacks? You’ve been attacking my FBI director. You’ve been attacking my office.”

Schiff continued talking about the Homan investigation, asking Bondi to respond.

“You’re the attorney general. This will be your decision. Will you support...” he asked, then Bondi cut him off.

“Don’t tell me what is my decision,” she snapped.

“Let me let me do this because I think it’s it’s valuable that the American people get a sense of what you have refused to answer today,” Schiff responded. “So these are just some of the questions you refuse to answer, or have answered with personal attacks on members of this committee. You are asked whether you consulted with career ethics lawyers, as you promised you would do during your nomination hearing, when you approved the president receiving a $400 million gift from the Qataris. You refuse to answer that question. You are asked who or what role you may have played, or who played the role, in asking the Trump’s name be flagged in any of the Epstein documents gathered by the FBI? You refuse to answer that question. You were asked whether Homan kept the $50,000 bribe money? You refuse to answer that question. You were asked whether Homan paid taxes on the $50,000 bribe money? You refuse to answer that question. You were asked, did career prosecutors find insufficient evidence to charge James Comey? You refused to answer that question. You were asked, how are military strikes on these boats in the Caribbean legal? And you refuse to even answer that question?”

That’s when Bondi asked him if he had a law degree. Schiff ignored her question.

“You were asked by my colleague whether you believe government officials, like immigration officials, have to abide by court orders? You wouldn’t even answer that question. This is supposed to be an oversight hearing,” Schiff said.

Bondi attempted to interject again.

“Attack me later,” Schiff said. “And I know you’ve got plenty of canned attacks. We’ve heard them all day today.”

Our Analysis:

The Evasion Olympics: Bondi vs. Schiff

In a recent spectacle that could only be characterized as the Evasion Olympics, Attorney General Pam Bondi attempted to deflect a barrage of critical questions with a maneuver so lacking in subtlety, it would make a bull in a china shop seem graceful. The setting was a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing, and the sport was dodging accountability. Bondi’s strategy? Question the credentials of U.S. Senator Adam Schiff (D-CA), with a snide, “Do you have a law degree?” Unfortunately for Bondi, her attempt at a rhetorical javelin throw fell embarrassingly short, as Schiff does indeed possess a law degree—from none other than Harvard Law School, ranked sixth in the nation. This is in stark contrast to Bondi’s alma mater, Stetson University, which sits at a humbler 99th place. If academic pedigree were the measure of this bout, Schiff was already leagues ahead before the match even started.

The Bribery Scheme: A Bag of Cash and a Barrage of Questions

The core of the hearing revolved around a reported 2024 bribery scheme involving White House Border Czar Tom Homan and a rather cinematic bag of cash containing $50,000. Despite the initiation of an FBI probe, Homan has not been charged, but that didn’t stop the senators, Schiff included, from pressing Bondi for answers. Answers that, unsurprisingly, were not forthcoming.

Bondi’s responses—or lack thereof—read like a master class in obfuscation. “The investigation was prior to my confirmation as attorney general,” she offered, a line that would be at home in a political thriller, right before the protagonist uncovers a deep-seated conspiracy. When pressed further, Bondi chose to pivot, launching personal attacks rather than providing substantive answers. Schiff’s retort, inviting her to stipulate all her personal attacks, was a moment of clarity in an otherwise murky exchange.

A Symphony of Evasion

What followed was a symphony of evasion, with Bondi refusing to answer not just one, but a litany of critical questions. From whether she consulted with career ethics lawyers regarding a $400 million gift from the Qataris, to her involvement (or lack thereof) in flagging Trump’s name in Epstein documents, to the legality of military strikes in the Caribbean—Bondi’s strategy seemed to be to not play at all. Her audacity to question Schiff’s credentials amidst her own relentless dodging was not just ironic; it was emblematic of a broader disdain for accountability.

Schiff’s insistence on highlighting the unanswered questions served a dual purpose: not only did it shine a light on Bondi’s refusal to engage with the committee’s queries, but it also underscored the gravity of the issues at hand. These weren’t mere procedural questions; they were inquiries into potentially serious ethical and legal breaches.

“Attack me later,” Schiff Says

In a moment that could only be described as a blend of frustration and resignation, Schiff tells Bondi to “attack me later,” acknowledging the plethora of “canned attacks” that had been levied throughout the day. This exchange speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse in the U.S.: a landscape where evasion is preferred over engagement, where personal attacks are the currency of the realm, and where accountability is as elusive as ever.

In the end, Bondi’s attempt to belittle Schiff’s qualifications backfired spectacularly, serving only to highlight her own evasiveness. The real question isn’t whether Bondi or Schiff has a law degree; it’s whether those in positions of power can be held to account. On that front, the jury is still out, but if this hearing is any indication, we’re in for a long and arduous trial.
