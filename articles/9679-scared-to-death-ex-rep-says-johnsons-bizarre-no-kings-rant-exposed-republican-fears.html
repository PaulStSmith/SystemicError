‘Scared to death’: Ex-rep says Johnson’s bizarre 'No Kings' rant exposed Republican fears

U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) holds a press conference, along with other members of House Republican leadership on the ninth day of a partial shutdown of the federal government at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S. October 9, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A former Republican leader says GOP leaders are “scared to death” after House Speaker Mike Johnson‘s bizarre rant Friday, calling the “No Kings“ protest later this month a “Hate America” rally.

Johnson ranted on Fox News, saying he is “angry” and “had it with these people,” pointing to the upcoming protests on Oct. 18 across the United States. He also tried to claim the shutdown timing is tied to the public demonstration, The New Republic reports.

People called him out online, including former Tea Party Rep. Joe Walsh.

“My former political party is scared to death of these #NoKings rallies. That’s great. So let’s have a gazillion Americans out rallying all around the country on October 18th! Let’s scare the shit out of Trump & his party,” Walsh wrote on X.

Others joined with a similar response, including the No Kings coalition, which organizes the nationwide event.

“The organizers behind the No Kings Day of Action on October 18 -- the event Johnson called a ‘hate America rally’ -- have this to say in response. ‘After a few moments of laughter, the No Kings coalition issued the following statement,’” Huffington Post reporter Jennifer Bendery wrote on X.

“&SpeakerJohnson just called every American who plans to attend a #NoKings event a hater of the US. This spineless fake christian seems to like public humiliation, so he can continue by bowing down and kissing our protesting arses! We are patriots,” Sally Dillon wrote on X.

“Peaceful protests don’t require military responses #NoKings and the Soros comment is just veiled anti-semitism. Very sad what has happened to the Republican Party,” producer Gary Michael Walters wrote on X.

“ Mike Johnson says the ‘No Kings’ protest is a Hate America rally. Wrong. It’s a We Love America rally—because real Americans don’t bow to kings. Read a history book, Mike. Or admit you’re just an errand boy too scared to tell the truth,” California Democratic Socialist and Congressional candidate Chris Ahuja wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: GOP Leaders’ Fear and the ‘No Kings’ Rant

In a spectacle of political theater that veers into the absurd, House Speaker Mike Johnson’s tirade against the upcoming ‘No Kings’ protests reveals a GOP leadership teetering on the brink of hysteria. Johnson’s portrayal of a patriotic protest as a “Hate America rally” on national television is not just a bizarre rant; it’s a glaring testament to the Republican Party’s existential dread.

The Fearmongering Frenzy

Johnson’s outburst on Fox News, where he decried the ‘No Kings’ protests as anti-American, is a classic case of fearmongering. Attempting to demonize a democratic expression of dissent shows a party leadership “scared to death” of the public’s growing discontent. This isn’t leadership; it’s paranoia masquerading as patriotism.

The Tea Party’s Ghost

Former Tea Party Rep. Joe Walsh’s gleeful endorsement of the ‘No Kings’ rallies underscores a seismic shift within the Republican Party. Once a bastion for the Tea Party’s brand of conservatism, the GOP now finds itself haunted by the specter of its own making. Walsh’s call for a “gazillion Americans” to protest is a siren song for democratic engagement, directly challenging the narrative peddled by Johnson and his ilk.

The Organizers’ Rebuttal

The No Kings coalition’s response to Johnson’s rant is both scathing and poignant. Labeling the protest as a ‘hate America rally’ not only betrays Johnson’s disconnect from the core values of democracy but also showcases his desperation. Sally Dillon’s and Gary Michael Walters’ retorts on X (formerly Twitter) highlight a growing consensus: dissent is not disloyalty. Their messages underscore a fundamental American principle — the right to protest is a cornerstone of democracy, not an act of hatred.

The Veiled Bigotry

Walters’ mention of “the Soros comment” as “veiled anti-Semitism” points to a darker undercurrent within the GOP’s rhetoric. This not-so-subtle nod to conspiracy theories surrounding George Soros is a tactic ripped straight from the playbook of bigotry. It’s a sad commentary on how low some leaders will stoop to vilify their opponents — all while claiming to stand for American values.

The Errand Boy Accusation

Chris Ahuja’s characterization of Johnson as an “errand boy too scared to tell the truth” strikes at the heart of the matter. The GOP’s leadership, as personified by Johnson, is not just out of touch with the American public; it’s actively engaged in a campaign of misinformation. Ahuja’s advice for Johnson to “read a history book” is both a rebuke and a reminder of what America stands for: a nation that doesn’t bow to kings or kowtow to fear.

Conclusion: A Call to Reason

Mike Johnson’s rant and the GOP’s broader narrative around the ‘No Kings’ protests are indicative of a party at war with the very principles it claims to defend. In their scramble to maintain control, GOP leaders are alienating the American populace, revealing a deep-seated fear of accountability and change.

The ‘No Kings’ rallies, far from being a “Hate America” event, represent a resurgence of American patriotism — one that cherishes democratic values over autocratic fears. It’s high time GOP leaders confront this reality, lest they find themselves on the wrong side of history.
