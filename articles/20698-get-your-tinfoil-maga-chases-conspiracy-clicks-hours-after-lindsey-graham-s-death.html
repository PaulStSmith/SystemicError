'Get your tinfoil': MAGA chases 'conspiracy' clicks hours after Lindsey Graham's death

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20262 min read

Death, Panic, and the Propaganda Machine

The Vacuum Was the Point

The source is simple enough: Graham’s office issued a bare-bones statement about a “brief and sudden illness,” Trump teased more information, and then MAGA influencers filled the silence with Russia-and-Iran fantasies. That is the whole mechanism. A thin official statement created a vacuum, and a well-trained ecosystem rushed in to turn uncertainty into spectacle.

Who Actually Controls the Story

The people with institutional power here are not the podcasters and online grifters pretending to investigate. They are Graham’s office, the hospital, Trump, and the political network around them. They control what gets released, when it gets released, and which version of events becomes the starting point for public consumption. Everyone else is freelancing on top of that power, trying to convert a human death into engagement, loyalty, or geopolitical theater.

Conspiracy as Cheap Politics

Clint Russell, Laura Loomer, Anthony Galli, and Matt Forney did not respond to evidence. They responded to a power vacuum with insinuation. Russia did it. Iran did it. Maybe both. Maybe “we can’t rule it out.” That is not inquiry. It is a performance of suspicion that rewards the speaker regardless of truth, because the point is not accuracy. The point is to keep followers agitated and the feed moving.

Foreign Enemies, Domestic Evasion

What makes this especially rotten is the reflex to externalize blame immediately. The impulse is always to reach for Russia, Iran, assassination, hidden war, anything except the boring reality that public information was sparse and the online right was eager to exploit that sparsity. This is how propaganda works in practice: it does not need proof, only a market for panic. It converts ignorance into allegiance.

The Real Pattern

This story is not about one death. It is about a political culture that treats every event as raw material for factional messaging and every gap in information as a license to manufacture enemies. The result is an environment where deliberate misdirection gets more reach than responsible disclosure, and where accountability is replaced by insinuation. That is the deeper decay: institutions withhold, influencers improvise, and the public is left to sort propaganda from fact after the damage is already done.
