Americans are being conditioned to accept 'violence beyond anything imagined': experts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 18, 20255 min read

The increasingly hostile political climate is showing no signs of abating, and some political scientists are worried about what that could mean for Americans in the near future.

That's according to a Tuesday essay by the New York Times' Thomas B. Edsall, who spoke with various experts about how the recent killing of far-right activist Charlie Kirk has inflamed political tensions across the United States. Some were particularly worried that President Donald Trump's harsh rhetoric toward the political left could significantly increase the chances of further political violence during his second term.

University of Chicago political scientist Robert Pape said that Kirk's death was the "most consequential assassination of an American political leader since the 1960s." He also pointed to past research he had conducted showing that tens of millions of Americans on both sides were showing an increasingly favorable attitude toward violence as a way to solve the country's problems, and harkened back to Americans' rush to go to war in the wake of the September 11, 2001 attacks.

"There are powerful reasons to worry how Republicans will react to the assassination of Kirk, but the main one is simply the most obvious: Kirk was beloved by millions of Republicans and now many millions more," he said. "As America saw after 9/11 in our politics leading to 70 percent of the public supporting the invasion of Iraq on flimsy evidence: mass sorrow can evolve into mass anger and then mass willingness to pursue aggressive policies that can lead to spirals of violence beyond anything imagined before the event."

Other experts pushed back on Trump's repeated claims that the political left bore sole responsibility for political violence. University of Krakow, Poland psychology professor Katarzyna Jasko said the president's argument was "not justifiable" given the ample examples of right-wing political violence in recent years. She noted that her past research found that far-right political violence was not only more frequent than violent acts carried out but the left, but the intensity of violence committed by right-wing actors was far greater.

"Among radicalized individuals in the United States, those adhering to a left-wing ideology were markedly less likely to engage in violent ideologically motivated acts when compared to right-wing individuals. By contrast, we found no such difference between Islamist and right-wing individuals," Jasko wrote in a 2022 study.

Despite Trump's insistence that the left is exclusively responsible for the rise in political violence, the Trump administration was found to have deleted a Department of Justice study finding that far-right political violence far outpaced "all other types of terrorism," including both left-wing violence and Islamic terrorism. There has so far not been a specific ideological motive tied to the alleged shooter who has been charged with Charlie Kirk's murder.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Political Climate: A Critical Examination

In a recent essay by Thomas B. Edsall for the New York Times, the spotlight is thrown onto the increasingly hostile political climate in the United States, a topic that demands our urgent attention. The assassination of far-right activist Charlie Kirk has ostensibly become a catalyst for escalating tensions, a situation that political scientists fear might deteriorate further, especially with President Donald Trump at the helm of affairs. Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned analyst.

The Potent Mix of Political Violence and Rhetoric

The assertion by Robert Pape, a political scientist from the University of Chicago, that Kirk's death is the "most consequential assassination of an American political leader since the 1960s," is a bold one. Pape draws a parallel between the current political atmosphere and the period following the September 11, 2001 attacks, suggesting that grief could morph into aggression, potentially leading to a surge in political violence. This analogy is not just dramatic; it's a stark warning. However, it's essential to probe deeper. Are we equating the assassination of a polarizing figure to a national tragedy that led to war? The contexts are vastly different, yet the underlying fear of a spiral into violence is indeed a common thread.

Trump's Rhetoric and the Blame Game

The focus on President Donald Trump's rhetoric is particularly noteworthy. Trump's inclination to pin the blame for political violence solely on the left, as highlighted by experts, is not just misleading—it's dangerously simplistic. Katarzyna Jasko, a psychology professor from the University of Krakow, Poland, offers a counter-narrative grounded in research. Her studies reveal a troubling reality: far-right political violence not only occurs more frequently but is also more intense than that perpetrated by the left. This fact, juxtaposed with Trump's assertions, paints a picture of a leadership strategy marred by selective blindness and a perilous game of finger-pointing.

The Erasure of Inconvenient Truths

Perhaps one of the most damning revelations in Edsall's essay is the Trump administration's alleged deletion of a Department of Justice study. This study found that far-right political violence far outstripped other forms of terrorism, including Islamic terrorism. This act of erasure is not merely an administrative oversight; it's a deliberate attempt to sculpt the narrative, to shape the public's understanding of where the threat lies. It's a manipulation of the narrative at the highest levels of government, with potentially grave implications for national security and social cohesion.

The Unidentified Ideology of the Assailant

A critical piece of the puzzle remains elusive—the ideology of Charlie Kirk's alleged murderer. This absence of information is a gaping hole in the narrative, one that prevents a full understanding of the incident's motivations. It's a stark reminder of the complexities surrounding acts of political violence and the dangers of jumping to conclusions based on incomplete information.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Understanding

The analysis presented by Edsall, through his consultations with various experts, underscores a volatile political environment teetering on the brink of further violence. However, as critical thinkers and engaged citizens, we must tread carefully, scrutinizing the narratives fed to us by those in power. The vilification of the political left by Trump, contrasted with the documented prevalence of far-right violence, serves as a cautionary tale against simplistic narratives. It's a call for a nuanced understanding of political violence, one that transcends partisan lines and confronts the uncomfortable truths of our time. In a climate bristling with tension and hostility, the path forward demands not just vigilance but an unwavering commitment to facts, however inconvenient they may be.
