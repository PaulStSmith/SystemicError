'That's not how Putin saw it': Analyst warns Trump's 'show of force' at summit backfired

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Summit Missteps: A Boon for Putin?Conclusion: A Strategic Misfire with Broad Implications

U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hand with Russian President Vladimir Putin, as they meet to negotiate for an end to the war in Ukraine, at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S., August 15, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

President Donald Trump may have done more to help Russian President Vladimir Putin than he did to help Ukraine during the summit in Alaska on Friday, according to one analyst.

Trump said he intended to negotiate a ceasefire in the three-and-a-half-year war in Ukraine during the summit. If Putin refused to agree, then he would impose "very serious consequences," including sanctions. Trump backed up his tough talk by staging a flyover of American bombers while he greeted Putin.

However, Eric Edelman, a retired foreign service officer, warned on Sunday that Trump's move backfired. Instead, Edelman suggested that Trump had "rescued" Putin from the international isolation he had endured since the war began.

Edelman joined The Bulwark's Editor at Large, Bill Kristol, on the outlet's Sunday podcast to discuss the summit.

"I think Trump may have thought that having a B-2 flyover accompanied by F-22s, the aircraft that, of course, were involved in Operation Midnight Hammer against Iran, was somehow suggesting a show of force to Putin," Edelman said. "I don't think that's how Putin saw it. I think he saw it as a mark of respect, actually. That's certainly the way it's being presented in the Russian press in the reaction to all this, which has been that this was an unqualified success for Putin."

"What it did was resuscitate him both domestically and internationally as a respected player on the international stage," Edelman continued. "This is very reminiscent of Kevin McCarthy going down to Mar-a-Lago in February of 2021 after Trump had left office disgraced by what happened on January 6th and his failure to stop the insurrection. That basically brought Trump back to life politically. That, in effect, is what Trump has done, I think, to Putin on both the domestic stage in Russia and on the international stage."

He added that the move could send a dangerous signal to countries America has sanctioned.

"It encourages countries like Turkey, for instance, to continue evading U.S. sanctions and sanctions on Russia, assuming that there will be no penalty for doing that," Edleman said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Summit Missteps: A Boon for Putin?

In a striking display of foreign policy flubs, President Donald Trump's recent summit in Alaska has raised eyebrows and ire, as analysts suggest he may have inadvertently bolstered Russian President Vladimir Putin more than advancing any substantive progress in Ukraine. The summit, ostensibly aimed at negotiating a ceasefire in the ongoing Ukrainian conflict, appears to have backfired spectacularly, according to Eric Edelman, a voice of experience and a retired foreign service officer.

Trump's Show of Force: A Misguided Gesture?

Trump's strategy to intimidate Putin with a military flyover, featuring B-2 bombers and F-22s, was seemingly more theatrical than practical. Edelman's insights suggest a gross miscalculation on Trump's part, interpreting a gesture intended as a show of strength as, paradoxically, a mark of respect towards Putin. This interpretation, especially considering how it's been received by the Russian press, paints a picture of success for Putin, rather than a stern warning.

The imagery and optics of power, so crucial on the global stage, have been turned on their head. Trump's attempt at flexing military muscle has not only fallen flat but has also served to reinvigorate Putin's standing both domestically and internationally. This miscalculation is likened to Kevin McCarthy's pilgrimage to Mar-a-Lago post-January 6 insurrection, a move that inadvertently breathed life into Trump's political aspirations. Similarly, Trump has, albeit unintentionally, revitalized Putin's image as a respected international player.

The Unintended Consequences of Diplomatic Blunders

Edelman's comparison of Trump's actions to McCarthy's ill-advised venture is scathingly apt. In both instances, attempts to project strength or solidarity have resulted in the opposite, emboldening figures who were, at moments, politically vulnerable. The summit, rather than isolating Putin further, has effectively ended his international pariah status, repositioning him as a significant global actor.

Furthermore, Edelman warns of the dangerous precedent Trump's actions may set. The implications extend beyond Russia, potentially encouraging other nations, such as Turkey, to flout U.S. sanctions with impunity. This underlines a critical misunderstanding of international relations and the delicate balance of incentives and deterrents that govern the behavior of nations.

Conclusion: A Strategic Misfire with Broad Implications

The Alaska summit serves as a cautionary tale of how not to conduct foreign policy, especially with adversaries. Trump's theatrics and tough talk, rather than cornering Putin, have inadvertently provided him with a platform to regain his stride on the world stage. This incident underscores the necessity of a nuanced, well-thought-out approach to diplomacy, where actions are carefully calibrated to achieve desired outcomes, rather than symbolic gestures that may backfire.

In essence, Trump's attempt to negotiate peace in Ukraine while projecting American strength has instead highlighted a fundamental flaw in his understanding of power dynamics and international respect. The result is a stronger Putin, a potentially emboldened cohort of U.S. adversaries, and a lesson in the pitfalls of diplomatic overreach.
