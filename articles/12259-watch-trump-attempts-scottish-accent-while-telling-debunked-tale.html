Donald Trump rolled out an attempt at a Scottish brogue Tuesday as he gave an impression of James Bond legend Sean Connery during the ribbon-cutting ceremony for his newest golf course.

The president was hawking his new Trump International Golf Links in Aberdeenshire when he launched into an attempt at a Scottish accent, complete with a tale about the late actor's supposed intervention on his behalf — the accuracy of which is widely questioned.

"I just want to thank everybody. This has been an unbelievable development. The land, they said, couldn't get zoned, it was an impossibility," Trump began before deploying his accent.

"And Sean Connery said: 'Let the bloody bloke build his golf course.' Once he said that, everything came into line."

Trump's story has been thoroughly debunked by people who actually know what happened.

Martin Ford, the Aberdeenshire councillor who chaired the planning committee that initially shot down Trump's application for the golf course, didn't mince words when he torched the former president's fantasy just days after Connery's death in 2020.

"Mr Connery was not involved in the due process that led to the granting of planning permission," Ford told the GuardianGuardian.

Trump has been in Scotland through the weekend, visiting his properties while meeting with European leaders.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Fabrication: Trump's Scottish Masquerade

In a display that could only emerge from the depths of Donald Trump's reality distortion field, the former president recently treated the world to a spectacle that was as baffling as it was inappropriate. During a ribbon-cutting ceremony for his latest venture, the Trump International Golf Links in Aberdeenshire, Trump decided to channel his inner Sean Connery — or, shall we say, his interpretation of Connery — in an accent attempt that was as cringe-worthy as it was inaccurate.

Theatrics Over Truth

Trump's penchant for theatrics knows no bounds, but his recent foray into Scottish brogue takes this to a new level. The former president, in a moment that straddles the line between comedy and tragedy, recounted a dubious tale involving the late James Bond legend, Sean Connery. According to Trump, Connery intervened on his behalf to help push through the golf course's zoning permissions, a claim that, much like Trump's accent attempt, fails to hold any substance.

"And Sean Connery said: 'Let the bloody bloke build his golf course.' Once he said that, everything came into line," Trump boasted, in a narrative that seems to have been plucked from the same realm of fantasy that often dominates his rhetoric. The truth, as confirmed by those actually involved in the situation, starkly contradicts Trump's account. Martin Ford, the Aberdeenshire councillor who chaired the planning committee that rejected Trump's application, unequivocally stated, "Mr Connery was not involved in the due process that led to the granting of planning permission."

The Reality Behind the Rhetoric

This incident is emblematic of a broader issue with Trump's approach to both politics and business: a flagrant disregard for truth in favor of a narrative that aggrandizes his achievements and persona. The fact that this latest fabrication came to light just days after Connery's death in 2020 adds a layer of distaste to the whole charade, exploiting the memory of a beloved actor to lend credence to a dubious anecdote.

Furthermore, the setting of this farcical performance — a golf course that has been mired in controversy due to environmental concerns and opposition from local residents — only adds to the layers of irony. Trump's ventures in Scotland have been a microcosm of his broader business dealings: marked by grandiose promises, fierce opposition, and a tangled web of claims and counterclaims about their actual benefits to the local community.

Conclusion: The Trump Show Must Go On

The spectacle in Aberdeenshire is a stark reminder of the Trumpian modus operandi: when faced with opposition, don't adjust or seek compromise, but instead double down with a mixture of bravado, fabrication, and a disregard for the facts. As Trump continues to visit his properties and hobnob with European leaders, one can only wonder what other tales he might spin and what other accents he might attempt.

In the end, Trump's misadventure in Scotland serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of conflating business with showmanship, especially when the former president is involved. It's a reminder that behind the bluster and the faux Connery impressions lies a deeper narrative about the manipulation of truth for personal gain — a narrative that, unfortunately, seems to be as enduring as the Trump brand itself.
