Trump is handing China a 'golden opportunity' to invade Taiwan: expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 2, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Fallacy of Pinning Taiwan's Fate on Trump's Ineptitude

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump's inability to manage affairs with China is creating an opportunity for the superpower to eventually invade Taiwan without fear of U.S. intervention.

That is according to longtime foreign affairs correspondent Simon Tisdale in a column published on June 1 in the Guardian.

According to Tisdale, "China’s president, Xi Jinping, has reportedly told his generals to be ready by 2027 to conquer the self-governing island, which he regards as stolen sovereign territory," with U.S. officials admitting the capabilities are already in place.

The columnist suggested Trump's on-again, off-again tariff war, combined with his innate "cowardice," has made the threat of invasion more likely because he is seen in China as weak.

The Chinese pushback on tariffs could lead President Xi to call Trump's bluff and use provocation about Taiwan as a tool to put him on the spot.

"Were China to obstruct Taiwanese maritime traffic, launch covert cyber-attacks on Taiwan, or impose a full naval and aerial blockade that fell short of all-out invasion, it could force Trump into a humiliating climbdown," he wrote before noting, "Trump doesn’t want a war in east Asia, and Beijing knows it. It also rightly suspects that, like bullies everywhere, his aggressive bluster conceals a coward’s weakness. He refuses to fight for Ukraine, a core western interest, and kowtows to Russian aggression. He’s terrified Israel will start full-scale wars with Iran and Syria, drawing in the US. His policies are driven by self-interest, money and fear, not principles, treaties or laws."

He then warned, "Xi surely frames the battle for Taiwan as part of the wider contest between the US and China for regional partners, military superiority and global hegemony. Now a golden opportunity is arising. Thanks to Trump’s chaotic tariffs, domestic firefights, isolationist policies and wanton disruption of European and Asian alliances, the US now looks beatable."

Our Analysis:

The Fallacy of Pinning Taiwan's Fate on Trump's Ineptitude

In an article dripping with a sense of foreboding, Simon Tisdale of The Guardian weaves a narrative that places former U.S. President Donald Trump at the epicenter of an impending geopolitical catastrophe: the potential invasion of Taiwan by China. At the heart of Tisdale's argument is the assertion that Trump's erratic handling of U.S.-China relations, characterized by a tariff war and alleged personal cowardice, has emboldened China to the point where an invasion of Taiwan is not only conceivable but increasingly likely.

The Core of Tisdale's Argument

Tisdale points to a series of Trump's foreign policy missteps and personal traits, such as his inconsistent approach to tariffs and his supposed aversion to conflict, as key factors that have weakened the U.S.'s stance against China. The columnist suggests that these actions—or lack thereof—signal to China a U.S. reluctance to engage in military conflict over Taiwan, thereby increasing the likelihood of Chinese aggression.

The Oversimplification of Complex Issues

However, the argument that Trump's policies alone have significantly altered the trajectory towards a potential conflict over Taiwan is an oversimplification of a complex and multifaceted issue. It is crucial to recognize that the tensions between China and Taiwan, and by extension the U.S., have deep roots that predate Trump's presidency. They are the result of historical grievances, nationalistic ambitions, and strategic geopolitical considerations that cannot be neatly attributed to the actions of a single leader.

Misplaced Focus on Personal Cowardice

Tisdale's emphasis on Trump's personal "cowardice" as a determining factor in international relations is both reductive and unhelpful. While the personality and disposition of leaders can indeed influence diplomatic and military strategies, the suggestion that Trump's personal traits alone are reshaping the global order belies the reality of institutional decision-making processes and the broader strategic considerations of the U.S. military and its allies.

The Real Issue: A Shifting Global Power Balance

The crux of the matter lies not in the whims of an individual but in the shifting balance of power and influence in the Asia-Pacific region and beyond. China's rise as a global superpower and its increasingly assertive foreign policy stance pose challenges that extend far beyond the tenure of any single U.S. president. The framing of the issue as a consequence of Trump's mismanagement risks obscuring the broader strategic challenges facing the U.S. and its allies in maintaining the status quo in the Taiwan Strait.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Analysis

While Tisdale's article succeeds in highlighting the escalating tensions between the U.S., China, and Taiwan, it falls into the trap of attributing complex international dynamics to the actions and personality of one individual. Such a narrative not only oversimplifies the challenges at hand but also detracts from the nuanced analysis required to understand and, ultimately, address them effectively. The fate of Taiwan and the broader geopolitical contest between the U.S. and China demand a more comprehensive and multifaceted examination than the one offered by pinning the blame on Trump's doorstep.
