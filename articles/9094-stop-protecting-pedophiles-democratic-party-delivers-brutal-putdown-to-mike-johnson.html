The Democratic Party delivered a brutal putdown to House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) on Saturday over his continued refusal to swear in Adelita Grijalva, despite her election victory in Arizona 40 days ago – the longest delay on record between a congressional candidate’s win and their swearing in.

Johnson has faced allegations that his hesitancy to swear Grijalva in stems from his fear of a discharge petition, a legislative tool introduced by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) to compel the Justice Department to release files on Jeffrey Epstein. The petition remains just one signature short of having garnered enough support to force a vote on the matter, and Grijalva has pledged to support it.

“It’s been 40 days since [Grijalva] was elected by her constituents,” wrote the Democrat Party on its official X account to its more than 2.4 million followers. “Mike Johnson: Stop protecting pedophiles, swear in Adelita Grijalva, and release the Epstein files.”

Grijalva accused Johnson of delaying her swearing in to prevent a vote on Massie’s discharge petition as early as Oct. 6, and now, nearly a month later, pressure against the speaker continues to rise. Johnson has insisted he was unable to swear in Grijalva until the government re-opens, having been shut down since Oct. 1.

Johnson has also been confronted by survivors of Epstein – who died in 2019 under mysterious circumstances while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges – who last week sent him a letter calling on him to stop obstructing Grijalva’s swearing in.

“Grijalva was duly elected and certified to serve in the U.S. House of Representatives,” the letter reads, signed by several Epstein survivors. “Her constituents have spoken, and their voice deserves representation without further delay. The continued refusal to seat her is an unacceptable breach of democratic norms and a disservice to the American people.”

Our Analysis:

The Unsworn Congresswoman: A Tangled Web of Politics and Conspiracy

In what reads like a script torn out of a political thriller, the Democratic Party has launched a scathing assault on House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) for his unprecedented delay in swearing in Adelita Grijalva, a duly elected congresswoman from Arizona. This saga, stretching over 40 days, isn’t just a procedural hiccup; it’s a glaring spotlight on the dysfunctional theatrics that plague our political system.

The Heart of the Matter

At the core of this controversy lies Mike Johnson’s apparent reluctance to swear in Grijalva, a move that the Democratic Party alleges is aimed at blocking a critical vote. The vote in question? A discharge petition introduced by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) demanding the release of files on the infamous Jeffrey Epstein. Johnson’s stonewalling, as per this narrative, isn’t just procedural; it’s politically motivated.

The Epstein Connection: More Than Meets the Eye

The Epstein saga has been a festering wound on the American body politic, a symbol of how wealth and power can manipulate the levers of justice. The discharge petition, now teetering on the edge of success, represents a rare bipartisan effort to pry open the files on Epstein, potentially revealing uncomfortable truths about the reach of his influence.

The Democratic Party’s Salvo

The Democratic Party’s attack on Johnson, accusing him of “protecting pedophiles” by delaying Grijalva’s swearing-in, is bold and brash. Utilizing their platform on X to rally their 2.4 million followers, they’ve taken a no-holds-barred approach to what they view as a clear subversion of democratic principles.

Johnson’s Defense: A Government in Shutdown

Johnson’s rebuttal—that he cannot swear in Grijalva due to the government shutdown since Oct. 1—rings hollow. The government’s operational status has rarely if ever, been a barrier to the essential functions of democracy, such as the swearing-in of duly elected officials. This excuse seems more like a convenient shield than a legitimate impediment.

The Voice of the Survivors

Perhaps the most poignant element of this debacle is the letter from survivors of Epstein’s crimes, urging Johnson to cease his obstruction. This letter isn’t just a request; it’s a demand for justice from those who have been denied it for too long. Their voices add a deeply human element to the political maneuvering, reminding us of the real stakes involved.

Conclusion: A Democracy at a Crossroads

This episode is emblematic of a larger crisis facing our democracy: the erosion of norms and the weaponization of procedural technicalities. Mike Johnson’s actions—or lack thereof—highlight a troubling readiness to place political gain over democratic principles and justice.

As this drama unfolds, one thing is clear: the American people deserve better. They deserve a system that upholds the values of transparency, accountability, and fairness. The saga of Adelita Grijalva’s unsworn status is more than a political skirmish; it’s a test of our collective commitment to those ideals. And it’s high time for Johnson to face the music: swear in Grijalva, release the Epstein files, and let the chips fall where they may. In a democracy, no one should have the power to silence the will of the people, nor the cries for justice from those who’ve been wronged.
