'Signs everywhere' suggest America’s MAGA 'nightmare' is almost over: writer

By Paulo Santos

Former pastor and author John Pavlovitz wrote Monday that “all brutal empires fall,” and the MAGA movement is no different — and signs of its inevitable demise are “everywhere.”

“Every time the pendulum has swung wildly toward barbarism in a society, it has invariably come back with even greater opposite force to bend the arc of the moral universe back toward justice again,” he wrote on his Substack.

Pavlovitz said that students of history may recognize that the end is likely nigh for MAGA.

“And history tells us that, despite the story in our heads or the one that comes through the partisan propaganda that we’re continually saturated by, our brutal national nightmare is likely in its final hours,” he wrote.

“Few cults survive the passing of their leaders, and MAGA will not outlive the departure of Donald Trump from the White House or from this earth, whichever comes first.”

Concerns and speculation regarding Trump’s health, particularly signs of aging and potential cognitive decline, have been widely discussed, but the White House maintains he is in “exceptional physical health.” Medical experts and political commentators have pointed to observable incidents and a reduced public schedule as potential indicators of decline, and a recent push for results of an MRI scan in October have exacerbated the speculation.

“His physical attrition and mental degradation can no longer be hidden or distracted from. His body is rapidly breaking down, laboring with simple tasks, hemorrhaging energy, and riddled with the unmistakable tells of a man whose systems are failing,” Pavlovitz wrote.

“His already hate-addled mind is becoming more and more clouded in an ever-thickening haze of deterioration, drugs, and almost certain dementia. His outbursts have grown more impulsive, his decisions have become more desperate, and the once indestructible facade of strength is steadily crumbling. The strongman is withering.”

Noting that Trump is a lame duck president, Pavlovitz wrote that his “once fierce death grip on political power is giving way to neutered impotence and growing irrelevance” as much of what has built him up is starting to collapse.

“The rapacious brutality of his regime is being exposed and condemned. The groundswell of public opposition is growing louder and more organized,” Pavlovitz noted.

“The defections from his party are accelerating, their unwavering allegiance weakening. His cockroach accomplices are scattering in the raking light of coming accountability. His supposed social media army of support is proving to be made largely of foreign bot factories,” he added.

Pavlovitz surmises that Trump is on the verge of political expiration, writing, “He has peaked as a galvanizing presence, and there’s no GOP spin doctoring that can change that.”

“He gave people permission to revel in the worst of their instincts, to give loud voice to their hidden hatreds, to discard their religious convictions, and to fully abandon empathy and decency,” he added.

Pavlovitz says that once Trump is gone, so will be MAGA, but, he said, they won’t “suddenly awaken from their cultic stupor and join the rest of us in benevolent and interdependent humanity.”

However, he writes, they will “never again have someone who embodies the zeitgeist of grotesque impulses the way Trump has.”

And while the coming days won’t be painless, as his supporters may become “more violent, more desperate, and more committed to destruction,” Pavlovitz warned, they know their time is short.

“That should strengthen our resolve, lift our spirits, and sturdy our steps,” he says. “Take heart, good people. Time, gravity, and term limits are undefeated, and one way or another, the end of MAGA is near,” he wrote.

“And the dawn of the promise of America will break once again.”

Our Analysis:

The Inevitable Fall of MAGA: A Critical Examination

John Pavlovitz’s latest piece on the MAGA movement’s demise is not just a commentary; it’s a scorching critique wrapped in a hopeful prophecy. He argues with a conviction that seems to pierce through the veil of current political chaos, suggesting that the MAGA era, helmed by Donald Trump, is not just faltering but is on the verge of collapse. Let’s dissect his arguments, the evidence at hand, and the implications of his bold assertions.

The Cycle of History and MAGA’s Downfall

Pavlovitz draws on historical patterns to support his thesis that “all brutal empires fall.” This is not merely poetic; it’s grounded in a reality we’ve seen unfold time and again across the globe. The idea that societies swing back towards justice after veering too close to barbarism offers a comforting narrative. However, it’s crucial to question whether the MAGA movement, under Trump’s leadership, can be directly compared to these “brutal empires.” Yes, MAGA has had a profound and divisive impact on American politics, but equating it to historical empires may oversimplify complex sociopolitical dynamics.

Trump’s Decline: The Beginning of the End?

Pavlovitz doesn’t mince words when discussing Trump’s health, suggesting his physical and cognitive decline is evident and accelerating. This argument is tantalizingly controversial. It’s true that there’s been widespread speculation about Trump’s health, with some commentators and medical professionals raising alarms. However, the leap from personal health to the collapse of a political movement is a gargantuan one. Political movements, especially those as fervent as MAGA, often have a life beyond their leaders. The suggestion that MAGA will not survive Trump’s eventual departure could underestimate the movement’s depth and breadth across the American political landscape.

The Crumbling Facade of Power

Pavlovitz’s description of Trump’s waning influence is bitingly vivid. He talks of defections, public opposition, and the scattering of “cockroach accomplices” as signs of an empire in its death throes. This imagery is powerful, yet it’s essential to approach it with a critical eye. Political power is multifaceted and doesn’t solely reside in the figure of Trump or his inner circle. The MAGA movement has infiltrated various levels of government, social discourse, and, crucially, the American identity for a significant portion of the population.

The Aftermath: What Comes Next?

The assertion that MAGA will vanish post-Trump is perhaps the most polarizing aspect of Pavlovitz’s piece. It’s an optimistic, if not idealistic, view. The belief that Trump’s departure will lead to a dissipation of the movement ignores the deeper roots of discontent and disenfranchisement that gave rise to MAGA. These issues won’t simply evaporate. Moreover, suggesting that MAGA supporters are in a “cultic stupor” risks further alienation rather than fostering understanding or dialogue.

Conclusion: Hopeful or Naive?

Pavlovitz ends on a hopeful note, invoking the “dawn of the promise of America.” It’s a beautiful sentiment, yet one must wonder if it’s overly naive. Political movements, ideologies, and the forces that drive them are resilient. They evolve, adapt, and sometimes, they rebrand rather than disappear.

In the end, Pavlovitz’s piece is a fiery call to arms for those opposed to MAGA, offering a vision of its demise as inevitable. However, the realities of political and social dynamics suggest the road ahead may be far more complex and winding than a simple narrative of fall and resurgence allows. The fall of MAGA, if it happens in the manner Pavlovitz predicts, will not be the end of the story. It will be the beginning of a new chapter, fraught with its own challenges, conflicts, and the continual struggle for the soul of America.
