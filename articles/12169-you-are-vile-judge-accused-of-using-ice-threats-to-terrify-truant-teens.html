'You are vile!' Judge accused of using ICE threats to terrify truant teens

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 1, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An officer with the U.S. Department of Homeland Security stands as people protest against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) outside the U.S. immigration court in, New York City, U.S., July 28, 2025. REUTERS/Shannon Stapleton

A municipal court judge in Somerset County who oversaw truancy cases threatened children who came before him and their mothers with deportation, according to a formal complaint issued by a judicial advisory board.

The 13-page complaint released Tuesday includes transcripts of court proceedings overseen by the now-former judge, Britt J. Simon, who was a municipal judge for Bound Brook, Bridgewater, Somerville, and Raritan Borough. It accuses Simon of violating the judicial conduct code by repeatedly threatening the children and using language that created the appearance of bias.

“Your mother’s going to get deported. You are going to end your mother’s life. You want other people involved in your family business? Your mother came to this country to get a better life,” he told one 16-year-old last August, the complaint says.

In January, eight days after President Donald Trump took office for his second term, Simon told a 14-year-old that “there’s a new sheriff in town” and that the courts were a “great place” to find people facing deportation.

“You think I’m kidding? The ICE officials wait outside the door for people like you,” Simon said.

Simon has been suspended from his judicial duties since Feb. 11. The state Supreme Court’s advisory committee on judicial conduct filed the formal complaint against him Monday.

Simon’s attorney did not return a request for comment.

The complaint includes details on three truancy cases overseen by Simon. It says he raised his voice, scolded the children in his courtroom, addressed them without a municipal prosecutor present, and roped in their parents for additional scoldings.

On Aug. 13, 2024, he told the 16-year-old that he’s going to be “a beggar, piece of garbage” because he doesn’t go to school. He then asked the student if his mom had legal documentation to be in the United States, to which the student said no.

“So you want to call attention to the school truant officer — two officers sitting here — that you get law enforcement involves (sic) in your lives. Don’t you? So your mother can get picked up and deported by ICE. That sound like a great idea? You’re a wonderful son. I say sarcastically because you’re not,” Simon told the teenager. “You are vile and contemptuous. You want your mother to get deported? You know what’s going to happen if she does, right?”

Simon then said he’d refer the student’s case to child protective services, adding that those workers may report his mother and “at some point, ICE is going to come and pick her up, all because of you.”

“You don’t be late. I am not kidding you. Look at your mother, get ready to say goodbye to her,” Simon said.

In another case that day involving a high school girl who was late to school every day and eventually stopped attending, Simon again turned his attention to her mother’s immigration status (the mother confirmed to Simon that she was not in the country legally).

“How are you going to feel when the law enforcement comes to the house — ICE comes to the house because they get reported this and they deport your mother because of you?” he told the girl.

On Jan. 28, Simon presided over the case of a 14-year-old girl who missed 67 of the previous 91 days of school. In this case, both the student and her parent were undocumented.

“You’re about to get plucked up by the ICE officials and sent back to El Salvador. Do you want to go there?” Simon asked the girl.

“No,” she responded.

“You miss another day for school and I’m going to personally have ICE here to pick you up,” Simon told her.

Our Analysis:

The Judiciary's Menace: A Deep Dive into Judicial Misconduct

In a recent exposé by the New Jersey Monitor, the case of Britt J. Simon, a former municipal court judge in Somerset County, has come to light, revealing a disturbing abuse of power that strikes at the very heart of judicial integrity and the immigrant community's trust in the legal system. Simon, who presided over truancy cases across several boroughs, has been accused of threatening children and their mothers with deportation, a tactic that not only violates the judicial conduct code but also showcases a blatant display of bias and cruelty.

A Pattern of Intimidation and Bias

The 13-page complaint issued by a judicial advisory board is not just a list of grievances; it is a testament to a systemic failure to protect the most vulnerable. According to transcripts of court proceedings, Simon's threats were not idle words but a deliberate attempt to instill fear. "Your mother’s going to get deported. You are going to end your mother’s life," he told a 16-year-old, exploiting the student's fear for their mother's safety to exercise control. This is not judicial discretion; it is judicial terror.

In another instance, Simon ominously declared to a 14-year-old, "there’s a new sheriff in town," referencing the then-recent inauguration of President Donald Trump for his second term. Simon seemed to relish the thought of ICE officials waiting "outside the door for people like you," weaponizing the very real fears of the immigrant community for his own purposes. This behavior is not only unethical but deeply inhumane, treating the threat of deportation as a tool for compliance rather than the life-altering consequence it is.

Misconduct Beyond Threats

Simon's misconduct did not stop at verbal threats. The complaint details how he raised his voice, scolded children in the absence of a municipal prosecutor, and involved their parents in these scoldings, further humiliating and pressuring the families. On one occasion, he demeaned a 16-year-old by calling him a "beggar, piece of garbage" for not attending school, and then cruelly questioned the legal status of the student's mother, insinuating that her deportation would be the student's fault. Such actions betray a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of a judge and a blatant disregard for the principles of justice and compassion.

The Judicial System’s Response

While Simon has been suspended from his judicial duties since February 11, the question arises: Is suspension enough? The formal complaint by the state Supreme Court’s advisory committee on judicial conduct is a step in the right direction, but it also highlights a troubling delay in addressing such egregious behavior. Simon's attorney's silence in response to requests for comment speaks volumes, leaving us to question the accountability mechanisms within the judicial system.

A Call for Accountability and Reform

This case is a stark reminder of the power judges hold and the catastrophic impact their abuse of this power can have on individuals and families. It underscores the urgent need for comprehensive judicial reform, including stricter oversight mechanisms and swift action against misconduct. The immigrant community, often marginalized and vulnerable, deserves protection from those who would use their position of authority to intimidate and harm.

The New Jersey Monitor's report is not just an indictment of Britt J. Simon but a call to action for all who believe in justice and the rule of law. It is incumbent upon us to demand accountability and to ensure that our judicial system is one that upholds the highest standards of fairness, compassion, and respect for all individuals, regardless of their immigration status.

In the face of such systemic error, silence is complicity. We must speak out and work tirelessly to ensure that our courts are places of justice, not venues for intimidation and fear. The case of Britt J. Simon should be a wake-up call for all who care about the integrity of our judiciary and the rights of the most vulnerable among us.
