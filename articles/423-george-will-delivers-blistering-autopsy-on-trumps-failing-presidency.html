George Will delivers blistering 'autopsy' on Trump’s 'failing presidency'

Trump’s Chaos Is Not a Mistake. It’s the Method.

The Useful Autopsy

George Will’s column is the source material here: a conservative “autopsy” of Trump’s second presidency, written in the language of elite disappointment. That framing is too polite for what it describes. The real story is not that Trump stumbled into disorder. It is that he continues to use the machinery of government to serve grievance, spectacle, and personal loyalty, while institutions around him absorb the damage.

Power Sits at the Top

Actual institutional power sits with Trump. Not the critics, not the columnists, not the ex-GOP strategists offering postmortems after the fact. Trump made the decisions or authorized the people who did. He launched the Iran war, claimed it “obliterated” Iran’s nuclear program, and then carried on as if intelligence reports were disposable inconveniences. He also keeps pressing the fiction that the 2020 election was stolen, because that lie remains politically useful to him.

The Lie That Governs

The election fraud fantasy is not a mistake in the system. It is part of the system now. According to the source, Trump is willing to sacrifice national security to get a voting-security law built around an event that did not happen. That is not confusion. That is governance through false premises. When a president turns an invented grievance into policy leverage, the lie stops being rhetoric and becomes administrative sabotage.

War, Then Revisionism

The Iran episode shows the same pattern in sharper form. Trump used America’s military power, alongside Israel’s, for a stated purpose that even his critics could recognize as defensible: stopping a genocidal regime from advancing its weapons program. Then he undercut that purpose with his own carelessness, including the absurd claim that the program had already been “obliterated” months earlier. The firing of the Defense Department intelligence chief after a contrary report says the quiet part out loud: truth is tolerated only until it interferes with the president’s narrative.

Cowardice in Uniform

The article also points to a more familiar form of institutional decay: adults in the room who are present mostly to be overruled. A vital anti-terrorism law expired while Trump toyed with nominating Bill Pulte for director of national intelligence, a move the source rightly treats as know-nothing theater. Todd Blanche, meanwhile, is described by National Review as everything Trump wants in an attorney general, which is precisely the problem. An attorney general should not be an instrument of presidential revenge. Under Trump, that is the job description.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple. Trump does not merely misstate reality; he weaponizes it. He rewards loyalty over competence, grievance over evidence, and prosecution over law. The conservative outrage in the source is itself revealing: it still treats Trump as a breach in the system rather than a stress test exposing how easily the system bends when power is concentrated in the hands of a man who regards facts as obstacles and institutions as props.
