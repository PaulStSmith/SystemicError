Trump Turns ICE Into a Patronage Post

Power Was Never Shared

Donald Trump’s announcement that he wants Lance Schroyer to run ICE is the story in miniature. Susie Wiles, Stephen Miller, and Tom Homan were all reportedly blindsided, which is supposed to sound chaotic. It is not. It is the operating system. Trump makes the call, loyalists scramble to explain it, and the rest of the administration is left pretending this is governance rather than a personality cult with a badge.

The White House even said the quiet part out loud: the president is the decision maker. Correct. That is the problem, not the defense.

The Real Broker Was Markwayne Mullin

The useful detail here is not that Trump made a surprise nomination. It is how Schroyer got surfaced at all. Sen. Markwayne Mullin, a Trump ally and Schroyer’s friend, allegedly got the meeting by showing up at the White House while Wiles was away. That is not merit selection. That is access politics in its purest form, where proximity to power matters more than competence, record, or public consequence.

This is how the right governs now. Not through institutions with standards, but through personal favors, friendships, and whoever can get a weekend audience.

Experience Is Decorative

The pitch for Schroyer is laughably thin. He has law enforcement experience in Oklahoma and participation in a 287(g) agreement, which is not the same thing as running ICE. It is not even close. Yet Senate Republicans are treating this as sufficient, as if the agency responsible for mass detention and deportation can be handed over like a county committee appointment.

That is the political logic of authoritarian drift. The job is not to manage a complex institution responsibly. The job is to make sure the right people are feared, targeted, and obeyed. Expertise becomes optional once cruelty is the point.

Misdirection Is Part of the Job

The coverage tries to frame this as a surprise, a revolt, a behind-the-scenes mess. That is too soft. The real story is not confusion. It is deliberate disorder serving a deliberate outcome.

Look at the excuses already being circulated. Rick Scott says Schroyer “seemed like a smart guy.” Bernie Moreno waves away inexperience by comparing a Senate seat to running a federal immigration apparatus. Those are not arguments. They are loyalty tests disguised as common sense. And the child support scandal gets folded in as if personal hypocrisy is the main issue. It is not. The issue is that Republicans are prepared to normalize obviously unserious people for serious coercive power.

ICE Cannot Be Sanitized

This is also a reminder that ICE itself is not a neutral agency waiting for the right manager. It is an enforcement machine built to separate families, terrify immigrants, and carry out state violence under administrative language. The answer is not better branding, better training, or a cleaner nominee. The answer is to abolish ICE.

Any political coalition that treats immigration enforcement as a branding problem rather than a moral and constitutional one is already conceding the terrain. You do not fix a machine designed for cruelty by finding a more polished operator.

The Pattern Is Patronage With a Badge

Trump’s ICE nomination shows the modern Republican method clearly: centralize power, reward personal loyalty, elevate amateurs who will obey, and demand that institutions absorb the embarrassment. The point is not competence. The point is domination, carried out through agencies that can punish people while pretending to administer policy.

That is why this story matters. It is not just about one embarrassing pick. It is about a governing class that treats federal power as private property, then expects everyone else to call that stability.
