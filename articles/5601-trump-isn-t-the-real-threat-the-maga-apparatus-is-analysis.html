Aging President Donald Trump with his cankles, slurred speech, drooping face and reports of a collapsing circulatory system is not the threat to democracy it once was, say a panel of New York Times columnist. The real threat, they say, is the power structure that put him in place — and could soon install another Trumpy president.

During a panel discussion, columnist David French admitted that the MAGA movement is already “wanting to have a life after Donald Trump.”

“If you look at [White House Advisor] Stephen Miller’s side of things, in many ways, what we’re seeing and reporting is demonstrating is that he’s often more ruthless than Trump’s own instincts. This is a big part of MAGA — they’re more vicious, more cruel — even than Donald Trump,” said French.

French added that the personalities behind MAGA, also unlike Trump, “have a greater degree of sophistication.”

“I always object when people call Trump dumb — but when you’re talking about political sophistication, turning around the next corner, I don’t think Trump thinks in those lines,” French told the panel. “But I think MAGA is fully aware of what it has done. They are governing as if they will never lose power, and they’re very keenly aware of that.”

While Trump is determined to pursue his debunked argument that he actually won the 2020 election, and to raid old Fulton County ballots from 2020, the shadowy figures behind the MAGA movement are looking to exploit the president’s bile for their own purposes.

“When you see a situation where Trump is raiding Fulton County, Bannon is saying, ‘Get ICE all around polling precincts,’” said French. “While Trump is waiting for that phone call from [U.S. National Intelligence Director] Tulsi Gabbard [confirming his 2020 victory], [Alabama GOP Sen.] Tommy Tuberville is saying, ‘Get rid of voting machines.’ You’ve got this environment where MAGA is very focused on the midterms because they’ve been governing like they’re never going to lose power.”

There is a cohort inside the administration that “is not backward-looking the way Trump is,” argued French. “It’s very forward-looking. So, I’m very concerned about the MAGA apparatus. I’m less concerned about Trump, the puppet master. … the Steve Bannons, the Stephen Millers, they have a generational project and I don’t think that they want to see their generational project go up in flames after two years, after 2024.”

As example, French noted MAGA’s recent attacks on mail-in voting after Trump noticed Democrats overtaking Republican in-person voters as mail in ballots get counted.

“Watch for this: After Election Day, in closely contested races where Republicans are ahead, watch for an enormous effort made to call into question the legitimacy of all counting going forward after that,” French predicted.

Our Analysis:

The Real Threat Behind the Curtain: A Closer Look at MAGA’s Power Play

In a recent panel discussion covered by a group of New York Times columnists, a grim picture is painted, not merely of former President Donald Trump’s physical and possibly mental decline but of the more insidious forces at play behind him. The focus of their concern isn’t Trump’s “cankles, slurred speech, drooping face and reports of a collapsing circulatory system” but the power structure that not only elevated him to the presidency but is also poised to perpetuate its agenda, with or without Trump at the helm.

The MAGA Machination: Sophisticated and Ruthless

David French, a columnist on the panel, pulls no punches in highlighting the transition within the MAGA movement—a shift towards a more sophisticated and ruthless approach than Trump’s own sometimes chaotic strategies. The MAGA movement, according to French, “is fully aware of what it has done,” operating under the belief that they are invincible, a sentiment that is as dangerous as it is delusional.

The mention of Stephen Miller as “often more ruthless than Trump’s own instincts” is particularly alarming. The implication here is stark: the individuals now steering the MAGA ship are not only more calculated in their approach but are willing to go to greater lengths to achieve their ends.

Beyond Trump: The Shadowy Figures Looming

The article delves into the activities and statements of key figures within the MAGA circle, like Steve Bannon and Tommy Tuberville, whose actions and words suggest a concerted effort to undermine the very foundations of democracy. From “raiding Fulton County” for ballots to advocating for the removal of voting machines, these maneuvers are not just about maintaining power but reshaping the rules of the game in their favor.

French’s point that the MAGA cohort is “very forward-looking” is a stark reminder that what we’re witnessing isn’t merely the last gasps of a fading administration but a “generational project” with long-term ambitions. This isn’t just about the next election cycle; it’s about fundamentally altering the political landscape in a way that could have repercussions for decades.

The Attack on Mail-In Voting: A Case Study in Manipulation

The strategic attacks on mail-in voting serve as a case study in the MAGA movement’s broader strategy. By casting doubt on the legitimacy of mail-in votes, which have been crucial in enabling broader electoral participation, the MAGA apparatus is not just attacking a voting method. It’s attacking the very principle of accessible, inclusive democracy.

French’s warning about the post-Election Day strategy in closely contested races is chillingly prescient. It’s a playbook we’ve seen hints of before, but the suggestion here is that what lies ahead could be a more concerted, more sophisticated effort to delegitimize electoral outcomes that don’t favor them.

Conclusion: The Threat is Real and Present

What the New York Times panel discussion lays bare is that focusing solely on Trump’s physical or mental state misses the forest for the trees. The real danger is the power structure and the ideologically driven actors behind Trump who are prepared to continue their assault on democratic norms and institutions with or without him.

This isn’t just about one man’s ambition or frailties; it’s about a movement that’s playing the long game. For those committed to the principles of democracy and the rule of law, the message is clear: the fight isn’t over. It’s evolving, and the stakes couldn’t be higher.
