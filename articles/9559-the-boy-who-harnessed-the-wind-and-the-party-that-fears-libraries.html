The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind — and the Party That Fears Libraries

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Library as a ThreatKnowledge vs. ControlThe Boy and the WindBurning the FutureLibraries Are Civilization’s BatteryThe Windmill and the BookThe Real Power Grid

In a small Malawian village, a teenager once scavenged a junkyard, flipped through discarded science books in a local library, and built a windmill that brought light where there had been none. His name doesn’t matter for the point of this story — because it’s not just about one boy, but about what he represents: the transformative power of knowledge freely shared, freely accessed, and freely used.

The windmill turned. The lights flickered on. And somewhere across the ocean, the Republican Party quietly doubled down on its war against libraries.

The Library as a Threat

Let’s be clear: libraries are not under attack because they’re inefficient. They’re under attack because they work. Because they embody the most dangerous idea imaginable in a society built on hierarchy — the idea that knowledge belongs to everyone.

To authoritarians, whether draped in the flag or clutching a Bible, a library is an arsenal of democracy. Every shelf is lined with weapons: books that dismantle propaganda, ideas that expose inequality, histories that refuse to stay buried. A teenager in Malawi used them to power his village; an American teenager might use them to question capitalism, racism, or the suffocating myth of American exceptionalism.

That, right there, is what the GOP cannot tolerate.

Knowledge vs. Control

Conservatism in its current form has abandoned the pretense of governing. It doesn’t build, it bans. It doesn’t educate, it eradicates. From Florida to Texas, Republican legislators have made it their mission to strip libraries of the very thing that makes them sacred: access to ideas without permission.

Banned books lists are metastasizing like tumors. Librarians are harassed, threatened, and fired for defending the right to read. Entire library systems are being defunded because they refuse to comply with ideological purges. In Missouri, lawmakers proposed bills to jail librarians for carrying materials that offend conservative sensibilities. In Texas, school boards have pulled books on race, gender, and even the Holocaust. This is not coincidence — it’s a coordinated campaign of ignorance.

Why? Because access to knowledge makes obedience harder. Because a child who reads learns to imagine. And imagination is fatal to authoritarianism.

The Boy and the Wind

Contrast that with the Malawian teenager who scoured junkyards for scraps and libraries for diagrams. With no corporate grants, no consultants, no government subsidy, he built a windmill that powered his entire village. He didn’t need permission. He needed a library card.

That boy did more for his community with a pile of trash and a handful of borrowed books than most politicians do in a lifetime of taxpayer-funded grandstanding. And that’s precisely what terrifies them: the idea that ordinary people, armed with knowledge, can build something better without waiting for permission.

He represents the power of what the GOP fears most — not socialism, not science, not secularism, but self-determination. He didn’t just light bulbs. He lit minds.

Burning the Future

Republicans wrap their book bans in the language of parental rights and moral protection, but strip away the pious wrapping and it’s the same ancient impulse that led inquisitors to torch manuscripts and Nazis to burn libraries in public squares. Control the books, and you control the memory of a people. Control memory, and you control the future.

When Florida bans books about Black history, it isn’t about shielding children from discomfort — it’s about preventing them from seeing the continuity between slavery, segregation, and the modern prison-industrial complex. When Texas censors literature on gender identity, it isn’t about decency — it’s about keeping power structures intact by erasing empathy. The less people know, the easier they are to rule.

Meanwhile, in Malawi, a boy discovered how to capture the wind. He found freedom in a few pages of physics and engineering. He learned, quite literally, how to make light from darkness.

Libraries Are Civilization’s Battery

Libraries are the great equalizer. They exist because the world once understood that access to information is as vital as food or shelter. They are the repository of human experience — the accumulated trial, error, and triumph of our species. Destroying them is not merely an act of censorship; it’s an act of civilizational sabotage.

The GOP’s assault on libraries is not about saving children. It’s about saving themselves from irrelevance. The modern Republican cannot survive in a world where people read critically, think independently, or question authority. Their ecosystem depends on ignorance, outrage, and fear — the intellectual equivalent of burning fossil fuels. Libraries, by contrast, are renewable energy for the human mind.

And like renewable energy, libraries threaten entrenched interests. They decentralize power. They teach people to build their own windmills.

The Windmill and the Book

In Malawi, the teenager’s windmill turned junk into power. In America, every banned book could do the same — turn ignorance into resistance. Imagine a generation of children who grow up reading what the GOP fears: Baldwin, Morrison, Atwood, Angelou, Ibram X. Kendi. Imagine what they might build if we just left the shelves alone.

The boy who harnessed the wind didn’t ask anyone’s permission to dream. He just did it. That’s the moral of the story the GOP cannot stand. Because if one kid with a library card and a pile of garbage can change the course of his village, what could a nation full of well-read citizens do to theirs?

The Real Power Grid

Electricity flows through wires; knowledge flows through minds. The first powers lights, but the second powers revolutions. And that, ladies and gentlemen, is precisely why the GOP wants to burn all the libraries.

Because a lit bulb illuminates a room. But a lit mind? That sets the whole damn world on fire.
