Clinton spokesperson curses in reply to Nancy Mace’s ‘unhinged’ and ‘screaming’ claims

Representatives Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Nancy Mace (R-SC) attend President Donald Trump’s State of the Union address in the House Chamber of the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C. on Feb. 24, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton‘s spokesperson had a sharp response Friday after Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) tried to claim that the former First lady was “unhinged” and “screaming” during her testimony before the House Oversight Committee.

During a press conference Friday, just before Clinton’s husband and former President Bill Clinton was set to testify before the committee about his relationship with late financier and convicted child sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, Mace made several claims about Clinton’s reactions to the lawmakers’ questions on Thursday. The former First lady was asked a series of questions — including some related to the Pizzagate conspiracy theory and aliens — during a closed-door deposition in Chappaqua, New York.

Her spokesperson Nick Merrill told CNN that Mace was lying about what she told reporters and that wasn’t what happened.

“Clearly, the sparks have been flying,” said MJ Lee, CNN senior national enterprise correspondent.

“The political theater has been on full display as it pertains to Hillary Clinton’s deposition, including Congresswoman Nancy Mace this morning saying that Hillary Clinton had been screaming and was unhinged as a part of the deposition yesterday,” Lee said. “When I asked the Hillary Clinton spokesperson, Nick Merrill, about this. This is what he said. He said ‘she is full of s---.’ This is talking about Congresswoman Mace and that Hillary Clinton was actually just appalled that the congresswoman wouldn’t let the former secretary of state answer a question about 9/11. Obviously, this is all completely out of context, and we’re not going to know the context until we get the transcript and the full videos of the deposition.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Political Theater: The Clinton-Mace Exchange

In the latest episode of what can only be described as the grand circus of American politics, we’ve witnessed an intriguing clash between former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton and Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC). The spectacle unfolded against the backdrop of a House Oversight Committee hearing, further spiced by the imminent testimony of former President Bill Clinton regarding his connections with the disgraced Jeffrey Epstein.

Mace’s Claims and Clinton’s Counter

Rep. Nancy Mace’s assertions that Hillary Clinton was “unhinged” and “screaming” during her testimony are, to put it mildly, bold. Such descriptions conjure images of a meltdown under the harsh lights of political scrutiny, a narrative Mace seemed eager to propagate. The choice of words, particularly “unhinged,” is loaded with connotations of irrationality and instability—adjectives that female politicians, regrettably, find disproportionately lobbed their way.

Nick Merrill, Clinton’s spokesperson, offered a scathing rebuttal, branding Mace’s characterization as outright falsehoods. His unfiltered response, “she is full of s---,” is a testament to the high tensions and low civility that currently underscore political discourse. Merrill asserts that Clinton’s frustration stemmed not from a loss of composure but from being interrupted during her response to a question about 9/11—an emotionally charged topic for any American, let alone a former Secretary of State.

The Spectacle of Political Theater

CNN’s MJ Lee aptly describes the situation as “political theater,” a phrase that captures the essence of this saga. The inclusion of topics such as the Pizzagate conspiracy theory and aliens in Clinton’s questioning borders on the absurd. It’s a reflection of how political agendas often veer into sensationalism, overshadowing substantive issues that demand attention.

The calls for context by Lee highlight a crucial point: without the full transcript or video of the deposition, the public is left to navigate a murky sea of claims and counterclaims. The truth, as is often the case in political arenas, becomes a casualty of strategic narratives designed to manipulate public perception.

Conclusion

This episode is emblematic of the broader systemic issues plaguing American politics—the prioritization of spectacle over substance, the weaponization of gendered language, and the diminishing space for civil discourse. As we await the release of the full deposition, one can’t help but ponder the state of political accountability and the role of media in perpetuating these theatrical displays.

In the grand scheme, the Clinton-Mace confrontation is but a single act in an ongoing drama. However, it serves as a poignant reminder of the systemic errors that undermine the integrity of political processes and distract from the pressing challenges facing society.
