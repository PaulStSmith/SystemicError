'Backfired': GOP lobbyist warns Trump did himself no favors with post

Trump wrote in a post on his Truth Social platform that his mass crackdown on illegal immigration led to significant labor shortages for farmers and the hospitality industry, sectors heavily reliant on migrant workers.

"Our great Farmers and people in the Hotel and Leisure business have been stating that our very aggressive policy on immigration is taking very good, long time workers away from them, with those jobs being almost impossible to replace. In many cases the Criminals allowed into our Country by the VERY Stupid Biden Open Borders Policy are applying for those jobs. This is not good. We must protect our Farmers, but get the CRIMINALS OUT OF THE USA. Changes are coming!" he wrote.

Trump also said people who have worked on a farm for "20, 25 years" shouldn't be forced to leave the country.

As of Friday, there have been no policy changes or exemptions enacted for farm workers. A White House official told The Washington Post that Trump "sought to soothe industry leaders" in those fields, but that there were "no plans" to alter their crackdown plans.

Tom Homan, President Trump's chief of immigration enforcement, has said there are no plans to change current immigration policies to exempt farm, hotel, or other leisure workers.

A Republican lobbyist told Notus on Friday the post was ill-advised. If it was intended to smooth over any anxieties, they said, it "backfired."

"We still have a worker shortage. How are you going to solve that? And I don't know if that tweet made people feel like they were going to solve it anytime sooner," the lobbyist added.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Immigration Paradox: A Rhetorical Maze Without an Exit

In what can only be described as a bewildering act of political contortion, President Donald Trump's recent social media proclamation has left both critics and supporters scratching their heads. The irony is as thick as the walls he once promised to build. In a Truth Social post, Trump lamented the labor shortages faced by farmers and the hospitality industry due to his own draconian immigration policies, promising "changes are coming." Yet, this admission is as hollow as it is hypocritical, given the lack of any concrete policy shift to address the self-inflicted wound.

The Conundrum of Trump's Own Making

Trump's post acknowledges a reality that experts and industry leaders have screamed into the void for years: aggressive immigration policies have dire economic consequences. His administration's mass deportation plans have not only torn families apart but have also left critical sectors of the American economy gasping for air. The President's sudden epiphany that his "very aggressive policy on immigration" is harming the very backbone of America's food and leisure sectors is a testament to either a profound disconnect from the outcomes of his policies or a belated realization wrapped in political convenience.

The Fiction of the "Very Stupid Biden Open Borders Policy"

Trump's attempt to juxtapose his administration's policies against the so-called "VERY Stupid Biden Open Borders Policy" is a masterclass in misinformation. This claim does not just stretch the truth; it obliterates it. The Biden administration has not enacted an open borders policy. This rhetorical flourish is nothing more than a scare tactic, a boogeyman conjured up to distract from the real and severe impacts of Trump's immigration crackdown.

The Hollow Promise of Protection

Trump's promise to protect farmers while simultaneously vowing to "get the CRIMINALS OUT OF THE USA" is a vacuous pledge that ignores the complexity of immigration issues. The post fails to acknowledge the vital contribution of immigrant workers, many of whom have lived and worked in the country for decades, to the U.S. economy. Instead, it perpetuates a dangerous narrative that conflates immigration with criminality, a baseless stereotype that has been debunked time and again.

The Reality: No Plan, No Relief

Despite the President's assurance of impending changes, key figures within his administration have been quick to clarify that no such plans are in the works. Tom Homan, Trump's chief of immigration enforcement, confirmed the absence of any policy shift, leaving industries reliant on immigrant labor in a state of continued uncertainty. The Republican lobbyist's remarks to Notus underscore the post's failure to reassure or provide any semblance of a solution to the labor shortage crisis. It's a classic case of Trump's rhetoric writing checks his administration can't cash.

Conclusion: A Maze Without an Exit

Trump's social media post is emblematic of his administration's broader approach to governance: create chaos, acknowledge chaos without taking responsibility, and then promise solutions with no intention of follow-through. His acknowledgment of the labor shortages caused by his immigration policies, paired with an absence of actionable plans to mitigate these issues, leaves industries and workers stranded in a rhetorical maze with no exit in sight. As the President's post backfires, it serves as a stark reminder of the dissonance between Trump's promises and his administration's ability—or willingness—to deliver meaningful policy changes.
