Medicare Fraud Isn’t Coming from the Border — It’s Coming from the Boardroom

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real Face of Healthcare FraudThe Immigrant Blame GameThe Machinery of DistractionJustice and AccountabilityThe Bottom Line

In the ever-churning machinery of American scapegoating, there’s a well-worn gear that politicians and media demagogues spin with disturbing ease: the myth that immigrants are the main culprits behind Medicare and Medicaid fraud. It’s a story designed to inflame, to distract, and most of all, to protect the real perpetrators. But every now and then, reality kicks in the door.

Meet Gary Cox. Or rather, meet the $1 billion Medicare fraud he orchestrated.

Cox, the 79-year-old CEO of a healthcare software company known as Power Mobility Doctor Rx (DMERx), was convicted on multiple federal charges including conspiracy to commit healthcare fraud, wire fraud, receiving kickbacks, and defrauding the U.S. government. The scheme? A sprawling operation to churn out fake medical equipment claims through illegal kickbacks and fraudulent prescriptions routed through telemedicine scams.

The total billed to Medicare? Over $1 billion. The amount actually paid by Medicare before the scheme was uncovered? Over $360 million.

And the kicker? Cox faces up to 60 years in federal prison. Not because he crossed a border. Not because he used a fake ID to get benefits. But because he wore a suit, sat in an office, and siphoned taxpayer money with boardroom precision.

The Real Face of Healthcare Fraud

Let’s be clear: fraud in federal healthcare programs is a serious problem. According to estimates from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), improper payments for Medicare and Medicaid can top $60 billion annually. And yes, some of that is fraud. But the overwhelming bulk of it is not committed by poor people gaming the system or immigrants sneaking across the border. It’s committed by insiders.

Doctors. Pharmacists. Medical equipment companies. Software firms. Executives. Corporate healthcare providers.

In other words: the very people inside the system, the ones who understand how to exploit billing codes, reimbursement pathways, and the trust of patients.

Cases like Gary Cox’s are not aberrations. They are emblematic. In 2023 alone, the Department of Justice prosecuted dozens of high-dollar healthcare fraud cases involving telemedicine scams, fraudulent genetic testing, fake hospice services, and more. The sums involved regularly stretch into the hundreds of millions.

The Immigrant Blame Game

So where do immigrants fit into all this? Conveniently, they don’t. That is, unless you’re trying to construct a political narrative divorced from reality.

Undocumented immigrants are ineligible for Medicare and have only extremely limited access to Medicaid — typically restricted to emergency services. Most documented immigrants face waiting periods and strict income qualifications. Even then, immigrants are often less likely to use public health services than native-born citizens.

One study published in Health Affairs found that immigrants, including undocumented ones, accounted for only 8.6% of total U.S. healthcare spending, despite making up over 13% of the population. Another analysis showed undocumented immigrants accounted for just 1.5% of national healthcare expenditures.

And yet, somehow, in the MAGA ecosystem of lies, immigrants are portrayed as the primary leeches on the healthcare system.

Let’s call that what it is: a racist smokescreen. A distraction strategy.

Because if you’re busy blaming an underpaid immigrant picking vegetables in California, you’re not looking at the CEO in Florida stealing $1 billion from your taxes.

The Machinery of Distraction

The American right has always been skilled at constructing myths to justify austerity and social cruelty. The “welfare queen.” The “illegal alien clogging the ER.” These are not new tropes — they’re recycled bigotry dressed in policy concerns.

But these lies have a function.

They divert attention from the actual thieves: the corporations and executives who defraud Medicare not to survive, but to fund their second yacht.

Meanwhile, right-wing politicians refuse to raise taxes on the rich, slash oversight funding for Medicare fraud enforcement, and then cry crocodile tears about the deficit while immigrants get demonized for using an emergency room.

It’s grotesque. And it’s deliberate.

Justice and Accountability

Gary Cox is facing a potential 60-year sentence. That’s justice in motion, but it’s rare. Most white-collar healthcare fraudsters either get minimal sentences, plea deals, or settlements that amount to a slap on the wrist.

We need systemic change. That means:

  • Fully funding fraud investigation units at CMS and DOJ.

  • Increasing oversight of telehealth, medical equipment billing, and kickback arrangements.

  • Cracking down on corporate repeat offenders with lifetime bans and clawbacks.

  • And yes, calling out the racist scapegoating of immigrants for what it is: a cruel diversion.

The Bottom Line

Next time a politician or pundit tells you that immigrants are the real problem draining our healthcare system, remember Gary Cox. Remember the $1 billion. Remember the suits and boardrooms and backdoor deals.

Medicare isn’t being drained by those trying to survive. It’s being looted by those who are already thriving.

And until we stop letting the criminals write the narrative, they’ll keep walking away with the bag.
