'Quite interesting': CNN reporter taken aback by Trump's remarks on Ukraine ceasefire

A CNN reporter appeared taken aback by President Donald Trump's eyebrow-raising remarks Monday afternoon during a summit with European leaders, in which he made remarks about a possible ceasefire in Ukraine, then shifted to a peace agreement.

Trump met with a delegation of European leaders and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy at the White House on Monday to discuss ending Russia’s ongoing invasion. The summit comes after Trump’s recent face-to-face meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Anchorage, Alaska, where discussions ended with no peace deal. European leaders included those from Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Italy, Finland, the European Commission, and NATO.

After a public roundtable where the leaders each expressed their desires, CNN's Kristen Holmes reacted to Trump's comments specifically.

"Those comments about a ceasefire were really interesting, particularly given that he seemed to walk away from the idea of a ceasefire after Alaska," she said. "Then he said, 'I would prefer, everyone here would prefer, a ceasefire. A ceasefire is better in some ways because it stops the killing."

She noted that Trump changed his rhetoric later in the meeting.

"But then later, after some of the leaders had talked about a ceasefire as well, he went back to this idea of a peace agreement, saying he solved other wars with just a peace agreement. So that was quite interesting there."

Holmes also highlighted Trump's remarks on land concessions and security guarantees for Ukraine.

"Now, President Trump said that there was quite a bit of things that they were going to have up for discussion — that during these talks today it was going to include potential land concessions. It was also going to include these security guarantees."

The latter, she said, were "incredibly notable" for a couple of reasons.

"One, you heard several of the leaders there saying that they were grateful for President Trump to engage in those conversations and to potentially lend U.S. assistance. Now, this is after we heard President Trump earlier today not ruling out the idea of U.S. troops on the ground. Clearly, this is a key item when it comes to Ukraine and to the European leaders — that they want U.S. buy-in when it comes to these security guarantees."

Additionally, she said, Trump appeared to signal they could come to a resolution about such security guarantees on Monday.

"They seem to want to come out of here with a couple of big things: one, potential security guarantees, as we’ve said; but the other part of this is that trilateral meeting. We heard several of these various leaders saying that they want to leave here with this trilateral meeting, at least in the works of being set up between Trump, Putin, and Zelenskyy."

Holmes also highlighted that Trump essentially said that any peace in Ukraine was "on Zelenskyy."

"But then he added that it was also on Russian President Vladimir Putin," she said. "That is notable given that earlier today, after he came out of those conversations in Alaska and earlier on Truth Social, he was saying essentially that the ball is all in Zelenskyy’s court now on a ceasefire."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Trump's Summit Remarks - A Ballet of Inconsistencies

In a recent summit that could only be described as a geopolitical rollercoaster, President Donald Trump's comments have once again commandeered the spotlight, revealing a baffling mix of contradictions and vague promises. CNN's Kristen Holmes delivers a report that, while trying to navigate the erratic narrative offered by Trump, unveils the complexity and confusion inherent in his approach to the Ukraine crisis.

Ceasefire vs. Peace Agreement: A Flip-Flopping Stance

At the heart of Trump's commentary lies a bewildering flip-flop between advocating for a ceasefire in Ukraine and pushing for a full-blown peace agreement. Initially, Trump's remarks seemed to lean towards a ceasefire, an approach that, while temporary, could at least halt the ongoing violence. However, as Holmes reports, Trump's rhetoric took a sharp turn back to the idea of a peace agreement, citing his past "successes" in resolving wars through such means. This pivot raises eyebrows, not least because of its timing - coming right after his fruitless discussions with Russian President Vladimir Putin and in the midst of a summit that demanded clear, decisive leadership.

The Promise of Land Concessions and Security Guarantees

Even more perplexing are Trump's comments on potential land concessions and security guarantees for Ukraine. Holmes points out the significance of these remarks, emphasizing the desperate need for U.S. support among European leaders and Ukraine. The mention of U.S. troops on the ground, while not definitively promised, dangles the possibility of direct military involvement, a commitment that would mark a significant escalation in U.S. engagement in the region.

Yet, despite the gravity of these discussions, Trump's assurance that these security guarantees could be resolved on the day of the summit feels overly optimistic, if not naive. The complexity of the conflict, entangled with the interests of powerful nations and the future of Ukraine's sovereignty, cannot be untangled with mere promises of support or vague discussions of land concessions.

The Burden of Peace: Placed on Zelenskyy and Putin

Perhaps the most striking, and indeed troubling, aspect of Trump's comments is the shifting of responsibility for peace onto the shoulders of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Putin. While it's true that these leaders play crucial roles in any potential resolution, Trump's framing suggests a hands-off approach, as if the success or failure of peace efforts rests solely with them. This stance not only undermines the influence and responsibility of the U.S. and its European allies in facilitating a peaceful resolution but also simplifies the complex dynamics at play.

Conclusion: Navigating a Labyrinth of Rhetoric

As Holmes's report highlights, Trump's comments during the summit serve as a microcosm of his broader approach to foreign policy: unpredictable, contradictory, and often lacking in substantive commitment. While the promise of U.S. support in the form of security guarantees and potential land concessions offers a glimmer of hope, the lack of clear direction and the shifting of responsibility away from the U.S. raise significant concerns.

In navigating the labyrinth of Trump's rhetoric, one thing becomes clear: the path to peace in Ukraine remains as convoluted and uncertain as ever. The leaders of the involved nations, along with their global partners, must cut through the noise and work towards tangible, realistic solutions. The stakes are too high, and the Ukrainian people have suffered too much, for anything less.
