Trump signals 'not fathomable' plan to topple South American regime: expert

President Donald Trump’s latest moves in South America show that he’s after “regime change,” according to one analyst.

On Sunday, the Trump administration struck a seventh boat with alleged ties to drug smuggling. The strike was conducted off the coast of Colombia and killed three people, according to reports. After the strike, Trump escalated his feud with Colombian President Gustavo Petro by posting a lengthy message on Truth Social announcing that he was cutting off foreign aid to the country.

“The purpose of this drug production is the sale of massive amounts of product into the United States, causing death, destruction, and havoc,” Trump posted. “Petro, a low rated and very unpopular leader, with a fresh mouth toward America, better close up these killing fields immediately, or the United States will close them up for him, and it won’t be done nicely.”

Sergio Guzmán, who heads Colombia Risk Analysis, a consultancy that studies U.S.-Colombia policy, told the Wall Street Journal that Trump’s actions appear to have a higher purpose.

“The U.S. is pushing the boundary of international law,” Guzmán told the outlet. “Essentially, the U.S. has now cut all of its foreign assistance to Colombia and it is also looking for regime change in Venezuela—two things that eight months ago or 10 months ago were not fathomable.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s South American Gambit: A Closer Look

In an escalation that could only be described as bold and recklessly aggressive, President Donald Trump has once again thrust the United States into the spotlight with his latest maneuvers in South America. According to a recent report, Trump’s administration is not just after drug smugglers; it appears to be gunning for regime change.

The Strike and Its Aftermath

The administration’s decision to strike a seventh boat allegedly tied to drug smuggling off the coast of Colombia, resulting in three fatalities, is a stark reminder of the lengths to which Trump is willing to go. The subsequent escalation of his feud with Colombian President Gustavo Petro, manifested through a venomous post on Truth Social, only adds fuel to the fire. Trump’s decision to cut off foreign aid to Colombia, as declared in his post, is not only punitive but also a clear indication of his disdain for Petro’s leadership and policies. Trump’s rhetoric, especially his characterization of Petro as a “low rated and very unpopular leader,” is not just undiplomatic but also a gross oversimplification of complex geopolitical dynamics.

The Bigger Picture

Sergio Guzmán’s observations to the Wall Street Journal that the United States is “pushing the boundary of international law” and seeking regime change not only in Colombia but also in Venezuela, highlight a broader and more alarming agenda. The abrupt cessation of all U.S. foreign assistance to Colombia, coupled with the aggressive posturing towards Venezuela, suggests a significant shift in U.S. policy in the region. This shift, according to Guzmán, was unimaginable just a few months ago, underscoring the unpredictability and volatility of the current administration’s foreign policy.

Analysis

The Implications of Trump’s Tactics

Trump’s actions raise several red flags regarding international law and the ethics of foreign intervention. The unilateral decision to strike a vessel in another country’s waters, presumably without conclusive evidence or a trial, is a dangerous precedent. It not only undermines the sovereignty of nations but also raises questions about the criteria and evidence used to justify such actions.

Cutting Off Aid: A Counterproductive Move

The cessation of foreign aid to Colombia is both punitive and shortsighted. Foreign aid, especially in regions plagued by drug production and trafficking, is not merely a tool of diplomacy but a critical component of broader strategies to combat these issues. By cutting off aid, the U.S. is not only abandoning its commitment to helping tackle the root causes of drug production and trafficking but also potentially exacerbating the very problems it aims to solve.

The Quest for Regime Change

Trump’s apparent desire for regime change in both Colombia and Venezuela is a troubling development. Regime change, especially when pursued through aggressive and unilateral actions, rarely achieves its intended outcomes. More often than not, it leads to instability, conflict, and human suffering. Furthermore, the pursuit of regime change in these countries represents a significant departure from the principles of sovereign equality and non-intervention that are supposed to underpin international relations.

Conclusion

President Trump’s latest moves in South America are not just a flexing of military muscle; they signal a dangerous pivot towards unilateral aggression and regime change. Such actions not only endanger the fragile balance of international relations but also risk plunging the region into further turmoil. The implications of these strategies, both for the United States’ standing in the world and for the people of Colombia and Venezuela, are profound and deeply concerning. It is imperative for the international community to critically examine and respond to these developments, lest we witness the unraveling of decades of diplomatic progress in the region.
