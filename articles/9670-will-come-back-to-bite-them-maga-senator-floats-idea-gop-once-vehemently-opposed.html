'Will come back to bite them': MAGA senator floats idea GOP once vehemently opposed

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 11, 20255 min read

Despite calls in 2024 for Senate Democrats to finally end the filibuster, it never happened. And now MAGA Republican Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) is floating the idea to do just that despite Republican cries of outrage when Democrats were considering it, reports MSNBC.

The Ohio senator suggested in an interview on Fox News that Republicans should get rid of the 60-vote threshold to end the shutdown, saying, “Let’s make this a Republican-only vote.”

Moreno, MSNBC says, complained that the filibuster was allowing Democrats to “hold us hostage” indefinitely.

“My point of view would be this: We have almost all Republicans on board,” Moreno told host Laura Ingraham. “Maybe it’s time to think about the filibuster. You say look, the Democrats would have done it. Let’s just vote with Republicans. We got 52 Republicans. Let’s go. And let’s open the government. It may get to that.”

Moreno also made false claims on Ingraham’s show about Democrats’ demands for reopening the government, claiming they include “re-funding USAID” and “abortions on demand.”

Instead, according to Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY), Democrats want any funding bill to include the extension of a set of Affordable Care Act subsidies that are due to expire at the end of the year.

As MSNBC opinion columnist Hayes Brown explains, “the GOP has benefited far more from the legislative filibuster than Democrats, as its agenda is less dependent on new legislation to enact.”

“And when Republicans are in the minority,” Hayes notes, “they’re more than happy to use the 60-vote threshold as leverage to water down Democratic legislation in the name of bipartisanship.”

But, as Politico noted Thursday, “nuking the legislative filibuster sparks unease and outright opposition with a number of GOP senators, who worry that it will come back to bite them when they are in the minority.”

Republicans, however, “are trying to have it both ways: refusing to treat Democrats’ votes as worth courting while still preserving the right to blockade future Democratic bills,” Hayes says.

As for ending the filibuster, Hayes says, “The onus now falls on Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.) to defend the institution, even as it hamstrings his party’s agenda.”

“It would probably be too much to hope that Moreno keeps this same energy should he find himself serving in the minority during his time in Washington,” Hayes adds.

On Friday afternoon, Office of Management and Budget director and Project 2025 architect Russell Vought announced “substantial” mass layoffs of federal employees.

The Hypocrisy of Political Expediency: A Closer Look at Senator Moreno’s Filibuster Flip-Flop

In a move that reeks of opportunism and stark hypocrisy, MAGA Republican Senator Bernie Moreno (R-OH) has suddenly become an advocate for ending the Senate filibuster—a mechanism his party vehemently defended when Democrats proposed its elimination. This about-face, as reported by MSNBC, is not just a testament to the fluctuating principles of political convenience but also highlights a broader issue of the filibuster’s role in American politics.

Senator Moreno, in an interview on Fox News, boldly suggested making the process to end the government shutdown a “Republican-only vote,” essentially sidelining any form of bipartisan agreement. His justification? Democrats are using the filibuster to “hold us hostage,” a claim that not only simplifies a complex issue but also conveniently ignores the numerous occasions Republicans have wielded the filibuster as a tool to block Democratic initiatives.

Moreno’s assertion, laden with false claims about Democrats’ demands for government reopening, further muddies the waters of honest debate. Contrary to his baseless allegations of Democrats pushing for “abortions on demand” and “re-funding USAID,” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) clarified that the Democratic stance is focused on extending critical Affordable Care Act subsidies.

The hypocrisy doesn’t stop at Moreno’s flip-flopping stance on the filibuster. As MSNBC opinion columnist Hayes Brown points out, the Republican party has historically benefited from the legislative filibuster, leveraging it to thwart Democratic legislation under the guise of promoting bipartisanship. Yet, the moment the filibuster becomes an obstacle to their agenda, its existence is suddenly up for debate.

This scenario is emblematic of a broader, troubling trend in American politics: the weaponization of procedural tools for partisan gain. The filibuster, originally intended to ensure a degree of consensus before passing significant legislation, has become a tool for obstruction and political brinkmanship.

Politico’s observation that ending the filibuster could spark unease among GOP senators—who fear its absence could haunt them when they are in the minority—underscores the short-sightedness of Moreno’s proposal. It’s a classic case of wanting to have one’s cake and eat it too, refusing to engage with the opposition’s proposals while preserving mechanisms to block any future initiatives they might introduce.

In an ironic twist, the responsibility to defend the integrity of the Senate and its procedural norms now falls on Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.). The challenge he faces is monumental: navigating the delicate balance between advancing his party’s agenda and preserving the institutional safeguards that prevent a descent into total partisanship.

The ultimate irony, however, lies in the fact that Senator Moreno’s newfound zeal for ending the filibuster might very well dissipate should he find himself in the minority. Such is the nature of political expediency, where principles are often sacrificed on the altar of immediate gain.

Meanwhile, the American public is left to witness the spectacle of political maneuvering that does little to address their needs or the pressing issues facing the nation. As the Office of Management and Budget announces mass layoffs of federal employees, one can’t help but question: Is this truly the governance we deserve?

In conclusion, Senator Moreno’s proposition to end the filibuster is not just a matter of political strategy; it’s a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic institutions in the face of partisan politics. It highlights the urgent need for a return to principled debate and genuine bipartisanship, lest we find ourselves trapped in an endless cycle of obstruction and retaliation.
