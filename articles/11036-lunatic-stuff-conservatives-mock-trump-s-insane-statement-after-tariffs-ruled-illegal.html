'Lunatic stuff': Conservatives mock Trump's 'insane statement' after tariffs ruled illegal

U.S. President Donald Trump talks to members of the press at Lehigh Valley International Airport in Allentown, Pennsylvania, U.S., August 3, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

Shortly after the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit ruled that the bulk of President Donald Trump's tariffs were unconstitutional, the president posted a lengthy rant on his social media platform slamming the decision and the judges. Some conservative and libertarian commentators responded to his statement with mockery.

On Friday, after the 7-4 decision was made public, Trump tore into the "highly partisan" judges who upheld a lower court ruling striking down most of his "Liberation Day" tariffs announced in April. He insisted that his unilaterally imposed import duties were both lawful and necessary for the safety of the country, and bemoaned the "enormous Trade Deficits" used to justify his tariffs.

"If allowed to stand, this Decision would literally destroy the United States of America," the president wrote.

"If Trump can’t impose illegal tariffs, he says, it will 'literally destroy the United States of America.' This is lunatic stuff," wrote Ramesh Ponnuru, who is the editor of the conservative National Review.

"Just can't stop laughing at this insane statement," tweeted investor Mayank Seksaria, of Liberty Mutual Investments.

Roger Hunt, who is a doctoral student at Bentley University, responded to Trump's statement by writing: "I'm MAGA, but this isn't how tariffs work." Reason magazine editor at large Nick Gillespie opined that Trump was "acting like a king — King George III" and celebrated the ruling by proclaiming: "No taxation without representation!" And Patrick Jaicomo, who is a civil rights litigator at the libertarian-leaning Institute for Justice, simply posted a screenshot of Article I, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution (which states that the power to impose tariffs exclusively belongs to Congress).

"Y'know what, I'm sick and tired of this huge trade deficit I've got with my local game store," wrote Ethan Blevins, who is a legal scholar at the conservative Pacific Legal Foundation. "I keep buying all their cards, and they never buy anything from me! Well, no longer. I'm going to go in there and tell them to pay me for legal advice they don't need!"

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tantrum: A Delusional Diatribe

In a spectacle that can only be characterized as both bizarre and deeply troubling, former President Donald Trump's response to a legal rebuke of his tariff policies plunges us into a new depth of political absurdity. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit recently handed down a 7-4 decision, striking a blow to what can only be described as Trump's constitutional creativity with his "Liberation Day" tariffs. Yet, the aftermath of this decision has been nothing short of a circus, with Trump at its center, juggling his own brand of legal interpretation and economic policy.

Trump's reaction on his social media platform was, predictably, a verbose rant lambasting the "highly partisan" judges. His assertion that the decision would "literally destroy the United States of America" borders on apocalyptic hyperbole. One cannot help but marvel at the sheer extremity of this claim. If Trump's tariffs were the only bulwark against the country's demise, one might wonder how the nation managed to survive until his presidency.

Mockery from the Right

What makes this episode particularly noteworthy is the reaction from conservative and libertarian commentators, traditionally seen as allies or at least sympathetic ears to Trump's policies. Their mockery sheds light on the fractures within conservative circles regarding Trump's brand of politics.

Ramesh Ponnuru's dismissal of Trump's statement as "lunatic stuff" and Mayank Seksaria's incredulity underscore a growing weariness with Trump's alarmist and often baseless assertions. Even among his supposed base, there are clear signs of fatigue with his antics. Roger Hunt's succinct critique, "I'm MAGA, but this isn't how tariffs work," speaks volumes about the disconnect between Trump's understanding of trade policies and their reality.

A Lesson in Constitutional Law

The cherry on top of this farcical situation is Patrick Jaicomo's response, which was to simply post a screenshot of Article I, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution. This article clearly states that the power to impose tariffs rests exclusively with Congress, not the presidency. This reminder serves as a stark rebuke to Trump's monarchical aspirations, illustrating his disregard for the foundational principles of American governance.

Ethan Blevins' satirical comparison of his trade deficit with a local game store to national trade policies encapsulates the absurdity of Trump's logic. It highlights the fallacy of Trump's justification for his tariffs, using humor to underscore the lack of reciprocity in his understanding of trade dynamics.

Conclusion: A Divided Right and a Constitutional Reminder

This episode not only highlights Trump's continued estrangement from reality but also signals a potentially significant shift within conservative and libertarian ranks. The mockery from these quarters is indicative of a broader exasperation with Trump's approach to governance and his cavalier attitude toward constitutional constraints.

The court's decision, and the subsequent fallout, serves as a necessary reminder of the boundaries of presidential power and the importance of adherence to the Constitution. As the conservative and libertarian commentators have made clear, Trump's attempt to govern by fiat, imposing tariffs as if he were an unchecked monarch, is not only misguided but fundamentally antithetical to American democratic principles.

In the end, this episode is a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the Republican Party as it grapples with Trump's legacy and the future direction of conservatism. The reaction to Trump's tariff tirade suggests that there may be limits to what even his staunchest allies are willing to tolerate, signaling a potential shift away from his brand of politics. Whether this will lead to a realignment within the party or merely a temporary moment of dissent remains to be seen. But for now, it's clear that Trump's constitutional and economic fictions are wearing thin, even among his erstwhile supporters.
