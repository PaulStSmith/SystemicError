Steven Greenhut reports in Reason Magazine that the MAGA world has three go-to approaches for jettisoning their so-called principals to follow President Donald Trump — but rest assured they will follow.

“First, they claim his latest notion is part of a 3D chess game even if, by all appearances, Trump would struggle to play one-level tic-tac-toe. Second, they distract our attention: Didn’t Barack Obama do this, too? Third, they get with the program and shamelessly back whatever the president is doing,” said Greenhut. “Sometimes MAGA-supporting influencers with their own agendas and philosophies take issue with some Trump policy. But rank-and-file MAGA always follows the Dear Leader even if it means contradicting some Deeply Held Principle they espoused weeks ago.”

It’s a shocking pivot, said Greenhut, but it’s their reality. Before he invaded Iran, Trump once touted himself as the peace candidate. And JD Vance has always made the case for opposing America’s endless international interventions.

“Trump warned that Joe Biden might start World War III. White House adviser Stephen Miller posted that ‘KAMALA WILL SEND YOUR SONS TO WAR’ if she won.

But Vance about-faced to serve a man he once called “America’s Hitler,” despite his intelligence, and MAGA are truthfully “no friends of freedom and democracy,” said Greenhut.

“Their ‘peace’ stance in Ukraine aligns seamlessly with Moscow’s, as they essentially argue the best way to stop the tragic killing is for victims to lay down their arms and submit to an invader,” said Greenhut. “Trump and MAGA have even blamed Ukraine and NATO for Russian aggression and warned that U.S. support could spark the next world war. At least I can contort their position, however muddleheaded, into some anti-war narrative. But Iran? A war of choice seems more likely to spark widespread convulsions.

Greenhut notes Trump’s ever-shifting rationale for invading a Middle Eastern wasp nest with 60 million angry people and a fist on the planet’s oil supply, but he says it doesn’t matter. Trump could have accused Iran of anything or fashioned any particular pretense. MAGA will stand with him nonetheless, despite the impending chaos that will come of Trump’s invasion.

“We can expect the usual: a destabilized region, years of unforeseen and negative consequences, billions of lost American dollars, and lost lives,” said Greenhut. “The one tiny potential upside of the populist movement was its apparent reluctance to plunge the nation into foreign debacles. That’s gone now, but it shouldn’t be a surprise given that MAGA isn’t so much a political philosophy as a cult of personality.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of “MAGA’s Shift on War and Peace”

Steven Greenhut’s article in Reason Magazine paints a vivid picture of the political maneuverings and apparent hypocrisy within the MAGA (Make America Great Again) movement, particularly in relation to its stance on war and peace. Greenhut critiques the movement’s shift in principles to align with Donald Trump’s actions and rhetoric, especially concerning international interventions and conflicts.

Identifying the Holders of Institutional Power

Firstly, it is crucial to pinpoint who holds the institutional power in the context Greenhut discusses. The primary figure is Donald Trump, the former president and a leading figure within the MAGA movement. His influence over the movement’s direction and the political landscape is undeniable. Secondary to Trump are MAGA-supporting influencers and the rank-and-file MAGA supporters, who, while individually holding less power, collectively contribute to the movement’s impact on public opinion and policy.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

The decisions at the heart of Greenhut’s critique — such as the shift in stance towards international interventions and the specific example of an invasion of Iran — are attributed directly to Donald Trump and, by extension, those within his administration or campaign who propagate his views. The outcome, as described, is a significant pivot from previously stated principles of non-intervention and peace, towards supporting actions that lead to conflict.

Article’s Framing and Responsibility

Greenhut’s framing focuses squarely on the MAGA movement’s followers’ willingness to abandon previous principles to stay in line with Trump’s evolving positions. This is not a misdirection of responsibility; it accurately places the spotlight on the individuals and groups who choose to shift their stances based on Trump’s directives, highlighting the cult of personality around Trump and its effects on rational policy discourse.

However, it is essential to differentiate between the influencers within the MAGA movement, who may have their own agendas but choose to align with Trump for various reasons, and the rank-and-file supporters, who may be acting out of loyalty to Trump rather than a nuanced understanding of the issues at hand. The responsibility for the decisions made — such as the hypothetical invasion of Iran — lies primarily with Trump and his administration, who have the actual power to enact such policies. The MAGA movement’s supporters, while influential in shaping public opinion, do not hold institutional power to make these decisions but are instrumental in creating a supportive environment for them.

Criticism and Misplaced Blame

Greenhut’s article does not fall into the trap of misplaced blame. It targets those who are indeed responsible for the shift in rhetoric and policy within the MAGA movement — its leadership and its most vocal supporters. This is not to say that every MAGA supporter blindly follows Trump’s shifts in policy; however, the critique is aimed at a significant enough portion of the movement to warrant attention.

Conclusion

Steven Greenhut’s analysis in Reason Magazine effectively identifies the actors and decisions behind the MAGA movement’s apparent abandonment of non-interventionist principles. It rightly places the responsibility on Donald Trump and, by extension, those who amplify and support his changing stances. While it’s vital to recognize the complexity within any political movement, Greenhut’s critique offers a necessary examination of how principles can be sacrificed at the altar of political loyalty, and the dangers of a cult of personality in political discourse.
