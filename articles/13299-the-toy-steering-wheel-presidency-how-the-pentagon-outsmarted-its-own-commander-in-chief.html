The Toy Steering Wheel Presidency: How the Pentagon Outsmarted Its Own Commander-in-Chief

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 26, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Leaker-in-ChiefThe Illusion of CommandEmpire in FreefallThe Fallout: Real and PoliticalWhat Comes Next?

In an unprecedented admission of institutional mistrust, recent reporting has confirmed what many feared but few dared to say aloud: the U.S. military deliberately fed then-President Donald Trump a fake military strike plan, fearing he would compromise real operations on social media. This is not merely a story about egos clashing in the Situation Room. This is about a democracy so brittle that the Department of Defense felt compelled to treat its own head of state like a toddler playing at war.

According to reporting from multiple sources, including Business-Standard and citing The New York Times, the Pentagon concocted a false ruse to mask the real strategy of a U.S. bomber strike over the Pacific. A visible group of B-2 stealth bombers was sent as a decoy, while the real strike force remained under wraps. The explicit motivation: prevent Donald Trump from leaking classified details online.

This was not hypothetical. It was contingency planning for a Commander-in-Chief they believed might broadcast troop movements mid-flight if it scored him social media points.

Let that sink in. The Pentagon, an institution notorious for its opacity and control-freak rigidity, found it safer to mislead the President of the United States than to trust him with real-time intelligence.

The Leaker-in-Chief

Trump's relationship with classified information has always been a dangerous cocktail of vanity and volatility. He exposed Israeli intelligence to Russian diplomats in the Oval Office. He tweeted out a high-resolution satellite image of an Iranian launchpad, baffling analysts with its clarity. He kept classified documents at Mar-a-Lago, among pool equipment and stage props. He showed off nuclear secrets to political donors like a child flashing rare Pokémon cards.

Given this history, it’s less surprising that the Pentagon feared operational compromise than that it took them so long to act on it. What’s shocking is the scale and audacity of their workaround. The military didn’t just keep him out of the loop—they gave him a fake one.

The Illusion of Command

The metaphor in the viral tweet that sparked attention is apt: the Pentagon gave Trump the equivalent of a toy steering wheel so they could drive the car without interference. But this wasn’t a child’s playpen; this was the world’s largest and deadliest military-industrial complex making up a pretend war plan to babysit the nuclear code-holder.

This calls into question not only Trump’s fitness to lead, but the very structure of American civil-military relations. If the military feels emboldened—or forced—to mislead the president to protect national security, then who is really in charge?

Democracy is supposed to rest on the principle of civilian control of the military. But if the military is running shell games to prevent the elected leader from torpedoing operational security, that principle has collapsed in all but name.

Empire in Freefall

This entire farce reads like a late-stage imperial psychodrama: the emperor sits on the throne, babbling and preening, while the generals quietly steer the war machine from the shadows. That’s not democratic governance. That’s a palace coup dressed in camouflage.

And the most damning part? It worked.

The real strike went off. The operation remained secret. Trump got to feel powerful without doing real damage—this time. But the price was steep: it normalized deceit as a tool of statecraft within the highest levels of American power.

Worse, it set a precedent. If you can deceive one president for the sake of national security, you can justify deceiving any president for any reason.

The Fallout: Real and Political

Trump, of course, denied everything. He called reports of the deception “fake news” and lashed out at the New York Times, CNN, and any other outlet daring to suggest he had fumbled a major intelligence operation. But his rage was impotent. The damage is done.

An intelligence assessment, leaked shortly after the strike, confirmed that Iran’s nuclear program was only delayed by a few months—not the obliteration Trump had claimed. The gap between Trump’s chest-beating rhetoric and the classified reality was so wide it bordered on the absurd.

Instead of reassuring the public, the Pentagon’s ruse has pulled back the curtain on just how fragile—and farcical—the U.S. chain of command had become under Trump. The emperor wasn’t just naked. He was given a cardboard crown and ushered into a padded throne while the adults in the room scrambled to keep the empire from imploding.

What Comes Next?

This story should be a wake-up call. Not just about Trump, but about the architecture of American power itself. When the military believes it must lie to the president to prevent catastrophe, the entire system has already failed.

The solution isn’t more secrecy. It’s accountability.

Trump should never again be allowed within striking distance of the nuclear football. But neither should we accept a Pentagon that reserves the right to trick its civilian leadership under the guise of national defense. That’s not stability—it’s a slow-moving coup.

The machinery of empire is rusted through. It can no longer pretend to run on consent of the governed. What we’re watching now is inertia, propped up by illusion.

And illusions, like fake strike plans and toy steering wheels, only last until reality breaks through the windshield.
