'Quite the moment': CNN shocked as FBI official exposed for offering 'zero details'

Rep. Bennie Thompson and FBI official Michael Glasheen/CNN

CNN’s Pamela Brown highlighted a moment from a Homeland Security Committee hearing where the panel’s top Democrat laid into an FBI official for his inability to provide details about the group he identified as the nation’s top violent domestic security threat.

FBI official Michael Glasheen told the committee the law enforcement agency agrees with President Donald Trump that the left-wing group posed the greatest national security threat to the U.S., but he was unable to substantiate that claim when Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS) pressed him for details.

“I’ll ask one question, sir: I just want you to tell us if you said antifa is the number one domestic terrorist organization operating in the United States,” Thompson said. “I just need to know where they are. How many people? I don’t want a name, I don’t want anything like that. Just how many people have you identified with the FBI that antifa is made of?”

Glasheen told the congressman the investigations remained active and described them as “very fluid,” and Thompson pounced.

“You wouldn’t come to this committee and say something you can’t prove,” Thompson said. “I know, you wouldn’t do that. But you did.”

Brown was stunned by the exchange, which she summed up as “quite the moment there,” and she invited former FBI deputy director Andrew McCabe to comment.

“You can’t come to the worldwide threats hearing and claim that something is the biggest threat in the United States, the biggest security threat that we face, and then provide zero details or facts about it,” McCabe said. “Now, of course, we can’t expect the FBI to talk about specific cases or identify individuals under under investigation. That’s sensitive information, but very typically the director or the representative should be able to say ‘This is what we think about them, here’s how we know about them, here’s how big we think they are, here’s the regions of the country we think they’re located in.’”

“The fact is, as any follower of and student of antifa can tell you, those things are just those facts are just not there for antifa,” he added. “Antifa is not has never been a structured formal organization with an identified leader or even an identified directive. They are a loose collection of people who exhibit the same sort of anti-establishment views and, you know, violent activity among protests.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of the “Top Threat”

In a remarkable moment of political theater, CNN’s Pamela Brown brought to light a deeply troubling exchange during a Homeland Security Committee hearing. The subject at hand? The FBI’s assertion, initially supported by President Donald Trump, that antifa represents the nation’s supreme violent domestic security threat. The reality of this claim, as laid bare in the exchange between Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS) and FBI official Michael Glasheen, is a stark reminder of the dangerous gap between political rhetoric and factual evidence.

FBI’s Claim on Thin Ice

Glasheen’s inability to provide concrete details about antifa’s membership or activities, despite labeling them as the top domestic terrorist threat, is not just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring indictment of the FBI’s approach to domestic security threats under Trump’s administration. Rep. Thompson’s interrogation revealed a fundamental flaw in Glasheen’s testimony: You can’t label an entity as the top security threat without substantial evidence to back it up. Glasheen’s description of the investigations as “very fluid” is as nebulous as it is unsatisfactory, painting a picture of an agency grasping at straws rather than presenting a well-researched threat assessment.

A Scathing Commentary from McCabe

Andrew McCabe, former FBI deputy director, provided a scathing critique of the FBI’s position, emphasizing the expectation for the agency to provide some level of detail when making such significant claims. McCabe’s remarks highlight a critical issue: the FBI’s failure to understand and accurately characterize antifa. As McCabe points out, antifa is far from a structured organization; it lacks formal leadership, a cohesive directive, and identifiable membership—characteristics typically associated with groups deemed national security threats.

Antifa: A Convenient Scapegoat?

This incident raises serious questions about the motivations behind the FBI’s focus on antifa. Is it a case of political convenience, aligning with Trump’s narrative to vilify left-wing activism? Or is it a symptom of a broader issue within the FBI, a failure to adapt to the nuanced reality of modern domestic threats? The fact remains: Labeling antifa, a decentralized movement without clear structure or leadership, as the top domestic terrorist threat is not only misleading but potentially diverts attention and resources from genuine threats.

The Real Threat to National Security

The real threat here is not just the supposed actions of a loosely affiliated movement; it’s the willingness of high-ranking officials to propagate unsubstantiated claims. When political narratives overshadow empirical evidence, the integrity of our national security apparatus is compromised. This episode serves as a stark reminder of the importance of grounding security assessments in tangible evidence rather than political expediency.

In conclusion, the exchange between Rep. Thompson and Michael Glasheen, as highlighted by Pamela Brown, underscores a troubling trend in the politicization of national security. The FBI’s stance on antifa, as critiqued by Andrew McCabe, reveals a dangerous departure from reasoned analysis, one that could have far-reaching consequences for the United States’ approach to domestic security. It’s high time for a reassessment of priorities, where factual accuracy trumps political narrative.
