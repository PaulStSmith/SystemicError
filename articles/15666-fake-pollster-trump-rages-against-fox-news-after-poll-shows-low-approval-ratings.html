A poll from Fox News released Wednesday showed that Trump's approval ratings are at a record low following the initial 100 days of his second term. While the president did not mention said poll in his statement, his remarks were an apparent reaction to the survey that showed overall approval of the president’s job performance was 44 percent — down 5 points from March.

“Rupert Murdoch has told me for years that he is going to get rid of his Fox News, Trump Hating, Fake Pollster, but he has never done so,” Trump wrote in a post on his Truth Social platform. “This ‘pollster’ has gotten me, and MAGA, wrong for years.”

According to the Fox News poll, Trump's approval rating, which stood at 45 percent after the first 100 days in 2017, was lower than the ratings of Biden (54 percent), Obama (62 percent), and George W. Bush (63 percent).

In 2020, Fox News notably became the first major news organization to announce Arizona for Joe Biden, a decision that infuriated Trump and his supporters. At 94 years old, Murdoch is the chairman emeritus of Fox Corp., the parent company of Fox News, as well as News Corp, which owns Dow Jones & Co., the publisher of the Wall Street Journal.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against Fox News: A Symphony of Irony and Inconsistency

President Donald Trump's latest outburst against Fox News over a pollster's alleged bias against him and his Make America Great Again (MAGA) movement is a rich tapestry of irony, hypocrisy, and the kind of self-victimization that has come to define his tenure and political ethos. In a recent post on his Truth Social platform, Trump lambasted Fox News and its owner, Rupert Murdoch, for harboring a so-called "Trump Hating, Fake Pollster." This diatribe was seemingly sparked by a Fox News poll that showed Trump's approval ratings hitting a record low in the initial 100 days of his second term.

The Poll That Broke the President's Back

The poll in question paints a grim picture for Trump, with his approval rating sinking to 44 percent, a 5-point drop since March. This decline in popularity is not just a number but a testament to the shifting sands under Trump's feet, a reality he seems incapable or unwilling to grasp. The fact that Trump's approval rating was lower than those of his predecessors at the same point in their presidencies – Biden (54 percent), Obama (62 percent), and George W. Bush (63 percent) – only adds salt to his self-inflicted wounds.

The Murdoch Conundrum

Trump's attack on Rupert Murdoch, a long-time media magnate and chairman emeritus of Fox Corp. and News Corp., is particularly noteworthy. Trump accuses Murdoch of failing to purge Fox News of the unnamed pollster, claiming years of misrepresentation of him and the MAGA movement. Moreover, Trump's call for Murdoch to overhaul the "China Loving Wall Street Journal" is a stunning display of Trump's expectation for media entities to serve as echo chambers for his ideology, rather than as institutions of free and fair journalism.

The Fox News and Arizona Episode

The article reminds us of the 2020 election incident where Fox News, much to Trump's dismay, was the first major news organization to call Arizona for Joe Biden. This event is a critical piece of the puzzle, illustrating Trump's volatile relationship with the truth and any entity that dares to acknowledge it. His reaction to the Arizona call and the recent poll highlights a pattern of attacking and discrediting any source that challenges his narrative or threatens his political viability.

The Bottom Line

Trump's reaction to the Fox News poll is not just about a dip in approval ratings; it's a microcosm of his broader war against the media, the truth, and anyone who refuses to pledge unwavering loyalty to him. It's a manifestation of his inability to tolerate dissent or criticism, a hallmark of his presidency and post-presidency life. The irony of Trump attacking Fox News, a network that has arguably been one of his biggest cheerleaders, cannot be overstated. It underscores the fragility of his ego and his perpetual quest for affirmation over accuracy.

In conclusion, Trump's tirade against Fox News and Rupert Murdoch over an unfavorable poll is less about the poll's methodology or findings and more about Trump's insatiable appetite for adulation and his intolerance for any narrative that does not glorify him. It's a stark reminder of the dangers of conflating leadership with sycophancy and truth with propaganda.
