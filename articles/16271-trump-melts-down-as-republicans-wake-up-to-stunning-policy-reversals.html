Trump melts down as Republicans wake up to stunning policy reversals

Trump’s Collapse Is Not Confusion. It Is Governance.

The Setup

The source story is simple: Molly Jong-Fast argues that Trump is heading into political trouble because he won by promising cheaper life and less foreign war, then delivered the opposite. Prices stay high, tariffs and trade wars bite, and the spectacle of triumphal vanity in Washington sits beside people who cannot pay for food or fuel.

Who Actually Holds Power

This is not a story about voters losing patience with an unruly politician. It is a story about the president using institutional power to do exactly what he wanted. Trump was elected after surviving indictments and convictions because his personal brand mattered more than any standard of accountability. Once in office, that same personal rule lets him steer economic pain, foreign policy, and public symbolism without having to answer to any meaningful internal restraint.

The Real Decision

The key fact is not that Trump “failed” to deliver. It is that he chose a governing style built on contradiction: promise lower costs, then push policies that raise them; promise no foreign war, then help produce one; promise ordinary relief, then perform grandeur. That is not drift. It is a decision to treat campaign vows as disposable branding.

Where the Blame Gets Smuggled

The soft framing here is the familiar political trick: turn deliberate harm into a weather report. Gas prices, wheat prices, beef prices, tariffs, trade wars, the war with Iran, and food-bank lines are presented as forces that simply happened to Trump. They did not. They are consequences of decisions made by the man in charge. The language of “meltdown” risks hiding the more useful truth: he is not being overwhelmed by events; he is producing them.

The Party’s Problem

Jong-Fast is right that there is a price point where this becomes toxic for Republicans. That does not make the damage accidental. It means the party built itself around a leader whose immunity depended on charisma, grievance, and a permissive media ecosystem that treated each violation as another day in the circus. The bill arrives only when material reality breaks through the performance.

Systemic Error

The larger pattern is not just Trump’s dishonesty. It is a political order that rewards promise-breaking as long as the delivery mechanism is theatrical enough and the costs fall somewhere else. Personalist rule thrives when institutions refuse to name responsibility plainly. Then, when the pain becomes impossible to disguise, everyone acts surprised by the consequences they helped authorize.
