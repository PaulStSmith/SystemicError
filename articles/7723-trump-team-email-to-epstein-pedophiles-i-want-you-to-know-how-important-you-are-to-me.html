An email account belonging to Jeffrey Epstein regularly received alerts from Donald Trump’s 2020 campaign and, after Epstein’s death, was addressed under a shocking new name, newly released records show.

“I need you right now, Pedophiles,” reads an Oct. 27, 2020 email from Trump’s campaign team, signed by Trump, and sent to Epstein’s email address, “jeeproject@yahoo.com.”

The new batch of emails were obtained by Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets), a nonprofit whistleblower group that shared them selectively with news outlets, including Raw Story. They differ from the document dump of 20,000 pages of files from Epstein’s estate, released by the House Oversight Committee last month.

“Pedophiles, I want you to know how important you are to me,” reads another email from Trump’s campaign to Epstein, dated Oct. 1, 2020, and signed off on by Trump.

“I’m turning to my strongest supporters, like Pedophiles,” reads another, dated Oct 25, 2020.

Epstein was reportedly “obsessed” with Trump for years, with the same batch of emails revealing that he had purchased “basically every major exposé” about the president. Trump was the single-most mentioned individual in the batch of emails released by the House Oversight Committee, and Epstein apparently would coordinate his flights around Trump’s, Raw Story previously reported.

Epstein once called Trump his “closest friend for 10 years,” with the House Oversight Committee’s release revealing that Trump might have “spent hours” with one of Epstein’s victims at his home, and may have spent Thanksgiving with Epstein during his first term in office. Trump has also flown on Epstein’s private jet at least seven times in the 1990s.

Our Analysis:

Unraveling the Epstein-Trump Email Quagmire: A Disturbing Nexus

The recent revelations around an email account belonging to the notorious Jeffrey Epstein have cast a long and unsettling shadow over Donald Trump’s 2020 campaign communications. This new information, unveiled by the whistleblower group Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets), showcases not just a bizarre and shocking chain of correspondence but also highlights the murky depths of Epstein’s connections with high-profile figures, including Trump. The emails, selectively shared with news outlets such as Raw Story, diverge significantly from the previously released 20,000 pages of documents from Epstein’s estate by the House Oversight Committee. The contents are not just alarming due to their sender and recipient, but also for the disturbing personalization used within these communications.

The Peculiar Case of the “Pedophiles” Salutation

The emails in question, sent from Trump’s 2020 campaign to Epstein’s address “jeeproject@yahoo.com,” were found to be addressed under a shockingly inappropriate moniker: “Pedophiles.” This term, used in multiple emails with contents ranging from soliciting support to expressing gratitude, is not just appalling in its insensitivity but also raises serious questions about the nature and extent of the relationship between Epstein and the Trump campaign. For instance, an email dated Oct. 27, 2020, blatantly states, “I need you right now, Pedophiles,” purportedly signed by Trump himself. Another, on Oct. 1, 2020, goes as far as to say, “Pedophiles, I want you to know how important you are to me,” again signed off by Trump.

Epstein’s Obsession and Connection with Trump

It is no secret that Jeffrey Epstein, a convicted sex offender before his death in 2019, had a complex and deeply entwined relationship with many high-profile figures, including Donald Trump. The emails reveal that Epstein was “obsessed” with Trump, going to the extent of purchasing “basically every major exposé” about him. Trump was the most frequently mentioned individual in the batch of emails released by the House Oversight Committee, indicating a relationship that goes beyond mere acquaintanceship. Trump’s own acknowledgment of Epstein as his “closest friend for 10 years,” coupled with the revelation that Trump may have spent significant time with one of Epstein’s victims and even shared Thanksgiving with Epstein, paints a deeply troubling picture.

The Blurred Lines between Campaign Communications and Personal Relationships

What stands out in this entire debacle is the grotesque misuse of campaign communications to address Epstein under a moniker that blatantly references criminal behavior. While it is important to note that the emails’ authenticity and the intent behind the use of such terminology have yet to be thoroughly investigated, the mere existence of such correspondence is alarming.

The Trump campaign’s decision (if these emails are verified to be intentionally sent as reported) to engage with Epstein, especially under such a disturbing alias, raises significant ethical and moral questions. It blurs the lines between political campaigning and personal relationships with individuals of questionable character and criminal backgrounds.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Political and Moral Accountability

The Epstein-Trump email saga is more than just a series of shocking communications; it is a glaring reminder of the systemic failures at the intersection of power, politics, and moral accountability. While Epstein’s death has closed the chapter on his legal reckonings, the individuals and institutions that enabled or maintained relationships with him must still face public scrutiny and historical judgment.

The normalization of such communications—should they be proven to be as reported—reflects a disturbing erosion of ethical standards within the highest echelons of power. As more information comes to light, it is imperative that the public demand transparency, accountability, and, above all, a reevaluation of the moral compass guiding those in positions of significant influence and authority.
