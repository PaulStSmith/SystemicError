Mike Collier to challenge Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick again — as an independent

by Alejandro Serrano, The Texas Tribune
November 13, 2025

Mike Collier, a two-time Democratic nominee for lieutenant governor, announced on Thursday that he is again running to be the state’s second-in-command — but this time as an independent.

Collier, an accountant and auditor, lost his previous bids to Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, the powerful Republican who presides over the state Senate, in 2018 and 2022. Running as an independent, he is branding himself as a protector of public education who also aims to “restore fiscal integrity” and return power to Texans from political insiders.

“Everything starts with our public schools,” Collier said in a statement. “But Dan Patrick is trying to dismantle them with vouchers and culture wars. I’m running to stop him and to fully fund our public schools, respect our teachers, and make sure every child in Texas has a chance to succeed.”

Patrick beat Collier by 5 points the first time the two went head-to-head and handily beat him four years later, claiming a third term with a 10-point margin.

Collier’s candidacy could complicate the path for the Democratic nominee if he ends up siphoning Democratic votes. State Rep. Vikki Goodwin, D-Austin, is the only major candidate to enter the primary to oppose Patrick.

Our Analysis:

Mike Collier’s Quixotic Quest: A Dive into Political Strategy and Desperation

In the latest chapter of Texas political theater, Mike Collier has announced his intention to once again challenge Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick. However, this time, he’s shedding his Democratic skin to run as an independent. This pivot is both a bold strategy and a desperate gamble, indicative of the tumultuous and unpredictable nature of modern American politics.

The Independent Illusion

Let’s cut through the noise. Collier, an accountant and auditor by profession, has faced off against Patrick twice before and lost — first by a margin of 5 points in 2018, and then by a more substantial 10 points in 2022. These defeats, particularly the latter, underscore a significant lack of traction within the electorate, at least under the Democratic banner. His decision to run as an independent is ostensibly a move to brand himself as a protector of public education and a warrior against fiscal irresponsibility. Yet, one cannot help but wonder if this rebranding is less about ideological purity and more about political viability.

Collier’s rhetoric, promising to “restore fiscal integrity” and empower Texans against “political insiders,” is a classic populist play. It’s sharp, it’s catchy, and it’s designed to resonate with those disillusioned by the current political landscape. However, his focus on public education and opposition to vouchers and culture wars, while noble, might not be enough to distinguish him in a state where these debates are already highly polarized.

The Spoiler Effect

The elephant in the room, or perhaps more aptly, the donkey, is the potential spoiler effect Collier could have on the Democratic nominee’s chances. With State Rep. Vikki Goodwin being the only major Democratic candidate to announce her run against Patrick so far, Collier’s campaign might inadvertently siphon votes from Democratic-leaning independents and moderates who are dissatisfied with Patrick but wary of aligning too closely with either party.

This isn’t just a theoretical concern. The dynamics of three-way races in American politics are notoriously unpredictable, often leading to unintended consequences. Collier’s insistence on running, despite his previous defeats, could risk handing Patrick another term by splitting the opposition vote, a scenario that would be rich in irony and frustration for those opposed to Patrick’s policies.

Conclusion: A Strategic Misstep or a Calculated Risk?

Mike Collier’s decision to run as an independent in the Texas Lt. Governor race is a fascinating case study in political strategy and personal ambition. While his motivations are clear — to offer Texans a middle ground focused on education and fiscal integrity — the potential ramifications of his candidacy are complex and fraught with risk.

In the grand scheme of Texas politics, Collier’s move is both a scathing indictment of the two-party system and a potentially self-defeating endeavor. Whether this will lead to a significant shake-up or merely a footnote in Texas’ political history remains to be seen. However, one thing is certain: the road to the lieutenant governor’s office in Texas just got a lot more interesting — and complicated.
