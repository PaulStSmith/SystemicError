'Fire, investigate, prosecute': Analyst urges Trump to go after DOGE workers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump-Musk Feud: A Political Spectacle or a Prelude to Policy Warfare?

(Screenshot via CNN)

President Donald Trump has threatened to escalate his feud with tech billionaire Elon Musk into weaponized federal policy, canceling his longtime ally's subsidies and federal contracts as Musk rails against Trump and attacks his signature tax cut bill currently under debate in the Senate.

But if Trump wants to really bring the heat and do something constructive for the American people in the process, analyst Van Jones said on a CNN panel Thursday, he should start investigating the workers Musk brought into the government through his Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) task force.

"Look, a couple of things," said Jones. "One is, you know, the power that Elon Musk still has is significant. Somebody said Trump has three and a half more years, Elon has 40. He's a billionaire. He's got a long-term play."

"I believe it was Elon Musk who said that," cut in anchor Anderson Cooper.

"Elon's correct. You good at math?" said Jones, to chuckles. "But the other piece of it is those DOGE employees ... are very dangerous. If Donald Trump is smart, the first thing he's going to do is fire, investigate and prosecute all of Elon's people who are all throughout government with their laptops downloading data right now."

"You think he should prosecute them?" Cooper pressed him.

"Listen, I don't think that what they're doing is legal," said Jones. "A lot of them don't have the proper clearances. And I think that if you investigate, you'll find they've uploaded data into servers that are that are the wrong, the wrong thing to do. So I think there should definitely be an investigation there. If Donald Trump is smart, the leverage that Elon has is he left, his kids didn't. They're still in there. So this fight, if it's going to be a fight that Donald Trump prevails on in the short term, go after DOGE. Long term, he's got to steal the Republican Party to deal with an Elon Musk that's promising to spend money now in a decade from now."

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Musk Feud: A Political Spectacle or a Prelude to Policy Warfare?

In what reads like a script ripped straight from the annals of a political thriller, the burgeoning feud between President Donald Trump and tech titan Elon Musk has taken a perilously sharp turn into the realm of federal policy threats. The narrative, as unpacked in a recent news piece, is not just a high-stakes drama between two of the most polarizing figures in contemporary American discourse but a potential harbinger of systemic abuses of power and a stark illustration of the vulnerabilities inherent in the intertwining of government and private enterprise.

Canceling Subsidies and Federal Contracts: Trump's threat to strip Musk of subsidies and federal contracts is a move that reeks of personal vendetta over policy principle. It's a stark reminder of how political power can be wielded as a weapon against dissenting voices in the private sector, a tactic that, while not novel, remains deeply troubling in its implications for democratic norms and the rule of law.

The DOGE Task Force: The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), spearheaded by Musk, ostensibly aims to streamline government operations. Yet, as analyst Van Jones points out, the real danger lies not in the initiative's stated goals but in the execution. The infiltration of Musk's employees throughout the government, armed with laptops and potentially unauthorized access to sensitive data, paints a chilling picture of how private interests can potentially exploit public resources for undisclosed ends.

The Legal and Ethical Quagmire: Jones' call for the firing, investigation, and prosecution of DOGE employees underscores the murky waters of legal and ethical standards in government-private sector collaborations. The allegation that these employees are operating without proper clearances and uploading data to unauthorized servers is a grave concern that merits serious scrutiny. It highlights the pressing need for transparency, accountability, and stringent oversight in such partnerships.

The Long Game: The Trump-Musk conflict, as Jones astutely notes, is not just a short-term skirmish but a protracted battle with significant implications for the future of the Republican Party and American politics at large. Musk's long-term influence, bolstered by his considerable wealth and ambition, poses a strategic challenge that Trump and his allies cannot afford to ignore.

Conclusion: The Trump-Musk saga is more than a mere political sideshow; it is a potent reminder of the fragility of democratic institutions in the face of personal rivalries and private ambitions. As this drama unfolds, it is incumbent upon policymakers, regulators, and the public to remain vigilant against the erosions of democracy and to demand greater accountability from those in positions of power.

In essence, the unfolding Trump-Musk feud encapsulates the broader systemic challenges facing American democracy today: the precarious balance between government authority and private power, the thin line separating legitimate policy disagreements from personal vendettas, and the critical importance of safeguarding public interest in the face of escalating private influence. The need for systemic reform has never been more urgent.
