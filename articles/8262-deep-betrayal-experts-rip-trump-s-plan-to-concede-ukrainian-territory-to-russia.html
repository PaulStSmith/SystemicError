'Deep betrayal': Experts rip Trump's plan to concede Ukrainian territory to Russia

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 29, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump is under fire over a report that claims he is proposing that the U.S. recognize Russian control of parts of Ukraine, including Crimea, which Russia has unlawfully annexed, as a means to end the war.

“The Telegraph understands that Donald Trump has sent his peace envoy Steve Witkoff and son-in-law Jared Kushner to make the direct offer to Vladimir Putin in Moscow,” the news outlet reported. “The plan to recognize territory, which breaks US diplomatic convention, is likely to go ahead despite concerns among Ukraine’s European allies.”

Russian President Vladimir Putin “on Thursday said Washington’s legal recognition of Crimea and the Donetsk and Luhansk regions as Russian territory would be one of the key issues in negotiations over the US president’s peace plan,” according to The Telegraph.

Critics are blasting President Trump.

Shaun Pinner, a former British soldier who served as a contracted Marine fighting in Ukraine’s armed forces, responded to the report:

“I’ve lived through the cost of losing ground. I’ve seen the bodies, the destroyed homes, and I’ve been tortured by Russia like so many others. Land is never ‘just land.’ It’s people. Families. Lives shattered.”

“So yes, watching Trump casually bargain away territory that isn’t even his to give feels like a deep betrayal,” he added. “It’s a lesson I wish none of us had to learn the hard way, and one far too many are being forced to relive again because one of our so-called allies is now suggesting we reward genocide.”

Michael McFaul, former U.S. Ambassador to Russia, remarked, “Trump would be rewarding imperial conquest, thereby encouraging other autocrats to do so, resulting in a very unstable world.”

Russian chess grandmaster Garry Kasparov, co-founder of the Renew Democracy Initiative, issued a warning:

“If the US recognizes territory taken by force, just replace ‘leader of the free world’ with ‘for sale’. Xi can come up with more cash than Putin for Trump and his pals to do the same for Taiwan.”

Marko Mihkelson, Chairman of the Foreign Affairs Committee of the Estonian Parliament, remarked, “If this is true, then we have a major problem, Houston.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Controversial Peace Plan: A Masterclass in Geopolitical Naivety

Donald Trump is once again at the epicenter of global controversy, this time for a proposal that reads like a page ripped straight from the manual of ‘How to Undermine International Law and Order 101’. According to a report from The Telegraph, Trump allegedly aims to end the war by conceding to Russian control over parts of Ukraine, including the internationally condemned annexation of Crimea. This move, which flies in the face of U.S. diplomatic norms, has sparked outrage and concern from various quarters, painting a picture of a former president dangerously out of touch with the principles of international relations and human rights.

Rewarding Imperial Conquest

Shaun Pinner’s poignant response to Trump’s proposed plan underscores the human cost of geopolitical chess games. Pinner’s firsthand experiences of loss, torture, and the destruction wrought by conflict starkly remind us that “Land is never ‘just land.’” His words cut to the heart of the matter: Trump’s casual bartering of territory, which he has no right to offer, is a “deep betrayal” of the values of sovereignty and self-determination.

Encouraging Autocracy

Michael McFaul, a former U.S. Ambassador to Russia, succinctly points out the inherent danger in Trump’s approach: it essentially rewards imperial conquest. Such a precedent would not only embolden autocrats worldwide but also plunge the world into a new era of instability and conflict. The message is clear: aggression pays, and international laws and norms are mere obstacles to be navigated or ignored.

For Sale: American Integrity

Garry Kasparov, never one to mince words, highlights the transactional nature of Trump’s foreign policy. By suggesting the U.S. could recognize territory seized by force, Trump effectively puts America’s global leadership and moral standing up for the highest bidder. Kasparov’s comparison to potential scenarios involving Taiwan and China is a chilling reminder of the far-reaching consequences of such a policy.

A Major Problem, Indeed

Marko Mihkelson’s reaction encapsulates the international dismay at Trump’s proposal. Recognizing Russian control over Ukrainian territory would not only betray a key ally but also signify a dramatic shift in U.S. foreign policy, undermining decades of diplomatic efforts and alliances. This is not just a problem; it’s a crisis in the making.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Trump’s proposed peace plan, if accurately reported, represents a dangerous departure from established norms of international diplomacy and respect for sovereignty. It sends a message that might makes right, that territorial conquests can be legitimized through backdoor deals, and that the U.S. is willing to compromise on fundamental principles for short-term gains or political expediency. At a time when the world is already navigating complex challenges, from climate change to the rise of authoritarianism, this is a step in the wrong direction — a move that could have disastrous consequences for international stability and the moral fabric of global leadership.

As the criticism mounts, one thing is clear: this plan is less about peace and more about appeasement, less about ending a war and more about undermining the very foundations upon which international peace and security are built. Trump’s proposal is not just unwise; it’s a systemic error of historic proportions.
