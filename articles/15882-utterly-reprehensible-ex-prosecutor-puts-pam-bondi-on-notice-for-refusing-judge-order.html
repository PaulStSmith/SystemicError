The Justice Department and Attorney General Pam Bondi are “leading the charge” to paint Kilmar Abrego Garcia as a “bad guy,” according to a new analysis from Joyce Vance.

Abrego Garcia was mistakenly sent to a prison used to house terrorists and gang members in El Salvador.

Vance laid out what we do know about the case: “Abrego Garcia came to this country illegally in 2011 at the age of 16. Since then, he has married, has a child, and is a father to his wife’s two previous children as well.”

The 29-year-old’s deportation was stopped by an immigration judge’s order.

However, Vance believes the backstory “doesn’t matter. What does matter is that Abrego Garcia, criminal or saint, was denied his due process rights by the government, and in this country, people are entitled to those rights.”

“Under our Constitution, people, whether criminals or not, whether citizens or not, are entitled to notice before action is taken against them and to a hearing in front of a judge to sort out any issues,” the former Prosecutor penned

“That’s why it’s utterly reprehensible that Pam Bondi would demean the office she holds to try to convince Americans that this is a bad guy. He might be,” Vance opined. “Although the evidence linking him to gangs is passably weak and seems suspect, it could be the truth.”

The pinnacle of her piece points to her belief that everyone has rights under the Constitution, and Abrego Garcia’s were violated. “It doesn’t make it right for the woman who heads the Justice Department to refuse to follow a federal judge’s orders. It makes it worse.”

The former Prosecutor believes that “the ones who are wrong here are Pam Bondi, Donald Trump, and the others who have forsaken the rule of law. Nothing they say can change the fact that they are the ones breaking the law here.”

Our Analysis:

Unjust Crusade: The Abrego Garcia Debacle

In a glaring display of justice miscarried, the saga of Kilmar Abrego Garcia unfolds, exposing the underbelly of a system marred by prejudice and a blatant disregard for due process. Joyce Vance, a voice of reason amidst the cacophony of political maneuvering, sheds light on a story that should trouble any purveyor of justice and civil liberties.

The Misguided Villainization by Pam Bondi

At the heart of this narrative is Pam Bondi, whose actions as Attorney General have sparked considerable controversy. Bondi, according to Vance, is "leading the charge" to demonize Abrego Garcia, a man whose only crime, it seems, is seeking a better life. This vilification campaign is not only baseless but dangerously misleading, painting a target on the back of someone who, by all accounts, embodies the aspirations of the American Dream.

Abrego Garcia, who arrived in the U.S. at the tender age of 16, has since built a life that many would commend. A husband and a father, his story is one of hope and perseverance. Yet, according to Vance, Bondi and her colleagues would have you believe he's a "bad guy," an unfounded accusation that reeks of political expediency over truth.

The Violation of Due Process Rights

The crux of Vance's argument, and rightly so, is the undeniable violation of Abrego Garcia's due process rights. The U.S. Constitution, a document revered for its protection of liberties, makes no distinction between citizens and non-citizens in the context of legal rights. Abrego Garcia, regardless of his immigration status and irrespective of the charges against him, is entitled to due process - a principle that seems to have been conveniently overlooked by those in power.

"Under our Constitution, people, whether criminals or not, whether citizens or not, are entitled to notice before action is taken against them and to a hearing in front of a judge," Vance rightly points out. The failure to adhere to this foundational aspect of our legal system not only undermines the integrity of the Justice Department but also sets a dangerous precedent for the treatment of non-citizens within our borders.

The Real Culprits

In her scathing critique, Vance does not mince words when she identifies the true perpetrators of this injustice. Pam Bondi, alongside Donald Trump and their cohorts, are branded as the ones who have "forsaken the rule of law." Their attempts to sidestep legal obligations and court orders in their pursuit of a misguided agenda highlight a disturbing trend of lawlessness among those tasked with upholding the law.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The situation surrounding Kilmar Abrego Garcia is a stark reminder of the ongoing battle for civil liberties and the rule of law in America. Vance's analysis is not only a condemnation of the individuals involved but a rallying cry for accountability, transparency, and a recommitment to the principles that define our legal system.

As this narrative unfolds, it serves as a litmus test for our national character: Will we stand by as due process is trampled underfoot, or will we demand justice not just for Abrego Garcia, but for all who seek refuge and opportunity within our borders? The answer to this question will define us for generations to come.
