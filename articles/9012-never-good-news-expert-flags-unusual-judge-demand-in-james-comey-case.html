'Never good news': Expert flags unusual judge demand in James Comey case

For a second time, U.S. District Court Judge Cameron McGowan Currie has ordered the Department of Justice to turn over transcripts from the grand jury that indicted former FBI Director James Comey after the department failed to include statements made by interim U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan.

In an order last month, McGowan first requested the transcripts after Comey alleged that the indictment should be dismissed because Halligan, President Donald Trump’s former personal attorney, was invalidly appointed to her position.

McGowan said that the transcripts were necessary “to determine the extent of the indictment signer’s involvement in the grand jury proceedings.”

On Tuesday, the judge revealed that the DOJ had handed over the transcripts, but had stripped out all of Halligan’s remarks.

“This court has reviewed the transcript and finds it fails to include remarks made by the indictment signer both before and after the testimony of the sole witness, which remarks were referenced by the indictment signer during the witness’s testimony,” the judge wrote. “In addition, the package contains no records or transcripts regarding the presentation of the three-count indictment referenced in the Transcript of the Return of Grand Jury Indictment Proceedings before the Magistrate Judge.”

In response, McGowan gave the DOJ 24 hours to come up with “a complete Transcript and/or recording of all statements made by” Halligan. She also said that she expected to see “statements made prior to and after the testimony of the witness and during the presentation of the three-count and subsequent two-count indictments.”

Former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance noted that it was “unusual for a court to ask for disclosure or a prosecutor’s comments in the grand jury.”

“And never good news for the government,” she added.

Our Analysis:

A Veiled Game of Judicial Cat and Mouse

In a striking display of judicial tenacity, U.S. District Court Judge Cameron McGowan Currie’s recent actions underscore a troubling narrative in the saga involving former FBI Director James Comey’s indictment. The Department of Justice’s (DOJ) apparent reluctance to provide unredacted grand jury transcripts, as ordered by Judge Currie, not only highlights a concerning opacity within a department tasked with upholding justice but also hints at a deeper entanglement of political interests and legal proceedings.

The Centerpiece of Contention

At the heart of this legal drama is the DOJ’s failure—twice—to comply with Judge Currie’s order to turn over complete grand jury transcripts related to Comey’s indictment. The omission of statements made by interim U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan, who also happens to be President Donald Trump’s former personal attorney, raises eyebrows. This maneuver by the DOJ reeks of a deliberate attempt to obfuscate the truth and perhaps protect vested interests, given Halligan’s direct connection to Trump.

Judge Currie’s insistence on these transcripts is not a mere procedural formality. It is a crucial step in determining the validity of the indictment against Comey, especially in light of allegations that Halligan’s appointment was invalid. The judge’s decision to request these transcripts underscores a fundamental principle of justice: transparency in the indictment process.

DOJ’s Dubious Response

The DOJ’s response to the judge’s order is, to put it mildly, baffling. By submitting transcripts stripped of Halligan’s remarks, the department not only defied a direct court order but also signaled a disturbing lack of respect for judicial authority. This act of defiance begs the question: What is the DOJ trying to hide?

Judge Currie’s subsequent order, giving the DOJ 24 hours to comply fully, serves as a stark reminder of the judiciary’s role as a check on the actions of the executive branch. The judge’s expectations were crystal clear: a complete transcript, including Halligan’s statements before, during, and after the testimony of the witness, as well as during the presentation of the indictments.

The Bigger Picture

Former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance’s comment that it is “unusual for a court to ask for disclosure of a prosecutor’s comments in the grand jury” and that such a request is “never good news for the government” illuminates the gravity of the situation. This is not business as usual. It’s a red flag signaling potential misconduct or, at the very least, a departure from standard legal procedures.

What makes this scenario particularly alarming is the potential impact on public trust in the DOJ and, by extension, the entire justice system. When the department tasked with enforcing federal laws and administering justice is perceived as playing political games, the foundational trust that the public places in it erodes.

Conclusion

Judge Currie’s battle to shed light on the grand jury proceedings in the Comey indictment case is more than a legal skirmish; it’s a fight for transparency, accountability, and the integrity of the judicial process. The DOJ’s resistance to this endeavor not only undermines these principles but also casts a long shadow over the impartiality of justice administration under its watch.

In a democracy, no individual or department should be above the law. The DOJ’s actions, as outlined in this saga, not only challenge this tenet but also expose a systemic error that must be rectified. Transparency, after all, is not just a legal requirement; it’s a cornerstone of trust and integrity in the justice system.
