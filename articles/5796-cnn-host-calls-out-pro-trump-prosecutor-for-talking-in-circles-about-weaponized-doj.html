CNN host calls out pro-Trump prosecutor for talking in circles about weaponized DOJ

By Paulo Santos

CNN host Boris Sanchez and Chad Mizelle, former acting general counsel to the Department of Homeland Security (Photo: Screen capture)

CNN host Boris Sanchez clashed with one of President Donald Trump’s former government attorneys on Monday, who was quick on allegations but thin on evidence.

Chad Mizelle, former acting general counsel to the Department of Homeland Security, alleged that the Trump Justice Department would begin acting on findings from its “working group” investigating President Joe Biden’s administration.

CNN reporter Paula Reid said that Trump recently “admonished Justice Department officials for not delivering on more of these kinds of investigations.”

Mizelle rattled off some of the “findings” he said have been uncovered, including matters related to former special counsel Jack Smith and allegations about John Bolton’s book published during the first Trump term.

Mizelle also argued that the Trump administration would not “weaponize the Justice Department” as was done previously. Yet Sanchez asked how that could be when the president has publicly ordered Attorney General Pam Bondi to weaponize the DOJ against his political foes.

Sanchez asked Mizelle whether there was any evidence for his allegations.

“It sounds like you’re insinuating that there’s something there and perhaps hiding under the cover of there being something in an investigation. Why don’t you just say it right now?” Sanchez asked. “Have you seen any evidence that Joe Biden directed the search of Mar-a-Lago for classified documents? Joe Biden, by the way, had his own documents at his property that he had his lawyers come out and say, ‘Hey, we have this stuff, come look for more stuff from the federal government.’ A very different posture than President Trump took.”

“Here’s what I am able to speak to,” Mizelle said. He went on to attack former President Barack Obama’s administration for “weaponizing” the intelligence community.

Sanchez called him out on it.

“I appreciate you not directly answering the question and going back to 2016, when there was, at the time, credible evidence regarding links between the Trump campaign and agents of a foreign government that were investigated and were looked into,” Sanchez said.

Our Analysis:

The Clash of Accountability and Allegation

In a recent encounter that can only be described as the epitome of our current political circus, CNN host Boris Sanchez found himself in a verbal spar with Chad Mizelle, a name that harks back to an era of contentious political maneuvering under the Trump administration. Mizelle, who once served as the acting general counsel to the Department of Homeland Security, presented allegations with the confidence of a man who believes fervently in the righteousness of his cause but seemed to stumble when pressed for the substance of those allegations.

The Allegations: Thin Air or Smoke Signal?

Mizelle’s claims, linked to the Trump Justice Department’s “working group” investigating the Biden administration, were bold, to say the least. Yet, when the conversation veered into the territory of evidence—or the glaring lack thereof—his arguments began to crumble like a house of cards in a gusty wind. It’s a familiar tactic: throw enough allegations against the wall and hope something sticks, evidence be damned. But here lies the rub—allegations without evidence are nothing but political theater, a performance meant to distract and divide.

Sanchez’s Interrogation: A Beacon of Journalism

Boris Sanchez, in his role, did not let Mizelle’s allegations fly without challenge. His questioning was a testament to what journalism needs more of today: a relentless pursuit of truth, especially when faced with evasion. Sanchez’s inquiry into whether there was any evidence of Joe Biden directing the search of Mar-a-Lago for classified documents was not just a question; it was a demand for accountability.

The Weaponization of the Justice Department: A Double-Edged Sword

Mizelle’s argument that the Trump administration would not “weaponize the Justice Department” is a claim so bold it borders on the absurd, especially considering President Trump’s public orders to Attorney General Pam Bondi to do precisely that against his political adversaries. This claim stands in stark contrast to the reality many observed during Trump’s tenure, where the lines between justice and political vendetta often seemed blurred.

Furthermore, Mizelle’s attempt to deflect by attacking the Obama administration only served to highlight a common diversion tactic: when confronted with uncomfortable questions about the present, pivot to the past. Sanchez’s response to this maneuver was particularly noteworthy, as he underscored the existence of credible evidence at the time regarding links between the Trump campaign and foreign agents—a stark contrast to the baseless insinuations being peddled by Mizelle.

Conclusion: The Unending Quest for Truth

This exchange between Sanchez and Mizelle is emblematic of the broader struggle within American political discourse. On one side, there are those who seek to uphold the integrity of our institutions by demanding evidence and accountability. On the other, there are those who seem all too willing to undermine that very integrity with baseless allegations for political gain.

As observers and participants in this ongoing saga, we must ask ourselves: what do we value more? The comfort of our preconceived notions, or the often uncomfortable truth? Until we can collectively answer that question with a resounding endorsement of the latter, the circus will continue, and the systemic errors that plague our political system will persist, unchallenged and unabated.
