Trump official declared most 'pathetic' of admin in scathing rebuke: 'Humiliated herself'

Former Representative Tulsi Gabbard, U.S. President Donald Trump’s nominee to be director of national intelligence, prepares to leave following a Senate Intelligence Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard drew a blistering rebuke Sunday from journalist Mehdi Hasan, who called her the most “pathetic” and “shameless” Trump administration official for repeatedly getting “humiliated by the very man she serves.”

A former Democrat, Gabbard has been on the outs of the Trump administration, with the White House frequently excluding her from briefings and Trump aides labeling her with the nickname “Do Not Inform,” a tongue-in-cheek reference to her title as DNI director.

“Even at her worst, I could at least respect that she seemed to be guided by a set of clear ideological convictions,” Hasan wrote in an op-ed published Sunday on Zeteo.

“What we’re left with now is something much grimmer: a political shapeshifter and sycophant so eager to curry Trump’s favor that she has abandoned every pretense of professional and ideological independence.”

Indeed, much of Gabbard’s previous statements and positions have been directly contradicted by the Trump administration, and without any meaningful response from Gabbard.

Gabbard publicly denounced those who stormed the U.S. Capitol on Jan. 6 as “criminals,” but stood silent as Trump issued them blanket pardons. She told Congress last year amid fears that the Trump administration would strike Iran that the Middle East nation was likely “not building a nuclear weapon,” only to reverse course and later claim that Iran was only weeks away from building a nuclear weapon.

Gabbard had also previously argued against U.S. military intervention in Venezuela, only to get caught vacationing in Hawaii during Trump’s attack on the South American nation last month after reportedly being left out of the operation’s planning process. She also remained silent on the attack in the days since.

“What is so pathetic is that we are barely a year into this administration and she has already humiliated herself – and been humiliated by the very man she serves,” Hasan wrote.

“Yet she continues to carry his water, to spin his narratives, to defend his baseless election claims, and to lend the prestige of her office to fake intelligence assessments. So I have to ask: does Tulsi Gabbard have any self-respect at all?”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Tulsi Gabbard Conundrum

In a political landscape that often reads more like a tragic farce than a functioning democracy, the saga of Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard stands out as a particularly bleak chapter. Mehdi Hasan’s recent op-ed tears into Gabbard with the kind of ferocity usually reserved for the most egregious of political actors. And, based on the evidence laid out, it’s not hard to see why.

The Pathetic and the Shameless

Hasan doesn’t mince words, labeling Gabbard as the most “pathetic” and “shameless” among Trump administration officials. This is a bold claim in an administration that, as critics argue, has often appeared to be in a competition for such titles. Yet, Gabbard’s actions—or rather, her inactions and flip-flops—provide a solid foundation for Hasan’s critique.

Gabbard’s political metamorphosis is nothing short of remarkable. Once considered a rising star within the Democratic Party, with a set of clear ideological convictions, she has morphed into what Hasan aptly describes as a “political shapeshifter and sycophant.” This transformation is not just bewildering but deeply concerning, given her role as the Director of National Intelligence.

The Sycophant’s Plight

It’s one thing to serve in an administration and advocate for its policies; it’s entirely another to abandon all pretense of professional and ideological independence. Gabbard’s silence on issues where she previously held strong, opposing views is telling. Her failure to condemn Trump’s pardon of those who stormed the U.S. Capitol, her about-face on Iran’s nuclear ambitions, and her apparent acquiescence to U.S. military intervention in Venezuela—all while being sidelined within the administration—paint a picture of a leader more concerned with currying favor than with standing by her principles or the truth.

The Price of Loyalty

Gabbard’s loyalty to Trump, as depicted by Hasan, is particularly galling because it seems to come at the cost of her own dignity and the responsibilities of her office. The nickname “Do Not Inform,” as cruel as it is revealing, suggests that even within the Trump administration, Gabbard is seen not as a valuable contributor but as a liability. Yet, she continues to “carry his water,” defending indefensible election claims and lending the prestige of her office to dubious intelligence assessments.

The Real Losers

While Hasan’s critique of Gabbard is scathing, it’s essential to remember who truly suffers from this charade: the American people and the integrity of the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. In an era where truth and facts are under constant assault, the role of intelligence agencies in providing clear, unbiased information is more critical than ever. Gabbard’s apparent willingness to subordinate the independence of her office to political expediency not only undermines her credibility but also erodes public trust in the institutions meant to protect and inform them.

Conclusion

As Hasan asks whether Gabbard has any self-respect left, we must also ask what’s left of the integrity of the office she holds. Gabbard’s saga is a stark reminder of the corrosive effect of blind loyalty to individuals or parties over principle and duty. In the end, this isn’t just about Gabbard’s personal or political failings; it’s a cautionary tale about the fragility of democracy and the institutions designed to safeguard it. Her story should serve as a wake-up call to all public servants about the true cost of such shameless sycophancy.
