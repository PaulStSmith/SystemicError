'Explosive resignations' to rock GOP as reps tire of being treated 'like garbage': insider

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) announced her retirement from Congress in a provocative manifesto that highlights growing tension in the Republican caucus — and party leaders are being warned it’s inspiring others.

Greene’s critique comes at a critical moment for Republicans, who have a precarious majority with 219 seats compared to 213 for the Democrats. Upcoming special elections in Tennessee, Texas and New Jersey could further destabilize their position, reported Punchbowl News.

“The crux of MTG’s message is that Trump and House Republicans are abandoning all of the president’s priorities, falling into complacency and are on the brink of squandering their razor-thin majority,” the outlet reported in its email newsletter.

“Let’s dispense with the caveats,” the newsletter added. “MTG has never been representative of the House Republican Conference writ large. She clearly has a bone to pick with Trump and the leadership. MTG may have her eye on the governorship — that chatter picked up again over the weekend. She was also never a fan of Speaker Mike Johnson.”

However, as Punchbowl reported, Greene’s viewpoint appears to be widely shared.

“This entire White House team has treated ALL members like garbage. ALL,” said one senior House Republican. “And Mike Johnson has let it happen because he wanted it to happen. That is the sentiment of nearly all — appropriators, authorizers, hawks, doves, rank and file. The arrogance of this White House team is off putting to members who are run roughshod and threatened. They don’t even allow little wins like announcing small grants or even responding from agencies. Not even the high profile, the regular rank and file random members are more upset than ever. Members know they are going into the minority after the midterms.”

“More explosive early resignations are coming,” that senior House Republican added. “It’s a tinder box. Morale has never been lower. Mike Johnson will be stripped of his gavel and they will lose the majority before this term is out.”

There’s a special election next week, on Dec. 2, to fill former Rep. Mark Greene’s (R-TN) seat, and both parties have poured massive amounts of money into that race, and Democrats are expected to gain a seat in Houston at the end of January to replace the late Rep. Sylvester Turner’s (D-TX), and New Jersey voters will choose Gov.-elect Mikie Sherrill’s replacement on April 16 in a district that Kamala Harris won by nine points in 2024.

“Let’s say Democrats are able to steal the Tennessee seat based on subpar GOP turnout — unlikely but possible — Johnson would have 218 members to Democrats’ 214. Texas and New Jersey would bring Democrats to 216,” Punchbowl reported. “If any members retire or fall ill, Johnson would be sunk.”

“House retirements and resignations are common after holidays,” the newsletter added. “How appealing is it to return to the Capitol when the House spends most of its time voting on censure resolutions or meaningless messaging bills?”

Our Analysis:

The Greene Quandary: A Symbol of Republican Disarray

In an era where political bombshells drop with the regularity of rain in London, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s (R-GA) retirement announcement thunders with particular resonance. Not merely for the act itself, but for the tempest it both symbolizes and potentially augurs within the Republican Party. Let’s dissect this with the meticulousness it deserves, peeling back the layers of Greene’s provocative manifesto and its implications for the GOP’s tenuous grip on its majority.

The Manifesto and Its Moment

At the heart of Greene’s exit is a manifesto that reads less like a farewell and more like a clarion call to arms—a critique aimed squarely at the party’s leadership and, by extension, its direction. It’s a moment that underscores a growing tension within the Republican caucus, one that cannot be dismissed as mere intraparty squabbling. Greene charges that Trump and House Republicans are “abandoning all of the president’s priorities,” a statement that should give pause considering her previously unflinching loyalty to the Trumpian vision for America.

The Precarious Majority

The numerical dance of power in the House is a precarious one, with Republicans holding a razor-thin majority. Upcoming special elections in Tennessee, Texas, and New Jersey are not just battles for seats but for the soul and future of the Republican majority. Greene’s departure and critique come at a critical juncture, threatening to destabilize an already shaky edifice.

The Wider Rebellion?

Punchbowl News’ reporting suggests Greene’s sentiments are far from isolated. There’s a palpable sense of disillusionment within the ranks, with accusations of mistreatment by the White House team and a leadership too complacent or complicit to intervene. It’s a picture of a party at war with itself, where “morale has never been lower.” The warning of “more explosive early resignations” should not be taken lightly—it indicates a potential unraveling at the seams.

The Electoral Implications

The special elections loom large in this drama. They are not just electoral contests but barometers for the GOP’s health and future prospects. The possibility of losing seats, and with them the majority, is real and present. The notion that Democrats could “steal the Tennessee seat based on subpar GOP turnout” speaks volumes about the internal crises affecting Republican mobilization and unity.

The Greene Legacy

Marjorie Taylor Greene has always been a polarizing figure, to say the least. Yet, in her exit, she casts a long shadow over the GOP, one that might loom larger in her absence than it ever did during her tenure. Her critique, whether one agrees with it or not, highlights a crisis of leadership and direction within the Republican Party, one that threatens to tear it apart from the inside.

In Conclusion

As we stand back and survey the unfolding drama, it’s clear Greene’s departure is more than a simple resignation; it’s a symptom of deeper malaise. The GOP stands at a crossroads, with its future direction and leadership in question. Whether this leads to a course correction or further chaos remains to be seen, but one thing is certain: the Republican Party cannot afford to ignore the seismic waves Greene’s manifesto has set off. The coming months may well determine if the GOP can unify and chart a path forward or if it will be consumed by the fires of internal dissent.
