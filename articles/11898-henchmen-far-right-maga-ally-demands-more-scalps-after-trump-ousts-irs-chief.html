President Donald Trump is firing former Missouri Rep. Billy Long, whom he nominated to be commissioner of the IRS, The New York Times reported Friday afternoon. Trump had nominated Billy Long to be commissioner of the agency. Long was confirmed by the Senate and ceremonially sworn in just last month.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will serve as acting commissioner until the administration finds a suitable replacement, according to the report.

Laura Loomer, a far-right political activist and conspiracy theorist known for her provocative stunts, took to X to call on Bessent to go even further.

"Now that Billy Long has been removed as IRS commissioner, when is @SecScottBessent going to fire all of the Lois Lerner holdovers who are still in senior roles at the IRS? Why won’t Bessent fire Obama’s henchmen at the IRS who continue to target and maliciously audit GOP donors and nonprofits?"

Loomer claimed the White House received a list of Obama-era holdovers at the IRS last month along with "examples of how they have targeted conservatives."

"Yet the people whose names were submitted are still employed at the IRS. Why? It appears that Bessent has acted to protect Lois Lerner’s hard left deputies and mentees more than the many Americans whose rights have been repeatedly violated by a rogue agency," she said. "Hard left activists at IRS and Bessent’s meddling inside IRS is one of the main reasons why he and @elonmusk got in a fight by the way. It was over the poor personnel decisions at IRS."

Musk and Bessent clashed primarily over the leadership and management of the IRS under Trump's administration, according to reports. Musk, as head of the Department of Government Efficiency, tried to install Gary Shapley as acting IRS commissioner without Bessent's approval. Bessent opposed the move and wanted his deputy, Michael Faulkender, installed instead.

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the IRS Fiasco: Trump, Loomer, and the Far-Right's Unhinged Narrative

In a move that reads like a script from a political thriller, President Donald Trump's decision to fire former Missouri Rep. Billy Long as commissioner of the IRS has stirred the pot in a way only the tumultuous Trump administration could. Long, who was confirmed by the Senate and had barely warmed his seat as commissioner, was shown the door with little to no explanation, paving the way for Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to step in as acting commissioner. This development, however, was just the prelude to a far more concerning narrative pushed by far-right political activist and conspiracy theorist Laura Loomer.

Loomer, never one to shy away from controversy or conspiracy, took to X (formerly known as Twitter) with a barrage of accusations and demands aimed at Bessent. Her tirade centered on the dismissal of supposed "Lois Lerner holdovers" from the Obama era, whom she accuses of targeting GOP donors and nonprofits through malicious audits. This claim, devoid of substantiation, smacks of the same baseless conspiracy theories that have become the hallmark of far-right rhetoric. It's a dangerous game of guilt by association, a tactic as old as politics itself, yet no less destructive.

Let's dissect the unhinged logic at the heart of Loomer's argument. She purports that Bessent, by not purging these so-called Obama-era holdovers, is somehow complicit in a grand conspiracy to undermine conservative groups. This leap in logic is not only baseless but dangerously misleading. It perpetuates the myth of a deep-state operation working against the conservative movement, a narrative with no grounding in reality but significant repercussions in deepening political divides.

Moreover, Loomer's attempt to drag Elon Musk into the fray, citing a nebulous conflict between him and Bessent over IRS personnel decisions, adds another layer of absurdity to the narrative. Musk's purported clash with Bessent over the appointment of an acting IRS commissioner is presented without context or evidence, serving only to muddy the waters further. It's a classic move in the conspiracy theorist's playbook: throw enough names and accusations against the wall and hope something sticks.

The real story here, which Loomer and her ilk conveniently ignore, is the troubling pattern of instability and capricious decision-making within the Trump administration. The firing of Billy Long, a Trump nominee, just weeks after his confirmation, raises serious questions about the administration's competency and coherence. Yet, for the far-right, these questions are secondary to their agenda of painting themselves as victims of a vast, left-wing conspiracy.

In conclusion, the narrative pushed by Laura Loomer and echoed by far-right factions is not just misguided; it's dangerously divisive. It serves to deepen the partisan divide and distract from the real issues at hand, such as the need for stable and effective governance. As we sift through the noise, it's crucial to remain grounded in facts and wary of those who would use conspiracy theories to further their own agenda. The saga of Billy Long's firing is a case study in the far-right's playbook, one that we must learn from if we are to navigate these turbulent political waters with our sanity intact.
