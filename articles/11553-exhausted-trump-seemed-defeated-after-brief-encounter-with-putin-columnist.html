'Exhausted' Trump 'seemed defeated' after brief encounter with Putin: columnist

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 17, 20255 min read

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Instead of coming away with progress being made toward a ceasefire in Ukraine, Donald Trump flew home from Alaska looking every bit his age after getting “played” by Vladimir Putin.

In his column for the Daily Beast, commentator David Rothkopf suggested that not only did the president fail to make any progress, based upon what was discussed at a press conference that was scheduled to last for an hour, but ended after only 12 minutes, but the president exposed himself as not being the force of nature he seems to believe he is.

According to Rothkopf, the president attended the summit with the “long-term goal” of getting ”Russian president Vladimir Putin to publicly support a series of lies that are now crucial to both Trump’s own deluded sense of himself and to his fake public narrative about who he is.”

That was achieved by Trump, along with a creating a distraction to get the Jeffrey Epstein files firestorm off the front pages, but little else that could be considered a positive.

Instead, Rothkopf wrote, “Trump framed himself as the host of the summit. But Putin was the center of attention, the person who spoke first at the post-event press conference, the one who was clearly setting the rules going forward.”

“There was a pathos to the whole event because if you watched closely, particularly during the closing press conference, it appeared Trump understood this as well,” he wrote. “He was low-energy. He seemed defeated. He was going through the motions. In fact, in a predictable irony, the 79-year-old Trump appeared to be just the president he asserted Joe Biden would’ve become had he been reelected, too old for the job, not up to the challenge, more elderly than he has ever appeared to be while on the world stage.”

He suggested the president may have finally realized his limitations.

“If you looked into the resignation and bewilderment in his eyes as he scuttled off the stage at Elmendorf, you couldn’t help but wonder if he wasn’t starting to think it will be easier to returning to the kind of low-level grift that is his main line of work when he is in the one place in the world he least likes to be…the one place he knows awaits all shallow, all-sizzle-no-substance TV personalities like him … and that is off camera,” he suggested.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Alaskan Fiasco: A Study in Diplomatic Failure

In what reads like a political thriller gone awry, the recent summit in Alaska has laid bare the stark inefficiencies and delusions of Donald Trump's approach to international diplomacy, particularly with Russia's Vladimir Putin. David Rothkopf's analysis in the Daily Beast, which serves as the basis for this scathing critique, paints a vivid picture of a president outmatched and outmaneuvered on the global stage.

The Summit of Disappointment

The expectation of progress towards a ceasefire in Ukraine was shattered, not with a bang, but with the whimper of a press conference cut abruptly from an hour to a mere 12 minutes. This was not a strategic truncation but a glaring testament to the absence of substantive dialogue or advancement. Trump, who has often prided himself on being a formidable negotiator and a "force of nature," was exposed as neither. His purported long-term goal of coaxing Putin into endorsing a series of fabrications—vital to both his inflated self-perception and his concocted public persona—yielded nothing but a distraction from the Jeffrey Epstein scandal.

Putin: The Puppet Master

The dynamics of the summit further underscored Trump's diplomatic naivety. Presenting himself as the host, he unwittingly ceded the spotlight to Putin, who assumed the role of the de facto leader, speaking first and dictating the terms. This inversion of roles was not just a procedural faux pas but a symbolic defeat, showcasing Trump's inability to command respect or assert influence on issues of global importance.

A Portrait of Decline

Perhaps the most poignant aspect of Rothkopf's critique is the depiction of a defeated Trump, a man whose energy seemed to have been sapped by the realization of his inadequacies. The comparison to Joe Biden, whom Trump has frequently derided as being too old and unfit for the presidency, is particularly biting. Here, Trump is portrayed as embodying the very frailties he projected onto his opponent, appearing "more elderly than he has ever appeared to be while on the world stage."

Moreover, the suggestion that Trump recognized his limitations, glimpsed in the "resignation and bewilderment in his eyes," is a damning indictment of his presidency. It hints at an awareness that his skills, better suited to "low-level grift," are insufficient for the demands of global leadership. This realignment from a self-perceived titan of international politics to a parochial conman is both a personal capitulation and a national embarrassment.

Conclusion: The Inevitable Retreat

The Alaskan summit, rather than being a forum for meaningful progress, became a theater of the absurd, with Trump as its unwitting protagonist. Rothkopf's analysis not only critiques a single diplomatic engagement but also symbolizes the broader failings of a presidency overwhelmed by its own fiction. It's a stark reminder of the consequences of entrusting global diplomacy to individuals driven more by ego and personal aggrandizement than by strategic acumen or a genuine commitment to peace and stability.

In this tale of diplomatic disillusionment, the real casualty is not just Trump's ego, but the global standing of the United States. As Trump retreats from the world stage, one is left to ponder the lasting impacts of his presidency on international relations and the grim realization that in the high stakes game of global diplomacy, being outplayed is not just a personal defeat but a national disaster.
