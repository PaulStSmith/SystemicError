Texas: Not a Red State — A Rigged State

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

For decades, Texas Republicans have relied on the same tired myth: that the Lone Star State is naturally and immutably conservative, a ruby-red bastion of right-wing orthodoxy. But credible data and relentless court battles tell a different story — one of systematic suppression, deliberate disenfranchisement, and a governing party that knows it cannot maintain power without rewriting the rules of the game. The latest numbers from the Independent Voter Project (IVP) and the newest naked power grab orchestrated by Greg Abbott — at the behest of his political messiah, Donald Trump — pull the curtain back entirely.

The IVP Data: A State More Purple Than Red

Let’s start with the raw math. Texas is one of the many states that does not formally register voters by party. Instead, it runs open primaries where you simply pick a party ballot on voting day. That means there’s no official ledger of “registered Democrats” versus “registered Republicans.” But political campaigns, advocacy groups, and analysts have long relied on sophisticated models to infer affiliation from voting history, demographics, and turnout.

The IVP, using L2 Data — one of the most respected voter file analytics firms in the country — estimates the Texas electorate at roughly:

  • 8.13 million Democrats (46.5%)

  • 6.60 million Republicans (37.8%)

  • 2.75 million unaffiliated/other (15.7%)

These are not wild guesses. L2 builds its databases from official state records, updated constantly, layered with actual primary participation data, National Change of Address filings, and demographic modeling. This is the same kind of data both parties use to target voters, raise money, and mobilize turnout. If you trust the strategy of any modern political campaign, you’re already trusting this methodology.

So the billboard making the rounds — proclaiming “Texas has 8M Democrats, 6.5M Republicans… we’re not a red state… we’re a rigged state” — isn’t a baseless meme. It’s a distillation of the numbers political insiders already know.

Gerrymandering: The Real Engine of Republican Power

If these figures are even close to reality, why do Republicans dominate the Texas legislature and congressional delegation? The answer is as blatant as it is damning: partisan gerrymandering. Texas Republicans have turned map-drawing into a blood sport, crafting districts with surgical precision to maximize GOP seats while minimizing the political influence of Democratic voters.

Their playbook is a mix of:

  • Packing: Concentrating as many Democratic voters — especially communities of color — into as few districts as possible, creating overwhelming Democratic margins in those areas while making surrounding districts more Republican.

  • Cracking: Splitting urban Democratic strongholds into multiple districts that are dominated by suburban and rural Republicans.

  • Incumbent Protection: Drawing lines that shield vulnerable GOP representatives from competitive races.

The result? A legislature and congressional map that produces Republican majorities wildly disproportionate to the electorate’s actual partisan makeup. The courts have smacked Texas Republicans on the wrist for racial gerrymandering before, but those rulings have done little to curb the overall strategy.

Enter Greg Abbott’s Latest Power Grab

As if the existing maps weren’t tilted enough, Abbott has now moved to seize even more control — and not coincidentally, at the urging of Donald Trump. Trump, who sees political loyalty as the only currency that matters, has been leaning on Abbott to ram through a mid-decade redistricting scheme that would hand Republicans even more congressional seats ahead of 2026.

This maneuver — ripping up maps well before the next census — flies in the face of both democratic norms and basic fairness. Redistricting was meant to follow population shifts every ten years, not be a tool for incumbents to redraw their own fiefdoms whenever they feel their grip loosening. Abbott’s willingness to torch precedent to please his “dear leader” shows just how desperate Texas Republicans have become.

The ‘Rigged State’ Framing

When you combine:

  1. Credible estimates showing Democrats outnumber Republicans in the voter pool,

  2. Decades of hyper-partisan gerrymandering,

  3. A mid-decade redistricting coup cooked up by Abbott and Trump,

…the claim that Texas is “rigged” stops being rhetorical flourish and starts sounding like a legal brief.

Rigging doesn’t have to look like ballot-stuffing or hacking voting machines. It can be baked into the structure of representation itself. You can have free elections on paper while locking in a system where the outcome is preordained. Texas has mastered this sleight of hand: the will of the people replaced with the will of the mapmakers.

Why It Matters Beyond Texas

This isn’t just a Texas problem. When one of the largest and most electorally important states rigs its political machinery to nullify the votes of nearly half its population, it distorts national politics. Texas sends 38 members to the U.S. House — soon to be more if population growth continues — and awards 40 electoral votes in presidential races. If those seats and votes are structurally skewed toward one party, it doesn’t just affect Texans; it tilts the entire federal balance.

It also sends a dangerous message: if you can get away with extreme gerrymandering, you can effectively choose your voters instead of letting voters choose you. That’s not democracy. That’s a cartel in suits.

The Path Forward

Texas will not reform itself voluntarily. The entrenched powers benefit too much from the status quo. The path forward will require:

  • Federal action: Strengthening the Voting Rights Act and restoring preclearance requirements for states with histories of racial and partisan gerrymandering.

  • Independent redistricting commissions: Taking map-drawing out of the hands of self-interested legislators.

  • Public pressure: Texans must keep shining a light on these manipulations, refusing to accept them as inevitable.

Until then, the billboard’s blunt verdict will stand: Texas isn’t a red state. It’s a rigged state — and every new maneuver from Abbott and his GOP allies only tightens the screws.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
