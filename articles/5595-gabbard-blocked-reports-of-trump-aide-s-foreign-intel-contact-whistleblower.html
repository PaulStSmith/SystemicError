Gabbard blocked reports of Trump aide's foreign intel contact: whistleblower

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 8, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Gabbard Gambit: A Masterclass in Obfuscation

Director of National Intelligence (DNI) Tulsi Gabbard speaks during a press briefing, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A whistleblower tells the Gurdian that National Intelligence Director Tulsi Gabbard blocked the publication of an intelligence report concerning an “unusual phone call between an individual associated with foreign intelligence and a person close to Donald Trump.”

The details of the communication are not known, but the Wall Street Journal reported it could cause “grave damage to national security” if it becomes public, according to an official. The information also “implicates another federal agency” and reportedly includes “claims of executive privilege that may involve the White House.”

Last spring, the National Security Agency (NSA) intercepted the highly sensitive communication, but when the info reached Gabbard, she ducked the standard procedure of allowing NSA officials to distribute the information further and instead delivered a paper copy of the intelligence directly to the president’s chief of staff, Susie Wiles, according to attorney Andrew Bakaj.

“One day after meeting Wiles, Gabbard told the NSA not to publish the intelligence report. Instead, she instructed NSA officials to transmit the highly classified details directly to her office,” reports the Guardian. “Details of this exchange between Gabbard and the NSA were shared directly with the Guardian and have not been previously reported. Nor has Wiles receipt of the intelligence report.”

On April 17, a whistleblower contacted the office of the inspector general alleging that Gabbard had blocked highly classified intelligence from routine dispatch, according to Bakaj, who added that the whistleblower filed a formal complaint about Gabbard’s actions on May 21.

The Guardian said the intelligence report was been “kept under lock and key” for eight months, even after the whistleblower pressed to disclose details of the report to congressional intelligence committees.

“Acting inspector general Tamara A Johnson dismissed the complaint at the end of a 14-day review period, writing in a June 6 letter addressed to the whistleblower that ‘the Inspector General could not determine if the allegations appear credible,’” reports the Guardian. Johnson added also informed the whistleblower that they could only approach Congress after receiving DNI guidance on how to proceed due to the highly sensitive nature of the complaint.

After nearly a year, Gabbard’s office issued its first public acknowledgment of the complaint to lawmakers on Tuesday, the day after the Wall Street Journal broke the news of the classified brief.

“Two attorneys and two former intelligence professionals who reviewed details of the incident and ensuing complaint shared with the Guardian have identified what they believe are a series of procedural anomalies that raise questions about Gabbard’s handling of national intelligence and the whistleblower disclosure,” the Guardian reports.

Two Republican lawmakers have dismissed the report and remain loyal to Gabbard, but Democrats are raising alarms.

“The law is clear: when a whistleblower makes a complaint and wants to get it before Congress the agency has 21 days to relay it,” said Sen. Mark Warner (D-Va.) who is on the Senate Intelligence Committee. “This whistleblower complaint was issued in May. We didn’t receive it until February.”

Warner said the months-long delay reflected an effort to “bury the complaint.”

Our Analysis:

The Gabbard Gambit: A Masterclass in Obfuscation

In what reads like a political thriller, but with the grim realism of today’s political landscape, National Intelligence Director Tulsi Gabbard finds herself embroiled in a controversy that could very well be ripped from the pages of John le Carré. According to reports from The Guardian, Gabbard blocked the publication of an intelligence report detailing an “unusual phone call” between a figure close to Donald Trump and an entity tied to foreign intelligence. This isn’t just another drop in the turbulent sea of Trump-era scandals; it’s a veritable storm, threatening to capsize what remains of transparent governance.

The Allegations: Grave and Graver Still

The heart of the issue lies in Gabbard’s deliberate deviation from established protocol, circumventing the National Security Agency’s (NSA) standard distribution process to instead hand-deliver a paper copy of this highly sensitive intelligence to the President’s chief of staff, Susie Wiles. The motivation behind this maneuver remains as shadowy as the details of the intercepted communication itself, described by the Wall Street Journal as potentially causing “grave damage to national security.”

The implications are as damning as they are concerning. Not only does the situation implicate another federal agency, but it also raises the specter of executive privilege being wielded to obscure potentially compromising information. The direct intervention by Gabbard, sidelining the NSA, and opting for a more clandestine route directly to the White House, is a bold move that raises eyebrows and questions alike.

The Whistleblower’s Cry in the Dark

Enter the whistleblower, whose attempt to bring light to this dark corner of intelligence handling was met with bureaucratic stonewalling. Despite following the labyrinthine path laid out for raising such concerns, their complaint was dismissed by Acting Inspector General Tamara A Johnson with a response that might as well have been a shrug, citing an inability to determine the credibility of the allegations. This dismissal, after a cursory 14-day review, not only undermines the gravity of the situation but also highlights a disturbing trend of opacity and unaccountability at the highest levels of intelligence oversight.

The Procedural Anomalies: Red Flags Raised High

What we witness here is a series of procedural anomalies that cannot and should not be ignored. Gabbard’s actions, as described, deviate so far from standard operating procedures that they don’t just raise red flags; they practically paint the sky in warning colors. When intelligence of this nature — implications of foreign interference, whispers of executive overreach — is funneled through back channels directly to the epicenter of political power, the checks and balances that underpin our democratic institutions are not just bypassed; they are blatantly ignored.

The Political Fallout: A Divided Response

As expected, the political response has been polarized. While Democrats, led by voices like Sen. Mark Warner, decry the handling of the whistleblower complaint and the ensuing delays as a deliberate attempt to “bury the complaint,” Republicans have largely stood by Gabbard. This partisan divide is emblematic of a larger crisis, one where loyalty to individuals or parties supersedes commitment to transparency and accountability.

Conclusion: The Imperative of Oversight

This incident, as reported, is a glaring example of the systemic failures that plague our national intelligence operations. It’s not merely a question of procedural missteps or political maneuvering; it’s a stark illustration of the erosion of oversight, the potential for abuse of power, and the urgent need for reform. When the avenues for accountability are so easily obstructed, the very foundations of our democracy are at risk. The Gabbard gambit, as it stands, is a masterclass in obfuscation — a case study in how to bury the truth under layers of bureaucracy and political loyalty. It is a reminder, if ever we needed one, of the importance of vigilance, transparency, and the unyielding pursuit of accountability in the face of obfuscation.
