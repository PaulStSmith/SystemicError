'That didn't last long’: Trump stuns with abrupt axing of his own IRS chief

President Donald Trump is firing a former Missouri Congressman whom he nominated to be commissioner of the IRS, according to a New York Times report on Friday.

Trump earlier this year nominated Billy Long to be commissioner of the agency, ousting Danny Werfel from the position. Long was confirmed by the Senate and sworn in just last month.

Four people familiar with the matter told The Times that Trump is removing Long, and he plans to nominate Long for an ambassadorship. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will serve as acting commissioner until the administration finds a suitable replacement, according to the report.

Long sent multiple emails to all IRS employees allowing them to leave work early on Friday afternoon, the Times reported.

“Please enjoy a 70-minute early exit tomorrow. That way you’ll be rested for my 70th birthday on Monday!” Long told workers on Thursday.

The news drew swift reaction from internet observers.

David Mark‪, managing editor at the Washington Examiner Magazine, wrote on Bluesky, "That was fast."

CPA Albert J. Campo, CPA, echoed the sentiment, writing: "Well that certainly didn’t last long."

As did tax expert Josh Youngblood, who wrote: "Wow. That didn’t last very long."

Alex Jacquez, chief of policy and advocacy at Groundwork, joked on X, "More like Billy Not-For-Long!"

‪Helen Kennedy‬, a former New York Daily News and Boston Herald journalist, wrote on Bluesky, "I shudder to think what Billy Long refused to do for Trump."

Kennedy flagged that the IRS has had six different people in charge this year.

"It is August," she quipped.

CPA Mike Sylvester added on Bluesky, "My goodness... I have a series of posts about the absolute dumpster fire the IRS will be next year; and it looks like that dumpster fire might have just increased in size!"

Meanwhile, Missouri educator Jon Turner wrote on Bluesky, "For those of us who have known Billy for a long time, this comes with little surprise."

Our Analysis:

The Carousel of Chaos: Trump's IRS Commissioner Shuffle

In what reads like a script torn straight from the most convoluted political satire, President Donald Trump has once again sparked a wildfire in the bureaucratic underbrush. This time, the blaze has engulfed the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), an agency no stranger to controversy but perhaps unaccustomed to the dizzying speed of musical chairs currently being played at its helm. The latest episode in this absurdist drama involves the swift firing of Billy Long, a former Missouri Congressman handpicked by Trump, only to be dismissed faster than one can say "tax audit."

The Flash-in-the-Pan Commissioner

Billy Long, whose tenure as IRS Commissioner could be clocked with a stopwatch, was confirmed by the Senate and sworn in merely last month. His rapid departure is not just a reflection of the Trump administration's capricious nature but a glaring spotlight on the instability rattling the bones of a vital governmental body. The New York Times reports that Long is being shuffled off to an ambassadorship, a move that smells suspiciously like a consolation prize or, more cynically, a quiet exile.

The Revolving Door and Its Consequences

This administrative merry-go-round is not without its casualties. The IRS, tasked with the Herculean job of managing the nation's taxes, has seen six different leaders this year alone. Helen Kennedy, with the brutal succinctness of a seasoned journalist, notes, "It is August." This revolving door of leadership not only undermines the stability of the IRS but also jeopardizes its operational integrity. As CPA Mike Sylvester ominously predicts, the IRS is on track to become an "absolute dumpster fire" next year, a forecast that should alarm anyone with even a cursory understanding of the agency's importance.

The Reaction: Between Humor and Horror

The internet's reaction, ranging from bemused quips to dire warnings, underscores the surreal nature of the situation. While the likes of Alex Jacquez and Albert J. Campo offer wry smiles with their puns, there's a palpable undercurrent of concern. Jon Turner's remark about Long's unsurprising short stint speaks volumes about the predictability of chaos within the current administration.

The Underlying Issue

At the heart of this farce lies a serious issue: the politicization of critical government agencies. When figures like Billy Long are installed and disposed of with all the thoughtfulness of changing socks, it sends a clear message about the administration's disregard for the agencies' missions and the public good. Helen Kennedy's shudder, contemplating what Long might have refused to do, hints at the darker implications of such instability.

Conclusion: A Government in Disarray

The IRS debacle is but a symptom of a larger disease eating away at the fabric of the U.S. government. This constant state of upheaval not only erodes public trust but also hampers the ability of crucial agencies to function effectively. As long as this carousel of chaos continues to spin, the prospects for stability and efficiency in governance seem as fleeting as Billy Long's tenure as IRS Commissioner. In this ongoing saga of political musical chairs, the only winners are those who find humor in the absurdity. For the rest of us, the joke wears thin.
