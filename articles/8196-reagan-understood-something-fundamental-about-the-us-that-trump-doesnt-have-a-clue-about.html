Reagan understood something fundamental about the US that Trump doesn’t have a clue about

This week’s shooting of two National Guard members by a gunman identified by the authorities as an Afghan national was horrific.

But Trump’s response has been disproportionate and bigoted. He vows to “permanently pause migration from all Third World Countries.” He intends to deport legal immigrants born in countries the White House deems “high risk.”

He threatens to strip U.S. citizenship from naturalized migrants “who undermine domestic tranquillity.” He plans to deport foreigners deemed to be “non-compatible with Western Civilization.” He wants to detain even more migrants in jail — in the U.S. or in other countries — without due process.

In addition to the unconstitutionality of such actions, these threats stir up the worst nativist impulses in America — blaming and scapegoating entire groups of people for the act of one gunman.

Apart from Native Americans, we are all immigrants — all descended from “foreigners.” Some of our ancestors came here eagerly; some came because they were no longer safe in their homelands; some came enslaved.

Almost all of us are mongrels — of mixed nationalities, mixed ethnicities, mixed races, mixed creeds. While we maintain our own traditions, we also embrace the ideals of this nation.

Here’s how Ronald Reagan put it in a 1988 speech, in which he explained:

“I received a letter not long ago from a man who said, ‘You can go to Japan to live, but you cannot become Japanese. You can go to France to live and not become a Frenchman. You can go to live in Germany or Turkey, and you won’t become a German or a Turk.’ But then he added, ‘Anybody from any corner of the world can come to America to live and become an American.’”
A person becomes an American by adopting America’s principles, especially those principles summarized in the “self-evident truths” of the Declaration of Independence, such as “life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.” Carl Friedrich wrote that “To be an American is an ideal, while to be a Frenchman is a fact.”
As an immigrant friend once put it to me: “I was always an American; I was just born in the wrong country.”

I don’t think I’ve ever quoted Reagan before. He was wrong about so many things. Yet Reagan understood something fundamental to this nation that Trump doesn’t have a clue about: America is an idea — a set of aspirations and ideals — more than a nationality.

The only thing Trump knows is that he needs to fuel bigotry. His Straight White Male Christian Nationalism requires prejudice against anyone who’s not.

Like dictators before him, Trump’s road to tyranny is paved with stones hurled at “them.” His entire project depends on hate.

America is better than this.

We won’t buy Trump’s hate. To the contrary, we’ll call out bigots. We won’t tolerate intolerance. We’ll protect hardworking members of our community. We’ll alert them when ICE is lurking.

We will not succumb to the ravings of a venomous president who wants us to hate each other.

Our Analysis:

The Venomous Path of Trump’s Response: A Critical Analysis

In the wake of a tragic shooting involving a gunman identified as an Afghan national, former President Donald Trump has unveiled a slew of proposals that not only trample upon the foundational ideals of the United States but also dangerously fan the flames of bigotry and nativism. His response, characterized by both disproportionality and discrimination, demands a vigorous dissection.

Draconian Proposals Under the Guise of National Security

Trump’s reactionary stance involves a “permanent pause” on migration from Third World countries, a sweeping and indiscriminate measure that paints vast swaths of the globe with a broad, prejudiced brush. The notion of deporting legal immigrants from “high risk” nations as determined by the White House is not only a gross violation of human rights but also an affront to the very essence of the American melting pot.

Furthermore, Trump’s threat to strip U.S. citizenship from naturalized migrants who supposedly “undermine domestic tranquility” is a chilling echo of dark chapters in world history where citizenship was weaponized to serve xenophobic ends. The idea of deporting foreigners deemed “non-compatible with Western Civilization” is both vague and venomously subjective, a policy that reeks of ethnocentrism and cultural supremacy.

Detaining migrants without due process, whether in the U.S. or abroad, is a blatant disregard for the rule of law and international human rights standards. These proposals are not only unconstitutional but also fundamentally un-American.

Stirring the Pot of Nativism and Bigotry

Trump’s rhetoric and proposed policies serve to exacerbate the worst nativist impulses in America, scapegoating entire groups for the actions of an individual. This approach is not just divisive; it’s destructively reductive, reducing complex human beings to mere stereotypes and nationalities.

The article astutely invokes the words of Ronald Reagan, a figure seldom quoted in progressive circles, yet relevant in highlighting the stark contrast between Trump’s vision and the more inclusive, albeit imperfect, aspirations of past leadership. Reagan’s acknowledgment of America as an idea, a set of ideals open to all who subscribe to its core principles, stands in direct opposition to Trump’s exclusionary, fear-mongering tactics.

America: An Idea, Not a Fortress

The fundamental flaw in Trump’s perspective is his failure to recognize America as more than a nationality — it’s an ideal, a promise of liberty, opportunity, and equality. This vision of America is antithetical to Trump’s doctrine of division and hate. It’s a vision built on the acknowledgment that diversity is not a threat but a strength, a recognition that America’s greatness derives from its ability to evolve and embrace.

The author’s invocation of an immigrant’s sentiment, “I was always an American; I was just born in the wrong country,” serves as a poignant reminder of the beacon of hope and opportunity the United States has historically represented for countless individuals fleeing persecution, poverty, or despair.

Conclusion: Rejecting Trump’s Bigoted Vision

In essence, Trump’s response to the shooting and his broader policy recommendations are not just misguided; they are morally reprehensible. They seek to undermine the principles of inclusivity, diversity, and fundamental human rights that have long been cornerstones of the American ethos.

As the article rightly concludes, America must stand firm against the tide of hatred and intolerance. It’s imperative to call out bigotry, to protect and support the members of our communities who contribute tirelessly to our nation’s fabric. We must refuse to succumb to the venomous rhetoric of a leader whose vision for America is predicated on division, fear, and prejudice.

In rallying against Trump’s hateful agenda, we reaffirm our commitment to an America that values and cherishes all its people, recognizing that our strength lies in our diversity, our openness, and our unwavering belief in the ideals of liberty and justice for all.
