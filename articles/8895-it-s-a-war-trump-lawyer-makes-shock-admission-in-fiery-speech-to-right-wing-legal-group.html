'It's a war!' Trump lawyer makes shock admission in fiery speech to right-wing legal group

FILE PHOTO: Todd Blanche, then attorney for President-elect Donald Trump, leaves the New York State Appellate Division of the Supreme Court in New York City, U.S., January 7, 2025. REUTERS/Adam Gray/File Photo

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche delivered a fiery defense of the Justice Department’s latest actions at the Federalist Society’s annual lawyers’ conference, using colorful language to dismiss accusations of weaponizing the justice system.

Blanche began with a provocative statement about the previous administration’s Justice Department, declaring, “What happened the past four years within the United States Department of Justice, I’m going to say a Latin term: bats---t crazy” — drawing laughter from the staunchly conservative audience. Politico reported on the speech.

Addressing claims of political prosecution, Blanche passionately argued, “When I read now that we’re weaponizing, I feel like I’m being gaslit, because we’re doing exactly the opposite. I take umbrage at the idea that the work that our prosecutors are doing is weaponization, because I have receipts. I know what happened the past couple years. I’ve lived it.”

He was particularly critical of judges, asserting, “They have a robe on but they are more political or certainly as political as the most liberal governor or [district attorney] … There’s a group of judges that are repeat players, and that’s obviously not by happenstance, that’s intentional, and it’s a war, man.”

Blanche also took aim at professional legal organizations, specifically targeting the D.C. Bar, calling it “one of the most activist, obnoxious bars when it comes to going after conservative lawyers. It’s out of this world.”

The deputy attorney general complained about media coverage of immigration policies, suggesting journalists unfairly criticize the administration’s approach while ignoring similar past statements by Democratic politicians.

Throughout his speech, Blanche maintained a combative tone, repeatedly framing legal challenges as a “war” and portraying the Justice Department as fighting against what he sees as politically motivated resistance.

His remarks came in the context of recent high-profile indictments of Trump’s political opponents, including former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James, both of whom have pleaded not guilty to their respective charges.

Our Analysis:

Theatrics Over Truth: Blanche’s Performance at the Federalist Society

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s spirited defense at the Federalist Society’s conference is less a showcase of the Justice Department’s integrity and more a theatrical dive into the world of political rhetoric. His colorful language and provocative statements are designed to entertain and provoke rather than enlighten. Let’s dissect the spectacle, shall we?

Latin and Lunacy: A Bold Opening

Blanche’s opening salvo, labeling the previous administration’s DOJ actions as “bats---t crazy,” serves two purposes. Firstly, it panders to his audience, eliciting laughter with a blend of shock value and shared disdain. Secondly, it’s a strategic move to preemptively discredit any and all critiques of the past in order to set a stark contrast with the present DOJ’s actions. However, the irony of decrying the politicization of justice while delivering such a politically charged statement at a staunchly conservative gathering seems to have escaped Mr. Blanche.

Weaponization or Self-Defense?

The deputy attorney general’s passionate denial of weaponizing the justice system is an intriguing dance around the facts. His claim of being “gaslit” and having “receipts” for the DOJ’s actions might carry more weight if not for the setting and the manner in which it was delivered. The assertion would be more convincing in a forum dedicated to legal analysis and critique, not in a venue known for its political leanings. Furthermore, Blanche’s lament about facing political prosecution rings hollow in the context of recent indictments against notable political figures, suggesting a selective approach to what constitutes “weaponization.”

Judges in the Crosshairs

Blanche’s critique of the judiciary is perhaps the most alarming aspect of his speech. By attacking judges as “more political” than politicians, he undermines the very foundation of the American legal system—the impartiality of the bench. This isn’t just throwing shade; it’s tossing the whole lamp. His comments about “repeat players” and intentional actions suggest a conspiracy at the heart of the judiciary, a claim that, if true, would warrant serious investigation and not merely be used as a punchline at a conference.

The D.C. Bar and Immigration: Selective Outrage

The attack on the D.C. Bar as “activist” and “obnoxious” is particularly rich, coming from a figure who should be the epitome of legal professionalism and restraint. This dismissal of professional legal criticism as mere activism is a dangerous precedent, suggesting that any legal body that does not align with his views is inherently biased and, therefore, invalid.

On immigration, Blanche’s complaint about media coverage is a classic deflection technique. Criticizing journalists for holding the current administration accountable, while pointing to past statements by Democrats, misses the point. Media scrutiny is not about scoring political points; it’s about holding those in power to account, regardless of their party affiliation.

A Combative Tone in Place of Coherent Policy

Throughout his speech, Blanche’s combative tone and framing of legal challenges as a “war” reveal a troubling approach to justice. Rather than focusing on the merits of the DOJ’s actions or the integrity of the legal process, he opts for a battleground mentality, portraying the department as under siege. This warlike stance is not conducive to the fair and impartial administration of justice; it’s the rhetoric of political campaigns, not legal governance.

In Conclusion, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s performance at the Federalist Society’s conference may have won him applause in the room, but it does little to inspire confidence in the Justice Department’s commitment to impartiality and the rule of law. His remarks reflect a profound misunderstanding of the roles of the DOJ, the judiciary, and the media in a democratic society. The real bats---t crazy aspect here isn’t what happened in the past—it’s that such a divisive and politicized speech is seen as a defense of the Justice Department’s actions.
