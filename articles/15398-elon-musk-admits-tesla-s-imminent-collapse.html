Elon Musk is looking for ways to boost electric vehicle sales amid growing competition

Tesla recorded a 9% drop in sales and a 71% collapse in net profit, raising concerns about its long-term viability.

Tesla's brand image has been tarnished by Elon Musk's political controversies, affecting the company's perception in key markets. Negative reactions to all things Musk are evident.

In the industry, Tesla's market share has fallen to 43%, facing fierce competition from Chinese and American manufacturers. It's precisely the US tariffs enacted by Trump that threaten the competitiveness of Tesla's batteries, hence the need to develop local production.

The latest brand surveys reveal that 60% of consumers have an unfavourable opinion of Musk, directly impacting the perception of his electric vehicle company.

This situation is particularly concerning in key markets, such as California and Europe, where the brand was previously popular and is now the subject of a smear campaign.

Our Analysis:

The House That Elon Built Is Crumbling

It finally happened. The canary in the cobalt mine stopped singing.

Tesla, once the gleaming beacon of clean energy innovation, now reads like a corporate autopsy in progress. A 9% drop in vehicle sales, a 71% plunge in net profit, and a CEO more interested in posting memes than running a car company. Elon Musk, the man who mistook his Twitter mentions for market fundamentals, is watching his empire slide into entropy.

And it couldn't happen to a more deserving demagogue.

Let’s be clear: Tesla didn’t just hit a bump in the road. It veered off a cliff of its own making. The competition was always going to tighten—Chinese manufacturers like BYD are relentless, and American giants are finally waking up. But what sank Tesla wasn’t just external pressure. It was Musk himself.

You don’t poison your own brand with extremist rhetoric, paranoid conspiracy theories, and performative tech bro tantrums without blowback. Musk transformed Tesla from a revolutionary automaker into a shrine to his ego. And now, the market is recoiling. Brand surveys show 60% of consumers hold an unfavorable view of Musk. That rot is spreading to Tesla's showroom floors, especially in places like California and Europe, where once-loyal buyers now associate the brand with arrogance and political toxicity.

What makes this all the more infuriating is who gets hurt. Not Musk. He'll be fine, insulated by billions and a fawning Reddit army still clinging to 2012-era messianic fantasies. No, it's the factory workers, engineers, and designers—the people who actually made Tesla great—who will pay the price. They're the ones facing layoffs, budget cuts, and instability, all while Musk rants about DEI and "wokeness" as if those were the reasons the Cybertruck is a punchline.

Tesla could have been an enduring symbol of the post-oil future. Instead, it’s become a cautionary tale: what happens when innovation is shackled to narcissism. The collapse isn’t just financial. It’s moral. It's cultural. It's reputational.

The house that Elon built is crumbling. And this time, no amount of spin, subsidies, or sycophants can stop the fall.
