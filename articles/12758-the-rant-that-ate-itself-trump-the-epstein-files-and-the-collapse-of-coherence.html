The Rant That Ate Itself: Trump, the Epstein Files, and the Collapse of Coherence

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Gaslighting on SteroidsThe Kitchen Sink ConspiracyThe Real Fear: Guilt by AssociationCollapse of CoherenceThe “Stable Genius” in Full CollapseConclusion: The Rot Behind the Rhetoric

If you’ve ever wondered what weaponized incoherence looks like, look no further than Donald Trump’s recent screed about the Epstein files. The man who once claimed to be the "stable genius" behind American greatness has now taken a nosedive into a swamp of conspiratorial slurry, slinging wild accusations, contradicting himself in real-time, and desperately trying to spin a narrative that no longer obeys logic, chronology, or basic sentence structure.

At the heart of this fever-dream is a rant — posted to Trump’s Truth Social account — that reads like it was dictated by a malfunctioning AI trained on Reddit conspiracies, Fox News chyrons, and a pinch of cocaine. Trump simultaneously claims that:

  1. The Epstein files don’t matter because "nobody cares."

  2. The Epstein files are fake.

  3. The Epstein files were written by Obama, Clinton, Comey, and a cabal of Democrats.

  4. The Epstein files prove a leftist conspiracy against him.

  5. The Epstein files should’ve been used against him, but weren’t.

  6. The Epstein files are being unfairly used against him by his own allies.

All of this in one sprawling, contradictory, barely-punctuated tirade that serves as a living monument to Trump’s utter detachment from reason.

Gaslighting on Steroids

Let’s start with the most insulting contradiction: Trump is outraged about files he claims nobody cares about. If nobody cares, why is he dedicating paragraphs to them? Why is he ranting about Pam Bondi and demanding fealty from every hanger-on in his orbit to combat these supposedly meaningless documents? It’s a classic Trump tactic — deny the significance of something even as you scream about it from every rooftop.

But it gets worse. Trump doesn’t just deny their importance; he claims they were authored by political enemies like Obama, Hillary Clinton, and James Comey. How exactly would these individuals be responsible for documents released by a federal court as part of a long-delayed judicial unsealing process? This isn’t the Steele dossier — it’s court procedure. Yet Trump, in his desperation, tries to reframe everything as part of an omnipotent left-wing psyop.

The Kitchen Sink Conspiracy

What makes this rant particularly unhinged is Trump’s need to drag in every grievance he’s ever had. He name-drops "THE LAPTOP FROM HELL," the Russia hoax, 51 intelligence agents, the Steele dossier, JFK, MLK, and somehow even manages to toss in nuclear policy and border security.

This is the conspiracy equivalent of emptying the entire fridge into one pot and calling it soup. It's not a coherent argument — it's an emotional purge masquerading as a strategy. When he asks, "Why didn’t the Radical Left release the Epstein Files if they had something damaging to MAGA?" he’s projecting his own panic. That question is rhetorical cover — if something emerges, he can say, “See? They sat on it for years!” If nothing does, he can claim vindication. Heads I win, tails you cheated.

The Real Fear: Guilt by Association

For all his ranting about Obama and Hillary, what Trump’s really afraid of is the long-standing association he had with Epstein — the parties, the photos, the quotes. He knows the files don’t have to say much to be damaging. The public already sees the connective tissue, and any further documentation could cement it. So, he’s preemptively muddying the waters, trying to discredit the material before anyone can read it.

His tactic is painfully familiar: accuse others of the very thing you fear being accused of. In this case, he's labeling the files as fake, accusing Democrats of fabricating them, and whining that his “so-called friends” are not defending him loudly enough. It’s the same strategy he used during the Mueller probe, the Ukraine scandal, the classified documents fiasco — deny, distract, deflect, and destroy.

Collapse of Coherence

Trump’s rant isn’t just desperate — it’s structurally incoherent. He jumps from Epstein to JFK to nuclear policy without so much as a transition. One minute he’s calling Epstein someone “nobody cares about,” and the next he’s claiming Epstein is the cornerstone of a grand conspiracy to destroy MAGA.

This isn’t just gaslighting. This is gaslighting that forgot it left the stove on.

In trying to attack the credibility of the Epstein files, Trump has created the illusion that they are both inconsequential and a threat so dire they must have been fabricated by his worst enemies. It’s a cognitive Mobius strip — a loop of bad faith, falsehood, and grievance that devours its own tail.

The “Stable Genius” in Full Collapse

Near the end of his rant, Trump signs off sarcastically as "the stable genius himself," a wink to his infamous self-designation that has aged like milk in the Sahara. But there’s no stability here — only mounting desperation. He’s lashing out in every direction, alienating allies, spinning wild narratives, and exposing the mental rot beneath the facade of MAGA machismo.

This is not the work of a cunning political operator. This is a man unraveling in real time.

And for once, the contradictions are loud enough that even his base might hear the echoes.

Conclusion: The Rot Behind the Rhetoric

Trump's handling of the Epstein file revelations is textbook projection fused with a bunker mentality. He denies, then blames. He belittles, then panics. He accuses others of creating fictions while spinning a tangled web of his own. But the Epstein documents are real. They weren’t written by Obama or conjured by Clinton. They’re the product of legal procedure — something Trump has never respected and now fears more than ever.

In a just world, the contradictions in Trump’s tirade would be enough to finally puncture the cult of personality. But if history has taught us anything, it's that facts are rarely enough to break through the fog of fanaticism. Still, his meltdown matters.

Because behind every rant like this, there’s always a deeper fear.

And fear is the one thing Trump can’t fake.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
