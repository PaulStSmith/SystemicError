'This is the big one': Ex-DHS official raises alarm over Trump's plans for LA

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 9, 20255 min read

Republican presidential candidate and former U.S. President Donald Trump attends the 2024 Senior Club Championship award ceremony at his Trump International Golf Club in West Palm Beach, Florida, U.S. March 24, 2024. REUTERS/Marco Bello

A former Department of Homeland Security (DHS) official during Donald Trump's first term as president claimed that what is happening in Los Angeles is exactly what the president has been hoping for so he can unleash the military on Americans.

Speaking on MSNBC's "The Weekend," former DHS chief of staff Miles Taylor stated the protests in Los Angles against militarized ICE agents do not rise to the level of insurrection that Trump's inner circle is claiming, but it has handed him the pretext to do what he has always wanted to do.

As he explained, "This is, in my opinion, the single most significant act you've seen yet in the Trump administration. People are going to start to come to that realization over the next few days. This is the big one, this is the one that people like me were warning about when I came forward first in 2020 and started talking about the things I was most worried about that I saw in the first Trump administration that the president wanted to do."

"At the top of that list was deploying the U.S. military on U.S. soil to enforce domestic law," he recalled. "We had stopped Donald Trump in 2019 from invoking the Insurrection Act because we and our lawyers didn't think the way he wanted to do it was legal. In fact, I rushed to the White House as he was planning to make the State of the Union address and he wanted to insert a line about how he was invoking the act and deploying the U.S. military on U.S. soil. We felt like that was a dangerous slippery slope so that Donald Trump would end up taking control of national law enforcement."

"Make no mistake, his own lieutenants were worried he would create a de facto police state if he was going to be deploying the military on U.S. soil," he added.

"What is happening in Los Angeles is not a rational response to what they're seeing on the ground," he pointed out. "This is indeed, in my view, pre-planned to be able to give the president justification to invoke the Insurrection Act and it made sense that where they decided to do these raids was Los Angeles."

"I very firmly believe in the White House they suspected the response would be protests, and then they could use the protests as a response to use the Insurrection Act," he elaborated. "But this is much, much bigger than Los Angeles. I'm telling you, from the first term, if Donald Trump had gotten the authority to use the military on U.S. soil to enforce domestic law, it was going to go much beyond enforcement of immigration law. That was our fear and we are seeing potentially the early innings of that play out in real-time."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Shadow of Autocracy Looms Over Los Angeles

The recent comments by Miles Taylor, a former Department of Homeland Security (DHS) official, on MSNBC's "The Weekend" have thrown a stark light on the Trump administration's darkest ambitions. Taylor's revelations suggest a scenario that reads more like a playbook for autocracy than the actions of a government in a democratic society. Let’s dissect the core issues and implications of his statements.

Deploying the Military as a Domestic Police Force: A Dangerous Precedent

Taylor's assertion that the protests in Los Angeles against militarized ICE agents do not constitute an insurrection yet have been used as a pretext for potential military deployment is chilling. It underscores a fundamental truth: The Trump administration eyed the military as a tool for domestic control, not just for defense against external threats or genuine national emergencies.

The fact that high-ranking officials like Taylor felt compelled to intervene to prevent the invocation of the Insurrection Act speaks volumes about the internal conflicts within the Trump White House. It reveals a battle between preserving democratic norms and advancing a dangerous agenda that could have led to a de facto police state.

The Strategic Utilization of Protests

Taylor’s commentary sheds light on a cynical strategy: exploiting protests as a justification for extreme measures. This tactic is not new in the history of authoritarian regimes, but to hear such a strategy attributed to the American presidency is both unprecedented and deeply concerning.

The choice of Los Angeles as the location for ICE raids, anticipating protests to justify further draconian measures, reveals a manipulative approach to governance. It’s a calculated move designed to engineer a crisis, then use that crisis as a rationale for expanding executive power.

The Bigger Picture: Beyond Immigration Law Enforcement

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Taylor's revelations is the implication that deploying the military on U.S. soil was intended to go "much beyond enforcement of immigration law." This admission points to a broader, more insidious ambition to use the military for wide-ranging domestic control.

The scenario Taylor paints is one where the initial use of the military against protestors could set a precedent for further deployments, effectively blurring the lines between military and police roles. Such a move would not only erode civil liberties but could also lead to an escalation of violence and unrest.

Conclusion: A Warning Heeded or Ignored?

Miles Taylor’s warnings paint a grim picture of a presidency flirting with autocracy. While these plans were ultimately not realized, the mere fact they were considered—and in some respects, acted upon—should serve as a wake-up call.

The use of military forces for domestic law enforcement, particularly under the guise of quelling protests, represents a threat to the very foundations of American democracy. It’s a scenario that requires not only scrutiny but active resistance from all who value democratic principles.

In analyzing these revelations, we must ask ourselves: How close did we come to crossing the Rubicon? And more importantly, how do we ensure that such a threat to democracy is never posed again?

Taylor’s testimony is not just a reflection on past dangers; it’s a cautionary tale for the future. In the face of such revelations, complacency is not an option. Vigilance and accountability for those who would use their power to undermine democratic institutions are paramount.
