'Sheer cruelty': Outrage as GOP votes to gut Planned Parenthood

As the U.S. House Energy and Commerce Committee on Wednesday voted along party lines to advance legislation that includes a measure which seeks to cut Medicaid funding for the abortion and healthcare provider Planned Parenthood, reproductive rights defenders are sounding the alarm on the impacts that such a move would have.

"This provision is about punishing Planned Parenthood health centers for providing abortion care, and threatening access to affordable birth control, wellness checkups, and cancer screenings for millions of people across the country in the process," said Alexis McGill Johnson, the president and CEO of Planned Parenthood, in a statement on Wednesday.

"It is indefensible how far abortion opponents, who claim to care about women and families, are willing to go to shut down health centers and line the pockets of billionaires and big corporations," McGill added.

The measure is part of a sweeping GOP spending and tax cuts bill currently making its way through Congress. The House Energy and Commerce Committee's portion of the legislation also includes cuts to Medicaid and takes back unspent funds from Inflation Reduction Act grant programs.

The outlet NOTUS reported Wednesday that the measure pertaining to Planned Parenthood would bar the organization from receiving federal funds, even via Medicaid payments. According to the 19th, the language of the measure does not specifically call out Planned Parenthood, but is crafted to apply to the organization.

The proposed cuts would impact Planned Parenthood's ability to offer services like birth control, cancer screenings, and pap smears. Medicaid is already barred from providing funds for abortion care, which only account for a small percentage of the services that Planned Parenthood's affiliates provide.

"It's no surprise that a goal of this reconciliation bill is to force Planned Parenthood health centers to shut down. Republicans have been trying—unsuccessfully—to shut down Planned Parenthood for decades," said Mini Timmaraju, president and CEO of Reproductive Freedom for All, an abortion rights and reproductive freedom group. "Plain and simple, this legislation will mean millions of people will have nowhere to go for basic health care."

"The sheer cruelty and enormity of their actions, and the harm they will inflict on the very people they were elected to represent, is unconscionable," Timmaraju continued.

Rachana Desai Martin, chief U.S. program officer at the Center for Reproductive Rights, on Tuesday denounced the fact that Trump has already frozen millions in funding for Planned Parenthood through the Title X program.

Planned Parenthood has said freezing that funding "effectively blocks people from getting birth control, STI testing and treatment, and lifesaving cancer screenings."

Speaking Tuesday, before the committee advanced its portion of the bill, Martin said that the Center for Reproductive Rights stands with Planned Parenthood and all reproductive healthcare providers and that "these baseless, politically motivated attacks against reproductive health care providers must stop."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Irony and Impact of Targeting Planned Parenthood

In an audacious move that epitomizes the current political climate's disregard for essential healthcare services, the U.S. House Energy and Commerce Committee has taken a step that could significantly undermine the healthcare landscape for millions. On Wednesday, the committee voted, strictly along party lines, to advance a piece of legislation that includes a particularly contentious measure aimed squarely at Planned Parenthood, a primary provider of reproductive health services in the country. This move is not just a political maneuver but a direct assault on the healthcare access of many, under the guise of fiscal responsibility.

The Veiled Attack on Planned Parenthood

At the heart of this legislative effort is a measure that, while not naming Planned Parenthood directly, is unmistakably designed to restrict the organization's access to federal funds, notably through Medicaid. This tactic is not novel but part of a long-standing campaign by some political factions to cripple an organization they oppose on ideological grounds. The claim made by Alexis McGill Johnson, President and CEO of Planned Parenthood, encapsulates the situation: the provision aims to punish Planned Parenthood for providing abortion care, simultaneously jeopardizing access to a swath of vital health services for millions. This includes affordable birth control, wellness checkups, and cancer screenings—services that form the backbone of what Planned Parenthood offers to communities across the nation.

The Misguided Justification

The justification for such a measure is wrapped in the rhetoric of abortion opposition. However, this stance blatantly ignores the fact that Medicaid funds are already prohibited from being used for abortion care, except in very limited circumstances. The vast majority of services provided by Planned Parenthood and targeted by this legislation are essential, non-abortion related healthcare services. The irony is palpable: those who claim to champion life and family values are pushing for a measure that would effectively reduce access to health services that prevent diseases and save lives.

The Economic Fallacy

Adding an additional layer of absurdity to the situation is a preliminary estimate from the nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office (CBO), which suggests that this vendetta against Planned Parenthood will cost taxpayers $300 million over the next ten years. This figure flies in the face of any claims that the measure is about reducing government spending or fiscal conservatism. Instead, it highlights a willingness to incur additional costs to further a specific ideological agenda, even when such actions contradict purported fiscal principles.

The Real Victims

Behind the political theater and ideological battles are real people whose access to vital healthcare services is at risk. As Mini Timmaraju, president and CEO of Reproductive Freedom for All, aptly puts it, this legislation will leave millions without basic healthcare services. The cruelty of this potential outcome cannot be overstated, particularly when considering the populations that Planned Parenthood serves—often low-income individuals who have few, if any, alternatives for affordable healthcare.

Conclusion: A Call for Rational Policy

The ongoing assault on Planned Parenthood under the guise of fiscal responsibility and moral high ground is neither fiscally nor morally justifiable. It is a targeted attack on an organization that provides essential health services to millions, wrapped in the veneer of political expediency. The implications of such a measure, should it become law, extend far beyond the political arena, threatening the health and well-being of countless individuals across the country. It is a stark reminder of the need for policy grounded in rationality, compassion, and a genuine commitment to the public good, rather than ideological zealotry. The actions of the House Energy and Commerce Committee, as reported, are neither an exercise in good governance nor in fiscal prudence. They are a systemic error that should not only be called out but rectified.
