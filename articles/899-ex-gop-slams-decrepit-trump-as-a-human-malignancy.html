Ex-GOP slams 'decrepit' Trump as a 'human malignancy'

Based on an article by David Badash

The Man, the Machine, and the Useful Distraction

Context

Steve Schmidt, now a Democrat after a career in Republican politics, used a public rant to describe Donald Trump as physically and morally collapsing, tied that image to a white-hot display of White House spectacle, and warned that Trump-aligned forces are already thinking past the constitutional limit to 2028. The target of the speech is obvious. The problem is bigger than the insult pileup.

Power Still Sits at the Top

The first fact that matters is simple: Donald Trump is not some commentator’s grievance. He is the president of the United States. That is actual institutional power. When Schmidt calls him “hated,” “decrepit,” and unfit, he is attacking a man who still commands federal authority, shapes agencies, and gives cover to loyalists who know exactly what they are doing.

That matters because power is not measured by how insulted someone feels. It is measured by who can issue orders, reward obedience, and turn private vendettas into public policy. Trump has that power. Schmidt does not. The article’s emotional energy is aimed upward, but the damage flows downward.

The Enablers Are the Story

Schmidt at least gestures toward the real mechanism when he blames the “cynical men” and “vandals” who “curated this fascism from day one.” That is the right instinct, even if the rest of his language drifts into performance. Trump did not build this alone. He was enabled by strategists, media loyalists, donors, operatives, and party officials who treated authoritarian behavior as a branding opportunity instead of a disqualifier.

Steve Bannon’s casual talk of a 2028 run, despite the constitutional ban, is not a joke. It is a signal. The point is not whether Trump personally intends to violate the rules. The point is that his circle keeps testing how far law can be mocked before anyone with institutional authority bothers to stop it.

Spectacle as Cover

The White House “Octagon” and birthday theatrics are not just vulgar. They are governing by stunt. Schmidt is right to call it a symbol of destruction, but the symbol works because it distracts. The stagecraft turns a presidency into a permanent campaign prop, where outrage over the circus replaces scrutiny of the machinery underneath it.

That is the deeper con. The administration can flood the zone with spectacle while policy, patronage, and institutional capture continue in the background. People are invited to fixate on Trump’s ankles, his slurring, his naps, his age. Those may be true observations. They are also politically convenient ones, because they keep attention on the body of the man instead of the structure he occupies.

Misdirection by Personality

Schmidt’s attack is not wrong to describe Trump as cruel, degraded, and unfit. But it still risks reproducing the same trap Trump thrives in: personality as destiny. The article wants Trump’s decay to explain Trump’s rule. That is emotionally satisfying and politically incomplete.

The real danger is not that a bad man is embarrassing himself in public. The danger is that institutions keep functioning around him. The bureaucracy absorbs the insult, the party rationalizes the abuse, and the media turns each outrage into another day of content. In that environment, even accurate contempt can become a form of misdirection if it obscures the collaborators who keep the system alive.

The Pattern

This is what modern authoritarian politics often looks like in the United States: not a clean break, but a tolerated degradation. The leader becomes a grotesque mascot, while the professional class around him normalizes the damage, excuses the cruelty, and waits to see whether the constitutional line will actually hold. That is not confusion. It is accommodation.

Schmidt’s tirade lands hardest where it names the enablers, not where it performs disgust. The larger lesson is colder and more useful than the rhetoric: systems rot when powerful people decide that preserving their side matters more than preserving the rules. Trump is the face of that decay. The people who built the room for him are the reason it keeps spreading.
