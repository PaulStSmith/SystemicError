'Spiritual warfare': MAGA host calls Texas floods an 'attack' on Trump

Real America’s Voice/screen grab

MAGA television host Shemane Nugent suggested that the recent deadly flood in Texas was an "attack" on President Donald Trump.

During her Sunday Faith & Freedom program, Nugent spoke to Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller about the tragedy.

"This is a terrible biblical tragedy," she asserted. "I believe it's spiritual warfare. Now, Satan, you know, comes to steal, kill, and destroy. And how best to do that, Sid, but to come after God's children, especially innocent young girls who just wanted to learn scriptures."

The host brought up the flood again during an interview with a guest selling protection from EMPs and other energy weapons.

"I really feel like this is just ramping up," Nugent said of an EMP attack. "Something's got to happen."

"Because with Trump back in office, the enemy, the deep state, doesn't want him to succeed," she continued. "And they know that their days are numbered. So, you know, I feel like what happened in Texas recently, that's just the beginning. We're going to see a lot more attacks like that."

Our Analysis:

A Flood of Nonsense: Shemane Nugent's Conspiracy Theorizing

In the latest episode of "How Far Can Conspiracy Theories Go?" MAGA television host Shemane Nugent takes the cake—or should we say, the ark?—with her breathtakingly bizarre suggestion that the recent deadly floods in Texas were not a result of, say, climate change or poor infrastructure, but an "attack" on President Donald Trump. Yes, you read that right. Not an attack by, but on Trump. Because, as we all know, natural disasters check the political calendar before making landfall.

The "Spiritual Warfare" Angle

Nugent's assertion, made during her Sunday Faith & Freedom program, paints the tragedy as a "terrible biblical tragedy" and a form of "spiritual warfare." This isn't just a stretch; it's an Olympic-level gymnastic routine of logic. To imply that Satan himself is orchestrating meteorological phenomena to target "God's children," particularly "innocent young girls who just wanted to learn scriptures," is to dive headfirst into the deep end of the conspiracy theory pool without checking for water.

The EMP Attack Connection

As if the claim wasn't already standing on shaky ground, Nugent doubles down by linking the flood to fears of an EMP (electromagnetic pulse) attack, during a conversation with a guest selling EMP protection. The leap from natural disaster to speculative energy weapon warfare is not just a jump; it's a quantum leap across realms of reality. Nugent suggests that this is all part of a grand scheme to sabotage Trump's hypothetical return to office, a narrative that fits snugly into the broader "deep state" conspiracy theory beloved by certain fringes of the political right.

The Deep State Boogeyman

Nugent's narrative relies heavily on the existence of a so-called "deep state" that is both omnipotent and desperate, capable of manipulating weather patterns and keen on preventing Trump's success at any cost. This is not just an overestimation of the deep state's supposed capabilities; it's attributing them with god-like powers. It's one thing to critique political opponents or government entities for actual policies or actions, but it's another to accuse them of being behind natural disasters.

The Bottom Line

What Nugent's comments underscore is not just a worrisome trend of divorcing from reality in political commentary but a dangerous conflation of spiritual and political warfare. By framing natural disasters as attacks against specific political figures or ideologies, it not only trivializes the real human suffering and loss that occurs but also muddies the waters of rational discourse. It's one thing to engage in political hyperbole; it's quite another to suggest that the weather is taking sides in political debates.

In essence, Shemane Nugent's remarks are a perfect storm of conspiracy theory, political idolatry, and spiritual manipulation. It's critical that we demand better from our public figures and media personalities. The stakes are too high and the waters too deep for us to entertain such baseless claims without pushing back with facts, reason, and a dash of sanity.
