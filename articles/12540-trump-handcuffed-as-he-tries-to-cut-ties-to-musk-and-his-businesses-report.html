Trump handcuffed as he tries to 'cut ties' to Musk and his businesses: report

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump's attempt to exact revenge on former advisor Elon Musk by hitting in him the pocketbook has come up short, according a new report from the Wall Street Journal.

Following the tech billionare's departure from being a daily presence in the Trump White House, the relationship between the two men soured to the point where Musk went so far as to post on his social media platform X that the president is in the Jeffrey Epstein files –– which has contributed to the president's latest problems.

As the Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, not long after Musk's departure Trump fired a shot across his bow by using Truth Social to announce that he was contemplating pulling the plug on government contracts with the billionaire's business.

The Journal's Brian Schwartz, Shalini Ramachandran and Micah Maidenberg reported "Just days after President Trump in early June raised the prospect of cutting ties with Musk’s businesses, the Trump administration initiated a review of SpaceX’s contracts with the federal government," adding, "The review was intended to identify potential waste in the multibillion-dollar agreements the company has with the government, the people said."

The report goes on to note that the review revealed that a great many contracts couldn’t be eliminated "because they are critical to the Defense Department and NASA."

The Journal is reporting that spreadsheets labeled as "scorecards" were used to log the value of the contracts and whether Musk's competitors could step in as a replacement.

"Instead of terminating SpaceX contracts after reviewing the data, White House and agency officials, including those at the Pentagon, determined that most of the deals were vital to the missions of the Defense Department and NASA, according to some of the people familiar with the matter. One of the people said some SpaceX contracts could face continued scrutiny," the report notes.

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Petty Politics: Trump vs. Musk

In a story that reads like a script from a political drama too absurd for even the most imaginative screenwriter, Donald Trump's vendetta against Elon Musk fizzles into the annals of pettiness, revealing not just the former president's vengeful instincts but also the inherent resilience of bureaucratic inertia against the whims of the powerful. According to a Wall Street Journal report, Trump's attempts to financially hamstring Musk by severing government contracts with SpaceX, following the billionaire's critical remarks about Trump, have spectacularly backfired.

The Plot

After Musk's departure from the daily operations of the Trump White House and his subsequent damning post on X (formerly Twitter), alleging Trump's inclusion in the Epstein files, Trump retaliated in a manner befitting a reality TV showdown rather than the governance of a superpower. He took to Truth Social, threatening to end SpaceX's government contracts, a move ostensibly aimed at striking Musk where it would hurt: his pocketbook. The administration then initiated a review of SpaceX’s contracts, ostensibly to identify "potential waste" in the multi-billion-dollar agreements the company holds with the federal government.

The Anticlimax

However, like many of Trump's most grandiose schemes, this one hit a wall of reality. The review found that the contracts with SpaceX were too critical to the missions of the Defense Department and NASA to be axed. Despite Trump's bluster, the infrastructure of the U.S. government proved resilient to his whims. The contracts were deemed vital, and Musk's business empire remained unscathed, with the report noting that spreadsheets labeled as "scorecards" were used to assess the value and irreplaceability of these contracts.

What This Reveals

This episode lays bare several unsettling truths about the state of American politics and governance:

  • Personal Vendettas Over Public Good: Trump's attempt to punish Musk for personal offenses underscores a governance style that prioritizes personal vendettas over the public interest, a disturbing trend in the conduct of public affairs.

  • The Illusion of Absolute Power: It reveals the limitations of presidential power in the face of established bureaucratic processes and the fundamental importance of these contracts to national interests. This is a double-edged sword; while it serves as a check on unbridled executive power, it also shows the difficulty in enacting swift policy changes, even when potentially necessary.

  • The Role of the Media: The Wall Street Journal's reporting on this matter highlights the crucial role of investigative journalism in uncovering the machinations behind closed doors. Without such reporting, these petty yet revealing episodes would remain hidden from public view.

Conclusion

In the grand scheme of things, the Trump-Musk saga is a sideshow, a distraction from the pressing issues facing the nation and the world. Yet, it serves as a potent reminder of the pettiness that can pervade the highest levels of government and the resilience of the system in the face of such pettiness. It underscores the need for a political culture that elevates public interest over personal grievances and for a vigilant media to hold those in power accountable. In the end, Trump's failed vendetta against Musk is a spectacle of petty politics, but it's one that offers valuable lessons about the state of American democracy.
