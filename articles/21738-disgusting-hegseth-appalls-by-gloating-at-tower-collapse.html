'Disgusting': Hegseth appalls by gloating at tower collapse

Based on an article by Jake Johnson, Common Dreams

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Bombing Infrastructure, Then Calling It Strategy

Power Makes the Target

The reporting here is not about a mysterious escalation. It is about a decision. Donald Trump authorized the strikes. Pete Hegseth celebrated them publicly. The United States hit bridges, railways, power facilities, and a port surveillance tower in Iran, while the Associated Press reported at least eight deaths in the latest wave alone.

That is the basic power structure: the White House ordered the campaign, the Pentagon amplified it, and civilians absorbed the damage.

The Civilian-Military Lie

The article repeats the usual official dodge: maybe the bridges had “nominal military use,” maybe the infrastructure was dual-use, maybe the label makes the destruction acceptable. That framing is designed to launder what is plainly being done. When a strike cuts roads, disrupts emergency services, blocks food and fuel, and leaves people without electricity in extreme heat, the civilian harm is not collateral detail. It is the effect.

This is not a misunderstanding about targeting. It is a political choice to treat civilian life as an acceptable pressure point.

Hegseth’s Gloating Is the Tell

Hegseth posting a collapse video is not an embarrassing side note. It is evidence of the regime’s moral posture. The point is not just to bomb; it is to broadcast contempt while doing it. That kind of online revelry is how state violence is normalized for domestic audiences. The cruelty is not hidden. It is performed.

The message is simple: if the destruction is public enough, the human cost can be converted into patriotic branding.

Trump’s Ultimatum Politics

Trump’s threat to “knock out all of [Iran’s] power plants” unless Iran came “to the table” makes the coercive logic explicit. This is not diplomacy backed by force. It is infrastructure sabotage used as leverage. The aim is to break a population’s material conditions and call it negotiation.

That matters because it reveals the real function of the strikes: not defense, not precision, but punishment. The civilians of Iran are being made to pay for the political goals of Washington.

War Crimes Are Not a Styling Issue

Kenneth Roth’s point is the important one: even if commanders dress up the targets as militarily relevant, the attacks can still produce large-scale civilian harm and may expose decision-makers to prosecution. That is not rhetoric. It is the legal and moral line the administration is trying to erase.

The source is strongest when it stops pretending this is fog-of-war complexity. A war crime is not transformed into legitimate statecraft because the press office uses technical language. Deliberately degrading civilian infrastructure during extreme heat is not a strategic accident. It is coercion by other means.

What This Story Reveals

The deeper pattern is familiar: great-power violence framed as necessity, civilian suffering translated into tactical abstraction, and elite impunity protected by language that blurs responsibility. The administration does not need to admit it is targeting ordinary people directly; it only needs to make sure ordinary people cannot live normally.

That is the political meaning of the story. The issue is not whether the Pentagon can produce a cleaner explanation. It is that a government led by Trump, enabled by Hegseth, is openly using the destruction of civilian life as an instrument of policy and expecting the public to call it seriousness.
