'Too damn bad': Conservative rips 'thin-skinned whiner-in-chief' Trump

Former GOP Communications Director Tara Setmayer (YouTube Screengrab)

Former GOP Communications Director Tara Setmayer whacked President Donald Trump’s 2025 attack on comedians and entertainment as a sign of a weak man on MS NOW’s “The Weekend.”

“He’s done [this] from day one,” said Setmayer. “He went after the media, calling them the ‘enemy of the people.’ which was straight out of the dictator playbook that we’ve seen from the 1930s and 40s with Mussolini and Hitler. This is exactly what strongmen do. And Donald Trump is the most thin-skinned, boring whiner. He is a whiner in chief. He whines constantly about anyone who dares to say anything negative about him, yet he tries to portray that he’s this big, tough guy. Really? Because that doesn’t sound like someone who is a well-adjusted adult who can handle criticism.”

Setmayer spoke directly to Trump’s Christmas Eve threat to terminate the broadcast licenses of networks featuring comedians who are “negative to President Donald J. Trump.”

“He doesn’t care about the Constitution. He doesn’t care about the freedoms that we enjoy. Here he is carrying out his dictator fantasies because he’s surrounded by people who are a bunch of yes men and women who are letting him do whatever the hell he wants,” ranted Setmayer. “They come for the arts. That’s one of the things that strongmen and authoritarian governments do. They come for the arts and comedy because it’s a freedom of expression. And they want to control all of that.”

She also blasted Trump as “a failed wannabe Broadway producer … living his fantasies out with the Kennedy center desecration.”

“And now with the late-night [comedy] hosts and the fact that they are able to make fun of him — we all know that him being mocked is one of the worst things ever, which is why we continue to do it. So, keep mocking him. And too damn bad if he doesn’t like it,” Setmayer said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Attack on Comedians: A Display of Authoritarian Tendencies

Former GOP Communications Director Tara Setmayer’s recent outcry on MS NOW’s “The Weekend” about President Donald Trump’s 2025 attack on comedians and the entertainment industry is a critical discourse that necessitates a deeper analysis. Setmayer’s comments illuminate the ongoing struggle between the political power wielded by figures such as Trump and the fundamental freedoms that define democratic societies. This analysis aims to dissect the implications of Trump’s actions and rhetoric, emphasizing the inherent dangers they pose to freedom of expression and democratic values.

Trump’s Authoritarian Playbook

First and foremost, Setmayer’s comparison of Trump’s tactics to those of historical dictators like Mussolini and Hitler is not only bold but alarmingly accurate. The strategy to undermine the media and entertainment industry, branding them as “the enemy of the people,” mirrors the authoritarian playbook where controlling the narrative is paramount. Trump’s thin-skinned responses to criticism and his portrayal as a “big, tough guy” are paradoxical, revealing a fragile ego behind the façade of strength. This behavior is not befitting of a leader in a democratic society; it’s indicative of a despot in disguise.

The Threat to Free Expression

The Christmas Eve threat to terminate the broadcast licenses of networks featuring comedians critical of Trump is a flagrant assault on the First Amendment. Setmayer’s outrage over this threat is justified and highlights a crucial point: Trump’s disregard for constitutional freedoms. The attempt to silence comedians, who are often the vanguard of societal critique and political satire, is a direct attack on freedom of expression. This move is less about safeguarding public decency and more about stifling dissent, emblematic of Trump’s authoritarian desires.

Mockery as Resistance

Setmayer’s encouragement of continued mockery towards Trump is not just a call for comedic relief; it’s a battle cry for defending democratic values. The significance of satire and the arts in challenging power cannot be overstated. When authoritarian figures attempt to control or censor the arts, they seek to eliminate a powerful tool for societal reflection and critique. The ability of comedians to mock Trump, despite his threats, showcases the resilience of free expression and the critical role it plays in a healthy democracy.

Conclusion: The Cost of Silence

Tara Setmayer’s scathing critique of Donald Trump’s latest attack on comedians and the entertainment industry serves as a stark reminder of the threats facing freedom of expression today. Trump’s actions are not just an assault on comedians but an attack on the very principles that underpin democratic societies. The cost of silence in the face of such authoritarian tactics is too great. It is incumbent upon all who value freedom and democracy to heed Setmayer’s call: to mock, to critique, and to resist, ensuring that the voices of dissent are never silenced by the whims of a would-be authoritarian.
