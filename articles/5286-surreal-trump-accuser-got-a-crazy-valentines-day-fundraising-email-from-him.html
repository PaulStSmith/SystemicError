'Surreal': Trump accuser got a 'crazy' Valentine’s Day fundraising email from him

She’s never signed up for his campaign emails and she’s never wanted to. Still, E. Jean Carroll posted on X that she got a Valentine’s Day note from President Donald Trump, the man she has accused of sexually assaulting her in a Bergdorf Goodman dressing room in the mid 1990s.

“Yes. This is a real email. No. I did not sign up on this mailing list. Yes. We are living in a crazy world,” said Carroll.

The email reads, in part, “I love you, and I was pretty sure you loved me back! Is everything okay?”

It isn’t clear how Carroll, who has successfully sued Trump for defamation twice, ended up on the campaign’s email list, but the campaign is known to purchase and trade email lists for fundraising.

“This is the stupidest time to be alive. The least plausible storyline. This entire era would never get greenlit,” said Dr. Karen Stenner, a political psychologist and behavioral scientist.

“Saying it’s surreal is an understatement,” commented Daniele Angrisani, founder of FocusAmerica.it

Several others expressed their disgust at the email.

Caroll’s civil defamation suit required a jury to find whether or not they believed that Trump assaulted her. It was ultimately called sexual abuse and she was awarded a combined total of $88.3 million. As of this month, she hasn’t seen a dime from Trump. Trump, who has appealed the case, lost at the 2nd Circuit Court of Appeals in September 2025, when it rejected his appeal, but the legal process continues.

He has petitioned the U.S. Supreme Court to overturn the judgment. They are scheduled to review the petition on February 20, 2026 in a closed-door conference. Typically, the high court takes up only a few such petitions annually.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Email Fiasco: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In what reads like a poorly scripted episode of the most absurd political drama, E. Jean Carroll, a woman who has twice successfully sued former President Donald Trump for defamation in connection to her accusations of sexual assault against him, received a Valentine’s Day email from Trump’s campaign. Yes, you read that correctly. The same man Carroll has accused of sexually assaulting her in the mid-1990s allegedly sent her a love note via email. Carroll’s bewildered response on X (formerly Twitter) encapsulated the sheer absurdity of the situation, affirming, “Yes. This is a real email. No. I did not sign up on this mailing list. Yes. We are living in a crazy world.”

Let’s dissect this episode, not just for its face-value shock but for what it reveals about the current state of political communication, privacy, and the ongoing saga of Donald Trump’s legal entanglements.

The Irony is Palpable

First, the irony. Carroll, who has been embroiled in legal battles against Trump, receiving such an email breaches so many levels of decency and common sense that one might be forgiven for thinking it’s a bad joke. Yet, it underscores a troubling reality about modern political campaigns: the indiscriminate and invasive use of personal data. Trump’s campaign is known to purchase and trade email lists for fundraising, a common practice that raises significant privacy concerns, especially when it results in such grotesquely inappropriate communication.

The Bigger Picture: Privacy in the Digital Age

This incident is a glaring reminder of the cavalier attitude toward privacy and consent in the digital age, particularly within political campaigns. The fact that Carroll, an outspoken critic and legal adversary of Trump, could end up on his campaign’s email list without her consent speaks volumes about the lack of safeguards in place to protect individuals’ data. It’s a stark illustration of how personal information can be commodified and used in ways that range from annoying to deeply disturbing.

Trump’s Legal Saga Continues

Beyond the immediate shock value, this episode is a mere footnote in the ongoing legal drama surrounding Trump. Carroll’s defamation suit against him, which a jury upheld as sexual abuse, resulting in a staggering $88.3 million award, epitomizes the serious allegations trailing Trump. Despite this, he has yet to pay Carroll a dime, appealing the case up to the U.S. Supreme Court, which is set to review his petition in a closed-door conference on February 20, 2026.

This legal battle is not just about Carroll and Trump; it’s emblematic of the broader issues of accountability, the treatment of sexual assault survivors, and the ability of the wealthy and powerful to evade consequences. Trump’s appeal to the Supreme Court, a move that only a fraction of petitions achieve, highlights the exceptional nature of his legal strategy, leveraging his resources and influence to fight a verdict that a jury, after due consideration of the evidence, deemed just.

Conclusion: A Stupid Time to Be Alive?

Dr. Karen Stenner’s remark that “This is the stupidest time to be alive” might resonate with many who find themselves bewildered by the surreal narratives unfolding in our political and social landscape. Yet, it’s crucial not to dismiss these events as merely absurd. They are symptomatic of systemic issues that demand serious attention and action.

The email Carroll received is a grotesque reminder of the ongoing challenges we face in addressing privacy, the use of personal data, and the need for accountability at the highest levels of power. As this saga unfolds, it’s essential to remain vigilant, critical, and engaged in advocating for a political and social environment that respects individual rights, privacy, and the rule of law.

In a world where the lines between the ludicrous and the alarming are increasingly blurred, events like these serve as a call to action. It’s time to demand more from our leaders, our legal systems, and ourselves.
