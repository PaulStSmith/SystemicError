The Oathless Circus: How Congress Enabled a Public Health Menace

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 8, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The world just watched a spectacle that should never have happened. A cabinet secretary sat before the Senate Finance Committee, red-faced and raging, spewing anti-scientific nonsense. But here’s the kicker: he wasn’t under oath. The committee chairman, in a staggering act of complicity, refused to swear him in. That single decision stripped the hearing of accountability. It gave him license to lie with impunity—and lie he did.

This isn’t just bad optics. It’s a rot at the core of governance. What unfolded was not oversight, but theater. And the price will be measured in sickness and death.

The Set-Up: A Hearing Without Teeth

Hearings are supposed to serve a purpose: oversight, transparency, accountability. They’re not casual chats. Testimony under oath carries weight because perjury has consequences. That safeguard ensures that what’s said in the chamber has at least the shadow of truth.

Except, in this case, the committee chair made sure there would be no such shadow. When asked by a senior senator to place the secretary under oath, the chair waved it off. “We’ll let him make his case in his own words,” he said. Translation: “We’ll let him lie without fear.”

This was not an accident. It was a deliberate choice. The chair knew that under oath, the secretary’s claims about vaccines, public health policy, and CDC dismissals could collapse into legal jeopardy. Better to give him a free pass than risk exposing the rot.

The Performance: Red-Faced Fury and Anti-Science Rhetoric

What followed was a three-hour meltdown disguised as testimony. The secretary dodged questions about his dismissal of CDC leadership. He fumbled basic vaccine data. He spread misinformation about childhood immunization. He lashed out at senators pressing him, raising his voice, jabbing his finger, sweating and sputtering as if cornered by truth itself.

Democrats hammered him for dismantling public health infrastructure. One senator accused him directly of “reckless disregard for science and the truth.” Others openly called for his resignation, describing him as a hazard to the health of the nation. Republicans weren’t far behind. Even members of his own supposed political base expressed alarm. One senator—a physician—warned that his rhetoric was endangering pregnant women and children.

And yet, because he wasn’t under oath, his words carried no risk. He could lie about vaccine safety, misrepresent infection rates, smear experts, and the only punishment was public shame—which, clearly, he lacked the capacity to feel.

The Loophole: No Oath, No Crime

This is the crux of the scandal. Without the oath, there is no perjury. Without perjury, there is no accountability. This loophole turns a hearing into a soapbox, a place where misinformation can be broadcast with the Senate’s official seal of legitimacy. It makes a mockery of the institution itself.

Think about it: ordinary citizens are sworn in for court appearances, depositions, and even local hearings. But a cabinet secretary, testifying about life-and-death matters of public health, was spared? That’s not just dereliction; it’s complicity.

The chairman’s refusal to swear in the secretary was not neutrality. It was protection. It was an act of partisan shielding that prioritized political theater over public health. And in doing so, it told the American people: “This chamber is fine with lies.”

The Stakes: Lives on the Line

This isn’t about political sparring. It’s about consequences in the real world. The secretary’s lies fuel vaccine hesitancy. His dismissals of medical experts embolden conspiracy theorists. His dismantling of health agencies creates chaos in a system that must be coordinated to function.

The result? Outbreaks of diseases once eradicated. Polio creeping back. Measles spreading in schools. Rubella threatening pregnancies. Each resurgence is preventable. Each resurgence is caused not by science failing, but by leadership failing.

When parents hear a cabinet official downplay vaccines, they hesitate. When they hesitate, children go unprotected. When children go unprotected, viruses spread. That is the chain of causation. And that chain leads to hospital wards filled with suffering kids, to coffins lowered into the ground, to grief that did not need to exist.

This is the cost of “letting him make his case.”

Bipartisan Revulsion

It’s telling that disgust at this display was bipartisan. Democrats were blistering, calling him dangerous and demanding resignation. Republicans were visibly uncomfortable, with some even joining the criticism. One conservative senator, himself a doctor, accused the secretary of denying life-saving care. Another admitted to being “deeply concerned” by his dismantling of vaccine access.

When even half the room that usually bends over backwards to excuse the inexcusable can’t stomach your act, you know it’s catastrophic.

The Larger Pattern: Anti-Science as Policy

The oath refusal was not a fluke. It’s part of a broader pattern: undermining scientific expertise, politicizing public health, and replacing evidence with ideology. This secretary has gutted advisory panels, sidelined experts, and dismissed long-serving public health officials—all while elevating his own paranoid suspicions into policy.

The result is not just incompetence; it’s sabotage. Agencies built to prevent disease are being hollowed out. The credibility of public health messaging is shredded. And diseases we once eradicated are slipping back in through the cracks.

The Moral Failure

Let’s strip away the politics. At its core, this is a moral failure. The oath refusal wasn’t just about shielding one man from legal risk. It was about denying the public the truth. It was about letting lies fester in the record of the United States Senate.

Parents who bury children from preventable disease don’t care whether a lie was technically perjury or not. They care that leaders told them vaccines weren’t safe. They care that officials dismissed science. They care that their government chose theater over truth.

The refusal to swear in the secretary is not a procedural quirk. It is complicity in child death.

What Must Happen Next

This cannot be allowed to pass as just another circus. The Senate must establish rules: all testimony from cabinet officials must be under oath, no exceptions. Any refusal should be grounds for contempt. Hearings must return to their original purpose: oversight with teeth.

But beyond procedural fixes, there must be a reckoning. A government that allows misinformation to flow unchecked from the very department tasked with protecting health is a government failing its people. The secretary must be removed. Public health must be restored to those who respect evidence, not destroy it.

Conclusion: Oversight or Complicity?

The question before us is simple: will Congress be an institution of oversight, or a stage for liars? By refusing to swear in the secretary, the chairman answered: a stage. By letting him rage and sputter without consequence, the Senate declared: lies are acceptable here.

That is unacceptable.

Oversight without an oath is not oversight. It is complicity. It is cowardice. And it is killing people.

The next time a parent buries a child from a preventable disease, the blame will not stop at the virus. It will reach into that chamber, to the man who refused the oath, to the senators who sat silent, and to the secretary who weaponized lies against the truth.

This hearing was not a debate. It was a death sentence, signed not with honesty, but with cowardice. And the victims will not be senators. They will be children.
