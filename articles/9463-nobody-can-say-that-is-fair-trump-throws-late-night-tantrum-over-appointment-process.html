'Nobody can say that is fair!' Trump throws late-night tantrum over appointment process

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Tirade Against the “Blue Slip” Tradition: A Critique

Donald Trump speaks to the press - (Photo by Jonathan Ernst for Reuters)

President Donald Trump took to social media late Thursday night to rant over a Senate tradition known as the “blue slip,” a process that he decried as unfair and unconstitutional.

“A ‘Blue Slip’ means that if you’re a Republican President, and there happens to be just one Democrat Senator in a state where you are appointing a U.S. Attorney or District Court Judge, you will never be successful in getting a Republican confirmed,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

“In other words, ‘Blue Slips’ are a disaster, and I have eight GREAT Republican U.S. Attorney Candidates who will not be able to fulfill their service to the people of a state that voted overwhelmingly for me.”

Trump first launched his campaign against the blue slip in July, demanding Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) illuminate the longstanding Senate tradition of allowing senators to block judicial nominees that were appointed to preside over their own districts. Grassley pushed back though, leading to Trump attacking Grassley as a “RINO,” or “Republican in name only,” and suggesting that Grassley must “hate America.”

Trump’s attacks on Grassley received strong pushback from GOP lawmakers, including from many Trump loyalists like Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA), who asked Trump, “pretty please, with sugar on top,” to “back off” the issue.

Nevertheless, Trump continued his campaign against the blue slip late Thursday night, declaring it to be wholly unfair.

“The only one I can appoint is a Democrat, and that’s not the deal,” Trump wrote. “Nobody can say that is fair or, even, Constitutional. Thank you for your attention to this matter!”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tirade Against the “Blue Slip” Tradition: A Critique

In a classic late-night social media outburst, President Donald Trump has once again showcased his profound misunderstanding of the democratic processes and traditions that underpin the functioning of the United States government. This time, his ire is directed at the Senate’s blue slip process, a tradition that he claims is both unfair and unconstitutional. Let’s dissect the inaccuracies and hyperboles in Trump’s argument with the kind of precision that seems to utterly elude him.

Firstly, Trump’s characterization of the blue slip as a tool exclusively wielded by Democrats to thwart Republican presidents is a gross oversimplification. The blue slip process, which has been a part of Senate tradition for over a century, is not some partisan weapon; it’s a courtesy that allows senators from a nominee’s home state to have a say in the judicial appointments that directly affect their constituents. Both Republican and Democratic senators have used (and, yes, sometimes abused) this privilege. To portray it as a one-sided affair is not just misleading—it’s laughably inaccurate.

Trump’s claim that the blue slip process is unconstitutional is another example of his tendency to declare anything he dislikes as unconstitutional, without any actual legal basis. The Constitution gives the Senate broad discretion over its advice and consent role, and historically, the body has developed various traditions like the blue slip to manage this duty. Unconstitutional? Hardly. Unconvenient for Trump? Absolutely.

The former president’s attack on Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) as a “RINO” who “must hate America” for pushing back against Trump’s demands is both childish and telling. Grassley’s defense of Senate traditions, even against the wishes of a president from his own party, is not an act of betrayal; it’s a defense of the institutional integrity of the Senate. Trump’s inability to tolerate dissent, even from within his ranks, underscores his authoritarian instincts.

Furthermore, Trump’s lamentation that he can only appoint Democrats in states with Democratic senators ignores the essence of the blue slip tradition: compromise and bipartisanship. It’s not about getting “your” people in by any means necessary; it’s about finding candidates that can be respected and accepted by both parties. Trump’s view that this is “not the deal” reveals his transactional and, frankly, myopic understanding of governance.

The pushback from GOP lawmakers, including loyalists like Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA), should be a wake-up call. When your own cheerleaders are begging you to “back off” an issue, perhaps it’s time to reflect on whether the problem lies not with the process but with your approach to it.

In conclusion, Trump’s late-night rant against the blue slip tradition is not just an attack on a Senate procedure; it’s an assault on the very principles of bipartisanship, senatorial courtesy, and the measured, thoughtful approach to judicial appointments that these traditions encourage. His inability to engage with these processes in a constructive manner reveals a deeper disdain for the democratic institutions and norms that he was sworn to uphold. As usual, Trump’s commentary is not just wrong; it’s dangerously misguided, prioritizing personal vendettas over the health of the American judicial system. Thank you, indeed, Mr. Trump, for drawing our attention to this matter.
