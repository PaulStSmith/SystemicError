Trump pulls out of Supreme Court hearing over fear of spectacular backfire: expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 4, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump’s decision Sunday to reverse course on attending a landmark Supreme Court case hearing could very well be due to the president hoping to avoid an “awkward environment” that could end up jeopardizing his trade policy, The New York Times reported Monday.

In mid-October, Trump floated the idea of attending the Supreme Court’s hearing scheduled for Wednesday where justices will rule on the president’s authority to impose tariffs, a case he’s called “one of the most important in the history of the country.” He ultimately decided against it, however, writing Sunday that he did “not want to distract” the court.

According to Harvard Law School Professor Jack Goldsmith, however, Trump’s decision not to attend the hearing could very well have been due to the president learning that his attendance – which would have been the first such instance in history of a sitting president to attend oral arguments – could have backfired spectacularly.

“I doubt the court wants to be perceived as bowing down to him,” Goldsmith said, speaking with the Times. “[If Trump did attend,] it’s just going to make it harder for them to rule for him.”

Trump has painted a grim picture for the country should the Supreme Court ultimately rule against his tariff policy, warning on Sunday that were his authority to impose sweeping tariffs revoked, the United States “could be reduced to almost Third World status.”

As to how the Supreme Court may rule in the case, Goldsmith said it was impossible to tell.

“At the end of this term, we’ll see wins and losses for Trump on presidential power,” Goldsmith told the Times. “This is the case I think is the closest, so I don’t know which way it will cut.”

While the authority to impose tariffs rests with Congress, Trump has argued that a 1977 law – the International Emergency Economic Powers Act – grants his office broad authority to impose tariffs during emergencies, declaring the United States’ trade deficits with other nations to be said emergencies.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Supreme Court Absence: Strategy or Cowardice?

In a move that surprised no one who’s been paying attention, President Donald Trump decided to skip out on a landmark Supreme Court case hearing, a decision dripping with the kind of strategic avoidance that has characterized his presidency. The New York Times reported this maneuver as an attempt to dodge an “awkward environment” that might jeopardize his beloved trade policy. But let’s peel back the layers of this political onion and see what’s really at stake.

First off, Trump’s claim that this case is “one of the most important in the history of the country” is a classic Trumpian exaggeration. Yes, the case’s implications for presidential power and trade policy are significant, but let’s not forget this is the same man who thinks every episode of his presidency deserves its chapter in the history books.

The Real Reason Behind Trump’s Absence

Trump’s decision to avoid the Supreme Court isn’t just about not wanting to “distract” the court, as he claims. This is about avoiding a scenario where the Supreme Court, an institution that prides itself on its independence, might be perceived as “bowing down” to Trump, as Harvard Law School Professor Jack Goldsmith insightfully points out.

Consider the optics here: the first sitting president to attend oral arguments, looming over the justices as they deliberate on his authority to impose tariffs. It’s not hard to see why Trump, or any advisers with a shred of foresight, might see this as a potential disaster. It’s less about distraction and more about the fear of tipping the scales so blatantly in his disfavor.

Trump’s Doomsday Warning

In his typical fashion, Trump has resorted to fear-mongering, warning that a ruling against his tariff policy could reduce the United States to “almost Third World status.” This hyperbolic claim is not only misleading but ignores the complexity of global economics and the role of tariffs within that system. It’s a scare tactic aimed at rallying support and painting himself as the nation’s savior from economic decline.

The Legal Grounds for Trump’s Tariff Authority

At the heart of this case is a 1977 law, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, which Trump argues grants him broad authority to impose tariffs during emergencies. The emergency in question? The United States’ trade deficits with other nations. This interpretation stretches the intent of the law, which was meant to address genuine national emergencies, not to serve as a workaround for pushing through controversial trade policies without Congressional approval.

Goldsmith’s Uncertain Prediction

Jack Goldsmith’s admission that it’s impossible to tell how the Supreme Court may rule is refreshing honesty in an era of hot takes and bold predictions. This case indeed represents a crucial moment for presidential power, with implications far beyond Trump’s administration. Whether it will result in a win or loss for Trump remains to be seen, but the broader question of the balance of power between the branches of government is what’s truly at stake.

In conclusion, Trump’s decision to not attend the Supreme Court hearing is a calculated move to avoid exacerbating an already tense situation. His warnings about the consequences of a ruling against his tariff policy are alarmist and unfounded, serving only to distract from the real issue: the extent of presidential power in matters of trade. As the court deliberates, one can only hope that the justices see through the smoke and mirrors of Trump’s rhetoric and base their decision on the law and the constitution, not the whims of a president who views the judiciary as just another obstacle to his agenda.
