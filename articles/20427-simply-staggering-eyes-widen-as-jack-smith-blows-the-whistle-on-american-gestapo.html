'Simply staggering': Eyes widen as Jack Smith blows the whistle on 'American Gestapo'

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Paulo SantosJuly 4, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Power Uses the Law to Protect Itself

The Story Beneath the Soundbite

Jack Smith’s account is not mainly about his personal fear. It is about a sitting president who, by Smith’s telling, said he wanted to jail the prosecutor who investigated him, and then used the Justice Department to target the law firm Smith hired. That is the substance: institutional power reaching for retaliation against accountability.

Who Actually Had Power

The people with leverage here were not pundits, not viewers, and not the outraged users performing shock online. The power sat with Donald Trump and the Justice Department under his command. Smith had already done the work of a federal prosecutor on two criminal investigations involving Trump’s effort to overturn the 2020 election and the classified documents at Mar-a-Lago. That is what made him vulnerable. That is also what made him a target.

Retaliation Is the Point

The source makes the sequence plain. Smith left his post, knew he needed a lawyer because Trump had said he wanted to jail him, retained counsel, and then, once that became public, the president and DOJ targeted the firm. That is not confusion. That is not happenstance. That is retaliation with administrative machinery behind it.

The important fact is not that Smith was worried. It is that his worry matched reality.

The Misdirection Machine

The online reaction instinctively turned Smith into a symbol: hero, martyr, moral first responder. None of that is wrong, but it is not the center of gravity. Hero worship can blur the more important point, which is that a president is alleged to have weaponized state power to punish someone for doing his job.

That is the deeper crime in the story, and the framing should stay there. The danger is not mainly that Trump is rude, crude, or vindictive in some abstract personal sense. The danger is that his grievance becomes policy through institutions that are supposed to restrain him.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics behaves when it still has formal democratic clothing. It does not always need mass arrests or dramatic decrees. Sometimes it just needs a president, a Justice Department, and enough institutional cowardice to let retaliation look procedural.

The broader lesson is brutal: accountability is most endangered when power can punish the people tasked with enforcing the law. A democracy does not decay only when bad actors win elections. It decays when they learn they can use office to make honest enforcement itself feel like a personal risk.
