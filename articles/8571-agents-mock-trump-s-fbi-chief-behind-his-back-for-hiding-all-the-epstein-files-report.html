Agents mock Trump's FBI chief behind his back for 'hiding all the Epstein files': report

The FBI and the Department of Justice (DOJ) are struggling to quash conspiracies that their current leaders used to espouse, according to a Tuesday report in CNN.

The first of these involves the ongoing saga surrounding deceased child sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, whose recently published emails link President Donald Trump directly to him.

When FBI Director Kash Patel attempted to tout what he thought was a major win for the administration on drug crackdowns last week, he was overshadowed by Epstein.

“For Patel and other FBI and Justice officials, the first 10 months in office have featured a series of headaches, with theories once peddled by the president’s loyal supporters-turned-government officials getting in the way of their new priorities,” CNN reports.

In 2023, Patel told far-right podcaster Benny Johnson the reason the files were not released was “Simple, because of who’s on that list.”

In the months since Attorney General Pam Bondi said there was no Epstein list, “Bondi, Patel, FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino, and other Trump administration officials have sought to close the books on Epstein — only to be confronted by the reality that the conspiracies they fueled won’t allow them to,” CNN says.

Patel has only made things worse, they write, noting that “as director, Patel has told Congress the FBI went through all investigative materials related to Epstein and found no evidence of a grand conspiracy of Epstein trafficking women to the wealthy and elite despite his previous support of the idea.”

In June, Patel told podcaster Joe Rogan that “we’re not gonna re-victimize women. We’re not going to put that s—— back out there. It’s not happening, because he wins … You want to hate me for it, fine.”

Bongino also fanned the conspiracy flames on his own podcast in May, 2024, when he said, “What the hell are they hiding with Jeffrey Epstein?”

Another conspiracy theory that has been resurrected to the annoyance of Justice Department officials,” is the one involving the identity of the person who planted pipe bombs outside the Republican National Committee and Democratic National Committee offices the night before the January 6, 2021 Capitol insurrection.

Weeks before he took his position in the second Trump administration, Bongino said on his podcast, “Folks, this guy was an insider. This was an inside job. And it is the biggest scandal in FBI history,” and on Saturday, right wing media outlet The Blaze thought for sure they had the suspect until their theory was quickly debunked.

“Soon, the FBI and Bongino himself were forced to defend the agency from the same type of allegations the deputy director had once made,” CNN writes.

This has angered the MAGA base, including former FBI agent and right-wing podcast host Kyle Seraphin, who angrily posted on X Sunday following Patel’s claims on Fox News that the FBI was thoroughly investigating the pipe bombs, saying “You can actually hear the shovel hitting the b——, right? Is it just me?”

To counter this frustration, the FBI created a new social media account on Wednesday called the “FBI Rapid Response“, CNN reports.

“The stated goal of the account: Combating ‘lies, smears and falsehoods from the fake news and others seeking to undermine our work and national security.’”

That account has struggled to do just that, CNN says, attempting in its second post to defend the FBI against “claims it had lied, this time from right-wing media star Tucker Carlson, who claimed the FBI had lied about Donald Trump’s would-be assassins’ online presence.”

“This FBI has never said Thomas Crooks had no online footprint. Ever,” the FBI posted about the man who shot Trump at a rally last summer and killed an attendee.

Patel sent out his own post following that one, saying, “The investigation, conducted by over 480 FBI employees, revealed Crooks had limited online and in person interactions, planned and conducted the attack alone, and did not leak or share his intent to engage in the attack with anyone.”

CNN also notes how Patel used to rail against the so-called ‘deep state’ and is now being mocked for becoming part of it, in the wake of recent firings in the bureau.

“In recent days, FBI agents have been sharing with each other an AI generated music video that pokes fun at Patel for ‘hiding all the Epstein files,’ among other public missteps by the director, sources inside the agency told CNN.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Conundrum of Conspiracies: The FBI, DOJ, and the Shadows of Their Past

In a revelatory piece by CNN, the intricate dance between the FBI, the Department of Justice (DOJ), and the ghosts of conspiracies past is laid bare, exposing a complex narrative of contradiction, challenge, and irony. The report underscores the struggle of the current leaders within these institutions to distance themselves from the very conspiracies they once championed. This analysis seeks to dissect the layers of this unfolding drama, highlighting the stark dichotomies and the inherent challenges faced by these officials in rebranding their stances amidst escalating scrutiny.

The Epstein Quagmire: A Tangled Web of Association and Denial

At the heart of this controversy are the recently unveiled emails linking President Donald Trump directly to deceased child sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein. This revelation comes as an inconvenient shadow over FBI Director Kash Patel’s attempts to spotlight the administration’s victories, such as significant drug crackdowns. However, the specter of Epstein looms large, undermining efforts to pivot away from past associations and toward new priorities.

Patel’s admission, during a far-right podcast, that the Epstein files remain unreleased due to the prominence of individuals on that list, juxtaposes sharply with Attorney General Pam Bondi’s denial of such a list’s existence. This contradiction not only fuels the conspiratorial fire but also encapsulates the internal conflict within the administration - a struggle between transparency and the preservation of reputations.

The irony is palpable as Patel, once a purveyor of grand conspiracy theories involving Epstein, now asserts a thorough FBI investigation uncovered no evidence of such a conspiracy. His attempts to defend the FBI’s discretion, citing a desire not to “re-victimize women,” clash with his previous stance, underscoring a significant shift in narrative that seems more a maneuver of convenience than a genuine change of heart.

Fanning the Flames of Conspiracy: The Bongino Paradox

Dan Bongino, FBI Deputy Director, exemplifies the precarious balance between former allegiances and current responsibilities. His podcast ruminations about the hidden truths behind Jeffrey Epstein and the insinuations of an “inside job” regarding the pipe bombs planted before the Capitol insurrection reflect a deep entanglement with conspiracy theories. Yet, his role within the FBI now necessitates a defense against these very theories, a role that has evidently frayed relationships with the MAGA base, as evidenced by the vitriolic response from former FBI agent Kyle Seraphin.

The FBI’s Battle Against Misinformation: A Sisyphean Task?

The creation of the “FBI Rapid Response” social media account is a testament to the bureau’s acknowledgment of the uphill battle against misinformation. However, its effectiveness remains questionable, as it struggles to counter allegations from influential figures like Tucker Carlson. This endeavor, while noble in intention, seems akin to a drop in the ocean of disinformation that floods the public discourse.

The incident involving Thomas Crooks and the subsequent clarification by Patel highlights the complexities of navigating public perception in the digital age. The FBI’s insistence on Crooks’ solitary planning and execution of his attack contrasts sharply with the rampant speculation and misinformation that often precedes official statements.

From Critic to Insider: Patel’s Paradoxical Journey

Patel’s transformation from a vocal critic of the ‘deep state’ to a key figure within it is marked by both irony and skepticism. His previous allegations against the so-called deep state, juxtaposed with his current attempts to purge the FBI of perceived dissent, paints a picture of a man caught between the ideologies he once espoused and the realities of governance. The mockery he now faces, highlighted by an AI-generated music video circulating within the FBI, signifies the cyclical nature of political allegiance and the fickleness of public perception.

Conclusion: The Unending Dance of Shadows

This CNN report sheds light on the intricate dance of shadows that encapsulates the FBI and DOJ’s current leadership, caught in a web of past conspiracies, current challenges, and the constant battle for credibility. The dichotomy between their former selves and their present roles underscores the complexities of governance, where past statements and actions can resurface with unforeseen consequences. In their quest to redefine their institutions and themselves, Patel, Bongino, and their colleagues face the Sisyphean task of distancing themselves from the very conspiracies they once fueled, a task complicated by the relentless tide of public scrutiny and the indelible nature of their past allegiances.
