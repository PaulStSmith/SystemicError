Cassandra Syndrome in MAGA America

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 25, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There is a moment that repeats itself with almost mechanical regularity in Trump-era politics.

A longtime supporter steps forward, voice trembling with sincerity, and says some variation of: “I didn’t know it would be like this.” They apologize. They express shock. They insist they’ve seen the light.

And those of us who spent years warning—pleading, documenting, shouting—are expected to receive this confession as a breakthrough.

It isn’t.

It’s the political equivalent of discovering gravity after jumping off the roof.

The Pattern: Cruelty Is Acceptable Until It’s Personal

MAGA behavior follows a grimly consistent logic:

Cruelty is fine when it’s abstract. Corruption is tolerable when it’s theoretical. Authoritarianism is entertaining when it targets others.

Immigrants? Fair game.

Trans people? Disposable.

Journalists? Enemies.

Courts? Obstacles.

Democratic norms? Bureaucratic annoyances.

The movement thrived on this selective moral blindness. Every warning was dismissed as hysteria. Every red flag reframed as “fake news.” Every indictment waved away as persecution.

Not because the evidence was weak—but because accepting it would have required moral responsibility.

And responsibility was never the point.

The Cassandra Problem

In Greek mythology, Cassandra was cursed to speak the truth while never being believed.

She saw Troy burn long before the first flame touched the walls. She warned of the horse. She named the disaster in advance. And she was mocked for it.

Liberals, journalists, historians, legal scholars, civil servants—we have been Cassandra for nearly a decade.

We warned that Trump was corrupt before the first indictment.

We warned he was authoritarian before January 6.

We warned he would undermine courts, weaponize agencies, normalize cruelty, reward loyalty over competence, and hollow out democracy from the inside.

And the response was always the same: laughter, derision, memes, chants, and smug confidence that “it won’t be that bad.”

Now the horse is inside the city.

And suddenly, some supporters are shocked.

What These Apologies Really Are

Let’s be brutally honest: most of these apologies are not moral reckonings.

They are damage assessments.

They arrive only after:

• A policy hits the apologizer directly.
• A benefit is cut.
• A right is threatened.
• A job is lost.
• A community becomes collateral damage.

The cruelty didn’t stop being cruelty—it just stopped being distant.

Notice what is often missing from these statements:

• No apology to the immigrants whose families were torn apart.
• No apology to women stripped of bodily autonomy.
• No apology to LGBTQ people targeted for political sport.
• No apology to election workers harassed and threatened.
• No apology to institutions deliberately weakened.

Instead, the apology is framed as a personal disappointment. “This isn’t what I voted for.”

Yes. It was.

The Lie of Being “Tricked”

One of the most persistent myths in post-Trump reflection is that his supporters were deceived.

They weren’t.

Trump did not hide who he was. He bragged about it.

He mocked the disabled on camera. He praised authoritarians. He celebrated violence. He promised revenge. He dismissed constitutional limits as inconveniences.

Supporters heard all of it.

They just decided it wouldn’t apply to them.

That’s not deception. That’s selective empathy.

The Emotional Asymmetry

Here’s the quiet cruelty of it all: those who warned early are expected to be gracious again.

We are asked to welcome late awakenings with patience, while bearing the scars of policies we opposed from the start.

We are told to “heal,” to “move on,” to “stop being divisive.”

But there is no symmetrical burden placed on those who enabled the damage.

The victims must forgive.

The enablers get to rediscover conscience.

That is not reconciliation. That is moral outsourcing.

Why This Keeps Happening

This cycle persists because Trumpism was never about policy coherence. It was about identity.

It offered:

• Belonging without accountability.
• Power without responsibility.
• Cruelty reframed as strength.
• Grievance elevated to ideology.

As long as the movement delivered emotional validation, facts were irrelevant.

Reality only intrudes when identity protection fails.

What Real Accountability Would Look Like

A genuine reckoning would sound very different.

It would say:

“I ignored the warnings because they conflicted with my sense of power and belonging.”

“I accepted cruelty as long as it targeted others.”

“I treated democratic norms as optional because I believed my side would always win.”

“I was wrong—not because it hurt me—but because it hurt people I dismissed.”

Anything less is not awakening. It’s reputational triage.

Cassandra Was Right

Cassandra was right.

She was right when she warned.

She was right when she was mocked.

And she was right when the city burned anyway.

The tragedy is not that people are finally listening.

The tragedy is how much had to be destroyed before they did.

And the cruel irony is this: even now, Cassandra isn’t asking for applause.

She’s asking whether anyone will listen next time.

History suggests the answer will come far too late.
