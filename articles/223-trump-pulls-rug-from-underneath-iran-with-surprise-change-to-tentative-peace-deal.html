Trump pulls rug from underneath Iran with surprise change to tentative peace deal

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 24, 20262 min read

Trump’s Sanctions Theater: Humanitarian Language Covering a Power Grab

The Setup

The source says Trump had already drawn scrutiny for agreeing to lift sanctions on Iran and unfreeze Iranian funds as part of a tentative peace deal. Then he added a new condition on Truth Social: the money would go into escrow, controlled by the United States, and be spent only on food and medical supplies bought from U.S. suppliers. That is the basic fact pattern. The rest is political control dressed up as relief.

Who Actually Holds Power

The institutional power here is not Iran’s, and not the public’s. It sits with the U.S. president and the Treasury apparatus he claims can release, freeze, and reroute Iranian assets at will. Trump is not describing a mutual agreement between equals. He is describing a mechanism in which the United States keeps the keys even while claiming to “help.”

That matters because power is not just the ability to sign a memorandum. It is the ability to redefine what the agreement means after the fact. Trump’s post suggests that even after the memorandum says the United States would “make fully available” the frozen funds upon implementation, he can still announce a different version on social media and expect everyone else to absorb it.

The Escrow Ruse

The escrow language is not neutral administration. It is a way to keep sanctions leverage alive while publicly pretending to loosen it. If the funds remain “controlled by the U.S.A.,” then the alleged relief is not relief in any meaningful political sense. It is supervised dependency.

The food-and-medicine framing is also doing too much work. It casts the U.S. as a benevolent gatekeeper while converting Iranian access to its own assets into a permission structure managed by Washington. That is not humanitarianism. It is conditionality with nicer branding.

The Misdirection

The framing is weak because it treats the controversy as a policy clarification rather than a demonstration of executive arbitrariness. The source itself shows the contradiction: the memorandum says the funds would be made fully available, while Trump says they will be held in escrow under U.S. control. Those are not compatible descriptions. One of them is either a retreat from the deal or a public attempt to rewrite it.

What gets hidden in this kind of reporting is the basic asymmetry of blame. If the talks falter, the weaker party is expected to absorb the consequences of the stronger party’s improvisation. If the arrangement is coercive, it gets described as a humanitarian safeguard. If it collapses, the collapse can be blamed on “details” instead of on the decision to keep the leverage and call it generosity.

The Pattern

This is a familiar imperial habit: preserve coercive control, rename it stewardship, and then describe the target of that control as the beneficiary. The point is not merely to manage Iran’s funds. The point is to assert that the United States can unfreeze money, condition access, and still present itself as the moral actor in the room.

That is the larger political pattern here. The strong party does not just impose terms. It also controls the narrative around those terms, using humanitarian language to launder a power relationship that remains intact. The result is a familiar kind of political fraud: domination with a charity accent.
