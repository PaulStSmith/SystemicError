A dirty little secret betrays a supposedly rosy report for Trump

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Trump Is Breaking the Labor Market, Then Demanding Applause for the Damage

The Number Is Not the Story

The Labor Department says the U.S. added 162,000 jobs in August. The source’s own point is more important than the headline: the monthly jobs report is getting shakier because the labor market has been warped by Trump’s mass deportations and the retirement wave of the baby boom generation. When the workforce is being forcibly thinned out and the data system is forced to guess, the headline number becomes less a measure of strength than a decorative distraction.

Low Wages Are a Choice

The real economic fact in the source is brutally simple: wages are not keeping up with prices. Average hourly earnings rose 3.1 percent over the year while prices rose 3.4 percent. That means workers are falling behind. This is not an accident, and it is not a mysterious market flaw. It is what low-wage job growth looks like when employers are allowed to keep wages down while raising prices. Republicans can pretend this is just an unfortunate trend. It is not. It is a transfer of power and income away from workers.

Stagnation Is Not Stability

The labor market described here is not healthy just because it is not collapsing. A low-hire, low-fire economy is a dead-end economy. It freezes advancement, blocks young people from entering, and tells everyone already inside to stop expecting movement. That is not prosperity. It is managed inertia, and it works best for employers who want labor cheap, replaceable, and quiet.

Trump Is the Active Threat to the Economy

The source gets the power structure right: Trump is not just reacting to bad conditions. He is making them worse. Tariffs raise prices. A costly war in Iran raises prices. Then he lashes out at the Federal Reserve, threatening embargoes on countries with trade deficits because he wants lower rates before the midterms. That is not economic strategy. It is erratic pressure on institutions meant to stand between one man’s impulses and the country’s price level.

Trump wants the benefits of cheap money without the consequences of inflation, and he wants the optics of growth without the discipline required to produce it. The result is a president treating the economy like a campaign prop while threatening policies that would make inflation and instability worse.

The Advisors Are Part of the Problem

The source’s contempt for Scott Bessent is deserved, but the larger point matters more: this is not a lone bad advisor problem. It is an administration in which nobody around the Oval Office appears capable of stopping reckless economic improvisation. That is what institutional cowardice looks like in practice. The people with access either cannot correct him or will not. Either way, the country pays.

The Real Pattern

This story is not about one jobs report. It is about a governing style that treats workers as inputs, prices as inconveniences, and institutions as obstacles. Trump harms the economy, then demands credit for the parts of it he has not yet wrecked. That is the pattern: sabotage followed by spin, coercion followed by denial, and a political class that keeps pretending the damage is a misunderstanding instead of a method.
