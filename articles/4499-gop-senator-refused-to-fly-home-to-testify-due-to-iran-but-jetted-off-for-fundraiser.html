GOP senator refused to fly home to testify due to Iran — but jetted off for fundraiser

Sen. Jon Husted (R-OH) reportedly made arrangements not to have to fly home to Ohio to testify in a major public corruption trial, with legal counsel suggesting his presence in D.C. was necessary due to the war in Iran — but then he flew home anyway, to attend a political fundraiser.

According to News 5 Cleveland, “the senator’s testimony won’t be at the Summit County Court of Common Pleas. It will be hundreds of miles away,” with the defense attorney for FirstEnergy Vice President Mike Dowling telling the court, “Given the war, his availability is not great. We have spoken with his counsel... Given everything that’s going on in the world, we don’t feel that he can or should insist upon him leaving his post in Washington DC right now.”

But per the report, Husted, “who doesn’t sit on any committees related to safety, military or foreign affairs,” was soon spotted in a security line at Reagan National Airport in D.C., then was spotted later that day walking in John Glenn Columbus International Airport. He rebuffed questions from both people who saw him about the FirstEnergy case.

According to the report, it turns out “that the senator is set to attend a fundraiser on Friday. Husted is the keynote speaker at the Greene County Republican Party Lincoln Day Dinner, where tickets were up to $1,000.” The campaign then clarified that the war was not the issue with testifying in person at trial, and there were simply other scheduling conflicts.

The news generated outrage, with state Sen. Casey Weinstein saying, “[He should] explain to Ohioans what he means by unavailable or dealing with the war, if he’s in fact here in the state raising money for his embattled Senate campaign. It’s despicable, and I can’t think of a more significant breach of trust.”

The FirstEnergy corruption scandal has spanned several years, with multiple executives and Ohio politicians implicated and charged with crimes. In essence, FirstEnergy, a utility company, paid bribes to politicians, including former Ohio House Speaker Larry Householder, in exchange for passage of a bill that would authorize a $1.3 billion bailout of the utility’s failing nuclear plants.

Husted, who is currently facing a tough election fight against former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown, has not been directly accused of a crime in the FirstEnergy case, but he was lieutenant governor at the time the deals unfolded, and, News 5 Cleveland said, Husted “worked closely” with FirstEnergy “to pass H.B. 6 in 2019, after they helped fund his campaign.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Political Deception: The Case of Sen. Jon Husted

In a move that can only be described as a breathtaking display of political acrobatics, Sen. Jon Husted (R-OH) has managed to weave a web of deceit so tangled it could rival Arachne’s finest. Ostensibly committed to his duties in Washington D.C. because of the war in Iran, Husted found it within his patriotic schedule to fly back to Ohio, not for matters of state or public concern, but to grace a political fundraiser with his presence.

Let’s dissect the layers of this farce with the precision of a scalpel cutting through the thick fabric of political duplicity.

The Grand Excuse: War in Iran

The assertion that Husted’s presence in D.C. was imperative due to the situation in Iran is laughable at best and insidiously misleading at worst. Husted, who doesn’t sit on any committees related to safety, military, or foreign affairs, suddenly found his role in the capital indispensable when it came to avoiding a court appearance in the Summit County Court of Common Pleas. The defense’s plea, as reported by News 5 Cleveland, that given the global turmoil, Husted couldn’t possibly be expected to leave his station, is a masterstroke of evasion.

The Sudden Flight Back

Yet, the senator’s patriotic resolve crumbled at the prospect of a political fundraiser. Spotted in the security line at Reagan National Airport and later in Ohio, Husted’s itinerary magically aligned for a fundraiser but not for a court appearance. The fundraiser, where tickets soared up to $1,000 a pop, was apparently significant enough to draw Husted back to Ohio, despite the dire international implications that purportedly anchored him to D.C.

The Outrage

State Sen. Casey Weinstein’s outrage encapsulates the public’s sentiment perfectly, branding Husted’s maneuver as despicable and a significant breach of trust. The audacity to prioritize a fundraiser over a corruption trial speaks volumes of Husted’s respect (or lack thereof) for the judicial process and the citizens he is supposed to represent.

The Corruption Scandal Backdrop

This debacle unfolds against the lurid backdrop of the FirstEnergy corruption scandal, a saga that has embroiled numerous Ohio politicians and executives in a sordid tale of bribery and political chicanery. Husted, as lieutenant governor during the scandal’s unfolding and a beneficiary of FirstEnergy’s campaign largesse, is no stranger to the machinations that powered the passage of H.B. 6 in 2019. His close work with FirstEnergy to pass the bill, after the company helped fund his campaign, paints a damning picture of complicity, if not direct involvement.

Conclusion: A Portrait of Political Opportunism

Jon Husted’s actions are a textbook example of political opportunism, underscored by a blatant disregard for accountability and public trust. His maneuver to dodge a court appearance under the guise of national duty, only to surface at a political fundraiser, is an affront to the principles of transparency and integrity. The senator owes the public a full explanation, not just for his dubious scheduling priorities but for his role in a scandal that has tarnished the state’s political landscape.

In the end, Husted’s escapades are a stark reminder of the systemic corruption and moral bankruptcy that plague our political institutions. It’s high time these practices are brought into the light and those responsible held to account.
