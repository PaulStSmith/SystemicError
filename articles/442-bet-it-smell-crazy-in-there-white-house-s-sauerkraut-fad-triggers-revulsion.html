'Bet it smell crazy in there': White House's sauerkraut fad triggers revulsion

Sauerkraut as Statecraft

The People Actually Making the Choice

A White House wellness fad has turned into a public spectacle: Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Howard Lutnick, Sean Duffy, and JD Vance are all reportedly eating sauerkraut-heavy diets promoted by Dr. Sean O’Mara, and they are bragging about weight loss and “glowing skin” while everyone else recoils at the smell. That is the whole story on the surface. The deeper story is that some of the most powerful people in the federal government are treating a fringe diet trend like governing wisdom.

Power Wears Cabinet Titles

This is not a random lifestyle experiment at the margin. Kennedy runs Health and Human Services. Lutnick runs Commerce. Duffy runs Transportation. Vance is the vice president. These are institutional actors with real authority and public legitimacy. When they adopt a quasi-medical program and publicly validate it, they are not merely choosing lunch. They are normalizing a worldview in which personal discipline, bodily optimization, and wellness branding stand in for policy, expertise, and accountability.

The Odor Is Not the Point

The social-media joke machine has latched onto the smell, and that is predictable because it is easier to mock flatulence than to examine governance. The article’s framing leans into the comedy of it all, which is its weakness. The stench is incidental. The political issue is that the same officials who command agencies and shape public messaging are signaling that they prefer a vibe of ascetic health mysticism to actual institutional seriousness.

Misdirection by Disgust

The commentary around the piece does something familiar: it turns the story into a gross-out gag, which makes the officials look eccentric rather than consequential. That is convenient for them. Eccentricity is reputational padding. It softens the fact that this is a power elite selling itself through body talk, diet doctrine, and pseudo-scientific self-optimization. The source notes O’Mara’s own transactional language about older patients as “a sinking ship” and “a company headed to bankruptcy.” That is not care. It is extractive contempt dressed as medical candor.

The MAHA Pattern

What this story really reveals is the political style behind the Make America Healthy Again brand: a fusion of resentment, self-purification, and status performance. The message is not just “eat better.” It is that elite rule can be recast as bodily discipline, that authority can be laundered through wellness language, and that policy failure can be displaced into private virtue. The result is a government that mistakes fetishized self-management for seriousness and asks the public to treat that as leadership.

The System Beneath the Joke

The sauerkraut itself is trivial. The pattern is not. When powerful officials turn bodily habits into political identity, they are not escaping politics; they are replacing it with branding. That is how institutional cowardice works now: it puts on a health halo, speaks in the language of optimization, and hopes nobody notices that the people in charge are still avoiding the hard work of governing.
