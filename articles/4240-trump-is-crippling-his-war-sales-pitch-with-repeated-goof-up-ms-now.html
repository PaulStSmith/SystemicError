Trump is crippling his war sales-pitch with repeated goof-up: MS NOW

Donald Trump has repeatedly called his Iran war an “excursion,” which drew rolled-eyes on MS NOW on Friday morning, with one host claiming the president doesn’t seem to know the true meaning of the word.

The “Morning Joe” segment began with a supercut of the president making statements like, “This is an excursion. We figured oil prices would go up, which they will. They’ll also come down,” and later claiming, “We took a little excursion because we felt we had to do that to get rid of some evil.”

That led co-host, Willie Geist to suggest, “President Trump repeatedly calling the war with Iran a ‘little excursion’; a war that has killed American troops, injured about 150 of them, destabilized the Middle East and driven oil over $100 a barrel. A little excursion is kind of like a half-day snorkeling trip you book on your vacation to the Bahamas, not a war with Iran.”

“In its definition of an excursion, [is] ‘a short, organized pleasure trip a la snorkeling, often lasting a few hours. Incursion, a sudden, often hostile entrance or invasion into a territory or place’,” Jonathan Lemire read to his colleague.

“The smart money would say that one of President Trump’s advisors used the word incursion with him: ‘Sir, it’s an incursion. It’s going to be a short military action.’ I mean, it may not be that short, but that’s the word that he was given,” he explained.

“He heard it as ‘excursion’ and no one has had the guts to correct him. And now he has said it over and over and over. And as you say, Willie, excursion, whether he means to or not, really diminishes the stakes in the lives lost in this conflict.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Article on Trump’s “Iran Excursion” Comments

In the scrutinized narrative surrounding President Donald Trump’s characterizations of the conflict with Iran as an “excursion,” several critical aspects demand our analytical attention. These include the institutional power dynamics at play, the decision-making process leading to the military action, and the framing of the media’s critique.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

At the core of this scenario is the President of the United States, Donald Trump, who, as the Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces, holds substantial institutional power in matters of national security and military engagement. The role grants him the authority to initiate and oversee military operations, albeit with certain checks and balances, primarily from Congress regarding declarations of war and funding.

Decision-Making Dynamics

The decision to engage militarily with Iran, as described, emanates directly from the executive branch under Trump’s leadership. While advisers and military officials undoubtedly play significant roles in shaping the policy and its execution, the final decision to label the conflict an “excursion” and to proceed with it falls squarely on the President.

Media Framing and Misdirection

The article from MS NOW focuses on Trump’s use of the term “excursion” to describe the military conflict with Iran. The critique centers on the perceived trivialization of the war’s consequences, including American and Iranian casualties, regional destabilization, and economic impacts like the rise in oil prices. However, the framing could obscure deeper issues of accountability and policy critique by focusing on semantics rather than the substantive outcomes of the decision to engage militarily with Iran.

Analysis

  • Semantics vs. Substance: The media’s emphasis on Trump’s diction (”excursion” vs. “incursion”) risks underplaying the gravity and implications of the military action itself. While the choice of words is indeed telling and reflects on the President’s perspective and possibly his understanding of the conflict, it is the action, its motivations, and its consequences that warrant rigorous scrutiny.

  • Misplaced Focus: By concentrating on the term “excursion” and its colloquial inappropriateness, there’s a risk of diverting the discourse from more pressing questions about the advisability, legality, and ramifications of the conflict. This includes the decision-making process, the intelligence used to justify the action, the lack of congressional authorization, and the impact on international relations and regional stability.

  • Holding Power to Account: Critiquing Trump’s language, while relevant to understanding his approach to governance and military action, should not overshadow holding him accountable for the decision itself. The institutional power he wields as President and the decisions made under his administration are where the focus should lie. The critique should extend to examining the broader administration’s policies, the role of advisers, and the systemic checks and balances—or lack thereof.

Conclusion

While the media’s focus on President Trump’s characterization of the Iran conflict as an “excursion” highlights important aspects of political communication and public perception, it is imperative not to lose sight of the broader context. The critique should squarely address the use of institutional power, decision-making processes leading to military action, and the substantial consequences thereof. Avoiding the trap of semantic distractions ensures that the discourse remains grounded in the substantive issues at stake, fostering a more informed and critical public dialogue.
