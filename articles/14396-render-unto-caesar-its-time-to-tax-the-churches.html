Render Unto Caesar: It’s Time to Tax the Churches

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 26, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Great Religious GriftThe Absurdity of “Charity”Separation of Church and AccountabilityIt’s Time to Repeal the Religious Free Ride

Let’s dispense with the pleasantries.

When a pastor owns a mansion worth more than a dozen homeless shelters, when megachurches install million-dollar sound systems while food banks beg for funding, and when entire religious empires operate tax-free under the flimsy guise of “charity,” then it’s no longer faith. It’s fraud.

The Great Religious Grift

In the United States alone, religious institutions are estimated to cost taxpayers $71 billion annually in lost revenue. Seventy. One. Billion. Dollars. That’s the GDP of a small country flushed down the holy toilet because Joel Osteen needs another vacation house and Kenneth Copeland needs a second jet to avoid “demons on commercial flights.”

These aren’t exceptions—they’re business models. Megachurches are spiritual Ponzi schemes where salvation is sold, opulence is sanctified, and tithes from working-class believers are funneled into luxury lifestyles for self-anointed messiahs.

And all of it? Tax-free.

The Absurdity of “Charity”

Under IRS law, churches are automatically considered 501(c)(3) charities. They don’t have to register. They don’t have to disclose finances. They don’t even have to prove they’re actually charitable. Meanwhile, actual nonprofits doing real work—housing the homeless, feeding the hungry, offering legal support—must grovel for grants and fill out tax returns longer than Leviticus.

Even worse? Pastors can claim housing allowances that are exempt from income tax. That means a multi-million-dollar home can be written off as a “parsonage” while that same pastor preaches about the virtue of humility.

And if you’re thinking this is just an American problem—don’t. It’s global. The Catholic Church is one of the largest landowners on Earth. Evangelical empires in Brazil, Nigeria, and South Korea operate as unregulated money-laundering pipelines with political clout and media control. Their only real doctrine? Profit.

Separation of Church and Accountability

Let’s be clear: belief is not the problem. Faith, in its purest form, is beautiful. What’s grotesque is the institutionalized exemption from reality. Churches demand to influence public policy, interfere with education, dictate healthcare choices—but they refuse to pay a dime for the infrastructure they exploit.

Want to preach politics from the pulpit? Pay taxes like every other political organization.

Want to run a global media empire? Pay taxes like every other business.

Want to buy beachfront property while claiming to serve the poor? Then let the IRS serve you.

It’s Time to Repeal the Religious Free Ride

Tax them. All of them. From the gilded cathedrals of Europe to the stadium-sized churches of Texas. From the televangelists fleecing the gullible to the bishops hoarding billions in Vatican vaults.

The days of tax-exempt snake oil are over. If you’re not feeding the hungry, housing the poor, or healing the sick—you’re not a charity. You’re a racket.

Render unto Caesar what is Caesar’s. And render unto the people a government that no longer cowers before pulpits lined in gold.
