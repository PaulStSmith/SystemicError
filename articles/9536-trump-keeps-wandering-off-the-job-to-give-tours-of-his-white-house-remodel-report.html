Trump keeps wandering off the job to give tours of his White House remodel: report

Donald Trump’s obsession with remaking the White House into something more like his Mar-a-Lago resort is interfering with his presidential duties, as he sets aside time spent running the government to give dignitaries and visitors tours of his handiwork, according to new reporting.

According to a report from Marc Caputo of Axios, the president has had dioramas built representing all the changes he makes and likes to play with them during his meals, with one insider remarking, “This is all the president’s baby. It’s the Trump White House.”

The report notes the president has “spent as many as 20 hours on some projects, aides say, engaging in impromptu design sessions between negotiating peace deals and talks over the government shutdown,” adding that, “After strong-arming Benjamin Netanyahu into a Gaza ceasefire two weeks ago, Trump led the Israeli prime minister on a 40-minute tour of White House renovations. Last week, Finnish Prime Minister Alexander Stubb got the same treatment.”

“Last month, a delegation of Florida House lawmakers spent even more time with the president — well over an hour — as he showed them the new tile floors in the old washroom of the Lincoln Bedroom (statuary marble Trump had selected),” Caputo wrote before noting that one member of the group explained that the president engaged them in voting of which tile should be used, which a White House insider confirmed by explaining, “He asks everyone for a vote on everything. We vote. Anybody that walks through gets a vote. He cares so deeply about perfection that this is what he does.”

During a recent meeting with Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, Trump walked the entourage outside to show off his new sound system in what he is now calling the “Rose Garden Club,” and boasted, “We have a great speaker system.”

As for Trump’s noodling around with the 3D models, while the government is shut down, one insider admitted, “The tenser things are, the more he moves the [diorama] pieces around in his spare time, or he takes a break and thinks about the marble he wants or the columns, whether they’re going to be Corinthian or not.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Mar-a-Lago Makeover Obsession: A Scathing Review

In what reads like a parody yet tragically is not, Donald Trump’s presidency appears to be morphing the White House into a bizarre blend of government seat and personal playground. According to a recent exposé by Marc Caputo of Axios, Trump’s fixation on transforming the iconic residence into something akin to his Mar-a-Lago resort is not just a side hobby; it’s a full-blown obsession that is eating into his presidential duties.

The Diorama Diaries

It seems the Oval Office now doubles as a child’s playroom, with Trump reportedly spending meal times rearranging dioramas of the White House. One can almost picture the scene – the leader of the free world, moving miniature trees and adjusting tiny columns while critical policy decisions and national crises queue up, waiting for a slice of his attention. An insider’s comment, “This is all the president’s baby. It’s the Trump White House,” sums up this surreal scenario with eerie precision.

A Misguided Tour Guide

The report outlines instances where Trump, post high-stakes negotiations – like arm-twisting Benjamin Netanyahu into a Gaza ceasefire – preferred to play tour guide, showcasing renovations rather than consolidating geopolitical gains or addressing domestic issues. The Finnish Prime Minister Alexander Stubb and a delegation of Florida House lawmakers were also victims of Trump’s vanity tours, the latter group subjected to a baffling referendum on bathroom tiles.

The Pursuit of Perfection or Procrastination?

Trump’s penchant for seeking validation over minutiae, such as flooring choices, under the guise of striving for “perfection,” is a thinly veiled attempt at procrastination. It’s easier, and certainly more gratifying, to solicit opinions on marble than to navigate the complex web of government policymaking.

The Sound System Side Show

In perhaps the most bewildering anecdote, Trump took time out of a meeting with Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg to flaunt his new sound system in the “Rose Garden Club.” It’s a poignant metaphor for his presidency: prioritizing the superficial and the sensational over substance and strategy.

The Diorama as a Distraction

Amid a government shutdown, the report chillingly notes Trump’s increased preoccupation with his 3D White House model – a childlike diversion from the weighty responsibilities of his office. It’s as if the more chaotic the situation, the greater his retreat into this miniature world, a stark visual of a president disengaged from reality.

In Conclusion

Trump’s infatuation with remodeling the White House, a symbol of the nation’s governance, into a personal monument of luxury and indulgence is not just an eccentricity; it’s a dereliction of duty. The image of a president more concerned with Corinthian columns than with the columns of American society – healthcare, education, and justice – is a damning indictment of his priorities.

In a time when leadership demands unwavering focus and dedication, the American people deserve more than a president who prefers the role of interior decorator and tour guide over that of a true statesman. The White House, an enduring symbol of American democracy, should not be reduced to a vanity project or a playground for presidential whims. Trump’s obsession with aesthetics over policy is not just a systemic error; it’s a national embarrassment.
