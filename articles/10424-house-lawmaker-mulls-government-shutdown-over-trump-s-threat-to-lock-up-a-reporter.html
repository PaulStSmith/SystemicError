House lawmaker mulls government shutdown over Trump's threat to 'lock up a reporter'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Tangled Web of Threats and Political Drama

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media before departing for a state visit to Britain, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 16, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

A prominent Democratic congressman is threatening to vote to shut down the federal government in response to President Donald Trump’s threat to target a journalist over a question about hate speech.

ABC News’ Jonathan Karl asked the president about Attorney General Pam Bondi’s claim that she will target those who engage in hate speech, which is largely constitutionally protected. Trump suggested the administration could go after the reporter.

“We’ll probably go after people like you because you treat me so unfairly. It’s hate. You have a lot of hate in your heart,” the president told Karl.

The federal government is again poised to shut down unless Speaker of the House Mike Johnson can pass legislation to fund the government. The bill, a 91-page continuing resolution, was published Tuesday afternoon. It pushes a shutdown deadline from Oct. 1 to Nov. 21.

According to Politico, the bill would “provide $30 million for lawmaker security and a total of $58 million in security assistance the White House requested for the Supreme Court and executive branch.”

“While GOP leaders plan to call a floor vote later this week, it’s still unclear whether Democrats will vote in support of the bill, with President Donald Trump calling on congressional Republicans to stiff-arm the minority party in government funding negotiations.”

In response to Trump’s comment, U.S. Rep. Eric Swalwell asked, “How can we fund this? I am being asked this week to fund a government that locks up a reporter Trump doesn’t like. This isn’t a close call folks.”

The Trump administration has not yet actually locked up a reporter because the president does not like them, but critics, including California Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom, appeared to suggest that was the implied threat that Trump was making.

“Donald Trump says he will send the DOJ after the press if they say things he doesn’t like,” Newsom declared.

CNN chief political analyst David Axelrod asked, “So is the AG going to go after journalists who displease the @POTUS?”

Our Analysis:

A Tangled Web of Threats and Political Drama

The latest episode in the ongoing saga of political theatrics comes with a heavy dose of irony, threats, and a clear disregard for the principles of free speech. President Donald Trump's response to ABC News' Jonathan Karl's question about hate speech is not just troubling; it's a blatant display of the authoritarian impulses that seem to drive the current administration. Trump's assertion that they "will probably go after people like you" to Karl is not just an off-the-cuff remark; it's a chilling threat to the very foundation of journalistic freedom.

The Irony of "Hate Speech"

Let's dissect the irony here. Trump accuses Karl of having "a lot of hate in your heart" simply for asking about the administration's stance on hate speech. The circular logic is dizzying: asking about hate speech is, in Trump's view, an act of hate. This is not just an attack on Karl but on journalism's role in holding power to account. Under this twisted logic, any critical inquiry could be labeled as hate, justifying retribution from the government.

A Shutdown Over Principles?

Enter U.S. Rep. Eric Swalwell, wielding a threat of his own: to vote against funding the federal government in response to Trump's threats against the press. Swalwell's stance, while dramatic, underscores the gravity of the situation. This isn't merely a budgetary disagreement; it's a stand against what he perceives as a slide towards authoritarianism. Yet, one might argue that using a government shutdown as leverage is a risky play that could harm countless individuals reliant on government services. Is the potential cost worth the stand being taken?

Funding Security or Suppressing Dissent?

The 91-page continuing resolution, with its provisions for lawmaker security and security assistance for the Supreme Court and executive branch, raises further questions. While security is paramount, in the context of Trump's comments, one can't help but wonder about the priorities it reflects. Is this about genuine security needs, or is it more about fortifying the administration against dissent?

A Misguided Administration or a Misunderstood One?

Critics like California Governor Gavin Newsom and CNN's David Axelrod perceive Trump's threats as an implied move to weaponize the Department of Justice against the press. This administration has not yet incarcerated a journalist for unfavorable coverage, but the mere suggestion by the President of the United States that it could happen is profoundly alarming. It suggests a misunderstanding (willful or otherwise) of the role of the press in a democratic society and of the principle of free speech.

The Larger Implications

This situation is bigger than Trump, Swalwell, or any individual player. It's about the enduring struggle between power and accountability, between authority and freedom. When a leader threatens the press for the simple act of asking questions, it sends a message that dissent will not be tolerated. This is not the hallmark of a healthy democracy.

In conclusion, this unfolding drama is a microcosm of the broader challenges facing American democracy today. The threats, the political posturing, and the looming government shutdown are symptomatic of deeper issues at play. The question of funding the government has become entangled with fundamental questions about free speech, the role of journalism, and the limits of executive power. As this situation develops, one thing is clear: the stakes are far higher than they may appear at first glance.
