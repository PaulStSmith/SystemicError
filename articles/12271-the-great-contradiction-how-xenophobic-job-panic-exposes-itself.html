The Great Contradiction: How Xenophobic Job Panic Exposes Itself

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Rhetorical Collision CourseThe Myth of the Job ThiefProjection and PrejudiceA Convenient Political ToolWhat You Actually Do for a LivingAbolish the Cruelty, Not the Worker

In the carnival of American political hypocrisy, one of the most grotesque clowns is the persistent lie that immigrants are both dangerous criminals and somehow economic supervillains capable of taking everyone’s jobs. It’s a contradiction so stupid it shouldn’t survive a minute of critical thought — yet it thrives, repeated endlessly by media personalities, armchair patriots, and keyboard fascists desperate for a scapegoat.

Let’s dissect this poison with the seriousness it never deserved.

The Rhetorical Collision Course

On one hand, the anti-immigrant crowd peddles the narrative that undocumented immigrants are drug dealers, rapists, and murderers — a grotesque caricature first mainstreamed by Donald Trump in his infamous campaign announcement speech. On the other hand, these same people turn around and shriek that immigrants are taking their jobs and ruining the economy.

This dual panic — of the brown-skinned criminal and the brown-skinned laborer — implodes under the weight of its own contradiction. If these people are truly criminals, then how are they simultaneously stealing your job at Applebee’s or underbidding you for a drywall gig? What is it exactly that you do for a living, that a supposed felon, non-English-speaking refugee from systemic violence can do it better, cheaper, and with more hustle than you?

Spoiler: You’re not actually worried about your job. You’re worried about your self-worth being measured against someone who started with less than nothing and still outpaces you.

The Myth of the Job Thief

Let’s be clear: immigrants — documented or not — are not the ones offshoring your factory jobs to Vietnam. They’re not the ones gutting union protections or automating warehouse work with soulless efficiency. That credit goes to CEOs in corner offices and the politicians they buy.

What immigrants do is fill the underpaid, overworked jobs that privileged Americans often refuse to do. They pick your produce, clean your hotel rooms, cook your food, raise your children, build your houses, and care for your aging parents. They do this for meager wages and little-to-no labor protections, in a system designed to exploit them while scapegoating them for the consequences of late-stage capitalism.

And yet, these same immigrants are vilified for being the cogs that keep your suburban fantasy afloat. They are painted as parasites, when in truth they are the economic engine quietly subsidizing your lifestyle.

Projection and Prejudice

What we’re witnessing isn’t a fear of economic displacement. It’s a crisis of identity.

To admit that an undocumented immigrant can do your job — and do it better — is to admit that your whiteness, your citizenship, your so-called cultural superiority doesn’t make you inherently more capable. It threatens the myth of meritocracy. And for many people, that’s unbearable.

So instead, they retreat into projection: if immigrants work hard, they must be cheating the system. If they succeed, it must be at your expense. If they endure the abuse and still thrive, they must be criminals. It’s not you who failed; it’s them who cheated.

This is why so much of anti-immigrant rhetoric is tinged with resentment and paranoia. It isn’t about crime or safety. It’s about a culture unable to reckon with its own mediocrity when it no longer gets to define success solely through whiteness.

A Convenient Political Tool

This contradiction isn’t accidental. It’s cultivated. Politicians and media demagogues understand that stoking this kind of fear is politically profitable. Immigrants make an ideal scapegoat: they can’t vote, they often can’t speak back, and they’re easily stereotyped.

The dual lie — that they are criminals and job thieves — is the perfect cocktail of fear and resentment. It inflames the base, distracts from corporate exploitation, and justifies militarized borders and carceral state expansion. It keeps people punching down instead of looking up.

And it works. It has worked for decades. From the Chinese Exclusion Act to the Bracero Program to Trump’s wall, this nation has always used immigration as a mirror to project its own anxieties. The faces change — Irish, Italian, Mexican, Syrian — but the tactics remain the same.

What You Actually Do for a Living

So let’s return to the viral tweet that launched this discussion:

"If you think that Mexico is only sending drug dealers and rapists, but also worry that Mexicans are going to take your job... What the fuck do you do for a living?"

It’s not just a clever jab. It’s a demand for accountability.

If your identity, your self-worth, your economic survival is so fragile that it crumbles in the face of a migrant willing to clean hotel toilets for minimum wage — then maybe you’re not the victim. Maybe you’re the soft underbelly of a society addicted to convenience and allergic to reflection.

What you do for a living, it turns out, is benefit from immigrant labor while whining that it exists.

Abolish the Cruelty, Not the Worker

We must stop pretending this debate is about safety, or economics, or national security. It’s about power, racial hierarchy, and fear of irrelevance.

The solution isn’t more ICE raids, more cages, or more walls. It’s a living wage, universal healthcare, labor protections, and a pathway to citizenship. It’s a society where no one is disposable and where labor is honored, not exploited.

And yes, while we’re at it — we must abolish ICE. It is a paramilitary organization designed to brutalize brown bodies, separate families, and terrorize communities. It has no place in a democracy.

Immigrants aren’t the threat. Your cowardice is. Your refusal to look in the mirror is. Your desperate clinging to a system that promised you unearned supremacy and is now delivering just deserts — that is the problem.

And until you confront it, you’ll keep screaming at the people mopping your floors while your boss steals your pension.

Wake the fuck up.
