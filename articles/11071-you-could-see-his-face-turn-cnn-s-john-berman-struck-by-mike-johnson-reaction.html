'You could see his face turn': CNN's John Berman struck by Mike Johnson reaction

House Speaker Mike Johnson/CNN

CNN's John Berman describes the reaction from House Speaker Mike Johnson when he was asked about Ghislaine Maxwell.

The Louisiana Republican appeared Friday morning on "CNN News Central," where Berman asked him about Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche interviewing the Jeffrey Epstein co-conspirator, who was subsequently transferred to a minimum-security prison camp after saying she had never witnessed President Donald Trump behaving inappropriately with women or girls of any age.

"When I asked Johnson about Ghislaine Maxwell, I read back a quote from Maxwell's interview," Berman said. "You could actually see his face turn. I mean, he really could not possibly hold her in lower regard, so he told me he discounts everything she says. But he also went on to say some interesting things about what might happen in Congress, because there is this discharge petition that could ripen in the next few weeks from Congressman [Thomas] Massie, from your home state of Kentucky, and also [Rep.] Ro Khanna from California, and what was interesting is he really was evasive on it."

"Speaker Johnson really seems to think that maybe the conditions have changed, maybe that won't be something that's followed through with with 218 signatures," Berman said. "So stand by for that, he won't give a solid answer."

The House speaker also declined to give a solid answer on the president's call for a sweeping crime bill, including billions of dollars of new funding for law enforcement in Washington, D.C., where Trump has called in the National Guard to help patrol the streets, and Berman asked why troops weren't deployed to Johnson's hometown of Shreveport, which has a much higher crime rate.

"It is interesting to hear the way Speaker Johnson does talk about that, because the only cities where President Trump has talked about sending National Guard are in states run by Democrats," Berman said. "There are high crime cities in states run by republicans like Louisiana, like Tennessee, Nashville and Memphis, have higher crime rates, but you don't hear the administration or senior Republicans talk about sending the National Guard there."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Dance of Evasion: Speaker Mike Johnson's Masterclass

In a recent display of political evasion that could only be described as balletic, House Speaker Mike Johnson managed to dodge, weave, and pirouette around critical questions posed by CNN's John Berman with the finesse of a seasoned politician. But let's not mistake evasion for eloquence. The topics at hand – Ghislaine Maxwell's comments and the deployment of the National Guard to combat crime – are of significant public interest and merit straightforward answers, not the rhetorical equivalent of smoke and mirrors.

Ghislaine Maxwell: A Convenient Dismissal

Johnson's reaction to Maxwell's statement is a classic example of selective hearing that plagues our political landscape. By emphasizing his disdain for Maxwell, he conveniently sidesteps the substance of her comments about President Trump. This maneuver is as transparent as it is tired. It allows Johnson to dismiss her statements without addressing the broader implications of her relationship with Epstein and the powerful figures she was connected to. The mention of a discharge petition that could shake up Congress adds intrigue but, true to form, Johnson gives us nothing but evasiveness. It's a tantalizing hint at possible political upheaval left frustratingly unexplored.

The Selective Deployment of the National Guard

The discussion regarding the deployment of the National Guard to combat crime in Washington, D.C., while ignoring cities like Shreveport, reveals a clear bias in the administration's approach to crime. Johnson's non-answer to why troops weren't deployed to his own high-crime hometown speaks volumes. It's an acknowledgment of the political calculus that prioritizes the optics of law and order over genuine safety concerns. The fact that only Democrat-run cities have been targeted by these federal interventions lays bare the partisan motivations behind these decisions. Berman's observation that high-crime cities in Republican states are overlooked is a damning indictment of the current administration's commitment to addressing crime across the board.

The Art of the Non-Answer

Throughout the interview, Johnson's reluctance to provide "solid answers" is a masterclass in political evasion. His non-committal stance on both the Ghislaine Maxwell comments and the selective deployment of the National Guard is emblematic of a broader issue in politics today: the unwillingness to engage with uncomfortable questions. This evasiveness does a disservice to the public, which deserves transparency and accountability from its leaders.

Conclusion: A Call for Clarity

House Speaker Mike Johnson's interview with CNN's John Berman was an exercise in obfuscation. In an era where trust in political leaders is waning, Johnson's evasive responses only fuel skepticism and disillusionment. It is imperative that our elected officials remember their obligation to the public extends beyond party lines and political expediency. The American people deserve clear, honest answers to the pressing questions of our time. Until politicians like Johnson can provide them, the dance of evasion will continue to erode the foundations of our democratic discourse.
