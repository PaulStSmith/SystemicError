'Absolutely false': CNN fact checker busts 'very important lie' buried in Trump tirade

The Egg Is the Alibi

One Useful Price in a Costlier Economy

Donald Trump told a Republican convention that prices were “rapidly going down.” The evidence presented by CNN’s Daniel Dale shows the opposite: overall food prices had risen 4.3 percent since Trump took office, grocery prices 3.4 percent, and recent inflation stood at 3.4 percent. Eggs were cheaper. That exception became the sales pitch.

Power Chooses the Comparison

This is not merely statistical sloppiness. The president has access to the institutional reach of the White House and the commanding attention granted to a nearly two-hour speech. He used that power to select one favorable price and present it as proof of a broad economic trend that the cited data contradicts.

The misdirection matters because families do not buy an inflation rate—or live on eggs alone. They pay across categories. Trump’s framing asks them to treat an isolated decline as more authoritative than their accumulated expenses.

A Governor Cast as the Jailkeeper

Trump also blamed former North Carolina governor Roy Cooper for releasing Carlos Brown Jr., the man charged with murdering Iryna Zarutska. But the reporting cited by Dale says Brown completed a separate sentence and was released in September 2020. A prison-conditions settlement came months later, in February 2021.

Brown’s name appeared on a list connected to that settlement because it retroactively counted some people who had already been released—not because Cooper personally freed him early. Trump erased that chronology and replaced it with a simpler political weapon: attach a horrific killing to a Democratic candidate and let administrative complexity disguise the false accusation.

Grief Converted Into Campaign Material

The institutional power here belongs to the president, not to the former governor being assigned personal responsibility for a completed criminal sentence. Trump made the accusation. The convention supplied the platform. The surrounding political apparatus benefited from transforming a murder into a story about Democratic permissiveness.

That is scapegoating: responsibility is moved away from the actual sequence of events and toward a designated partisan target. The victim’s death becomes evidence for a claim the timeline does not support.

The Perfect Record That Cannot Exist

Trump ended by claiming he had delivered on every campaign promise. Dale identified obvious failures, including pledges to end the Ukraine war within 24 hours, tame inflation quickly, and make Mexico pay for a border wall.

The function of such a claim is not to invite an audit. It is to abolish the idea that an audit is necessary. A president declaring himself perfectly faithful asks supporters to substitute loyalty for memory and assertion for measurement.

Propaganda by Exception, Accusation, and Exhaustion

The pattern is larger than three false or misleading claims. A favorable exception becomes the whole economy. A bureaucratic footnote becomes personal culpability for murder. A record containing broken promises becomes flawless performance.

Fact-checking can correct each statement, but the political method operates through volume and repetition. The president possesses the platform to manufacture the frame; journalists arrive afterward to litigate its details. That imbalance is the systemic error: executive power gets to produce political reality at scale, while correction is treated as a technical supplement rather than evidence of deliberate governance through misdirection.
