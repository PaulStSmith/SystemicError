'Shelves will be empty': How CEOs of US’ 3 largest retailers convinced Trump to reverse economic policy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump in the White House on April 7, 2025 (Noamgalai/Shutterstock.com)

Critics of President Donald Trump's steep new tariffs have been warning that a variety of bad things could occur if he doesn't reconsider, from inflation to a deep recession to a weakening of the U.S. dollar to burning bridges with longtime U.S. allies all over the world. But on Monday, April 21, another troubling possibility was raised: "empty shelves" at major retail chains.

According to Axios reporters Marc Caputo and Ben Berkowitz, the CEOs of three major retail chains — Target, Walmart and Home Depot — "privately warned" Trump "that his tariff and trade policy could disrupt supply chains, raise prices and empty shelves, according to sources familiar with the meeting."

A Trump Administration official, interviewed on condition of anonymity, told Axios, "The big-box CEOs flat-out told him (Trump) the prices aren't going up — they're steady right now — but they will go up. And this wasn't about food. But he was told that shelves will be empty."

In an article published on April 23, Caputo and Berkowitz explain, "Another official briefed on the meeting said the CEOs told Trump disruptions could become noticeable in two weeks. While that was happening, financial markets were slumping — stocks, bonds, the dollar — as investors panicked about Trump's latest threats to oust Fed Chair Jerome Powell and step on the central bank's independence."

The Axios reporters continue, "Then on Tuesday, (April 22), he turned the dial down. His Treasury secretary, and then his press secretary — and then Trump himself — all indicated that trade talks with China were imminent, starting on a good foot, and would result in a deal with much lower tariffs than the current 145 percent. Trump then told reporters in the Oval Office that he had 'no intention' of firing Powell, even though his top economic adviser said last week the White House was studying the details of doing exactly that."

Caputo and Berkowitz note that Trump's "shift in tone comes at the same time as a shift in Trump's orbit."

"Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been exerting more influence in recent weeks, reportedly getting in Trump's ear — to the point of rushing the Oval Office when other advisers aren't around — to get him to ease off for the sake of markets," the Axios reporters observe. "The big picture: For the first time since he entered political life, polls show most voters disapprove of Trump's handling of the economy…. Around the world, the 'sell America' trade is taking hold, as investors start to realize decades of orthodoxy about U.S. assets as safe havens may no longer be the case."

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Turmoil: Trump's Trade Troubles

In a world where the words of a president can sway markets, rattle alliances, and alter the economic forecast in mere moments, President Donald Trump's decision to impose steep tariffs has sent shockwaves far beyond the confines of Wall Street. Critics have long cautioned against the myriad of adverse outcomes these tariffs could precipitate, ranging from inflation to a deep recession, from a weakened U.S. dollar to strained relationships with age-old allies. Yet, a new specter looms on the horizon, one that could hit the average American where it hurts the most: empty shelves in the nation’s beloved retail chains.

According to an Axios report by Marc Caputo and Ben Berkowitz, the CEOs of Target, Walmart, and Home Depot delivered a stark warning during a private meeting with Trump. They outlined a grim future where the president's trade policies could severely disrupt supply chains, inflate prices, and, most alarmingly, lead to empty shelves. This isn't about a shortage of luxury goods but a potential scarcity of everyday necessities.

An anonymous Trump Administration official provided Axios with a rather ominous quote, saying, "The big-box CEOs flat-out told him (Trump) the prices aren't going up — they're steady right now — but they will go up. And this wasn't about food. But he was told that shelves will be empty." This stark warning is a clear indication of the tangible impacts Trump's tariffs could have on American consumers.

The situation became even more dire when financial markets began to slump, reacting not just to the tariffs but also to Trump's threats against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell and the central bank's independence. In the midst of this chaos, Trump's administration signaled a potential softening of their stance, with Treasury Secretary, press secretary, and Trump himself hinting at imminent trade talks with China which could lead to much lower tariffs.

Yet, this shift in tone and the subsequent revelations about internal pressures within Trump's orbit—such as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's influence—paint a picture of a tumultuous administration grappling with the economic realities of its policies.

The Big Picture

For the first time since Trump's political ascent, polls indicate a majority of voters disapprove of his handling of the economy. This is monumental, considering the economy was once Trump's stronghold among his base. The global perception of U.S. assets as safe havens is waning, a sentiment that could have far-reaching consequences for American economic dominance.

The narrative spun by Caputo and Berkowitz is not just a tale of tariffs and trade wars; it is a cautionary account of a president's policies in direct conflict with the well-being of the very electorate he claims to serve. The looming question now is: At what point will Trump voters realize the tangible consequences of his administration's economic policies?

The Axios article serves as a critical piece of journalism, shedding light on the intricate and often obscured machinations of trade policy and its real-world impacts. Yet, it also underscores a broader issue: the need for a coherent, thoughtful approach to trade that considers both the macroeconomic picture and the everyday lives of Americans.

In this era of global economic interconnectedness, the actions of a single nation—especially one as economically pivotal as the United States—can have unprecedented global ramifications. It's high time for a reevaluation of trade policies, with a keen eye on sustainable economic growth, global stability, and, crucially, the welfare of the average American consumer.
