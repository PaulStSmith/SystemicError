US teetering on edge of a cliff thanks to Trump’s deep insecurities: conservative

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20263 min read

Insecurity Is Not the Story. Power Is.

The Source, Briefly

The reporting here is not about a new policy detail so much as a familiar Washington habit: turning Donald Trump into a personality puzzle. Bret Stephens and Frank Bruni argue that his “titanic insecurities” drive destructive decisions, including tariffs, antagonizing allies, and his ceasefire agreement with Iran, which Bruni calls a debacle. Stephens adds that Trump benefits from a “polluted information ecosystem” and a loyal base immersed in propaganda.

The Wrong Diagnosis

This framing is convenient because it sounds analytic without requiring accountability. Trump’s psychology may shape how he governs, but the office he occupies is not a therapy case. He is the holder of institutional power. He makes decisions. Those decisions have state-backed consequences.

Calling the problem “insecurities” softens what is plainly a political fact: a president can use the machinery of government to impose costs on millions of people, damage alliances, and reward loyalists. That is not confusion. That is executive power being used badly and often deliberately.

Who Actually Decides

The article circles around Trump’s personality, but the decisive actor is still Trump. He is not a passive patient acted on by mood swings. He chooses tariffs. He chooses diplomatic rupture. He chooses which conflicts to escalate and which to declare solved.

Stephens comes close to this truth when he says Trump “never pays the price he should” and “makes sure of that.” That is the real story. The political system, the party structure around him, and the media environment all help insulate him from consequences. The damage is not produced by insecurity alone. It is produced by a coalition of enablers, courtiers, and institutions willing to absorb the blast radius so he does not have to.

Propaganda Is Not an Accident

Stephens is right that Trump benefits from a polluted information ecosystem, but the way this gets framed matters. The loyalist base is described as if it lives in a separate reality, as though propaganda simply drifted in from nowhere.

It did not. It was built, maintained, and exploited. Trump feeds on it. His allies profit from it. Parts of the right-wing media system exist to convert failure into identity and abuse into belonging. That is not ordinary confusion among voters; it is organized political conditioning. When the source text calls supporters gullible, it risks smearing the audience while leaving the machinery that manipulates them only partially named.

The Article’s Safe Distance

There is also a familiar elite dodge in the piece: Trump is portrayed as unstable, vulgar, and psychologically broken, but not fully as an agent of political destruction. That lets commentators sound appalled without confronting the harder reality that a man like this keeps winning because many powerful actors decide, repeatedly, that his usefulness outweighs the damage.

Even Stephens, who is harsher than Bruni, still frames the issue around whether Trump will “pay” politically for “foreign policy fecklessness.” That is the wrong measure. The first consequence is not electoral embarrassment. It is the exercise of power itself. Once the president can turn personal grievance into state action, the country is already paying.

The Pattern

This is the recurring American arrangement: elite commentary reduces governance to temperament, then acts surprised when cruelty becomes policy. Personal pathology is made to stand in for structure. Deliberate harm gets narrated as emotional excess. The office remains intact. The enablers remain in place. The blame gets scattered across personality, media noise, and tribal gullibility.

That is the system working exactly as designed. Trump is the visible face of it, not the whole machine. The deeper scandal is how easily political culture treats concentrated power as a character flaw instead of what it is: a method of rule.
