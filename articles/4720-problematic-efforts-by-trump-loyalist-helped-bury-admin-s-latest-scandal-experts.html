'Problematic' efforts by Trump loyalist helped bury admin's latest scandal: experts

One of President Donald Trump’s fiercest loyalists appeared to play a key part in burying the administration’s latest scandal, according to two experts.

Last week, it was reported that Department of Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer’s husband, Shawn DeRemer, had been barred from the agency’s office in Washington, D.C., after he was accused of sexually assaulting a female employee. That step is extraordinary in and of itself, but even more so considering that DeRemer has not been charged with a crime.

The Washington Metropolitan Police Department concluded its investigation on Thursday and found insufficient evidence to substantiate the allegations against DeRemer. U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, a former Fox News host, also declined to bring charges against DeRemer.

Even so, Pirro appeared to play a central role in making the whole thing go away, according to Legal “Diva” Melba Pearson and former Florida state attorney Dave Aronberg, who discussed the scandal on the latest episode of the “Legal AF” podcast.

Pearson noted that Pirro’s decision not to bring charges was questionable, given that the agency had decided to prohibit DeRemer from being on the premises.

“What perturbs me is that Jeannie Piro ... she’s like, ‘Yeah, I saw the tape. Nothing doing.’ which under normal circumstances, I would take the prosecutor’s side,” Pearson said.

“Why would you ban him from the building if there was nothing to see?” she added.

Pearson added that the Department of Labor did not bring in an outside investigator to look into the allegations against DeRemer. Instead, that duty fell to the agency’s Inspector General, she said.

Aronberg said the episode made him question how the accuser could get a fair shot at justice at all.

“The number one criterion for Trump is not job performance, it’s loyalty,” he said.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Dance of Politics and Justice: Unpacking the DeRemer Scandal

In the latest episode of the political theatre that seems to perpetually orbit the Trump administration, we find ourselves dissecting an incident that teeters on the precipice between scandal and whitewash, involving Lori Chavez-DeRemer’s husband, Shawn DeRemer. At the heart of this story is an accusation of sexual assault within the hallowed halls of the Department of Labor, an accusation that seems to have been met with a curious blend of action and inaction, raising eyebrows and questions in equal measure.

The Allegations and the Acquittal

Shawn DeRemer, accused of sexually assaulting a female employee, finds himself in the peculiar position of being barred from the Department of Labor’s premises without being charged with a crime. The Washington Metropolitan Police Department, after its investigation, concluded there was insufficient evidence to substantiate the allegations against DeRemer, a decision mirrored by U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, whose credentials as a former Fox News host only add layers of intrigue and potential bias to her decision-making process.

The Role of Jeanine Pirro: A Question of Bias?

Pirro’s decision not to bring charges against DeRemer, despite the Department of Labor’s drastic step to ban him from its offices, is a focal point of contention. Legal experts Melba Pearson and Dave Aronberg, voicing their concerns on the “Legal AF” podcast, highlight the dissonance between the agency’s protective measure and the judicial system’s reluctance to pursue the matter further. Pearson’s skepticism towards Pirro’s dismissal of the case, especially considering her connection to Trump’s circle of loyalists, underscores a fundamental concern: Is justice being overshadowed by political loyalty?

The Investigation: Internal vs. External Scrutiny

The decision not to employ an outside investigator further muddies the waters. Instead, the task of investigating the allegations fell upon the agency’s Inspector General, a move that could be seen as keeping the investigation “in-house” and, potentially, more controllable. This choice begs the question: Could an external investigation have brought a different perspective, or even a different outcome?

Trump’s Criterion: Loyalty Over Justice?

Aronberg’s observation that loyalty trumps job performance in the Trump administration is a damning indictment of its priorities. The implication that justice might be bent, circumvented, or even ignored for the sake of protecting one of its own is deeply troubling. It paints a picture of an administration where loyalty is not just valued but is a prerequisite for survival. This ethos, where loyalty potentially outweighs truth and justice, is a dangerous precedent.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Need of Correction

The DeRemer scandal, with its blend of allegations, political loyalty, and questionable judicial decisions, is emblematic of a broader issue within the Trump administration and, indeed, within parts of the American political system. When political loyalty can cast a shadow over the pursuit of justice, the very foundations of democracy and fairness are at risk.

In a system where the scales of justice are unbalanced by the weight of political affiliations and loyalty, the call for transparency, impartiality, and accountability becomes not just necessary but urgent. The DeRemer case should not just fade into the backdrop of political scandals that have come to characterize the Trump administration; it should be a clarion call for systemic change.
