Political commentators and journalists challenged Vice President JD Vance over his claim on the social platform X that there were 20 million undocumented immigrants in the Biden administration.

Lawyer and Democratic researcher Will Stancil called the Vice President's statement "an insane fabrication that overstates the real number by a factor of 20 or more."

Vance, while responding to another post on X, said, "[Former President] Biden overwhelmed the system with illegal migration. Is your proposed solution to give a jury trial to all 20 million illegal aliens (more if you count those already here)?"

"You are hiding behind 'due process' while ignoring that your actual solution means the ratification of a Biden border crisis that was rejected at the ballot box," he added.

"Vance is a treasonous fool who is openly demeaning the Constitution, but don’t overlook that his claim of 20 million undocumented immigrants in the Biden administration is an insane fabrication that overstates the real number by a factor of 20 or more," Stancil wrote in response to Vance's post.

"Part of why due process is important is that without it, fascist fools like Vance can make claims like 'we need to suspend the rule of law and deport 20 million people under emergency conditions' and there’s no way to challenge the fact it’s an extraordinary and malicious lie," he continued.

"If you’re thrown in a foreign gulag, you become a terrorist, child predator, drug smuggler by default, because if you weren’t Trump and Vance wouldn’t have put you there. It’s completely authoritarian," he added.

Sportswriter Pablo Torre also weighed in, calling Vance's argument "tragically desperate."

"The tragically desperate argument that the VP of this country is making in the replies here is that America does not have the resources to listen to the Supreme Court anymore," Torre wrote on X.

Vance often gets into arguments on X, defending the administration's policies.

On Tuesday, he had a similar exchange with renowned musician Mikel Jollett, over the Supreme Court ruling that ordered the administration to bring back a Maryland man who was wrongfully deported to a prison in El Salvador.

"The entire American media and left wing industrial complex has decided the most important issue today is that the Trump admin deported an MS-13 gang member (and illegal alien)," Vance wrote on X on Tuesday, sharing a video from the Oval Office that showed the Trump team aggressively responding to CNN reporter Kaitlan Collins' question about the deported man, Kilmar Abrego Garcia.

In response to the vice president's post, Jollett wrote, "No, the most important issue is that the Supreme Court ruled by a 9-0 decision that you must return him."

Some political commentators on X agreed with Jollett's view that the vice president is simply pretending not to know what the Court ruling says.

"Intentionally obtuse. JD Vance thinks you’re too stupid to understand the facts. — he’s not a gang member — they’ve been ordered to facilitate his return — [El Salvador President Nayib] Bukele has already returned a dozen migrants — all they have to do is ask for him to be released and put him on a plane," wrote Democratic strategist Sawyer Hackett.

Our Analysis:

JD Vance's Dance with Disinformation: A Closer Look

In a recent flurry of activity on the social platform X, Vice President JD Vance has found himself at the center of a storm, spinning a narrative that veers dangerously into the realm of the absurd. His claim that the Biden administration is somehow responsible for an influx of 20 million undocumented immigrants is not just an exaggeration—it's a gross misrepresentation that plays fast and loose with the facts.

"An insane fabrication" aptly describes Vance's assertion, as pointed out by lawyer and Democratic researcher Will Stancil. By inflating numbers to astronomical proportions, Vance isn't merely stretching the truth—he's creating a parallel universe where facts are malleable and reality is negotiable. This isn't just misleading; it's a deliberate attempt to manipulate public perception.

The Real Cost of Overblown Rhetoric

Vance's rhetoric is more than just political posturing; it has real-world implications. By suggesting that the U.S. is swamped by an impossible number of undocumented immigrants, he's painting a picture of a nation under siege. This isn't just fearmongering; it's a calculated move to justify extreme measures. The suggestion to suspend due process and deport millions under "emergency conditions" is a chilling proposal that runs counter to the very fabric of American justice and democracy.

The Dangers of Dismantling Due Process

Stancil's response to Vance underscores a critical point: due process is a cornerstone of the American legal system. It's what prevents the government from arbitrary actions against individuals, ensuring fairness and justice. Vance's cavalier attitude towards discarding due process in favor of mass deportations is not just reckless; it's a leap towards authoritarianism. His stance is a red flag, signaling a disdain for legal protections when they stand in the way of his agenda.

The Tyranny of Unchecked Claims

What's particularly alarming about Vance's approach is his apparent belief that he can make outlandish claims without consequence. This isn't just about being wrong; it's about fostering a culture where truth is irrelevant. As Stancil and others have pointed out, without the mechanisms to challenge these falsehoods, we're left in a landscape where might makes right and the truth is whatever those in power say it is.

The Accountability of Public Figures

Public figures like Vance have a responsibility to engage in honest discourse. When they choose instead to propagate lies and engage in demagoguery, they erode public trust and undermine the principles of democratic debate. It's concerning that Vance opts to engage in these tactics on social platforms, where sensationalism often overshadows substantive discussion.

Conclusion: The Urgent Need for Fact-Based Discourse

The controversy surrounding Vance's statements is a stark reminder of the importance of fact-based discourse in our political conversations. It's crucial that public officials be held accountable for their words, especially when those words have the power to shape perceptions and policies. As citizens, we must remain vigilant, questioning the claims made by our leaders and seeking out the truth. In an age of misinformation, our democracy depends on our ability to discern fact from fiction.
