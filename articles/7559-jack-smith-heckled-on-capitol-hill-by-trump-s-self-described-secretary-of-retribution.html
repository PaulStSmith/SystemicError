A former U.S. Army Reserve lieutenant colonel and Green Beret who has become a far-right political operative and activist recorded himself heckling former special counsel Jack Smith on Capitol Hill, following Smith and shouting derogatory names.

Ivan Raiklin, who previously billed himself as Donald Trump’s “secretary of retribution” during the campaign, recorded himself hassling Jack Smith for targeting him with a subpoena.

“Jack Smith. Jacka-- Smith,” Raiklin says for the camera. “Hey, Jack? Why did you come after me in your subpoenas?”

Raiklin said four subpoenas targeted Save America PAC, Save America Joint Fundraising Committee, MAGA PAC, and Trump MAGA. He claimed Smith wanted to find out of Trump campaign official Chris LaCivita and now Chief of Staff Susie Wiles had communicated with Raiklin

“Sir, you can hear me,” Raiklin said, following Smith through the hallways as he ignored Raiklin. “Respond. Jack, why do you think many people out there call you Jacka-- Smith, sir? With all due respect. Sir!”

Raiklin continued shouting at Smith and shouting questions about his January 6th investigation.

“Why do you have to be so ignorant, just like Jamie Raskin, just like Bennie Thompson, just like the entire Pelosi Fedsurrection cover-up committee? Care to respond, Jack? I’m waiting Jack! And so is the rest of America! How about you respond, Jack? The hunters become the hunted, huh? Come on Jack!”

Raiklin previously fantasized about “live-streamed swatting raids” that round up politicians, government officials and journalists

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Raiklin’s Harassment of Jack Smith

In a scene that could only be described as a descent into the surreal, Ivan Raiklin, a former U.S. Army Reserve lieutenant colonel turned far-right operative, took it upon himself to transform Capitol Hill into a stage for his own brand of political theater. His target? Former special counsel Jack Smith. The act? A one-man show of harassment, a spectacle that underscores not just the personal decay of Raiklin but the broader erosion of political discourse in America.

Raiklin, who once fancied himself as Donald Trump’s “secretary of retribution,” recorded his own juvenile antics as he followed Smith, hurling insults and posing loaded questions. “Jack Smith. Jacka-- Smith,” he begins, a juvenile attempt at wit that sets the tone for the entire encounter. He pesters Smith about subpoenas, seemingly proud of his own perceived persecution, and insinuates a vast conspiracy involving Smith’s investigation into the January 6th insurrection.

The Art of Ignorance: Raiklin’s Quest for Attention

It’s a spectacle of ignorance, a man who once served his country with honor now reduced to echoing the basest elements of political discourse. Raiklin accuses Smith of being part of a “Fedsurrection cover-up committee,” a claim as unsubstantiated as it is inflammatory. His shouting in the hallways, demanding attention and responses, mirrors the larger problem of a political landscape that rewards noise over substance, confrontation over conversation.

Raiklin’s past fantasies of “live-streamed swatting raids” against politicians and journalists reveal a disturbing glorification of violence and intimidation against perceived political enemies. This is not the behavior of a man interested in justice, democracy, or the truth. It’s the tantrum of an individual lost in his own echo chamber, a chamber that amplifies the most destructive impulses of political discourse.

The Real Tragedy

The tragedy here isn’t just Raiklin’s fall from grace, or even the harassment of a public servant like Jack Smith. It’s the fact that such acts are no longer outliers but part of a growing trend of political engagement that eschews debate for derision, investigation for intimidation.

What Raiklin fails to understand, in his quest to become the hunter, is that he’s not exposing a conspiracy; he’s contributing to the very fabric of disunity and discord he claims to despise. His actions don’t serve as a beacon of truth but as a mirror reflecting the ugliest parts of our current political ethos.

In the end, Raiklin’s harassment of Smith is a microcosm of a larger, more concerning phenomenon. It’s a symptom of a political culture that’s becoming increasingly tolerant of, if not outright encouraging, behaviors that undermine the principles of democracy and civil discourse. As Raiklin’s video fades to black, one can’t help but wonder: when did the pursuit of justice become a sideshow attraction, and how can we find our way back from this brink?
