'Extremely unusual' DOJ move just put U.S. in 'red zone for danger': ex-prosecutor

By Paulo Santos

As Trump’s Justice Department moves ahead with its investigation into the Obama administration over claims of “treason,” former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance warned that the DOJ’s latest actions have just put the United States “in a red zone for danger.”

First announced by Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, the investigation alleges that former President Barack Obama and other administration officials knowingly pushed false claims of Russian interference in the 2016 election, claims that President Donald Trump has characterized as an effort to politically target him.

And while the investigation has already been described as a dud and without merit by many critics, including by many of Trump’s own supporters, Vance suggests that its very existence sets a new, dangerous precedent going forward.

“Survival among Trump’s appointees is about one thing and one thing only: keeping the boss happy,” Vance wrote Tuesday in a legal analysis on Civil Discourse with Joyce Vance.

“(Attorney General Pam) Bondi understands that. But keeping the boss happy is not an attorney general’s job. The Justice Department isn’t supposed to be a political tool in the president’s hip pocket. We are in a red zone for danger.”

This week, Bondi moved to direct federal prosecutors to launch a grand jury investigation into the DOJ’s claims against Obama administration officials. This development, Vance said, suggests that the case “is quite far along,” or that “a serious investigation is underway,” which itself, she argued, “smacks of political interference.”

“It's extraordinary that we know about it at all,” Vance wrote. “Even though witnesses aren’t bound by the same grand jury secrecy rules that prosecutors and others are, it’s extremely unusual to have this sort of public revelation of a grand jury process.”

Experts have largely decried the claims behind the DOJ’s investigation as “wildly misleading,” and unlikely to lead to any criminal indictments. However, Trump, who as president is expected to maintain a distance from investigations carried out by the judicial branch, continues to champion Bondi’s efforts, telling CNBC this week that Obama officials “deserve” to be investigated, and that he was “happy to hear” it had been launched.

“If the president can dictate who gets indicted and who doesn’t, all pretense of fairness would be replaced with a world where prosecutions could be politically motivated,” Vance wrote. “Insulating (the) DOJ from direct presidential control is essential to preventing weaponization of the justice system by an administration against its political opponents or for personal gain.”

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Precedent of Politicizing Justice

In a move that can only be described as audaciously brazen, Trump’s Justice Department has initiated an investigation into the Obama administration over allegations of “treason,” claims so outlandish they would be comical if they weren’t so perilously serious. Spearheaded by Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, this inquiry targets former President Barack Obama and other officials for supposedly disseminating false narratives of Russian interference in the 2016 election. President Trump has labeled these efforts as a personal attack against him, a stance that not only distorts reality but dangerously politicizes the Department of Justice (DOJ).

Joyce Vance, a former federal prosecutor, has aptly warned that these recent actions by the DOJ signal that the United States is now "in a red zone for danger." This investigation, already dismissed as baseless by a broad spectrum of critics—including some within Trump’s own ranks—nonetheless sets a harrowing precedent for the future of American democracy.

The Misguided Pursuit of Justice

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s directive for federal prosecutors to convene a grand jury signifies, as Vance suggests, either the advanced stage of this investigation or an alarmingly serious commitment to it. The transparency of this grand jury process, typically shrouded in secrecy, is unprecedented and unsettling. It suggests a level of political theater that undermines the sanctity of the judicial system.

The claims fueling this investigation have been widely denounced as “wildly misleading” by experts, casting significant doubt on the likelihood of any resulting criminal indictments. Yet, Trump’s enthusiastic endorsement of Bondi’s efforts, coupled with his assertion that Obama officials “deserve” to be investigated, underscores a grotesque manipulation of justice. It reveals an administration more concerned with vendettas than veracity.

The Real Job of the Attorney General

Vance’s analysis reminds us that the role of the Attorney General is not to serve as the president's personal lawyer, but to uphold the laws of the United States impartially and without political bias. The DOJ's venture into these waters, therefore, is not just a deviation from its mission—it's a dereliction of duty.

The principle of insulating the DOJ from presidential influence is foundational to the integrity of the American legal system. This barrier prevents the weaponization of the justice system for political retribution or personal gain. Trump’s overt interference, cheered on by Bondi, obliterates this vital safeguard, threatening to replace the presumption of fairness with a regime of fear and favoritism.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This investigation into the Obama administration for spurious claims of "treason" is more than a misguided legal pursuit; it's a blatant political maneuver designed to distract, divide, and delegitimize. It exacerbates the already polarized state of American politics and erodes trust in public institutions.

The actions of Trump’s DOJ under the guise of this investigation do not merely flirt with authoritarianism; they embrace it. It is a clarion call for all who care about the principles of justice and democracy to stand in vigilant opposition. The weaponization of the justice system—under any administration—must be recognized for what it is: a direct threat to the rule of law and the democratic fabric of the United States.
