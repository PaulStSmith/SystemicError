Aromatherapy by GLaDOS: The Rise of AI-Enabled Snake Oil

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 1, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Somewhere between the collapse of rational thought and the monetization of human gullibility, someone decided the world needed Astrology Birth Chart GPT. Yes, you read that right. A machine-learning model wrapped in the veneer of science fiction, deployed to tell you Mercury is in retrograde and that your failed job interview wasn’t your fault—it was the stars conspiring against you.

This is the end point of two grifts colliding. On one hand, astrology: the eternal carnival game of vague platitudes that promise to explain the chaos of existence. On the other, the current AI hype cycle, where slapping “GPT” on a product guarantees clicks, investment, and at least a few subscription fees from the credulous. It’s less about divining your future and more about cashing in on your desperation. Welcome to the 21st century: snake oil, now with machine learning.

The sales pitch writes itself: “Expert astrologer GPT that needs your birth info to answer queries.” Translation: a chatbot armed with astrological boilerplate and a calendar. Instead of a boardwalk psychic, you get a server farm regurgitating the same nonsense, but with the added insult of pretending it’s data-driven.

We shouldn’t be surprised. Once AI became a buzzword, it was inevitable that charlatans would graft it onto their age-old scams. After all, it’s a perfect symbiosis: astrology gives the illusion of cosmic order, AI gives the illusion of precision. Together, they form the unholy offspring of mysticism and technobabble—something that sounds smart, feels personal, and drains your wallet.

Imagine the roadmap. Today: Astrology GPT. Tomorrow: Past Life Regression GPT. By next year, why not Homeopathy Analyzer GPT? Just feed it your symptoms, and it’ll recommend water with “memory” for only $19.99 a month. Or perhaps Chakra Alignment GPT, offering real-time advice on which USB-C crystal adapter to buy in order to balance your “energetic field.” Don’t laugh—it’s probably already in beta somewhere.

The cruel joke is that these AI-driven scams exploit the same vulnerabilities humans have always had: fear, loneliness, uncertainty. The tools evolve, but the manipulation remains constant. The astrologer’s robe has simply been replaced by a slick UI and a glowing “Powered by GPT” badge. And because people trust what looks technical, the grift now comes with an aura of legitimacy it never deserved.

Here’s where the satire stops and the warning begins: AI should never be reduced to a con artist’s sidekick. When powerful technology is bent toward old-school superstition, it doesn’t just fleece the gullible—it corrodes the very idea of rational discourse. It normalizes pseudoscience by dressing it in lab coats and algorithmic jargon. That erosion matters. It bleeds into politics, into health, into how societies make decisions. If we can’t draw a hard line between science and astrology, between knowledge and hocus-pocus, we’re sunk.

So yes, laugh at Astrology Birth Chart GPT. Mock it as the absurdity it is. But also recognize the playbook: AI is being stapled onto every scam, every placebo, every magical thinking enterprise that sees profit in confusion. What’s next isn’t just “Aromatherapy by GLaDOS.” What’s next is an entire industry of weaponized nonsense, cloaked in the credibility of algorithms.

And the worst part? The cake is still a lie, but the subscription fee will be auto-renewed.
