‘I can destroy the country!’ Trump doesn't hold back on reaction to Supreme Court ruling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 21, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Tariff Tantrum: A Closer Look

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a press briefing at the White House, following the Supreme Court’s ruling that Trump had exceeded his authority when he imposed tariffs, in Washington, D.C., U.S., January 20, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump attacked the Supreme Court’s 6-3 ruling against his tariff authority, calling the decision “ridiculous.” The court determined Trump acted outside his authority when imposing tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, or IEEPA, rendering a significant portion of his tariffs as unlawful. Trump expressed outrage at the decision during a White House address, claiming the court prevented him from charging tariffs while permitting embargoes and trade restrictions. He said, “I can do anything I want to do to them, but I can’t charge any money,” adding, “so I’m allowed to destroy the country, but I can’t charge them a little fee.” Trump has centered much of his presidency on tariff policy and previously warned the ruling could cause a $2 trillion “catastrophe” and reduce the United States to “almost Third World status.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Tariff Tantrum: A Closer Look

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as the Trump Tariff Tantrum, the former President lambasted the Supreme Court’s 6-3 ruling against his use of tariff authority. Let’s dissect this display of outrage and the implications of the court’s decision with the precision of a scalpel cutting through the fluff.

The Supreme Court’s Decision: A Blow to Autocratic Economics

First, the heart of the matter: The Supreme Court, in a decisive 6-3 vote, found that Donald Trump overstepped his bounds when imposing tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). This ruling isn’t just a slap on the wrist; it’s a full-on body slam to the notion that the President can unilaterally dictate economic terms under the guise of an emergency. The court essentially said, “Not so fast, cowboy,” to Trump’s fast-and-loose interpretation of his economic powers.

Trump’s Reaction: A Masterclass in Hyperbole

Never one to take things in stride, Trump’s reaction was as subtle as a bull in a china shop. He called the decision “ridiculous,” a term he seems to reserve for any outcome that doesn’t align with his personal agenda. In a fiery White House address, Trump lamented, “I can do anything I want to do to them, but I can’t charge any money,” and, “so I’m allowed to destroy the country, but I can’t charge them a little fee.” This hyperbolic rhetoric is classic Trump, blending victimhood with grandiose claims of power. It’s as if he’s saying, Look at me, the all-powerful leader, now shackled by the chains of reason and law.

The Catastrophe Hyperbole

Trump warns of a $2 trillion “catastrophe” and suggests that the ruling could reduce the United States to “almost Third World status.” This claim is as inflated as it is unfounded. It’s an attempt to use fear-mongering to sway public opinion, a tactic as transparent as it is tiresome. The idea that limiting the President’s ability to impose tariffs willy-nilly would lead to economic downfall is not just a stretch; it’s a leap across a chasm of logic.

The Bigger Picture: Checks and Balances in Action

What Trump views as an affront to his authority is actually a cornerstone of American democracy at work: the checks and balances system. The Supreme Court’s ruling is a reminder that the President is not above the law, nor can he sidestep the constitutional boundaries that define his powers. This decision reinforces the idea that no single branch of government should have unchecked authority, especially when it comes to decisions that can have wide-ranging impacts on the economy and international relations.

Conclusion: A Reality Check for Trump

The Supreme Court’s ruling against Trump’s use of tariff authority is more than a legal setback for the former President; it’s a reality check. It underscores the limitations of presidential power, especially when it comes to making unilateral economic decisions under the guise of emergency powers. Trump’s reaction, filled with outrage and hyperbole, is a testament to his often tumultuous relationship with the concept of checks and balances. As we move forward, let this be a lesson in the importance of adhering to the principles of our Constitution, even when it’s inconvenient for those in power.
