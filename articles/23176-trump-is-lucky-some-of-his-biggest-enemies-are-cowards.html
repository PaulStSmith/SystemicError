Trump is lucky some of his biggest enemies are cowards

Based on an article by Ralph Nader

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Is Not Winning by Accident. Democrats Are Helping Him

Power, Not Atmosphere

The source’s basic point is clear: Donald Trump keeps moving because the people with the power to slow him down keep choosing not to. Trump holds the executive branch. Senate Republicans have repeatedly protected him. Democratic leaders control neither chamber in this moment, but they do control their own response, their own agenda, and their own willingness to turn abuse into public record.

That distinction matters. The article is right to treat “cowardice” as a political fact, but it blurs the mechanics. Democrats cannot single-handedly remove Trump from office. They can, however, use minority power to build a case, force votes, stage hearings, and make governing cost something. Instead, the leadership is acting like silence is a strategy rather than a surrender.

The Decision Was the Refusal

The source describes Trump issuing illegal executive orders, surrounding himself with Elon Musk’s DOGE operation, and taunting Democrats with “This is just the beginning.” It also notes that Rep. Al Green introduced a privileged impeachment resolution on June 24, 2025, only to be opposed by Jeffries and Schumer.

That is the key political decision in the story. Not Trump being Trump. Not the expected Republican cover-up. The decision was Democratic leadership declining to weaponize the tools still available to them. They did not need a majority to start a public fight. They needed will. They declined.

Misdirection as Institutional Habit

The article is strongest when it identifies how blame gets redirected downward. The public is told to focus on process anxiety, polling fear, and strategic caution. Staffers say they do not know “how it would play out.” Leadership says it does not want to “get ahead” of discussion. This is the language of managed helplessness.

That framing lets deliberate harm masquerade as uncertainty. Trump is not confusing the issue; he is daring opponents to normalize it. The problem is that Democratic leadership keeps responding like the point is to avoid embarrassment rather than to build power. When the stakes are impeachment, shadow hearings, and public documentation of abuse, hesitation is not neutrality. It is accommodation.

The Media Is Following the Cue

The source also lands a real critique on the press. Editorial pages can describe Trump’s damage in exhaustive detail and still refuse to name the remedy. That is not balance. It is evasion. A press that can diagnose the disease but will not say what institutional action follows is helping convert outrage into wallpaper.

The same weakness shows up in the progressive ecosystem the article names. Mentioning impeachment without organizing around it is cheap. It creates the appearance of seriousness without the burden of escalation. Trump benefits from that gap. So does leadership that prefers performative concern to a concrete fight.

What This Pattern Reveals

The deeper pattern is not just Trump’s brutality. It is the Democratic elite habit of treating confrontation as a risk to be minimized rather than a duty to be used. That is how democratic institutions rot from the inside: not only through authoritarian aggression, but through opposition that keeps lowering its own standards for resistance.

If Nixon was pushed out over lesser conduct, the lesson is not nostalgia. It is that accountability used to mean something more than commentary. Today, the party that claims to be the institutional barrier to authoritarianism keeps acting like its job is to manage the optics of collapse. That is the real scandal in the source material: not that Trump is pushing limits, but that Democratic leadership keeps answering with delay, caution, and excuses while the damage compounds.
