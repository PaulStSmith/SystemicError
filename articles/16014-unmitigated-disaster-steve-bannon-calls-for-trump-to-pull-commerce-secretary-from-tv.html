'Unmitigated disaster': Steve Bannon calls for Trump to pull commerce secretary from TV

MAGA influencer Steve Bannon lashed out at Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, calling him "close to an unmitigated disaster" for television appearances to sell President Donald Trump's trade war.

During his Monday War Room program, Bannon suggested Lutnick was wrong to think new manufacturing in the U.S. would be automated.

"We're not doing this so we can set up so robots have a better life," Bannon said. "In fact, I don't know why Lutnick is still doing media."

"Let me be blunt, Lutnick, who is Elon's pick for Secretary of Treasury, I think he's close to being an unmitigated disaster," he continued. "We should see a lot less of Lutnick on TV."

Instead, Bannon said that Trump should encourage TV hits by economic adviser Kevin Hassett, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, trade adviser Peter Navarro, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

"Stephen Miller should be doing more about trade and about this new economic order that we're trying to hammer through," he added.

Lutnick is on record saying that an "army" of Americans would assemble iPhones in the near future.

"The army of millions and millions of human beings screwing in little screws to make iPhones — that kind of thing is going to come to America," he told CBS News.

Our Analysis:

Bannon's Blast: A Closer Look at the MAGA Influencer's Tirade Against Lutnick

In a recent eruption that can only be described as vintage Bannon, MAGA influencer Steve Bannon has taken a sharp turn against Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Labeling Lutnick as "close to an unmitigated disaster" for his television appearances aimed at promoting President Donald Trump's trade war, Bannon's critique is as scathing as it is revealing. Let's dissect the layers of this outburst and what it signifies about the internal dynamics of the Trump administration's economic strategy.

Lutnick's Automation Faux Pas

Bannon's primary grievance with Lutnick appears to be a fundamental disagreement on the future of American manufacturing. Lutnick's vision of a highly automated manufacturing sector seems to clash with Bannon's more populist and labor-intensive approach. "We're not doing this so we can set up so robots have a better life," Bannon quips, a statement dripping with his characteristic blend of sarcasm and populist rhetoric. This underscores a deeper ideological rift within the administration: Do we prioritize technological advancement and efficiency, or do we focus on creating jobs, even if they're less efficient?

The Bannon Solution: Less Lutnick, More Traditionalists

Bannon's solution to what he perceives as Lutnick's media missteps is straightforward: sideline him in favor of more traditional, perhaps protectionist, voices within the administration. He names a roster of preferred spokespersons, including economic adviser Kevin Hassett, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, trade adviser Peter Navarro, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Bannon's picks are telling, singling out figures known for their hawkish stance on trade and their alignment with Bannon's own protectionist views.

The Underlying Tension

What we're witnessing is more than just a spat between high-profile personalities. It's a microcosm of the ongoing struggle within the Republican Party and the broader conservative movement over its future direction. Should the party embrace globalization and the technological advancements that come with it, or should it stick to a more nationalist, protectionist agenda that prioritizes American jobs over all else?

Lutnick's "Army" of iPhone Assemblers

Lutnick's comment about an "army" of Americans assembling iPhones is particularly illustrative. It's an ambitious, if not wholly unrealistic, vision of bringing manufacturing jobs back to the U.S. This notion, while appealing to American workers displaced by globalization, overlooks the complex realities of global supply chains and the inevitable march towards automation.

Bannon's Broader Agenda

Bannon's critique of Lutnick is not just about trade policy; it's about asserting his vision of what Trump's economic policy should embody. In advocating for figures like Stephen Miller to take a more prominent role in trade discussions, Bannon is pushing for a harder line on immigration and trade, two issues he believes are central to Trump's appeal and the future of the country.

Conclusion: Bannon's Call to Arms

Steve Bannon's tirade against Howard Lutnick is more than just a personal vendetta or a policy disagreement; it's a clarion call for a return to the populist, nationalist policies that he believes propelled Trump to the White House. Whether one agrees with Bannon or not, his influence within MAGA circles cannot be underestimated. As the Trump administration navigates its trade policy, the debate between automation and job creation, between globalism and nationalism, will continue to rage. And in this debate, Bannon has made his position crystal clear.
