Donald Trump is a huge problem, but there is a “much, much, much, much, much bigger” one, according to a former lawmaker who is now a registered Democrat.

Joe Walsh was once a Tea Party Republican congressman, but he abandoned his party with the coming of Trump, became an independent, and is now a Dem. He sounded the alarm on Thursday about the lengths Republicans will go to avoid criticizing Trump.

“If Donald Trump next week began demolishing the Washington Monument—because he wanted to change the structure of the Washington Monument and make it look more like Mar-a-Lago or the shape of a T for Trump or whatever—would any Republican say anything?” he asked. “I mean that. Would any Republican object? Would anybody on Fox News say, ‘Stop. That’s too far’? Would any of Trump’s voters, any of his MAGA supporters say anything?”

Walsh concludes that, no, they would not stand up to the president.

“Donald Trump is demolishing the White House right now as we speak, and no Republican has said anything. No Trump supporter has said anything. Nobody on Fox News or OAN or Newsmax or right-wing talk radio, my former home, has said anything. Donald Trump has basically torn the East Wing of the White House down all by himself, and Republicans haven’t said s---,” the former lawmaker added.

Still, he warns, there’s a larger concern than Trump’s actions.

“You see, Trump is a problem, a big problem. He’s everything our Founders feared. He’s an utterly corrupt, dishonest, cruel, ignorant, psychopathic, lawless fascist. That’s a big, big problem to have that in the White House,” he wrote. “But the much, much, much, much, much bigger problem are all the fascist enablers—every enabler and appeaser over the years, these past 10 years, who have allowed Trump to do what he does. Trump could not be demolishing the White House as we speak if his enablers didn’t let him.”

Walsh also added, “Trump would not be demanding $230 million from the Justice Department if his enablers didn’t let him. Trump could not have tried to overthrow an American election if his enablers didn’t let him. Trump couldn’t have dismantled USAID, leading to the deaths of hundreds of thousands of children around the world, if his enablers had not let him. Trump couldn’t be going after and trying to imprison his political opponents if his enablers did not let him.”

Our Analysis:

The Enabler’s Dilemma: Tracing the Roots of Trump’s Unchecked Power

In an era where political discourse often verges on the surreal, former Republican congressman turned Democrat Joe Walsh delivers a narrative that reads less like hyperbole and more like a harrowing glimpse into the soul of the current American political landscape. His thesis? Donald Trump is a monumental problem, but the legion of enablers behind him poses a threat of even greater magnitude.

Trump: The Symptom, Not the Disease

Walsh’s comparison of Trump to a bulldozer tearing through the sanctity of American democracy - imagining him literally demolishing the Washington Monument to reshape it in his own image - serves as a potent metaphor. It underscores not just the reckless abandon with which Trump operates but the silent acquiescence that empowers him. But here’s where Walsh cuts deeper: he asserts that Trump, with all his despotic tendencies, is merely a symptom of a much larger, more systemic issue.

The Enablers: A Silent Coup

The crux of Walsh’s argument rests on the “fascist enablers” - a term he uses to describe the collective of individuals and institutions that have not only allowed Trump’s rise but have facilitated his grip on power. This includes, but is not limited to, Republican lawmakers, certain media outlets like Fox News, OAN, and Newsmax, and, by extension, the MAGA base itself.

Walsh’s indictment of these enablers is scathing. He characterizes their silence and complicity as the real driver of Trump’s ability to flout norms with impunity. Whether it’s dismantling parts of the White House (a metaphor for his broader impact on American institutions) or making outlandish demands from the Justice Department, Walsh posits that none of it could happen without a network of supporters willing to look the other way or actively support his actions.

Beyond Trump: A Culture of Enabling

What Walsh illuminates is not just a Trump problem, but a systemic failure—a culture of enabling that predates and, likely, will outlast Trump himself. This culture is not confined to the political arena; it permeates various facets of American society, where power, once unchecked, continues to grow at the expense of democratic principles and human lives.

His mention of Trump’s alleged attempts to overthrow an election, dismantle USAID, and target political opponents speaks to a broader, more insidious pattern of behavior. It’s a pattern facilitated by a network of enablers who, through their silence or actions, have allowed such conduct to become normalized, or at least insufficiently contested.

The Verdict

Joe Walsh, in his transformation from a Tea Party Republican to a Democrat critic of Trump, embodies a political journey that mirrors the broader existential crisis within the Republican Party and American politics as a whole. His warnings, steeped in the regret of a former insider, serve as a clarion call to recognize and confront not just the figureheads of corruption and authoritarianism, but the structures and individuals that empower them.

In essence, Walsh’s narrative is a stark reminder: the fight for the soul of America is not merely against individual demagogues like Trump, but against the far-reaching network of enablers who, through their action or inaction, pave the way for such figures to thrive. Ignoring this network, and failing to dismantle it, may well lead to the erosion of the very foundations upon which American democracy stands.
