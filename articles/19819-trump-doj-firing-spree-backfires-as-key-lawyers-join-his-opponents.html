Trump DOJ firing spree backfires as key lawyers join his opponents

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump speaks to members of the media on board Air Force One en route from Scotland to Maryland, July 29, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Donald Trump appears to have made a major mistake with his purge of top lawyers from the Department of Justice, as many of these “all-star” professionals are now joining the institutions fighting back against his presidency, in a turn of events one former attorney dubbed “idiocy.”

On Tuesday, CBS News’ top justice correspondent Scott MacFarlane shared a video to X and BlueSky detailing this trend, likening it to a sports team trading its best players to rival teams.

“It’s hard enough to get by without your best players on the field, but you certainly don’t want to play against them, and have huge adversaries when you play your opponents,” MacFarlane explained.

The Trump administration, he argued, “has been getting rid of some of its all-stars” in the DOJ, and it is starting to become a huge “problem” as they join the efforts to “challenge the administration,” essentially “joining the rival team.” That is on top of the severe drain of professional experience and “institutional memory” that these hiring purges have cost the government across every department.

As MacFarlane laid out in the video, Trump’s purge of these top lawyers was driven primarily by political motivations and a desire for personal retribution. Many of the attorneys who got the ax when Trump returned to the White House were the ones who had worked to convict Jan. 6 rioters, whom he gave a mass pardon early on in his term. He also cut many individuals who took part in the federal investigation of him during his time away from Washington, D.C.

“Some have been fired, some have been pushed out, some have said, ‘You know what? I’ve had about enough of this politicized Department of Justice, I’m out of here,’” MacFarlane said. “I’m moving on to greener pastures.”

MacFarlane gave the example of Mike Romano, a “storied, prolific” former DOJ lawyer known for his “incredible record” prosecuting Jan. 6 defendants. Since leaving the federal government in 2025, he has joined a firm handling “labor cases, among other things.” He also recently testified before the Senate last week in a hearing where he “reinforced the truth about Jan. 6, that it was a righteous prosecution and an important prosecution, pushing back on the administration’s false claims and lies.”

He also highlighted the Washington Litigation Group, a nonprofit staffed with many individuals who previously worked with Jack Smith on the criminal cases against Trump. This group is now taking part in the fight against such things as Trump’s effort to add his name to the Kennedy Center and the closure of a “key civil rights division” within the DOJ.

“These nonprofits and firms are capitalizing on the Department of Justice’s idiocy,” one anonymous former prosecutor told MacFarlane.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s DOJ Purge: A Strategic Blunder of Epic Proportions

In what can only be described as a spectacular miscalculation, Donald Trump’s decision to purge top lawyers from the Department of Justice (DOJ) has backfired in a manner that borders on political comedy, were the stakes not so alarmingly high. The ousted “all-star” professionals, rather than disappearing into obscurity, are banding together, forming a formidable front against his presidency. This scenario, aptly termed “idiocy” by a former attorney, should be a lesson in the dangers of letting personal vendettas dictate professional decisions.

The Counterproductive Purge

Trump’s move to rid the DOJ of top talent, particularly those involved in convicting Jan. 6 rioters or investigating him, reeks of political motivations and a desire for personal retribution. This isn’t just a purge; it’s a self-inflicted wound. As CBS News’ Scott MacFarlane deftly points out, it’s akin to a sports team trading its best players to a rival team—an analogy that captures the absurdity and the self-defeating nature of Trump’s actions.

The Folly of Undermining Institutional Memory

The drain of professional experience and institutional memory cannot be overstated. The DOJ, a cornerstone of American democracy and law enforcement, operates at its best when it builds upon the cumulative knowledge and expertise of its professionals. By ejecting these seasoned lawyers, Trump hasn’t just lost their individual competencies; he’s eroded the foundational strength of the institution itself.

The Boomerang Effect

In an ironic twist, these ousted professionals are now joining or forming institutions that stand in direct opposition to the Trump administration. Take Mike Romano, a former DOJ lawyer with an impressive record in prosecuting Jan. 6 defendants, who has since transitioned to fighting for labor cases and reinforcing the truth about Jan. 6 against the administration’s narrative. Or consider the Washington Litigation Group, staffed with veterans from the criminal cases against Trump, now engaged in battles that directly challenge the administration’s policies.

These developments are not just a testament to the resilience and integrity of these professionals but also highlight a glaring strategic blunder by Trump. The administration’s efforts to suppress dissent and control the narrative have, in fact, empowered a new cadre of legal adversaries, well-versed in the inner workings of the DOJ and highly motivated to uphold the principles of justice they believe Trump is undermining.

Conclusion: The Price of Political Retribution

What we’re witnessing is not merely a shuffling of personnel but a fundamental shift in the battle lines being drawn against Trump’s presidency. The administration’s attempt to install loyalty over expertise has resulted in a significant and potentially lasting blow to its own legal defenses. This scenario underscores a broader cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing essential government institutions for personal gain.

In their zeal to consolidate power and shield themselves from accountability, Trump and his allies have inadvertently strengthened the resolve and resources of their opposition. This isn’t just idiocy; it’s a strategic misfire that could have profound implications for the legal battles looming on the horizon for Trump and his administration. The irony and folly of this situation would be amusing if the consequences for American democracy and justice weren’t so dire.
