When Propaganda Rebrands: 'Europa', Neo-Nazis, and the GOP's Shadow

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 4, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Last week, YouTube recommended a video to me from a channel I follow: Into the Shadows. The video was titled "The Modern-Day Answer to Mein Kampf." Intrigued and disturbed in equal measure, I clicked. What I found wasn't just a content warning—it was a siren.

The subject? Europa: The Last Battle — a ten-hour propaganda epic that oozes through the internet's underbelly with high production value, moody narration, and a premise so grotesque it's almost parody: that Hitler was a misunderstood peace-seeker, the Allies were the true warmongers, and the Holocaust? Fabricated. A lie. An elaborate myth crafted to vilify Nazi Germany.

If your bile hasn't risen yet, let it. Because this film isn't some obscure Reddit post or fringe podcast. It's a fully weaponized, algorithm-optimized digital poison. And here's the kicker: until I saw the takedown video by Simon Whistler, I'd never even heard of Europa. Now? Over 170,000 people have watched that exposé, 8,000 have clicked "like," and nearly 5,000 have commented. That, my friends, is not niche. That's momentum. That's the Streisand Effect screaming from the digital rafters.

The video, to its credit, doesn't flinch. Whistler and his team watched every second of this bile and disassembled it with surgical precision. But exposing propaganda always carries a risk: that in naming it, you lend it oxygen. And right now, oxygen is the one thing fascism thrives on in the algorithmic age.

This isn't about the YouTube algorithm alone. It's about the deeper infection: ideology. How dangerous revisionist narratives like Europa aren't just lurking in Telegram channels and 4chan threads anymore—they're being laundered through the rhetorical rinse cycle of American conservatism. Tucked neatly behind euphemism and misdirection, echoed—softly, strategically, plausibly deniably—on CPAC stages and Fox News primetime.

Let’s be clear about what Europa is selling:

  • That Hitler didn’t want war.

  • That the Jews started it.

  • That the Holocaust never happened.

  • That capitalism and communism are Jewish conspiracies.

Sound familiar? It should. That rot isn't confined to one film. It’s metastasized. These are not novel lies—they are the foundational dogmas of every fascist resurgence since 1945. The difference now? They're coded into phrases your average GOP congressman can parrot without breaking a sweat:

  • "Globalists."

  • "Soros."

  • "White replacement."

  • "America First."

They've traded the swastika for the Stars and Stripes and made hate patriotic again. And the right-wing media complex is complicit. When Tucker Carlson pushes Great Replacement Theory with a straight face, when MTG talks about "Jewish space lasers," when Ron DeSantis censors AP African American Studies because it includes discussions of systemic racism—you’re not watching political theater. You’re watching ideological laundering. The toxic laundry list of Europa given a red, white, and blue rinse.

And let’s not kid ourselves. There is overlap. Not perfect, not uniform, but undeniable. The same talking points that once required white hoods or skinhead tattoos now come in a "concerned parent" Facebook post. They've mainstreamed.

The modern American right, particularly its MAGA wing, may not be broadcasting Europa on Jumbotrons—yet—but it's dancing with the same ghosts. When January 6th rioters wear "Camp Auschwitz" hoodies, when a sitting congressman compares COVID restrictions to Nazi Germany, when candidates openly muse about the "purity" of American heritage—we're not flirting with fascism. We're brushing shoulders with it in the grocery store.

Simon Whistler's video did a commendable job debunking the film’s lies. He laid out the historical facts, exposed the distortions, and showed the receipts. But there’s a deeper layer we have to excavate: Europa is not an outlier. It's a fossilized roadmap for authoritarianism repackaged for 21st-century consumption. It is not being kept in a bunker. It's mainstreaming. And the GOP has not just failed to resist it—it's opened the door.

We should not shy away from discussing Europa. But we must be crystal clear in our purpose: not to give it reach, but to autopsy it. Dissect its bones. Trace the sinews that now stretch into school boards, statehouses, and Senate hearings.

Holocaust denial is not just a lie—it's the final desecration. The last violence done to the dead. And rehabilitating fascism isn't just some nerdy historical misread—it's a prelude. A throat-clearing before the chant starts again.

This isn’t a historical curiosity. It’s a fire alarm.

And it's deafening.

History doesn’t repeat itself. But it echoes. And Europa is just another echo of that old, lethal melody. We ignore it at our peril. Because this time, it doesn’t need a Nuremberg rally. It just needs a viral TikTok and a shrug from your congressman.

Speak louder. Speak sharper. The bastards are already organizing.
