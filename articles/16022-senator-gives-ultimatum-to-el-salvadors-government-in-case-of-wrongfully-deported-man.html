Senator gives ultimatum to El Salvador’s government in case of wrongfully deported man

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 17, 20254 min read

Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.) has requested a meeting with El Salvador President Nayib Bukele during his current visit to the United States to discuss the return of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland man who was wrongly deported to an infamous Salvador prison due to an "administrative error" by the Trump administration.

In a letter to the Salvadoran ambassador to the U.S. Milena Mayorga written Monday, Hollen said U.S. Supreme Court ruled unanimously that Abrego Garcia was wrongly deported to a prison in El Salvador.

"I have met with Mr. Abrego Garcia’s wife, mother and brother and, as you can imagine, they are extremely worried about his health, safety, and continued illegal confinement, as am I," he wrote in the letter.

"I look forward to your response," the Maryland Democrat told the envoy.

During a press conference alongside Bukele in the Oval Office on Monday, Trump claimed that the Supreme Court had ruled 9–0 in his favor and that the ruling in the case meant that the U.S. government would have to provide a plane only if the Salvadoran president chose to return Abrego Garcia, a Maryland man wrongfully deported to an infamous Salvadoran prison.

“Of course I’m not going to do it,” Bukele said when asked by reporters if he would help return the man, adding that returning him would be akin to smuggling “a terrorist into the United States.”

In a statement sharing the letter, the Senator said, “If Kilmar is not home by midweek — I plan to travel to El Salvador this week to check on his condition and discuss his release."

Democratic strategists and commentators are calling on other Democratic lawmakers to follow in Sen. Van Hollen's footsteps and call for Abrego Garcia's return.

"What’s happening to him is wrong, and we need Dems to draw attention to it," Democratic strategist Mike Nellis wrote on the social platform X.

Academic Keith Orejel, while sharing the letter on X, said, "Make Chris Van Hollen minority leader. Oust Schumer and all the other spineless collaborationists who have said next to nothing about this issue."

Sparky's Analysis:

The Kafkaesque Nightmare of Kilmar Abrego Garcia: A Scathing Indictment of Administrative Error and Political Apathy

In what reads like a plot straight out of a dystopian novel, the plight of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland man wrongfully deported to an infamous Salvadoran prison, lays bare a chilling narrative of bureaucratic blunder and cold-hearted political maneuvering. Senator Chris Van Hollen's (D-Md.) recent entreaty to El Salvador President Nayib Bukele to discuss Garcia's return underscores the profound injustice perpetrated by an "administrative error" during the Trump administration. Yet, the layers of this saga reveal a deeper, more systemic rot within the U.S. deportation and immigration machinery, as well as a glaring disregard for human rights by international leaders.

The Supreme Injustice

The U.S. Supreme Court's unanimous ruling in favor of Abrego Garcia's wrongful deportation stands as a rare beacon of judicial clarity in this murky tale. However, the Trump administration's perverse interpretation of this ruling during a press conference with President Bukele is nothing short of a mockery of the legal system. Trump's claim that the Supreme Court ruled 9–0 in his favor, coupled with Bukele's outright refusal to assist in Garcia's return—branding the act as akin to "smuggling a terrorist into the United States"—is a harrowing testament to the distortion of justice at the highest levels of government.

A Senator's Lone Crusade

Senator Van Hollen's determination to address this grievous wrong, including his plans to visit El Salvador to check on Garcia's condition and discuss his release, is commendable. Yet, it also highlights a disturbing silence and inaction from other quarters of the Democratic Party. The calls by Democratic strategist Mike Nellis and academic Keith Orejel for more Democratic lawmakers to rally behind Van Hollen's efforts, and the suggestion to replace current leadership with figures willing to confront such issues head-on, underscore a desperate need for moral backbone and assertive action within the party.

The Larger Picture: A Systemic Malaise

This incident is not an isolated aberration but a symptom of a broader systemic failure. The ease with which an individual can be wrongly deported to potentially life-threatening conditions abroad—due to an "administrative error"—raises alarming questions about the mechanisms and safeguards (or the lack thereof) within the U.S. immigration system. Moreover, the brazen politicization of human lives, as evidenced by Bukele's and Trump's cavalier dismissal of a Supreme Court ruling and a man's plight, reflects a troubling erosion of democratic norms and human rights.

Conclusion: The Need for Vigilance and Action

The case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia should serve as a wake-up call. It is a stark reminder of the vulnerability of individuals within a labyrinthine and often callous immigration system, and of the ease with which political leaders may trample rights when it suits their agenda. Senator Van Hollen's initiative is a step in the right direction, but it requires robust support and follow-through. The Democratic Party, and indeed all stakeholders in the democratic process, must intensify their efforts to safeguard the principles of justice and human dignity. Anything less would be a tacit endorsement of the systemic error and political apathy that led to this egregious situation.
