'The irony': Trump social media guru whines as 'hysterical' influencers turn on president

Based on an article by Jordan Green, Investigative Reporter

Paulo SantosJuly 7, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The ContextPower Is Not On Social MediaThe Scam Called “Influence”The Coalition Is Fraying Because It Was Never HonestWhat This Story Actually Shows

The Troll Who Outgrew His Own Lie

Alex Bruesewitz is not exposing a propaganda machine. He is describing the machine he has helped run, then pretending he has just discovered the gears. The current flare-up inside MAGA, over the war in Iran and the Epstein files, is less a mystery than a familiar collapse: an operation built on manufactured outrage now eating itself when the propaganda stops serving the bosses.

The Context

Bruesewitz, a Trump-aligned social media strategist paid by a PAC tied to the president, went on Tucker Carlson’s podcast and David Freiheit’s show to complain about “coordinated influence campaigns,” social media hysterics, and foreign money warping conservative discourse. He used that framing to defend Trump’s handling of the Epstein files and to attack online critics of the administration’s Iran policy, even as his own history includes helping amplify the racist Springfield lie about Haitians “eating pets” and running influencer operations for Trump’s campaign.

Power Is Not On Social Media

The central lie in this story is that social media chaos is the cause of political damage. It is not. Social media is the delivery system. The decisions came from institutional power: Trump’s campaign, Trump’s PAC network, Trump’s administration, and the Supreme Court ruling that reinforced deportation authority over Haitians after the administration moved against temporary protected status.

Bruesewitz is useful precisely because he is close to that power. He is paid $25,000 a month by a Trump-affiliated PAC through his firm. That is not an outsider looking in. That is an operative inside the apparatus, trying to reassert discipline when the coalition starts slouching out of formation.

The Scam Called “Influence”

Bruesewitz now wants the public to believe that anger over Iran and Epstein is being artificially planted by enemies. Maybe some accounts are manipulated. Maybe some are paid. The source does not prove otherwise. But the deeper point is that this accusation comes from a man whose career is built on the exact tactics he condemns: coordinated influencer blasts, manufactured narratives, algorithmic pile-ons, and racist disinformation laundered through “authentic” grassroots energy.

That is the game. Run a propaganda shop, call it organic, then when the audience notices the wiring, accuse everyone else of being the plant. This is not democratic discourse being corrupted by a few bad actors. It is political theater openly financed, professionally managed, and then cynically disguised as spontaneous outrage.

The Coalition Is Fraying Because It Was Never Honest

Bruesewitz is not worried about democracy. He is worried about coalition management. He says the MAGA split could tear apart a “once unbeatable juggernaut.” That is the only real crisis here: factional discipline inside a movement that depends on permanent grievance, permanent enemies, and permanent submission to Trump.

The cracks are obvious. Carlson breaks with the GOP. Marjorie Taylor Greene drifts. Vance tries to soothe the anti-interventionist wing while signaling around the Iran agreement. Bruesewitz rushes onto podcasts to police the rupture because this movement cannot survive honest disagreement. It needs a steady supply of enemies, not a functioning argument.

The irony is brutal. The same ecosystem that turned a racist rumor about Haitian migrants into a national campaign also wants credit for being the guardian of truth now. The same people who used lies to move Trump’s campaign are suddenly alarmed that other people might be manipulating opinion. The performance is too thin to hide the contradiction.

What This Story Actually Shows

This is not a story about online confusion. It is a story about how authoritarian politics gets maintained in public: paid intermediaries, grievance laundering, race-baiting, foreign-policy panic, and constant attempts to reframe deliberate harm as messy chatter. The objective is not persuasion in any honest sense. It is control.

Bruesewitz’s complaint that “hysterics” are taking over the government is revealing only because it accidentally names the truth. The system he serves is governed by hysterics, monetized by manipulators, and enforced by institutions willing to reward them. The outrage is not a bug. It is the governing style.

The democratic lesson is not to debate this ecosystem more gently. It is to identify who pays for it, who amplifies it, who benefits from it, and who gets hurt when the lies graduate from posts to policy.
