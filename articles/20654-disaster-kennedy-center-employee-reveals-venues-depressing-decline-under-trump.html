'Disaster': Kennedy Center employee reveals venue’s 'depressing' decline under Trump

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosJuly 10, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Turned the Kennedy Center Into a Loyalty Purge

The Point Was Control

The source story is simple enough: since returning to the White House, Donald Trump has treated the Kennedy Center like another property to be conquered, not a public cultural institution to be stewarded. The result has been cancellations, empty halls, dismissals, and a venue run by political appointees with no serious arts-management credentials.

Power, Not Confusion

This did not happen by accident. Trump holds the institutional power; Trump chose the takeover; Trump’s board and Richard Grenell executed it. The people inside the building are not the authors of this collapse. They are the ones absorbing it. When a president installs loyalists and then watches the operation hollow out, that is not administrative turbulence. It is a deliberate capture of an institution.

The Misdirection Machine

The official story around these moves is always some version of competence theater: new leadership, fresh direction, improved efficiency. But the details in this report expose the fraud. Employees were warned out with Friday HR meetings. Positions were filled by people with no relevant background. Grenell’s own justification, as described here, is a perfect emblem of Trump-era unseriousness: political loyalty and personal proximity substituted for expertise.

Culture as a Target

The Kennedy Center matters because it is not just a venue. It is a public-facing symbol of national culture, funded and protected through state power. That makes it useful terrain for a president who wants institutions to perform obedience. Renaming it, remaking it, and staffing it with loyalists are not separate gestures. They are one project: convert a civic institution into a personal brand asset.

Congress Cannot Play Innocent

The article’s call for Congress to amend the charter is not decorative. If lawmakers leave the current structure intact, they are accepting a system where a president can walk in, purge the staff, install amateurs, and degrade the institution while pretending this is normal governance. That is congressional cowardice dressed up as procedure.

The Larger Pattern

What happened at the Kennedy Center is a smaller version of a broader authoritarian habit: seize an institution, replace competence with loyalty, manufacture decline, and then blame the decline on everyone except the people who caused it. The venue’s emptiness is not just an arts story. It is what political vandalism looks like when it gets a federal paycheck.
