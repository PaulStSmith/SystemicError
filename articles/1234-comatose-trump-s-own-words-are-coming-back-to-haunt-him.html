Comatose Trump's own words are coming back to haunt him

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 10, 20263 min read

The President Who Can’t Stay Awake Still Wants Absolute Obedience

The source story is simple enough: Trump keeps being caught with his eyes shut at official events, Rubio denies the obvious, the White House insists he is merely blinking, and the administration keeps converting a basic capacity question into a meme war. That is the surface. The political substance is uglier.

Who Actually Holds Power

Trump is still the center of authority here. He convenes the meetings, sits at the desk, issues the spectacle, and claims credit for the consequences. Rubio does not hold the real power in this scene. He is a subordinate performing loyalty in public, not an independent actor shaping the regime’s direction.

That matters because the article’s real subject is not sleep. It is the structure around a weakened executive: aides, spokespeople, and senior officials who keep moving the furniture while the person at the top visibly loses control of the room.

The Denial Machine

Rubio’s claim that he has “never seen” Trump fall asleep is not evidence. It is an act of institutional obedience. Then the White House response to a Reuters photo, insisting Trump was “blinking,” shows the same reflex: deny the visible fact, insult the questioner, and hope repetition can substitute for reality.

That is not confusion. It is message discipline in service of a fragile boss. The point is not to clarify what happened. The point is to train everyone around power to pretend they did not see it.

A President Built on Performative Strength

Trump spent years weaponizing “Sleepy Joe” as a political insult. The source text lays out the hypocrisy plainly: he mocked Biden for nodding off, then turned around and defended his own closed eyes as concentration, boredom, or an especially intense form of leadership. That is not irony. It is the standard strongman trick: accuse your opponent of decline, then demand exemption from the same standard.

The deeper pattern is that Trump’s politics depend on projection. He turns weakness into ridicule when it belongs to someone else, then converts his own weakness into a joke, a brand, or a denial campaign when it becomes visible.

What Gets Hidden Behind the Eyes Shut

The more serious question is not whether Trump dozed off at a ceremony. It is what happens when the cameras are off. The source text rightly points to the Situation Room, Cabinet meetings, and routine governing moments that do not produce easily mockable clips. A president who cannot stay engaged through official events is not just a punchline. He is a risk vector.

And the article’s framing should be read as an indictment of the political culture around him. The aides keep talking. The spokespeople keep denying. The party keeps covering. The system keeps absorbing obvious signs of incapacity so the myth of control can survive one more day.

The Cost of Pretending This Is Normal

This is how institutional cowardice works. A president who promised competence on “Day One” is instead presented as a man too busy, too sharp, or too important to stay awake, while the government asks the public to ignore what is plainly happening in front of them. The story is not that Trump is old, though he is. The story is that the surrounding political machinery has decided honesty is more dangerous than farce.

That is the larger pattern: personalist power protected by propaganda, where the leader’s visible decline is managed as a communications problem instead of treated as a governing failure. When the state starts covering for the boss’s eyelids, it is already covering for much worse.
