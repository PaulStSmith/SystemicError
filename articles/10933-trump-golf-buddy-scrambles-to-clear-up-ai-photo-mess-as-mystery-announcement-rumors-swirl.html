Trump golf buddy scrambles to clear up AI-photo mess as mystery announcement rumors swirl

U.S. President Donald Trump departs from the Trump National Golf Club in Sterling, Virginia, U.S., August 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump's golfing buddy scrambled Monday to deny multiple claims that a photograph showing the two over the weekend was an AI-generated fake — and that the president was actually seriously ill.

It comes amid wild speculation that a mysterious announcement scheduled for 2 p.m. Tuesday was to address rumors that reached fever pitch over the Labor Day weekend concerning Trump's unusual silence. Hashtags like #TRUMPISDEAD trended on social media, with approximately 158,000 posts suggesting the president had already passed away.

An alarming photograph of Trump, notably lacking his trademark orange complexion, had been viewed over 13 million times by Monday night — with thousands suggesting it was a fake.

MAGA radio host John Fredericks shared the photograph, apparently taken at Trump's National Golf Club, and took to X Monday to argue that it was authentic, stating it was taken by his wife with Secret Service permission.

"I talked to him on Sunday, he looked and felt fabulous," Fredericks said on X. "He had just played 18 holes of golf—he did that for three straight days. The number of lies on social media is stunning."

He added, “The Trump deniers are basically saying this is a fake photo and they ran it through AI. The number of people who went on X and disputed a photo my wife took... was unbelievable... We took [the photo] at President Trump’s direction not ours.

“The number of people that engaged in this and thought this photo was fake and somehow believed that President Trump is dead or he’s sick or he’s in the hospital or he’s not there or these are AI photos is incredible.”

Trump's recent public appearances have done little to quell health concerns. Photographs show a weary-looking president, with ongoing bruising on his hands and strategic attempts to hide swollen ankles in White House photographs. His recent public missteps, including forgetting the name of the Pacific Ocean and failing to recognize the president of Finland, have further fueled speculation about his mental acuity.

The president attempted to dispel rumors through his Truth Social account, posting a flurry of content ranging from AI-generated images to observations about the White House Rose Garden. On Sunday, he directly addressed health concerns, declaring he had "NEVER FELT BETTER IN MY LIFE".

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Trump's Alleged Illness and Digital Doppelgangers

In a saga that reads more like a poorly scripted reality TV show than a chapter from the annals of U.S. presidential history, we find ourselves dissecting the bizarre whirlwind of rumors, AI-generated images, and vehement denials surrounding Donald Trump. The central plot twist? A photograph with his golfing buddy, which has become the eye of a speculative storm concerning the former president's health.

The Photograph Heard Around the World

A picture, they say, is worth a thousand words, but in this case, it seems to be worth a thousand speculations. Donald Trump’s appearance, or rather the lack thereof, in his usual vibrant—dare we say, orange—complexion has set the internet ablaze. The image, garnering over 13 million views, has been the catalyst for a social media frenzy, with hashtags like #TRUMPISDEAD not just trending but exploding. Yet, John Fredericks, a MAGA radio host and the man in the eye of this storm, refutes claims of the photo's artificiality, asserting it was genuinely snapped at Trump's National Golf Club.

Fredericks' defense on X (formerly Twitter, we presume) is a testament to the surreal nature of this entire episode. He claims the photo was taken "at President Trump’s direction," a line that sounds eerily like a script from Trump's reality TV playbook. But here’s where the plot thickens—or rather, where it becomes as thin as the evidence supporting Trump’s recent health claims.

Claims of Vigor Versus Visual Evidence

Trump's recent public outings have been anything but reassuring. Photographs revealing a "weary-looking president" with bruising on his hands and swollen ankles paint a contradictory picture to the narrative of robust health being peddled by his camp. Moreover, his apparent forgetfulness and confusion have only fueled the fires of speculation regarding his mental acuity.

Yet, in true Trump fashion, the counter-narrative is as loud as it is unsubstantiated. Trump's Truth Social account has been a flurry of activity, showcasing a mix of AI-generated images and grandiose claims about making Washington D.C. the "safest city in America." One can't help but marvel at the audacity—or perhaps the desperation—behind the declaration of "NEVER FELT BETTER IN MY LIFE" amidst such damning visual evidence to the contrary.

The Verdict?

In the court of public opinion, the verdict is still out, but in the hallowed halls of reason and logic, the evidence is mounting. The juxtaposition of Fredericks' fervent denials and the concerning visual cues from Trump's recent appearances creates a narrative more befitting a dystopian novel than the reality of American politics.

This entire debacle raises more questions than it answers. Is the reliance on AI-generated imagery an attempt to mask a more troubling reality about Trump's health? Or is it merely another chapter in the long book of Trump's media manipulation tactics? Either way, the truth remains as elusive as ever, buried under layers of digital facades and vehement denials.

In the end, the saga of the AI-generated fake—or not—photograph of Donald Trump serves as a stark reminder of the bizarre era we find ourselves in, where technology, politics, and health rumors collide in a cacophony of chaos. And as always, the truth is the first casualty in this war of perceptions.
