The Constitution can't save us from Trump — and only one thing can

The Mirage of Removing Trump: A Lesson in Power and Misdirection

Constitutional Mechanisms Debilitated by Partisan Control

In the scenario described, where President Donald Trump’s second term spirals into what’s characterized as chaos, incompetence, and corruption, the U.S. Constitution ostensibly provides mechanisms for removal: impeachment and the 25th Amendment. Yet, these structures are fundamentally undermined not by legal inadequacies but by a lack of political will from those in power, specifically the Republican Party. This is the same party that, once a bastion of constitutional governance, has morphed into what can be described as a neofascist cult of personality around Trump. The institutional power lies firmly in the hands of Republican leaders who have shown unwavering support for Trump, regardless of the allegations or evidence against him.

The Illusion of Impeachment

The impeachment process, while historically significant, has proven to be more of a theatrical display than a practical tool for accountability, especially in Trump’s case. Despite repeated charges and substantial evidence, particularly during his second impeachment concerning the January 6 insurrection, the Senate’s Republican majority has acted as a protective barrier for Trump, nullifying the process’s potential. This is not an accident but a calculated manipulation of constitutional mechanisms by those who hold power, clearly illustrating their priorities.

The Ineffectiveness of the 25th Amendment

Similarly, the 25th Amendment, designed to address presidential incapacity, is rendered impotent by the composition of Trump’s administration. With Vice President JD Vance and a Cabinet described as sycophants, the likelihood of them collectively moving to declare Trump incapacitated is fantastical at best. This section of the Constitution, never invoked, remains a theoretical tool, one that assumes a level of integrity and courage absent in Trump’s inner circle.

Misdirection and the Real Battle for Democracy

The focus on impeachment and the 25th Amendment may seem like viable strategies to those desperate for a quick resolution to the perceived threat of Trump’s presidency. However, this is a misdirection. It distracts from the more arduous but necessary task of building a genuine pro-democracy movement. The real battle isn’t in the halls of Congress or the clauses of the Constitution but in the collective action of the electorate to demand and uphold democratic principles.

Toward a Pro-Democracy Movement

The article suggests a strategic pivot towards fostering a broad-based democratic movement, using the examples of impeachment and the 25th Amendment as tools to galvanize public support, not as ends in themselves. This approach mirrors historical shifts in other nations where sustained public pressure and electoral participation have dismantled authoritarian regimes. The focus must be on engaging and educating the electorate, building coalitions that transcend party lines, and creating resilient structures that can uphold democratic values.

Conclusion: A Systemic Call to Action

The story of trying to remove Trump from office reveals a deeper truth about American politics: the real power does not lie in the legal texts of the Constitution but in the hands of those who wield political power. The Republican Party’s transformation into a vehicle for Trump’s authoritarian tendencies is a stark reminder of this fact. The path forward is not through reliance on legal mechanisms alone but through a robust, inclusive movement that reclaims the principles of democracy from the grasp of partisan exploitation. This is not just about one president or one administration but about the systemic resilience of American democracy itself.
