Trump releases details about major trade deal ahead of his own teased announcement

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 9, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Grandiose Announcement: A Closer Look

President Donald Trump highlighted a new detail about a "major trade deal" he's been teasing all week.

The U.S. president first let the cat out of the bag Tuesday, during a White House meeting with Canadian prime minister Mark Carney, and then Wednesday night announced that a trade deal – with "a big, and highly respected country," no less – would be announced Thursday morning at 10 a.m. EST in the Oval Office, but he could barely contain his excitement hours ahead of the event.

"This should be a very big and exciting day for the United States of America and the United Kingdom," Trump posted on Truth Social at 5:42 a.m. EST. "Press Conference at The Oval Office, 10A.M. Thank you!"

Not even a half hour later, he spooled out a few additional details about his less-and-less surprising announcement.

"The agreement with the United Kingdom is a full and comprehensive one that will cement the relationship between the United States and the United Kingdom for many years to come," Trump posted at 6:08 a.m. EST. "Because of our long time history and allegiance together, it is a great honor to have the United Kingdom as our FIRST announcement. Many other deals, which are in serious stages of negotiation, to follow!"

The White House and British officials declined to comment on the president's post on Wednesday night, but both nations have discussed lowering British tariffs on U.S. cars and farm goods, in addition to lifting British taxes on U.S. technology companies.

It's not clear why Trump characterized the U.S.-British relationship as an "allegiance," which refers to loyalty and support for a ruler, country, group or belief, according to the Cambridge Dictionary, rather than an "alliance," which is defined by the same dictionary as a group of countries, political parties or people who have agreed to work together because of shared interests or aims.

International trade attorney Timothy C. Brightbill cautioned the New York Times not to expect much from the announcement, which he predicted would probably be “just an agreement to start the negotiations, identifying a framework of issues to be discussed in the coming months.”

“We suspect that tariff rates, nontariff barriers and digital trade are all on the list — and there are difficult issues to address on all of these,” added Brightbill, an international trade attorney at Wiley Rein.

Our Analysis:

The Grandiose Announcement: A Closer Look

President Donald Trump's flair for the dramatic has once again taken center stage with his announcement of a "major trade deal," this time with the United Kingdom. Trump, ever the showman, teased the deal earlier in the week and built up anticipation for a grand reveal in the Oval Office. However, a careful dissection of the facts surrounding this announcement raises questions about the substance behind the spectacle.

The Premature Celebration

Trump's announcement, disseminated through posts on Truth Social, promised a "very big and exciting day" for the United States and the United Kingdom, hinting at a comprehensive agreement that would "cement the relationship" between the two nations for years to come. Yet, this premature celebration seems to hinge more on pomp and less on circumstance. The White House and British officials' refusal to comment on the president's posts adds a layer of opacity to proceedings that should, in theory, champion transparency and mutual benefit.

Terminological Tangles

Trump's use of the term "allegiance" instead of "alliance" when referring to the U.S.-British relationship is not just a semantic slip; it's a revealing miscue that underscores a fundamental misunderstanding of international relations. An "allegiance" suggests a fealty that does not accurately represent the complex, reciprocal nature of international trade agreements. This choice of words, whether intentional or not, betrays a potentially skewed perception of the dynamics at play.

Skeptical Experts

Timothy C. Brightbill's cautious stance towards the announcement is a stark reminder of the chasm that often exists between political rhetoric and real-world outcomes. Describing the deal as likely "just an agreement to start negotiations" rather than a finalized, substantive agreement, Brightbill injects a necessary dose of reality into the conversation. His prediction that the announcement would center on a framework for future discussions rather than concrete commitments is particularly telling. It suggests that the fanfare surrounding the announcement is more about political theater than about tangible progress in U.S.-UK trade relations.

Conclusion: Substance or Showmanship?

In the final analysis, Trump's announcement appears to be less about the intricate details of international trade and more about the optics of leadership and negotiation prowess. The lack of concrete information, combined with the mischaracterization of the relationship between the two nations, points to a scenario where showmanship outweighs substance. As negotiations presumably continue, one must wonder if the real work of hammering out a trade deal will live up to the grandiosity of its introduction—or if this announcement is merely another chapter in the ongoing saga of political performance art.
