Report of possible Noem firing ignites fears Trump will pick someone even worse

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 8, 20255 min read

Kristi Noem speaks in the Oval Office next to Donald Trump. Photograph: Nathan Howard/Reuters

On Saturday afternoon, December 6, The Bulwark’s Adrian Carrasquillo reported that President Donald Trump, according to three sources, is considering replacing Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

“For weeks,” Carrasquillo explained, “a rumor has been circulating in political circles that Noem may soon be on the outs. It briefly surfaced in a CNN report a few weeks back that listed her first among the Cabinet officials who could be caught in a year-end turnover, noting that while Trump himself has been happy with Noem, top White House officials have grown frustrated with her tenure — specifically, her employment of her divisive and combustible chief adviser, Corey Lewandowski. Noem has downplayed any tensions or concerns. And in a text to me, Lewandowski called b------ on any talk of turnover. ‘None of that is true,’ he said Thursday evening.”

Carrasquillo continues, “But in conversations with three former DHS officials — who served in both the Biden and Trump Administrations and are still in touch with current staff — it was made clear that Trump is indeed considering moving on from Noem.”

Carrasquillo’s reporting is drawing a lot of responses on X, formerly Twitter.

The Bulwark’s Matt Marshall wrote, “Three former DHS officials — who served in both the Biden and Trump administrations and are still in touch with current staff— made it clear to @Carrasquillo that Trump is indeed considering moving on from Noem. Two of those officials said they believed such a move could happen ‘really soon,’ relaying that they’ve been told Noem has been taking on less of a role in directing department memos.”

Amy Coplan, a philosophy professor at Cal State University, Fullerton, argued, “I hesitate to celebrate since I’m pretty confident her replacement won’t be a winner but I would he EXTREMELY happy if she were ousted. What does it say that at this point I think it would be a marked improvement if Tom Homan took over.”

Pediatrician Zev Waldman tweeted, “For Steven Miller? Bovino? Hard to see things getting better over the next year.”

Self-described “determined Democrat” Sue Hagadorn posted, “Good...though it’s always wise to be careful what you wish for.”

The Decatur Daily’s news editor, Franklin Harris, wrote, “If true, it means he just wants an even more draconian execution of his policies.”

Self-described “angry liberal” Goblinsburg joked, “I hear he has an ivory poacher on his shortlist.”

Our Analysis:

A Carousel of Chaos: Trump’s Homeland Security Shuffle

In a political landscape that often feels like it’s scripted by the writers of a particularly dystopian TV drama, the latest report from The Bulwark‘s Adrian Carrasquillo about President Donald Trump’s consideration to replace Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem is both unsurprising and deeply concerning. According to Carrasquillo, who cites three sources, this move is less about effective governance and more about the perpetual turmoil that seems to define Trump’s administration.

The Rumor Mill Churns Again

Let’s dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned politico. For weeks, whispers of Noem’s potential ousting have circulated like a bad rumor in high school hallways—fleeting but persistent. These rumors, initially brushed off as mere speculation, gained traction with a CNN report suggesting dissatisfaction at the top echelons of the White House. The reason? Noem’s alignment with Corey Lewandowski, a figure as divisive as he is combustible. Lewandowski’s rebuttal, branding the turnover talks as “b------”, adds a layer of denial thick enough to cut with a knife.

Between the Lines

The article’s reliance on sources—three former DHS officials spanning both the Biden and Trump administrations—presents a narrative of internal discord and a President ready to pull the trigger on yet another high-profile replacement. This potential move, as noted by The Bulwark‘s Matt Marshall, could be imminent, given Noem’s diminishing role in directing departmental memos.

Public Reaction: A Mixture of Hope and Resignation

The public’s response, as chronicled by Carrasquillo, oscillates between cautious optimism and resigned cynicism. Figures like Amy Coplan and Zev Waldman express a guarded readiness for change, albeit with low expectations for an improvement. The sentiment underscores a broader disillusionment with the administration’s revolving door of officials, where the exit of one controversial figure merely sets the stage for another’s entrance.

Franklin Harris of The Decatur Daily cuts to the heart of the matter, suggesting that a replacement would likely signal a push for even more draconian policies. This perspective, shared by others, hints at a grim reality where change at the Department of Homeland Security could herald a shift towards more extreme measures.

The Bottom Line

In essence, this article lays bare the tumultuous nature of Trump’s administration—a world where loyalty is fleeting, and job security is an oxymoron. The potential ousting of Kristi Noem, with all its implications, serves as yet another episode in the ongoing saga of an administration that thrives on chaos and uncertainty.

While the report from Carrasquillo offers a glimpse into the possible future of the DHS under Trump’s capricious leadership, it also serves as a reminder of the broader implications of such instability. In a realm where policy impacts real lives and global perceptions, the constant shuffle of key positions is not just a political sideshow—it’s a matter of national concern.

In Conclusion, as we stand on the precipice of yet another high-level shake-up, one can’t help but wonder: In the grand chess game of Trump’s administration, are these moves strategic brilliance or the flailing of a regime in constant crisis? Only time will reveal the true impact, but for now, skepticism remains a prudent stance.
