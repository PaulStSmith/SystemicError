'Biggest scandal in American history!' Newt Gingrich shares fury with Lara Trump

Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich believes President Joe Biden’s use of the autopen could become the “biggest scandal in American history,” according to a Fox News report.

The revelation came on Lara Trump’s Fox News show “My View.”

"The reason is, sometimes, I'd think it's probably in 2023… Joe Biden was no longer there,” Gingrich said. “And that means that other people were exercising the power of the presidency without any constitutional limitations, and I really think the key is not to look at Biden, but to look at the various Obama people who had infiltrated that system."

"The one I find the most interesting is that on one day after the election, he commuted 2,400 people. Now, you know he didn't have a clue.” The former House Speaker added, “Somebody was running the autopen. Somebody was giving them instructions and somebody was making deals somewhere. That's just a tiny example of how bad it was."

Fox paraphrased Gingrich on its website, writing, “The backdrop of the ordeal was the decay of the president and the use of his identity as a front for the decisions that may have happened at the hands of some members of his administration behind the scenes.”

Gingrich called this "elder abuse."

President Donald Trump has called for a probe of Biden’s use of the autopen — a tool which Trump himself has used — and he believes many of the documents signed by his predecessor using the device are invalid.

Biden, for his part, denies the allegations and has publicly condemned the probe.

"Let me be clear: I made the decisions during my presidency. I made the decisions about the pardons, executive orders, legislation, and proclamations. Any suggestion that I didn’t is ridiculous and false," Biden said.

In the statement, Biden later added, "This is nothing more than a distraction by Donald Trump and Congressional Republicans who are working to push disastrous legislation that would cut essential programs like Medicaid and raise costs on American families, all to pay for tax breaks for the ultra-wealthy and big corporations."

Our Analysis:

The Autopen Scandal: A Manufactured Drama?

In the latest episode of political theater, former House Speaker Newt Gingrich has declared President Joe Biden's use of the autopen to potentially be the "biggest scandal in American history," as reported by Fox News. Let's dissect this claim with the fierce clarity it deserves, shall we?

The Allegations

Gingrich's allegations came to light on Lara Trump’s Fox News show “My View,” where he suggested that Biden's use of the autopen was indicative of a presidency operated by shadow figures, specifically targeting "various Obama people" who had supposedly infiltrated the system. He highlighted a particular instance where Biden commuted the sentences of 2,400 people in a day, insinuating that Biden was essentially a puppet, with others pulling the strings.

The Reality

First off, the use of the autopen in presidential administrations is neither novel nor scandalous. It's a tool that has been utilized by presidents dating back to Harry Truman. Its use is a practical solution to the logistical impossibility of manually signing every document in the modern era of the presidency. To frame this as a scandal is to ignore historical precedent in favor of sensationalism.

Moreover, Gingrich's claim hinges on the assumption that Biden's physical inability to sign a document himself equates to a lack of decision-making. This is a gross oversimplification of how presidential decisions are made, reducing complex processes to mere penmanship. It's an argument that seems to willfully ignore the myriad of advisers, discussions, and considerations involved in presidential decisions, portraying a caricature of governance rather than its reality.

The Accusation of "Elder Abuse"

Labeling these supposed revelations as "elder abuse" is a particularly low blow. This term should be reserved for actual instances of neglect and exploitation, not bandied about as a political attack. It's a serious accusation to make and trivializing it in the context of a political feud is both irresponsible and disrespectful to actual victims of elder abuse.

The Counterpoint

President Biden has outright denied these allegations, stating, "I made the decisions during my presidency." He dismisses the controversy as nothing more than a distraction from more pressing issues, such as proposed legislation by Republicans that he argues would harm American families. This rebuttal points to a larger trend in modern politics: the creation of scandals to divert attention from substantive policy discussions.

The Bottom Line

The so-called "autopen scandal" is a manufactured drama, a tempest in a teapot designed to stir up controversy where there is none. It's a distraction from real issues and a disservice to the American public, who deserve thoughtful debate and discussion about the policies that will actually affect their lives. Let's not allow political theater to overshadow the substantive policy discussions that are desperately needed. In the grand scheme of things, the use of an autopen is the least of America's concerns.
