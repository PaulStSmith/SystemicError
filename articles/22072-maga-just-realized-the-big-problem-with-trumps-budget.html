MAGA’s Deficit Panic Is Just Selective Amnesia

What happened

The U.S. budget deficit has surged to its highest level in more than five years, and suddenly some MAGA-aligned analysts are discovering arithmetic. They are posting shocked reactions to a July in which the government collected $334 billion and spent $766 billion, while Bloomberg reports the gap is being driven by faster federal spending, higher Medicare outlays, tariff refunds, and weaker revenue.

Who owns the damage

The people with actual institutional power are not the commentators pretending to be alarmed. Donald Trump set the policy direction. His tariff regime created a $33 billion hit in refunds. His One Big Beautiful Bill Act is projected to add $4.6 trillion to the deficit over 10 years. His administration is also pushing a war bill that has already cost roughly $37.5 billion and is asking for another $70 billion. This is not an unfortunate drift. It is executive and congressional governance producing a predictable fiscal result.

The fake surprise

The spectacle here is not fiscal responsibility. It is selective outrage from people who voted for the outcome and now want to pose as disappointed auditors. When a Heritage Foundation figure calls the numbers “absolute insanity,” the missing follow-up is obvious: who put the insanity in motion? Conservative analysts are framing the deficit as if it arrived from nowhere, rather than from a deliberate mix of tax cuts for the wealthy, tariff chaos, and militarized spending.

The class transfer at the center

The biggest lie in this story is that the deficit is an abstract bookkeeping problem. It is also a transfer mechanism. According to the source, the wealthiest 1 percent are slated to pay at least $1 trillion less over 10 years than they would have otherwise. That is the political content of the fiscal plan: drain revenue, protect the rich, then perform shock when the ledger breaks. The borrowing is not an accident. It is how elite giveaways are financed when the bill comes due.

War, tariffs, and the price of performance

Trump’s economic nationalism is doing what it always does: raising costs, distorting trade, and handing taxpayers the tab. The source notes that the war with Iran has compounded the damage through direct spending, inflation, energy prices, and borrowing costs that could push the total burden far beyond the headline war figure. Meanwhile, the tariff refunds reveal another layer of institutional recklessness: a policy sold as toughness that turns into another liability for the public.

The deeper pattern

This is how authoritarian politics manages contradiction. It breaks the budget, redistributes upward, creates a mess, and then recruits friendly commentators to act surprised by the wreckage. The point is not consistency. The point is domination, spectacle, and insulation for the powerful. The deficit story is not really about numbers. It is about which class gets protected, who gets the bill, and how often the people causing the damage can still sell themselves as the adults in the room.
