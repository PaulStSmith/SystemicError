'Literally every claim was false': DHS official busted for lying about teen's ICE arrest

A Department of Homeland Security official was busted for lying about a teenage girl’s violent arrest by federal agents.

DHS Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin claimed video of the arrest Friday actually showed Chicago police detaining a burglar last year, but a Daily Beast analysis confirmed the teen – a U.S. citizen – was violently thrown to the ground and taken into custody by officers from a department outside the city with help from DHS Enforcement and Removals Operations (ERO).

“Imagine being so desperate to demonize law enforcement you post a video from a burglary arrest Chicago Police made over a year ago,” McLaughlin posted. “This isn’t even ICE.”

McLaughlin’s misinformation fell apart quickly, as separate video clips were posted on social media taken in Hoffman Estates, a village outside Chicago. The 18-year-old’s parents confirmed to CBS News that their daughter and two friends were held for hours in the parking lot outside the suburban police station without explanation.

Videos also show an officer wearing body armor affixed with “ERO — Enforcement and Removal Operations” branding, and Hoffman Estates confirmed to the Beast that Immigrations and Customs Enforcement had been in the area.

“The Hoffman Estates Police Department follows the Trust Act and does not participate in immigration enforcement with ICE,” the department said in a statement. “On Friday we had a squad car in the area that was responding to a separate call for service”.

“The only interaction we had with ICE was when they came to the police department to file a police report [in] reference [to] an incident that occurred during their enforcement,” the department added. “They ultimately decided to complete the report this week with us and have not completed that yet.”

McLaughlin did not respond to requests for comment, and her misleading post remains up on her official government account.

“Tricia McLaughlin claimed the video was of a burglary arrest last year by Chicago police, and ICE wasn’t involved,” said lawyer and author Loren Collins, an expert on misinformation. “Literally every claim she made was false. It wasn’t a burglary arrest. It was last Friday, not last year. Zero Chicago police were involved. And it was plainly ICE.”

Our Analysis:

The DHS Debacle: A Masterclass in Misinformation

In a recent turn of events that can only be described as both shocking and depressingly predictable, a high-ranking Department of Homeland Security (DHS) official was caught red-handed in a web of lies. Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin’s attempt to mislead the public about a violent arrest of a teenage girl reveals a disturbing willingness to distort facts to demonize law enforcement criticism and, by extension, protect the image of federal agencies at all costs. This incident, dissected below, lays bare the systemic issues plaguing our law enforcement and immigration enforcement practices.

The Misinformation Campaign

Tricia McLaughlin, in a move that reeks of desperation, falsely claimed that a video depicting a violent arrest was actually footage of the Chicago police detaining a burglar last year. This claim was not just incorrect; it was a blatant fabrication aimed at discrediting critics of law enforcement practices. The reality, as confirmed by a Daily Beast analysis, was starkly different. The footage in question showed federal agents from a department outside the city, with assistance from DHS Enforcement and Removals Operations (ERO), aggressively apprehending a U.S. citizen teenager.

McLaughlin’s post, which read, “Imagine being so desperate to demonize law enforcement you post a video from a burglary arrest Chicago Police made over a year ago. This isn’t even ICE,” stands as a testament to how far some officials will go to manipulate public perception. Her statement, dripping with irony, inadvertently highlights her own desperation to whitewash the actions of federal enforcement agencies.

The Unraveling of a Lie

The lie unraveled quickly when separate video clips surfaced on social media, clearly showing the incident occurred in Hoffman Estates, a village outside Chicago, and involved federal agents, not city police. The 18-year-old’s parents confirmed their daughter and two friends were detained without explanation, highlighting a blatant disregard for civil liberties and due process. Furthermore, video evidence showcased an officer donning body armor marked with “ERO — Enforcement and Removal Operations,” unequivocally contradicting McLaughlin’s claims.

The Hoffman Estates Police Department’s statement further complicates the narrative McLaughlin attempted to peddle. By confirming that Immigrations and Customs Enforcement (ICE) had been in the area and clarifying their limited interaction with ICE, the local police department inadvertently exposed the extent of McLaughlin’s misinformation.

The Silence and Persisting Misinformation

Despite the mounting evidence against her claims, McLaughlin has opted for silence, failing to respond to requests for comment. Her misleading post still adorns her official government account, a brazen symbol of unaccountability. This silence is not just a personal failure but indicative of a broader systemic issue where officials face little to no repercussions for spreading falsehoods.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

What this incident unequivocally illustrates is a systemic error within the DHS and potentially other federal agencies. It’s a scenario where officials feel emboldened to spread misinformation, secure in the knowledge that accountability is scarce. This isn’t just about one official’s deceit; it’s a symptom of a larger, more insidious problem. The ease with which McLaughlin lied, coupled with the lack of immediate corrective action, suggests a culture where the truth is malleable, and transparency is an afterthought.

Loren Collins, a lawyer and author, succinctly summarized the situation, stating, “Literally every claim she made was false. It wasn’t a burglary arrest. It was last Friday, not last year. Zero Chicago police were involved. And it was plainly ICE.” This debacle should serve as a wake-up call for a thorough review and overhaul of how information is disseminated by public officials and a reevaluation of the checks and balances in place to prevent such egregious misrepresentations in the future.
