'Sour grapes': Musk reportedly turned on Trump after being forced out of WH

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20254 min read

Elon Musk holds the key to the White House, a gift received from U.S. President Donald Trump, at a press conference in the Oval Office at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Elon Musk, the former de facto head of the Department of Government Efficiency, reportedly wanted to stay on in the White House beyond his 130 days as a "special government employee" to continue cutting government waste, "but was rejected," according to The Telegraph.

The outlet quoted a source as saying that Musk "had asked to stay on to make more progress towards his target of slashing $1 trillion from spending but was told that would not be possible."

After being ushered out of the West Wing last week, taking with him a gold key presented in a keepsake wooden box bearing Donald Trump's signature, Musk immediately turned on the president and his "big, beautiful bill," the report said.

Musk lashed out on his social media platform X, slamming the bill as a “disgusting abomination” and threatening to work against Republicans who voted for the measure.

"A day later, a Trump world insider said administration officials were 'disappointed' with his reaction," the report said, adding, "And a source close to Mr Musk said it was a case of 'sour grapes'".

Musk was known for wearing a MAGA baseball cap with the words, "Trump was right about everything," but that sentiment obviously disintegrated.

The Telegraph quoted "a figure close to the White House" as saying "officials were upset by the nature of his attack."

“It’s safe to say people are disappointed,” the source said. “He’s done a lot of really great work, and he’s fundamentally changed the way things like fraud, waste and abuse are viewed in Washington. But he left. So he speaks now as a business person, not as a government entity and that’s the way to understand what he says.”

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt responded to Musk's outburst by saying, “The president already knows where Elon Musk stood on this bill. It doesn’t change the president’s opinion."

On Wednesday, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) said he tried to phone Musk but was ignored.

"I called Elon last night but he didn't answer," Johnson told reporters. "But I hope to talk to him today...I'd love to talk to him this week and I hope he calls me back today."

Our Analysis:

Musk's Melodrama: A Tale of Sour Grapes and Government Grandstanding

In what reads like a script from a political satire rather than the annals of respected governance, Elon Musk, the tech mogul turned temporary government crusader, finds himself at the center of a rather petulant saga following his departure from the White House. Musk, who briefly donned the cap of the Department of Government Efficiency's de facto head, has descended into a public spat that underscores the perils of blending populist bravado with the nuanced realm of policy-making.

The Billionaire's Bid for Extended Influence

According to The Telegraph, Musk's ambition to carve out a permanent niche within the White House was unceremoniously rebuffed. Citing a desire to "slash $1 trillion from spending," Musk's plea to extend his tenure beyond the 130-day mark of a "special government employee" was met with a firm no. This rejection seemingly ignited Musk's ire, leading him to decry President Trump's "big, beautiful bill" as a “disgusting abomination” on his social media platform, X. The irony of Musk turning his back on the administration with such alacrity, despite previously sporting a MAGA cap proclaiming "Trump was right about everything," is as rich as it is revealing.

The Fallout: Political and Personal

Musk's subsequent tirade did not go unnoticed, with reports of disappointment swirling within Trump's camp. The characterization of Musk’s actions as "sour grapes" by a source close to him might just be the understatement of the year. His transformation from a government ally to a vocal critic over the course of a rejection showcases a volatile blend of ego and entitlement at play.

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt's response to Musk's outburst, “The president already knows where Elon Musk stood on this bill. It doesn’t change the president’s opinion," serves as a diplomatic yet pointed dismissal of Musk's theatrics. Meanwhile, House Speaker Mike Johnson's (R-LA) unrequited attempts to reach Musk adds another layer of awkwardness to this unfolding drama.

Musk's Legacy and Lessons Unlearned

A source quoted in the article lauds Musk for fundamentally changing Washington’s view on "fraud, waste, and abuse." Yet, one must question the sustainability and depth of these purported changes, especially when considering the transient nature of Musk's government stint and his explosive exit strategy. The saga paints a picture of Musk not as a reformer, but as a mercurial figure whose contributions, while potentially significant, are overshadowed by his penchant for controversy and confrontation.

Conclusion: The Danger of Hero-Worship in Governance

Elon Musk's brief foray into government efficiency, capped by his acrimonious departure, serves as a cautionary tale against the hero-worship of business moguls in political spheres. The melding of Silicon Valley swagger with Washington's intricate dance of policymaking and governance is fraught with potential for conflict and disappointment. Musk's saga underscores the need for a more nuanced, collaborative approach to governance that transcends the allure of billionaire bravado and focuses on sustainable, substantive reforms.
