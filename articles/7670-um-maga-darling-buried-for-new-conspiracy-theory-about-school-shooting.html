'Um….?': MAGA darling buried for new 'conspiracy theory' about school shooting

Tomi Lahren speaking with supporters of former State Senator Kelli Ward at a campaign rally at the Pioneer Living History Museum in Phoenix, Arizona. (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

Conservative commentator Tomi Lahren came under fire on Saturday night after some observers said she was resorting to conspiracy theories in the wake of a Brown University shooting.

The shooting, which reports say led to at least two deaths, was a hot topic going into the weekend. Donald Trump was criticized for spreading false information, which some said may have endangered lives, and JD Vance’s prayer-based response was shot down by a “fed up” survivor.

For Lahren, however, there was a different focus than others.

“Over 2 hours and they don’t have surveillance or video footage? Um….?” Lahren wrote Saturday in reference to the lack of videographic evidence that has so far been released in connection with the school shooting.

That led to several outbursts.

Former GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger said, “Oh here we go. Always a conspiracy.”

Former prosecutor Ron Filipkowski said, “Someone tell Bari Weiss they’re about to do it again,” in reference to the editor-in-chief of CBS News.

Sports journalist Tyler Hetu chimed in, “Always a conspiracy.”

One X user, @TheyCallMeNans, wrote, “Jeebus, it took them 33 hours to find out it was indeed Tyler Robinson, (after his confession) Tomi. And that was done with surveillance DURING DAYLIGHT.”

“Give them time,” the user then added.

Our Analysis:

Tomi Lahren’s Controversial Spin on the Brown University Shooting

In the wake of a tragic shooting at Brown University, resulting in at least two deaths, the response from public figures has been, predictably, a mixed bag. However, conservative commentator Tomi Lahren’s reaction stands out—not for its empathy or constructive dialogue but for its flirtation with the murky waters of conspiracy theories.

Lahren’s tweet, questioning the absence of surveillance or video footage just over two hours after the event, implies a deeper, darker narrative at play—a tactic that is as dangerous as it is irresponsible. Her insinuation feeds into the growing trend of skepticism towards factual reporting and law enforcement processes, a trend that is all too common in today’s political discourse.

“Oh here we go. Always a conspiracy,” remarks former GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger, a sentiment echoed by others like sports journalist Tyler Hetu and former prosecutor Ron Filipkowski, who sarcastically suggests informing Bari Weiss about the impending conspiracy theory resurgence. These reactions highlight the frustration with the knee-jerk move towards conspiracy theorizing that figures like Lahren are known for.

A particularly poignant response comes from One X user @TheyCallMeNans, who points out the logistical and practical realities of crime investigation. The user references a previous case where it took 33 hours to identify a suspect, even with surveillance footage captured during daylight. This serves as a reality check to those quick to jump on the conspiracy bandwagon, underlining the time and effort involved in thorough investigative work.

The implications of Lahren’s tweet are far-reaching. Firstly, it undermines the gravity of a tragic event, shifting the focus from the victims and their families to unfounded speculation. Secondly, it contributes to the erosion of trust in media and law enforcement, institutions that, despite their flaws, are foundational to the functioning of a democratic society. And lastly, it exemplifies the polarization of American political discourse, where even a tragedy becomes fodder for partisan point-scoring.

In an era where conspiracy theories can mobilize individuals to dangerous ends, the responsibility on public figures to speak with caution and respect for the facts has never been greater. Lahren’s failure to adhere to this principle not only diminishes the discourse but potentially endangers lives by fostering distrust and paranoia.

As we navigate the aftermath of such events, the need for empathy, patience, and reliance on verified information is paramount. Public figures should lead by example, offering solace and solidarity rather than fueling the fires of division and suspicion. In a world rife with challenges and tragedies, our focus must remain on unity, support for the affected, and a commitment to the truth—principles that seem to have eluded Tomi Lahren in her recent commentary.
