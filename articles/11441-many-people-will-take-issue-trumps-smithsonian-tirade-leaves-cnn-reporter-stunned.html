'Many people will take issue': Trump’s Smithsonian tirade leaves CNN reporter stunned

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

CNN reporter Kristen Holmes was taken aback by President Donald Trump's angry Truth Social rant against the Smithsonian on Tuesday.

In the new tirade, Trump raged that "The Smithsonian is OUT OF CONTROL, where everything discussed is how horrible our Country is, how bad Slavery was, and how unaccomplished the downtrodden have been — Nothing about Success, nothing about Brightness, nothing about the Future."

"Kristen, the president on social media, just escalated his attacks against the Smithsonian," said anchor Boris Sanchez. "Of course, he's ordered a review of everything inside these museums in Washington, D.C. What is Trump saying?"

"Yeah, and just a reminder to our viewers, he ordered this review," Holmes noted. "The White House said that this was an order to make sure that our historical museums, the U.S. historical museums, were aligned, essentially, with the Trump agenda. But then he put out this post, and it's not surprising because he uses the word 'woke.' But there are certainly elements of it that I think many people will take issue with."

Regarding Trump's post, she continued, "I think it's probably clear from here that the part that many people might take issue with is the idea that the Smithsonian is somehow saying how bad slavery is, when slavery was a very dark part of the United States' history. It also appears from this that he wants to take out reference to slavery, or to use the word, 'how unaccomplished the downtrodden have been,' which is not entirely clear what that means."

"But these museums do exist in order to tell the story of the United States and of America, and of our history and culture, and the tragedy of slavery is certainly a part of that," she added. "This is really exactly what people were concerned about when they first heard about this review, that it would somehow remove critical parts of American history. Of course, if he is talking about removing reference to slavery, that would fall right into that category."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against The Smithsonian: A Dissection

In an era where the distortion of historical narratives seems to be a favorite pastime for certain political figures, President Donald Trump's recent outburst against the Smithsonian Institution stands out as a particularly brazen attempt to rewrite the American past. The essence of Trump's rant, as reported by CNN's Kristen Holmes, encapsulates a troubling desire to sanitize history, prioritizing a narrative that aligns with the so-called "Trump agenda."

The Wrath Against Reality

Trump's tirade, vented through his platform of choice, Truth Social, lambasts the Smithsonian for focusing on "how horrible our Country is, how bad Slavery was, and how unaccomplished the downtrodden have been." Such a statement reveals a fundamental misunderstanding or, more alarmingly, a deliberate misrepresentation of the role of historical institutions. Museums, especially those dedicated to history, are not cheerleaders for national pride; they are custodians of truth, tasked with presenting history in its full complexity, warts and all.

The Misguided Crusade

The order to review the contents of U.S. historical museums to ensure they are "aligned" with the Trump agenda is a chilling proposition. It suggests a move towards authoritarian control of educational resources, where history is not explored but dictated. The notion that discussions of slavery or the struggles of marginalized communities are somehow indicative of an institution "OUT OF CONTROL" is not just misguided; it's dangerous. It attempts to erase the suffering and struggles that are integral to understanding the American experience.

The Smithsonian's Role

Holmes rightly points out that the Smithsonian exists "to tell the story of the United States and of America, and of our history and culture," including the "tragedy of slavery." Museums serve as a reflection of a nation's journey, capturing both its highest achievements and its darkest hours. To sanitize this narrative for the sake of presenting a more palatable story to future generations is to engage in a form of cultural vandalism.

The Irony of the "Woke" Accusation

Trump's use of "woke" as a pejorative is particularly ironic in this context. Being "woke" is essentially about being aware and acknowledging the injustices and complexities of societal structures. The Smithsonian's commitment to presenting a nuanced view of American history, including the horrors of slavery and the ongoing challenges faced by marginalized communities, is not a sign of an institution that has lost its way. It is, in fact, the very essence of its purpose.

Conclusion

Trump's attack on the Smithsonian is not just an attack on a revered institution; it's an assault on the very concept of historical truth. It's a stark reminder of the power of narratives and the lengths to which some will go to reshape those narratives to fit a particular worldview. In the face of such attempts, it is crucial that institutions like the Smithsonian remain steadfast in their mission to educate, inform, and reflect upon the full spectrum of American history. To do otherwise would be to fail not just the present generation, but all those that follow.
