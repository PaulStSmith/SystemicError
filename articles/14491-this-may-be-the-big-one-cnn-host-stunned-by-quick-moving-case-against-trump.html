'This may be the big one': CNN host stunned by 'quick moving' case against Trump

By Paulo Santos

CNN justice correspondent Katelyn Polantz and Wolf Blitzer (CNN screenshot)

CNN's Wolf Blitzer was stunned Friday by analysis of Harvard University's lawsuit against the Trump administration's decree that international students are no longer permitted to attend the institution.

The Department of Homeland Security, under Kristi Noem, announced Thursday that it was barring Harvard from admitting international students as punishment for defying the president on DEI and alleged antisemitism on campus, among other things. Trump has already banned the university from receiving $2.2 billion in federal funding.

Justice correspondent Katelyn Polantz characterized the suit as being a huge test of the administration's powers over U.S. academic institutions.

"Well, Wolf, this may be the big one," Polantz began. "The administration of Donald Trump versus one of the most significant cultural institutions in the United States — academic institution — Harvard University. This is a case where Harvard says what the administration has already done, yesterday, by revoking its ability to have foreign students as part of its programs on campus — that it is an immediate and devastating effect to this university."

Polantz said Harvard has more than 7,000 students on its campus with visas, and that the Trump order will "throw into disarray" its academic programs, research laboratories, and clinics.

She classified the suit as "very fast moving," as international students prepare to return to campus for summer programs.

"The Department of Homeland Security does have a statement in response to the lawsuit so far," Polantz continued. "They say, 'This lawsuit seeks to kneecap the president's constitutional vested powers under Article II.' What that means? The president's the president; he should be able to do what he wants here on visas, immigration, even related to this university."

Polantz said Harvard's "argument in court is that it's a constitutional encroachment of their ability to decide what their curriculum is and what they do as a university."

The case was assigned to a judge Friday morning, according to Polantz — Judge Allison Burroughs, "an Obama appointee on the federal court in Massachusetts."

Blitzer remarked that the international students being affected, "can't believe this is happening here in the United States of America."

Our Analysis:

Harvard vs. Trump Administration: A Battle Over Academic Freedom and International Students

In a move that can only be described as both audaciously punitive and blatantly political, the Trump administration, through the Department of Homeland Security under Kristi Noem, has escalated its warfare on the bastions of higher education - this time setting its sights on none other than Harvard University. The charge? A concoction of accusations ranging from defying the president on Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) initiatives to alleged antisemitism on campus. The weapon of choice? Barring Harvard from admitting international students.

Let's dissect the layers of absurdity here, shall we?

First off, the Trump administration's decision to strip Harvard of $2.2 billion in federal funding was a significant blow, signaling a dangerous precedent where the federal government feels emboldened to financially strangle institutions for policy disagreements. But to escalate this to barring international students? That's not just a blow; it's an attempt at academic asphyxiation.

CNN's Justice correspondent Katelyn Polantz aptly describes the lawsuit filed by Harvard against this decree as a "huge test of the administration's powers over U.S. academic institutions." And indeed, it is. This isn't merely a legal battle; it's an ideological war over the autonomy of academic institutions and the sanctity of international academic collaboration.

Harvard, with more than 7,000 students holding visas, is now in a state of chaos, with its academic programs, research laboratories, and clinics thrown into disarray. This isn't just an immediate and devastating effect on Harvard; it's a direct attack on the international students who contribute immensely to the intellectual and cultural vibrancy of academic institutions across the United States.

The Department of Homeland Security's statement in response to the lawsuit is as laughable as it is terrifying. To claim that this lawsuit seeks to "kneecap the president's constitutional vested powers" is to argue that the president has carte blanche to undermine the educational and research missions of universities simply because he can. This is not about the president's powers under Article II; it's about a blatant disregard for the constitutional protections of academic freedom and the rights of international students.

Polantz's report highlights that the case has been assigned to Judge Allison Burroughs, an Obama appointee. This detail, while seemingly minor, hints at the potential for a legal showdown that could have far-reaching implications for the relationship between the federal government and academic institutions.

Wolf Blitzer's reaction, noting that international students "can't believe this is happening here in the United States of America," underscores a bitter truth. The U.S., once a beacon of higher education and intellectual freedom, is now a stage for a government's vendetta against its own educational institutions.

In conclusion, the Trump administration's actions against Harvard University are not just an affront to Harvard but to all academic institutions and international students who view America as a place of learning, discovery, and opportunity. This lawsuit is more than a legal battle; it's a stand against an administration's attempt to politicize and control academic freedom. The outcome of this case will resonate far beyond the halls of Harvard, potentially setting a precedent for the future of academic freedom and international collaboration in the United States.

Harvard's fight is not just for its international students; it's for the soul of American higher education.
