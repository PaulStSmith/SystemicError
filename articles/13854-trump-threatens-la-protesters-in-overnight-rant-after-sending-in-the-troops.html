Trump threatens LA protesters in overnight rant after sending in the troops

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a press conference with Elon Musk (not pictured) in the Oval Office of the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Hours after sending in federalized National Guard troops into Los Angeles to support DHS and ICE agents who have been taking part in highly militarized raids on businesses in California and snatching up immigrants, Donald Trump threatened the pro-immigrant protesters.

After days of raids, involving CS gas, "less than lethal" rounds fired at protesters, and use flash bang grenades that have infuriated Californians, Trump fell short of invoking the Insurrection Act but still sent in a reported 2,000 National Guardsman.

He then took to Truth Social, to warn protesters that they will not be allowed to wear masks in the same manner that ICE and DHS agents have done to protect their identities.

The president wrote, "Great job by the National Guard in Los Angeles after two days of violence, clashes and unrest. We have an incompetent Governor (Newscum) and Mayor (Bass) who were, as usual (just look at how they handled the fires, and now their VERY SLOW PERMITTING disaster. Federal permitting is complete!), unable to to handle the task."

He then warned, "These Radical Left protests, by instigators and often paid troublemakers, will NOT BE TOLERATED. Also, from now on, MASKS WILL NOT BE ALLOWED to be worn at protests. What do these people have to hide, and why??? Again, thank you to the National Guard for a job well done!"

Our Analysis:

A Cloaked Threat: Unmasking Trump's Latest Assault on Pro-Immigrant Sentiment

In a move that reeks of authoritarianism lightly veiled in a facade of public safety, former President Donald Trump has escalated his war on immigrants and their supporters with a disturbingly familiar blend of force and intimidation. Hours after dispatching federalized National Guard troops to Los Angeles—a city already under siege from highly militarized raids targeting immigrants—Trump has now set his sights on the pro-immigrant protesters, those who dare to voice opposition to his draconian policies.

A Militarized Response to Civil Unrest

The dispatch of 2,000 National Guardsman to Los Angeles in the aftermath of days filled with CS gas, "less than lethal" rounds, and flash bang grenades is not only a disproportionate response but a clear message that militarized force is Trump's preferred method of dealing with dissent. This approach, which pits military against civilians, is a dangerous escalation in a country where freedom of speech and the right to protest are foundational principles.

Trump's praise for the National Guard's actions, amidst what he describes as "violence, clashes and unrest," conveniently ignores the violence instigated by the very presence of federal forces in the community. It is a narrative that seeks to vilify the protesters while glorifying the use of force, a tactic straight out of the authoritarian playbook.

The War on Masks: A New Front in the Battle for Civil Liberties

In a particularly Orwellian twist, Trump's declaration that masks will no longer be allowed at protests represents a chilling attack on anonymity and privacy rights. This mandate, ostensibly to prevent the concealment of identities, strips away a layer of protection for protesters facing a government increasingly hostile to dissent. In the same breath that Trump criticizes the governor and mayor for their competency, he undermines the fundamental rights of the citizens they represent.

The irony of forbidding masks for protesters while DHS and ICE agents conceal their identities to operate with impunity is stark. It lays bare the hypocrisy at the heart of Trump's administration: a demand for transparency from those who oppose him, while offering none in return.

The Radical Left: A Convenient Scapegoat

Trump's reference to the protesters as "Radical Left protests, by instigators and often paid troublemakers" is a tired, yet dangerous, trope. It seeks to delegitimize genuine civic engagement and protest by painting it as the work of fringe elements or external agitators. This narrative not only dismisses the concerns of Californians infuriated by the federal government's actions but also attempts to fracture solidarity by casting doubt on the authenticity of the protest movement.

Conclusion: A System Under Siege

As Trump threatens and postures on social media, the real story unfolds on the streets of Los Angeles and in the homes of immigrants across California. The militarized raids, the suppression of protest, and the vilification of dissenters are symptoms of a democracy in distress. Trump's latest actions are not just an attack on immigrant communities and their supporters; they are an assault on the civil liberties of all Americans.

In the face of such authoritarian tactics, it is imperative that citizens, activists, and leaders at all levels of government stand up for the principles of freedom and justice. The battle for the soul of America is not just fought in ballot boxes but in the streets, in the courts, and in the steadfast refusal to be silenced.
