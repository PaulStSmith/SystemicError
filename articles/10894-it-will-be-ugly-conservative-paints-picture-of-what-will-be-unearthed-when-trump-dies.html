'It will be ugly': Conservative paints picture of what will be unearthed when Trump dies

Following the brief online speculation that President Donald Trump had suffered a medical emergency, or even died, conservative strategist and co-founder of the Lincoln Project Rick Wilson laid out what a post-Trump world may look like, and what revelations would follow, in a column published Wednesday.

“When Donald Trump dies, the myth will begin to decay almost instantly; his cult will keep the flame alive for a while, but the records will outlive the rally faithful,” Wilson wrote on his Substack “Against All Enemies."

“History will not remember him as a king, or a savior. History will remember him as a small, ugly, sick man who happened to seize great power, who wielded it recklessly, and who left behind a trail of destruction, corruption and cowardice unmatched in American history.”

Sticking with Trump’s health, Wilson predicted that following Trump’s passing, a trove of documents related to his physical and mental condition would be unearthed, documents that would reveal that his “cardiac and mental decline was charted in careful, hidden memos.”

Wilson also anticipated that revelations around the president’s ties with Jeffrey Epstein – the convicted sex offender who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges – would come to light, and made several startling predictions about who in Trump’s cabinet may be held to account for potentially covering up those ties.

“Epstein’s web of power, blackmail, and sexual exploitation reached deep into America’s elite, and we’ll learn Trump was in the thick of it,” Wilson wrote. “The [Justice Department’s] illegal Epstein coverup and corrupt pardon of Ghislaine Maxwell will unravel, and by the end, [Attorney General] Pam Bondi, [Deputy Attorney General] Todd Blanche, [FBI Director] Kash Patel, and [FBI Deputy Director] Dan Bongino will be in prison.”

Details on Trump’s business dealings, many of which have enriched the president to the tune of billions of dollars, would also be laid to bare following his passing, Wilson noted, details that he argued history would not look well upon.

“The Trumpcoin scams, the garbage social media platform, the grifty deals with foreign powers, the bribes for pardons…all of it will be seen for what it is; a criminal enterprise, a mobster bustout of an entire nation,” Wilson wrote.

“History will learn about the shell companies, the overseas accounts, the backroom deals where American policy was auctioned off like a Mar-a-Lago dinner table.”

Ultimately, Wilson argued that through the wave of revelations following the president’s passing, a clear picture of Trump would be painted: that of someone whose “only interest was control.”

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of Rick Wilson's Predictions: A Post-Trump Prophecy or a Preemptive Eulogy?

In the labyrinth of political speculation, Rick Wilson's recent column transcends mere conjecture to paint a dystopian tableau of a post-Trump America. Wilson, with the precision of a surgeon and the zeal of a zealot, forecasts a future where the curtain is finally pulled back, revealing the sordid underbelly of Donald Trump’s tenure. But how much of this is grounded in imminent reality, and how much is the product of a fervent imagination?

The Myth of the Man

Wilson posits that Trump's death would catalyze the disintegration of his mythos, suggesting that the absence of the man would lead to a rapid decay of his cult of personality. Yet, to claim that history will remember Trump as "a small, ugly, sick man" seems more a reflection of Wilson's personal disdain than an objective forecast. History is notoriously kinder to former presidents than their contemporaries ever are. Figures such as Richard Nixon and George W. Bush have seen their reputations undergo significant rehabilitation after leaving office, despite the controversies that marred their presidencies. Could Wilson be underestimating the longevity of Trump's myth?

The Revelation of Records

Wilson's anticipation of posthumous revelations concerning Trump's health and mental state, albeit tantalizing, rests on shaky grounds. The promise of "careful, hidden memos" charting Trump's decline suggests a level of internal opposition and documentation that seems optimistic at best. The Trump administration's tight-lipped loyalty and the president's own penchant for nondisclosure agreements cast a long shadow over this prediction. Are we to believe that these documents will conveniently surface, unraveling the tightly wound threads of secrecy?

The Epstein Connection

The assertion that Trump's ties to Jeffrey Epstein will lead to a domino effect, toppling high-ranking officials and exposing a corrupt pardon of Ghislaine Maxwell, is a bold one. Wilson names names, implicating figures such as Pam Bondi and Dan Bongino in what he describes as an "illegal Epstein coverup." While the Epstein scandal remains a festering wound on the American elite, the leap to a widespread incarceration of Trump's cabinet members feels more like wishful thinking than a grounded prediction. The legal system's glacial pace and the complexity of proving such allegations make this scenario seem more fiction than fact.

The Business Dealings

Wilson's critique of Trump's business empire as "a criminal enterprise" taps into a well of existing public skepticism and outrage. His reference to "Trumpcoin scams" and "grifty deals with foreign powers" resonates with documented concerns about the intertwining of Trump's business and presidential duties. However, the assertion that all of this will be unequivocally unveiled and universally condemned posthumously seems overly optimistic. The tangled web of Trump's finances, marred by allegations of fraud and misconduct, has yet to unravel in a conclusive manner despite years of scrutiny.

Control as the Ultimate Aim

At the heart of Wilson's prophecy is the depiction of Trump as a man whose "only interest was control." This assessment, while not unfounded, simplifies the complex motivations that likely drove Trump's actions in office. The desire for control, power, and influence is hardly unique to Trump, echoing across the spectrum of American politics.

Conclusion: A Prophecy or a Wish List?

Rick Wilson's column reads less like a prophecy and more like a cathartic release of pent-up frustrations and desires. While it’s grounded in some observable truths and patterns, it ventures into the realm of speculative fiction when predicting the specifics of Trump's posthumous reckoning. The real question is not whether Wilson's predictions will come to pass, but why they resonate so deeply with a segment of the American populace. In the end, Wilson's vision of a post-Trump world serves as a mirror, reflecting the polarized hopes and fears of an America still grappling with the legacy of its 45th president.
