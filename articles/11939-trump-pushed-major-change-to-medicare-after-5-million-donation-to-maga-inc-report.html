Trump pushed major change to Medicare after $5 million donation to MAGA, Inc: report

By Paulo Santos

Back in April, President Donald Trump paused the rollout of a Biden-era rule that sought to tamper down “abusive (Medicare) pricing practices” from private companies, and on Thursday, new records indicate that Trump’s own MAGA Inc. received a $5 million donation from one such company that stood to lose out on billions of dollars in earnings, The New York Times reported.

That company is Extremity Care, a biotech company that has been paid billions of dollars from Medicare for its skin substitute products, or organic bandages designed to mimic natural skin. Its owner, Oliver Burckhardt, was invited to a private dinner hosted by Trump for major donors at his Mar-a-Lago resort in Florida, where he got the chance to speak one-on-one with the president.

Trump would go on to post an image of a flier given to him by Burckhardt the following day on social media, and ultimately made good on Burckhardt’s investment by delaying the rollout of the Biden-era policy that would have ended a loophole in Medicare rules, specifically as it relates to payouts for skin substitute products.

“It was the culmination of an expensive influence campaign by Extremity Care that underscored Mr. Trump’s willingness to grant access and preferential treatment to people and companies that fill the coffers of his political groups,” The New York Times wrote.

“The February donation by Mr. Burckhardt’s company, which was revealed in a report filed late last week with the Federal Election Commission, was among dozens of seven- and eight-figure contributions to MAGA Inc. from donors, many of whom were rewarded with presidential face time to plead for their causes.”

Medicare payouts for skin substitutes have exploded in recent years, rising from $256 million in 2021 to $10 billion in 2024. In a July report, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services condemned what it described as “abusive pricing practices,” and recounted one notable example.

“This dramatic spending increase is largely attributed to abusive pricing practices in the sector, including the use of products with limited evidence of clinical value,” the report read.

“In one notable case, the CMS Fraud Defense Operations Center stopped more than $1 million in improper payments for skin substitutes to a medical group practice. The practice was submitting Medicare claims for wound care services allegedly performed by the owner, a psychiatrist.”

In a statement to The New York Times, a MAGA Inc. spokesperson denied the $5 million donation played any role in Trump’s decision to pause the Biden-era rule, telling the publication that the president “always works for the best interest of the country,” and that “any suggestion otherwise is false.”

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of Influence: Trump, Medicare, and a $5 Million Donation

In a revelation that should shock precisely no one, former President Donald Trump has once again found himself at the center of a controversy that blurs the lines between political influence and outright corruption. The New York Times recently reported that Trump’s MAGA Inc. received a whopping $5 million donation from Extremity Care, a biotech company with billions to lose if a Biden-era rule aimed at curbing "abusive (Medicare) pricing practices" came into effect. This rule specifically targeted the lucrative loophole that companies like Extremity Care have been exploiting with their Medicare billings for skin substitute products.

The Pay-for-Play Playbook

Let's break this down, shall we? Extremity Care, a company that has fattened its wallet with Medicare funds for its so-called organic bandages (a product with a dubious distinction between actual medical necessity and profit-driven snake oil), seemed to have bought its way into Trump’s good graces. Oliver Burckhardt, the owner, not only secured a private dinner with Trump at the opulent Mar-a-Lago resort but also managed to leave such an impression that Trump personally showcased a flier from Burckhardt on social media. The culmination of this cozy relationship? Trump delayed the implementation of a policy that threatened Extremity Care's golden goose.

This sequence of events is not just alarming; it's a textbook example of how money can buy influence in the highest echelons of power. The New York Times described this as “the culmination of an expensive influence campaign by Extremity Care that underscored Mr. Trump’s willingness to grant access and preferential treatment to people and companies that fill the coffers of his political groups.” If that doesn't scream quid pro quo, I'm not sure what does.

The Price of Influence

The financial implications here are astronomical. Medicare spending on skin substitutes skyrocketed from $256 million in 2021 to an estimated $10 billion in 2024. This isn't just growth; it's a bonanza for companies like Extremity Care, all under the guise of providing medical services of “limited evidence of clinical value.” The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services themselves have called out these practices as abusive, citing cases where improper payments were made for dubious claims of wound care services.

Denials and Deflections

In what can only be described as a laughable attempt at damage control, a MAGA Inc. spokesperson denied any connection between the $5 million donation and Trump’s policy decisions, proclaiming that Trump “always works for the best interest of the country.” This statement flies in the face of the apparent pay-for-play scenario that unfolded. It's an insult to the public's intelligence and a blatant disregard for ethical governance.

The Systemic Error

This situation is emblematic of a larger, systemic issue in American politics where the wealthy and powerful can exert undue influence over policy decisions, often at the expense of the public good. Trump's actions, as reported, are a stark reminder of the need for comprehensive campaign finance reform and stricter regulations on lobbying and political donations.

In conclusion, the saga of Trump, Extremity Care, and the $5 million donation is not just a one-off scandal; it's a symptomatic manifestation of the rot that permeates our political system. It underscores the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and reforms to ensure that policy decisions are made in the public interest, not as a return on investment for the highest bidder. We must demand better from our leaders and hold them to account when they fail to meet those standards.
