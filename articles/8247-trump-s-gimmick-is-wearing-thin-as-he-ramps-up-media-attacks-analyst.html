Trump's 'gimmick is wearing thin' as he ramps up media attacks: analyst

Going beyond Donald Trump personally insulting reporters as “stupid” and addressing a reporter as “piggy,” the Trump administration is launching a broader assault on the press as his poll numbers spiral downward.

During the news lull in Washington, D.C., over the Thanksgiving holiday weekend, a new page appeared on the White House website attacking mainstream media organizations that have been breaking news on missteps, blunders, and infighting within the administration.

Headlined, “Misleading. Biased. Exposed,” Trump’s regular foils are listed, including a section called the “Offender Hall of Shame,” that features the Washington Post, CBS News, CNN and MS NOW (still listed by the administration as MSNBC).

Trump has long attacked the press when his actions have been scrutinized, labeling anything that shows him in a bad light as “fake news,” which has lost much of its sting with overuse.

According to one media analyst, the new effort to malign reporting is likely headed nowhere.

Speaking with the Post, Seth Stern, director of advocacy at the Freedom of the Press Foundation, claims Trump’s real beef is with “journalists not flattering him and regurgitating his lies.”

“People understand the obvious conflict inherent in a presidential administration appointing itself the arbiter of media bias, and I expect that after the initial wave of publicity, few Americans will be paying attention to this latest stunt,” he elaborated before suggesting. “The gimmick is wearing thin.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s War on the Press: A Desperate Gambit as Polls Plummet

In what reads like a dystopian novel where the protagonist has declared war on truth itself, the Trump administration has taken its vendetta against the press to a new, alarming level. Beyond the juvenile name-calling—referring to reporters as “stupid” and “piggy”—there’s a sinister strategy unfolding. As Donald Trump’s popularity continues to nosedive, his administration has launched a more concerted assault on the press, a move that reeks of desperation and authoritarianism.

A New Low: The White House’s Media Offender Hall of Shame

During a time when most of Washington, D.C., was quiet, reveling in the Thanksgiving holiday spirit, Trump’s team decided to add a new page to the White House website. This wasn’t an attempt to improve transparency or foster unity among Americans. Instead, it was a brazen attack on mainstream media organizations that have dared to report on the administration’s countless missteps and internal discord. Titled “Misleading. Biased. Exposed,” this page lists Trump’s usual targets: the Washington Post, CBS News, CNN, and MS NOW (erroneously still labeled as MSNBC), dubbing them occupants of the “Offender Hall of Shame.”

This tactic is not just petty; it’s dangerous. It represents a clear attempt to undermine the very foundation of a free press, suggesting that any critical reporting is not just unwelcome, but a target for retribution.

The “Fake News” Mantra: Losing Its Bite

Trump’s incessant cries of “fake news” whenever he’s subjected to scrutiny have become so frequent that the phrase has lost much of its impact. Yet, this doesn’t deter him from doubling down on his attacks against the press. The irony is palpable—Trump, who has made an art form of misleading statements and outright falsehoods, appointing himself and his administration as the judges of media accuracy and bias.

A Futile Effort?

Seth Stern, the director of advocacy at the Freedom of the Press Foundation, offers a glimmer of hope, suggesting that Trump’s latest stunt is unlikely to gain much traction. According to Stern, the public sees through the administration’s thinly veiled attempt to discredit journalism that doesn’t serve its narrative. The conflict of interest is glaringly obvious: a presidential administration cannot, and should not, declare itself the arbiter of media bias.

Stern’s words resonate with a truth that Trump seems to ignore: journalists’ primary duty is not to flatter those in power but to hold them accountable. This latest endeavor to malign the press is likely to be seen for what it is—a gimmick. A gimmick that, like Trump’s repeated cries of “fake news,” will wear thin with the American public.

In a functioning democracy, the press serves as a check on power, not as an echo chamber for the whims of those who hold it. As Trump’s administration continues its quixotic battle against the media, one can only hope that the resilience of the press and the discernment of the public will prevail. The stakes—a well-informed electorate and a robust, free press—are too high for anything less.
