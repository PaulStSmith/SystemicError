Desperate MAGA turns to Epstein’s accomplice as Trump ‘humiliates’ his own base: column

New Republican writer Greg Sargent penned a Monday column alleging that President Donald Trump is "humiliating" MAGA with his comments over the scandal involving trafficker Jeffrey Epstein.

Speaking in the United Kingdom on Monday, Trump again claimed that the Epstein scandal is nothing but a hoax, claiming that if his name was in the files, then former President Joe Biden would have already released the files.

Meanwhile, Sargent pointed to Trump's comments on Friday before he left for his golf club in Scotland.

“I’m allowed to do it," Trump said when asked if he would pardon Epstein's convicted accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell.

Trump's former lawyer, Todd Blanche, visited a Jacksonville, Florida, courthouse on Thursday and Friday to question Maxwell personally, but he claimed it was in his capacity as the Deputy Attorney General.

Writing for The Bulwark, however, Philip Rotner, said that the move by Blanche is sketchy and looks to be motivated by protecting Trump.

Rotner thinks it's "plausible" that Blanche could endeavor to get Maxwell to exonerate Trump in exchange for a pardon.

"It’s hard to overstate how humiliating all this is for MAGA," wrote Sargent. "After demanding the 'Epstein files' for years, they got their people in at the Justice Department—and now they’re taking new steps to cover it all up."

Meanwhile, Sargent noted, some in MAGA world "are telling themselves that Maxwell will finally reveal the whole truth." It is something that makes them look "more absurd."

"I think what he is supposed to be talking to her about is to see if there is any information she can provide [that indicates] complicity by anyone in Jeffrey Epstein’s really despicable conduct with young women," Rotner said. "After all, these girls were trafficked. Epstein was accused of trafficking, and Ghislaine Maxwell was convicted of trafficking. They must have been trafficked to somebody. And one would suppose that Maxwell would have a lot of information about to whom these girls were trafficked."

The Q&A between Blanche and Maxwell looks to Rotner, he said, as if it's a "sham."

"He is Trump’s lawyer. He says even recently that he continues to have an attorney-client relationship with Donald Trump," Rotner recalled. "He not only was Trump’s lawyer but he also reports to Donald Trump now. And he’s the one who’s going out to talk to Ghislaine Maxwell to unearth evidence that clearly involves Donald Trump—at least as a witness and maybe even as a subject. So, it just seems completely phony, and any pretext that there’s a real investigation going on, I think, is just that."

And already Rotner thinks that Maxwell is starting to adopt some of Trump's language about an "unfair" trial and that there were broken promises from prosecutors. "They're setting up what I would call a quasi-legal argument— that there was something wrong with her conviction and that Trump can and should remedy that by a commutation or pardon or something of that ilk.

Sargent thinks it is likely MAGA will buy such an excuse if Trump pardons Maxwell.

"So is there a scenario where that does happen: Trump pardons Maxwell; Maxwell exonerates Trump; the “Justice Department” closes everything down and they just hunker down and ride it out," he said, describing it as a grim future.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Mirage: Trump, Epstein, and the Art of Distraction

In the latest chapter of political theater that would make even Kafka blush, Greg Sargent of The Bulwark delves into the convoluted narrative where former President Donald Trump appears to be leading his MAGA following into a labyrinth of humiliation and contradiction. The subject at the core of this saga? The scandal involving notorious trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, and Trump's bewildering claims that it's all a hoax. Let's dissect the absurdity piece by piece.

Trump's Hoax Narrative

Trump's assertion that the Epstein scandal is nothing but a fabrication—if his name were truly in the mix, Biden would've leveraged it against him—rings hollow. This is a classic Trump maneuver: deflect, deny, and muddy the waters. It's a tactic designed not to clarify but to confuse, to wear down the listener's capacity for critical thought until they're willing to accept even the most outlandish statements as plausible.

The Pardon Prelude

Trump's casual "I’m allowed to do it," in response to a query about pardoning Ghislaine Maxwell, Epstein's convicted accomplice, is alarming not for its audacity (we've come to expect as much) but for the blatant disregard for legal and ethical standards it represents. This isn't just about whether he can; it's about whether he should. The fact that this question doesn't seem to cross his mind—or if it does, doesn't stay long—is a testament to the moral vacuity at play.

The Blanche Connection

Enter Todd Blanche, Trump's former lawyer, now acting in a capacity that strains credulity. His courthouse visit to Maxwell, purportedly on behalf of the Deputy Attorney General, reeks of subterfuge. Philip Rotner is spot on in his skepticism. The idea that Blanche is seeking to exonerate Trump in exchange for a pardon for Maxwell is not just plausible; it's predictable. This isn't legal strategy; it's political chicanery masquerading as due process.

MAGA's Self-Delusion

The MAGA crowd's hopeful narrative that Maxwell will "reveal the whole truth" is a study in cognitive dissonance. They've hitched their wagon to a star that refuses to acknowledge the gravity of Epstein’s crimes, much less his own potential complicity. The insistence on seeing this through a lens of conspiracy rather than confronting the uncomfortable reality is, as Sargent points out, absurd.

The Sham of Investigation

Rotner's characterization of the Q&A between Blanche and Maxwell as a "sham" is charitable. This isn't an investigation; it's a farce. Blanche's continued allegiance to Trump, coupled with his role in these proceedings, strips any pretense of impartiality or genuine pursuit of justice. It's a maneuver designed to obfuscate, not illuminate.

The Quasi-Legal Argument

Maxwell's adoption of Trumpian language about her trial being "unfair" and the broken promises of prosecutors is the groundwork for a narrative designed to appeal to Trump's base. It's setting the stage for a potential pardon, not on the merits of justice, but as a political gambit. The implication that Trump could "remedy" her conviction through executive clemency is not just alarming—it's a perversion of justice.

The Grim Future Scenario

Sargent's hypothetical scenario—Trump pardons Maxwell, Maxwell exonerates Trump, and the "Justice Department" shuts everything down—is not just grim; it's a dystopian vision of the future of American politics where accountability is nonexistent, and loyalty to an individual trumps (pun intended) allegiance to the principles of justice and democracy.

In conclusion, Sargent's column is a scathing indictment of the MAGA machinery's willingness to distort, deny, and distract from the truth. The Epstein scandal, and Trump's handling of it, isn't just a humiliation for MAGA—it's a litmus test for the American political system. And the results are deeply troubling.
