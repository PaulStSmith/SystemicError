Ex-FBI official thinks Dan Bongino's days are numbered after public 'disrespect'

Former deputy FBI director Andrew McCabe told CNN on Tuesday that the bad blood between the FBI and Attorney General Pam Bondi's office was like nothing he's ever witnessed before, and would almost certainly lead to at least one head rolling.

Current deputy director Dan Bongino, who made his name touting Jeffrey Epstein conspiracy theories, has clashed with Bondi over her failure to produce files and an alleged client list that MAGA for years has clamored for. Bongino even failed to show up for work at least one day last week in protest.

"So, the tension between the deputy director and the AG is really untenable," McCabe said. "This is a bad thing for the FBI, bad thing for the Department of Justice, and really not the kind of thing that, in my experience, having worked for many attorneys general, I've never seen anything like this before."

McCabe continued, "And I can't imagine any other attorney general ever tolerating an act of disrespect along the lines of what allegedly took place between Pam Bondi and Dan Bongino in the White House when they got into some trading accusations and some sort of shouting match in front of the president's chief of staff. I can't imagine that happening."

McCabe said it's not unusual for the DOJ and FBI to have differing opinions on how to handle investigations, "but those conversations always take place in the house within the family. You never let those things spill over into the public."

McCabe said since President Donald Trump has thrown his support behind Bondi, she "makes the final call on everything, period. No exceptions."

And, since Bongino has already taken it on himself to challenge Bondi, he may not be long for the White House.

"I find it almost hard to believe that Dan will be able to continue in that role, with all of the drama that they've put forth in the last week," McCabe said, adding that Bongino, "could be convinced to resign if he's leaning that way."

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling Threads of Justice and Order: An Analysis

In a recent development that reads more like a screenplay for a political thriller than the workings of the United States' highest legal institutions, former deputy FBI director Andrew McCabe illuminated a disturbing tableau of internal conflict, disrespect, and dysfunction within the FBI and the Attorney General's office. The narrative not only exposes the fraying edges of these esteemed bodies but also paints a grim picture of the current state of American justice and governance.

The McCabe Revelation: A Symphony of Discord

Andrew McCabe's commentary on CNN regarding the unprecedented bad blood between the FBI and Attorney General Pam Bondi's office is nothing short of alarming. McCabe, a seasoned figure in the FBI's echelons, expressed his astonishment and concern over the tension, marking it as unparalleled in his extensive experience. The crux of the issue appears to hinge on Deputy Director Dan Bongino's overt clash with Bondi over critical files related to the MAGA movement's interests. Bongino's actions, including a failure to appear for work in protest, underscore a stark deviation from professionalism expected at such levels of governance.

The Bondi-Bongino Brawl: A Disgrace to Decorum

The alleged altercation between Pam Bondi and Dan Bongino within the hallowed walls of the White House, in view of the president's chief of staff, is a spectacle of degradation. McCabe's inability to fathom such an "act of disrespect" occurring under any other attorney general's watch speaks volumes about the current administration's tolerance, if not endorsement, of such behavior. This incident not only strips the involved parties of their dignity but also erodes the public's trust in their leadership.

A Breach of Protocol and Principle

McCabe rightly points out that while disagreements between the DOJ and FBI are not uncommon, these disputes are traditionally resolved internally, "within the family." The airing of such disagreements in the public sphere is a breach of protocol and an affront to the principle of united governance. The public spillover of internal conflicts not only undermines the institutions' integrity but also betrays a lack of unity and common purpose at the highest levels of law enforcement and legal oversight.

Trump, Bondi, and the Final Say

The narrative takes a darker turn with the revelation that President Donald Trump has thrown his unequivocal support behind Pam Bondi, effectively making her the ultimate arbiter in matters of justice, "period. No exceptions." This autocratic tilt in decision-making, where loyalty to the president seems to trump (pun intended) expertise, experience, and perhaps even ethics, is a dangerous precedent. It not only centralizes power but also potentially politicizes and biases the administration of justice.

Bongino's Precarious Position

Finally, the spotlight turns to Dan Bongino, whose future within the White House appears increasingly tenuous. McCabe speculates on the likelihood of Bongino being pressured to resign, a scenario that would not only mark the culmination of this sordid saga but also serve as a stark reminder of the consequences of challenging the established order in an environment that seems to increasingly reward loyalty over integrity.

Conclusion: A System in Crisis

What the McCabe commentary lays bare is a system in crisis, a tableau of a justice system being torn asunder not by external threats but by internal strife, disrespect, and a departure from foundational principles of governance. This scenario should not merely be a cause for concern but a clarion call for introspection, reform, and a return to decorum, professionalism, and principled leadership. The events described, while currently confined to the corridors of power, have far-reaching implications for the rule of law, the administration of justice, and the very fabric of American democracy.
