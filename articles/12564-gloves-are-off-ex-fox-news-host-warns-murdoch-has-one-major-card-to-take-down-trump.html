'Gloves are off': Ex-Fox News host warns Murdoch has one major card to take down Trump

Gretchen Carlson, a former "Fox and Friends" host who in 2016 made headlines by filing a high-profile sexual harassment lawsuit against then-Fox News chairman Roger Ailes, joined CNN on Friday evening to discuss the president's newly released lawsuit against her former employer.

"You worked, of course, for a [Rupert] Murdoch-owned outlet for a long time. You sued Fox. You said Murdoch fostered a culture of misogyny, toxicity," anchor Brianna Keilar said, addressing Carlson. "Are you surprised that Trump did this? And how do you think Murdoch might react?"

"Yeah, I’m shocked. I mean, up until a few hours ago, I would have told you that I didn’t think he was going to do this," Carlson said.

She noted that Trump may be emboldened after emerging successful with ABC and CBS and "bribing law firms to give him free services."

"So I do think that the gloves are off now," she said. "I think that this presents a conundrum for Murdoch and for Trump."

Carlson stressed that Trump was not Fox’s pick for president in 2016, and likely wasn’t their pick in 2024 either. Murdoch, she said, likes to "poke Trump with his power," as evidenced by The Wall Street Journal coming out to hammer him more in his second term.

"Up until now, we have not seen Fox News do the same, right? They’ve been avoiding this story," she noted.

Carlson said Murdoch may have another trick up his sleeve when it comes to taking on the president.

"The card that Murdoch has is that he can tell Fox News to start covering these stories. So that’s also his conundrum, because the last time that that happened, all the viewers went to other outlets," she warned. "So there’s risk for Murdoch in that scenario. But for Trump, there’s also a conundrum, because if Murdoch decides to tell Fox to start covering these stories, that’s a huge risk for Trump."

She concluded, "So they both have a lot to lose in this scenario."

The Fox News-Trump Saga: A Dance of Titans

In the ever-dramatic theater of American politics, a new act unfolds — Gretchen Carlson vs. The Behemoths. Carlson, a former Fox News anchor, has thrown a spotlight on the murky corridors of power, intertwining media moguls and former presidents with a tale that could rival any prime-time drama. Let's dissect the layers of this unfolding saga with the precision of a scalpel.

The Unlikely Challenger: Donald Trump

Trump's lawsuit against Fox News marks a pivotal moment in what could only be described as a bizarre twist of fate. For years, Trump and Fox News have engaged in a symbiotic relationship, each feeding the other's agenda. But now, Trump, the master of unpredictability, has turned on his once-faithful ally. Carlson's shock is reflective of a broader sentiment: Trump's actions are as unpredictable as they are unprecedented.

The Murdoch Conundrum

Rupert Murdoch, a name synonymous with media influence, finds himself in a peculiar position. His empire, built on the pillars of conservative commentary, is now under assault from the very figure it helped ascend to the presidency. Murdoch's power play, as Carlson suggests, involves "poking" Trump with subtle defiance through outlets like The Wall Street Journal. Yet, Fox News has hesitated to join the fray directly. Why? The answer lies in the delicate balance of viewer loyalty and political influence.

The Viewer's Dilemma

At the heart of this saga is the viewer — the ultimate kingmaker in the ratings-driven world of cable news. Murdoch's hesitation to unleash Fox News' full critical potential against Trump isn't just a matter of political allegiance; it's a calculated risk assessment. The last time Fox News alienated its base, the viewership migrated elsewhere. In the high-stakes game of media influence, losing viewers is akin to ceding power.

Carlson's Insightful Analysis

Gretchen Carlson's analysis unveils the intricate dance between Trump and Murdoch. It's a game of chess, with each player anticipating the other's moves. Trump's lawsuit isn't just a legal battle; it's a strategic maneuver in the larger war for political and media dominance. For Murdoch, the challenge is to maintain his empire's influence without alienating its foundation — the viewers. For Trump, the risk is becoming the target of the very machine that once propelled him to power.

Conclusion: A High-Stakes Game

As Carlson eloquently points out, both Trump and Murdoch stand on the precipice of a potentially disastrous fallout. The dynamics of their relationship, once symbiotic, now threaten to unravel, leaving both parties vulnerable. This saga is more than just a lawsuit; it's a reflection of the complex interplay between media, power, and politics.

In a world where truth often intertwines with narrative, the outcome of this battle will not just define the futures of Trump and Murdoch; it will shape the landscape of American media and politics for years to come. The stakes? Nothing less than the soul of conservative media and the allegiance of its vast audience. The Fox News-Trump saga is a testament to the age-old adage: in politics and media, there are no permanent friends, only permanent interests.
