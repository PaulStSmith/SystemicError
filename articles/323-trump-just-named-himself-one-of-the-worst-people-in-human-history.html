Trump just named himself one of the worst people in human history

Based on an article by Jason Miciak

Paulo SantosJune 22, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Obsession Isn’t Power. It’s Unaccountable Permission.

The list is the distraction

The source article opens with Trump admiring a list that places him beside history’s worst mass murderers, then spends pages marveling at the insult to civilization. That reaction is understandable, but it still misses the cleaner, uglier fact: the list is not the story. Trump’s response to it is. He did not hear a warning. He heard recognition.

This is not about a man accidentally comparing himself to monsters. It is about a president who treats domination as proof of legitimacy, and publicity as proof of greatness. The article is right to reject the shrugging normalization of that instinct. It is less useful when it frames the problem as a bizarre personality quirk rather than a governing philosophy.

Who holds power

Actual institutional power sits with the presidency, backed here by Trump’s control of the executive branch and the “personal fealty of nearly every Republican in government,” as the source puts it. That is the machine. The power is not his bragging. The power is the ability to convert bragging into policy, coercion, and fear.

The article correctly names the stakes: Trump has already used the office to push toward state violence, contempt for courts, and symbolic self-worship. The source mentions masked agents rounding up brown people, threats against Greenland, talk of suspending habeas corpus, and a violent transfer of power. That is not random chaos. It is an organized attempt to make personal will outrank law.

The caddy, the con, the cover

The “historian’s” list turned out to be assembled by Gary Player’s caddy. That detail matters because it exposes the fraud at the center of the performance: not just Trump’s vanity, but the ease with which faux authority gets injected into elite conversation and then laundered as significance.

Still, the list’s bogus provenance is not the main con. The bigger con is the expectation that this should be read as harmless ego-stroking. It should not. Trump is not being misled into power by flattery alone; he is choosing a world in which flattery substitutes for accountability. The article correctly mocks the phony scholarship, but the institutional scandal is that a president can be surrounded by so much deference that even a caddy-made ranking becomes a stage prop for self-mythology.

Deliberate harm, dressed as confusion

The source is strongest when it stops pretending Trump is merely uninformed or emotionally stunted. He is not confused about power. He understands it as extraction: what can be taken, what can be broken, what can be done without personal cost. That is the throughline connecting the threat to courts, the use of masked force, and the fantasy of placing his face on money.

This is where softer political framing becomes misleading. The article’s language about “astonishing obliviousness” is too polite for a man who has spent years teaching the system to accommodate him. The issue is not that Trump failed a civics test. The issue is that he passed the one exam he cares about: whether he can get away with it.

The pattern beneath the spectacle

The source makes the right historical comparison when it links Trump to rulers who pursued power as an end in itself. That pattern is the point. These leaders do not need a public theology of cruelty. They need institutions that reward escalation, subordinates who confuse obedience with loyalty, and opponents who keep treating the abuse as eccentricity.

That is why the article’s strongest line is also its least sentimental one: Trump seeks power to improve his fortunes, and only his fortunes. Everything else is decoration. The flags, the slogans, the martyr pose, the fake scholarship, the monster list. It is all costume around a familiar political appetite.

The system made for him

The real lesson is not that Trump is uniquely weird. It is that modern American power has been built to tolerate, amplify, and excuse this kind of person when enough elites decide the damage is manageable. Mitch McConnell’s engineering, Republican submission, media fascination, and institutional cowardice all helped build a system where self-glorification can sit inside the presidency and call itself strength.

That is the larger story: a political order that mistakes raw domination for leadership will eventually hand the keys to someone who wants the office for the domination alone. Trump is not an aberration from that logic. He is its purest expression.
