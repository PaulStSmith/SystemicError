Trump got 'rolled' by Republicans as he looks elsewhere for support: Morning Joe

As Donald Trump watches Republican Party lawmakers balk at his demands, he is seeking support elsewhere to soothe him after another legislative defeat.

That is the opinion of MS NOW’s Joe Scarborough and Politico’s Jonathan Martin, who both reacted to Tuesday’s events where the House and Senate both vote overwhelmingly -- and in a bipartisan manner -- to release the Jeffrey Epstein files at the same time Trump was buttering up Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud in the Oval Office.

After sharing clips of Trump praising the Saudi prince and then holding a state dinner for him, Scarborough noted the president was seemingly trying to avoid the personal Epstein loss in Congress despite his earlier protestations that the files are a “hoax.”

According to the “Morning Joe” co-host, “This all comes with the background of — Mika [Brzezinski] is going to get into the news in a minute here — of Donald Trump being rolled for the first time in an extraordinarily dramatic way by Republicans in the House and the Senate.”

“And by backbenchers, frankly,” he added. “I mean, look, no offense to [Kentucky Republican] Thomas Massie, but he’s somebody who’s sort of been a bit of a bomb thrower, you know, not somebody who was the modern Sam Rayburn there, necessarily. And he basically said, ‘I got the votes, man.’ And once that was clear, Trump had no choice but to fold.”

“Think about the timing of this,” he suggested. “So two weeks ago, up and down the ballot, coast to coast, the Republican Party loses every election decisively. Even the White House gets the message.”

“Two weeks later to the night, two weeks later, they got one more foreign leader coming through Washington for one more grip and grin and they have this lavish state dinner. Talk about not getting the message right. The voters say, why aren’t you focused on us, man? And two weeks later, what are they doing? They’re once again focused on foreign policy. Some other country. Who are these folks here? Who is this guy?”

That led Martin to suggest, “I just think it’s so, it’s so contradicts the message from two weeks ago. And it shows that Trump is going to do what he wants to do. And what does he want to do? He wants to sort of buddy-buddy up with these folks. He wants to enjoy the trappings of the office. And as long as you can’t change that, you can’t change the fundamental political challenge this White House has.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: Trump’s Defeat and Distraction Strategy

In a striking display of political maneuvering, Donald Trump attempts to divert attention from his legislative defeat concerning the Jeffrey Epstein files by cozying up to Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud. This move, as dissected by Joe Scarborough of MS NOW and Jonathan Martin of Politico, lays bare the former president’s penchant for optical diversions rather than confronting his failures head-on. Here’s a breakdown of the situation:

The Distraction

As the House and Senate, in a rare bipartisan decision, voted overwhelmingly to release the Jeffrey Epstein files, Trump was busy hosting the Saudi prince. This juxtaposition is no mere coincidence but a calculated attempt to shift the media’s and, consequently, the public’s focus. Trump’s praise of the prince and the subsequent state dinner are textbook examples of his strategy of distraction. It’s a clear demonstration of his inability to deal with defeat and his preference for the pomp and circumstance of his office over the pressing issues that concern the American people.

The Defeat

Scarborough’s commentary sheds light on a significant moment: Trump, for once, being squarely defeated by his own party. This isn’t just any defeat; it’s a public and bipartisan rebuke of Trump’s stance on the Epstein files, which he dismissed as a “hoax.” The involvement of figures like Kentucky Republican Thomas Massie, who is not known for his subtlety or influence, only adds insult to injury. It’s not just a defeat; it’s a humiliation at the hands of those Trump presumed to have under his thumb.

The Disconnect

The timing of these events reveals a deeper issue within Trump’s administration and its approach to governance. Following a comprehensive loss in elections “up and down the ballot, coast to coast,” the White House’s decision to focus on a glitzy state dinner with a foreign leader instead of reflecting on the electoral message sent by the American public is tone-deaf, to say the least. This disregard for voter concerns in favor of “buddy-buddy” diplomacy and enjoying “the trappings of the office” underscores a fundamental disconnect with the electorate.

Analysis

Jonathan Martin’s observation that Trump’s actions starkly contrast with the message sent by voters two weeks prior is on point. It’s not just a contradiction; it’s a blatant disregard for the priorities and concerns of the American people. Trump’s determination to do as he pleases, regardless of the political consequences or the optics, highlights a significant challenge for his administration. It’s not just about political strategy; it’s about the fundamental values and priorities that guide that strategy.

Conclusion

In essence, Trump’s reaction to his legislative defeat and the optics of hosting Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud in the immediate aftermath serves as a microcosm of his presidency: avoidance, distraction, and a profound disconnect with the electorate. This incident is not merely a blip on the political radar but a telling glimpse into the modus operandi of a leader more concerned with appearances than addressing the substantive issues at hand. The question remains—how long can such a strategy sustain a presidency already marred by controversy and division? Only time will tell, but the signs are less than promising.
