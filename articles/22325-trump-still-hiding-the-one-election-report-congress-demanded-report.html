Trump still hiding the one election report Congress demanded: report

Selective Secrecy Is Not National Security

Trump Controls the Leak Valve

The basic fact here is simple: Trump holds the power, and he is using it to decide which intelligence the public gets to see and which intelligence stays buried. The source says he has released documents that flatter his political narrative, while still withholding the election-related material Congress ordered made public. That is not transparency. It is gatekeeping by a president who treats classification as a political weapon.

The Story He Wants You to Believe

Trump is trying to sell a story in which foreign interference, undocumented voters, and vote-by-mail all collapse into one giant excuse machine for his 2020 defeat. The reporting undercuts that performance. The 2020 assessment cited in the piece described meddling by Iran against Trump, Russia for Trump, and only a dissenting minority view that China took some limited steps aimed at undermining him. In other words: the evidence does not match the propaganda. But Trump is not confused about that. He is choosing a usable villain and calling it intelligence.

Classification as Partisan Infrastructure

Former intelligence officials in the article describe a pattern that should be named plainly: Trump has weaponized classification in a way that goes beyond normal presidential discretion. The point is not simply that presidents can declassify. The point is that this president appears to be using disclosure as a political sorting mechanism, handing out the documents that serve him and burying the ones that do not. Releasing UFO files and Epstein-related material while keeping the 2024 election assessment hidden is not random. It is a hierarchy of self-interest.

Congress Ordered Disclosure. The White House Ignored It.

This is where the accountability lies. Congress ordered the release, and the law requires a public assessment within 60 days of the election. The administration has not complied. That is not an abstract institutional failure; it is a decision by the executive branch to violate a reporting requirement that exists precisely so Congress can see the full scope of foreign interference. Democrats on the House Intelligence Committee asked for the report in July. Rep. Jason Crow then tried to attach funding consequences to the intelligence bill. That is the correct direction: follow the money, not the press release.

Misdirection Is the Method

The article’s framing is weak wherever it lets Trump’s claims linger as if they are competing interpretations rather than strategic distortion. He blames China, but the source material shows the actual record is more complicated and less convenient for him. That mismatch matters. Authoritarian politics depends on manufacturing the appearance of uncertainty where the facts are already available, then using that fog to excuse selective disclosure, institutional defiance, and revenge-driven governance. The lie is not that Trump has no evidence. The lie is that he is honestly weighing evidence at all.

The Pattern Is Bigger Than One Report

What this story reveals is a ruling style built on asymmetry: power for the executive, scrutiny for everyone else, and truth only when it can be weaponized. Trump does not need to persuade the public that he is consistent. He only needs to keep enough information hidden long enough to make outrage partisan and accountability optional. That is the system at work here: classification turned into campaign material, intelligence turned into narrative control, and the machinery of government bent toward the needs of one man and the coalition that protects him.
