Trump raves at 'treasonous SCUM' as military warns of weakening U.S. firepower

Trump’s Ammunition Panic Is Really a Power Panic

Power Speaks First

Trump spent Thursday raging on Truth Social after reports that the war in Iran has thinned U.S. munitions stockpiles and may weaken readiness against China and Russia. He denied the shortage, attacked reporters as “treasonous,” bragged about production, and blamed Joe Biden for material he says was handed to Ukraine and NATO.

The Real Fact Pattern

The institutional power here sits with Trump. He is the president. He is the one overseeing the military posture that reporters say has raised concerns inside the national security apparatus. So when he starts yelling about “virtually unlimited” ammunition, that is not analysis. It is a political shield thrown over a material problem.

He does not answer the central question: if U.S. firepower has been spent, who authorized the spending, and what did it cost? Instead he reaches for volume, insult, and denial. That is not leadership. It is performance designed to make accountability disappear inside noise.

Blame the Ghost, Spare the Decision-Maker

Trump’s Biden attack is the standard move of weak accountability politics: inherit the consequences, deny the present, punish the predecessor. Biden is no longer in office. He cannot command current munitions policy, current war planning, or current stockpile management. Trump can.

The deflection matters because it changes the target of scrutiny. Instead of asking what Trump’s Iran policy has consumed, the public is supposed to argue about old transfers to Ukraine. That is deliberate misdirection. It turns a question about present-day capacity into a grievance sermon about the last administration.

The Ukraine Scapegoat

Trump’s claim that Ukraine and NATO got “Hundreds of Billions of Dollars” “free of charge” is doing two jobs at once. It recycles resentment against support for Ukraine, and it frames allied defense as theft from Americans. That is familiar nationalist fraud: recast collective security as charity abused by outsiders, then use that invented abuse to excuse domestic shortfalls.

The move also reveals the political utility of permanent blame. If everything is someone else’s fault, then nothing in Trump’s own war-making has to be judged on its results. The shortage becomes a partisan talking point instead of a metric of governance.

Munitions, Misinformation, and Authoritarian Reflex

The most revealing line in Trump’s rant is not the boast about production. It is the need to label reporters “treasonous” for accurately describing a military capacity problem. That is authoritarian reflex in plain view: treat scrutiny as betrayal, treat facts as sabotage, treat institutional oversight as disloyalty.

When a president answers a stockpile question with abuse, the issue is no longer just ammunition. It is whether the public is being governed by someone who can tolerate reality at all.

The Larger Pattern

This is how power protects itself when it is under strain. First comes the denial. Then the blame shift. Then the attack on the people describing the damage. The underlying fact remains unchanged: military resources are finite, wars consume them, and the consequences are political whether Trump wants to admit it or not.

The deeper pattern is not confusion. It is governing by camouflage. When the costs of decision-making become visible, Trump reaches for scapegoats and spectacle instead of responsibility. That is the point.
