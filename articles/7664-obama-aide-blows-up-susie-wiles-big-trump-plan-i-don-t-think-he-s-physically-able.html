Obama aide blows up Susie Wiles' big Trump plan: 'I don't think he's physically able'

Republican presidential nominee former President Donald Trump sits with Susie Wiles as he attends the New York Jets football game against the Pittsburgh Steelers at Acrisure Stadium, Sunday, Oct. 20, 2024, in Pittsburgh. Evan Vucci/Pool via REUTERS

Donald Trump Chief of Staff Susie Wiles has a plan to help Republicans win the 2026 midterm elections, but a former Barack Obama campaign staffer threw cold water on that idea Sunday.

Former Obama campaign advisor Ameshia Cross, who often appears on MSN NOW and its predecessor MSNBC, was asked on the network about Wiles’ stated plan to combat the common pattern that Republicans lose when Trump himself isn’t on the ballot. According to Wiles’, she’s going to “put him on the ballot” by making him “campaign like it’s 2024 again.”

But not so fast, according to Cross, who said things won’t be that simple.

After being asked her take, Cross said, “I don’t think Donald Trump is physically able to campaign like it’s 2024 again, but all that withstanding, he also has something now that he didn’t have against him in 2024.”

According to Cross, “He is now holding the bag for the disaster of peoples’ pocketbooks, for the massive unemployment that we’re seeing, for underemployment, for people lacking affordability.”

“Putting him out on the campaign trail when he’s saying specifically that affordability is a hoax, is probably not the best thing to do,” she added before pointing out that the affordability issue goes across party lines.

Because Trump hasn’t enacted policy to help struggling citizens, “That’s going to hurt him as well,” according to the strategist.

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Trump’s Midterm Magic

In the latest swirl of political strategy and counterstrategy, Susie Wiles, the Chief of Staff for Donald Trump, has unveiled a plan that she believes will secure Republican victories in the 2026 midterm elections. Her strategy, to essentially “put Trump on the ballot” by having him campaign as if it were still 2024, is a fascinating glimpse into the desperation creeping into the Republican camp. However, Ameshia Cross, a former Barack Obama campaign staffer, has promptly doused this plan with a healthy dose of reality.

The Misguided Nostalgia of Trump’s Campaign Strategy

Wiles’ plan hinges on the nostalgia for Trump’s previous campaign successes, a strategy that reeks of desperation. It presupposes that simply wheeling Trump out as the centerpiece of the campaign will magically rally the Republican base to secure midterm victories. This approach fails to account for the significant shifts in the political and economic landscape since Trump’s last dance in the political arena.

Cross’ Cold Water: A Reality Check

Ameshia Cross, leveraging her experience and insight, brings up crucial points that the Trump camp seems to willfully ignore. Firstly, the question of Trump’s physical ability to campaign as vigorously as he did in 2024 casts doubt on the feasibility of Wiles’ plan. More importantly, Cross highlights the albatross around Trump’s neck: the tangible economic grievances of the American populace.

Under Trump’s watch, the issues of massive unemployment, underemployment, and a general lack of affordability have become more pronounced. Cross points out that Trump’s dismissal of affordability issues as a hoax is not only out of touch but is a political strategy likely to alienate voters across party lines. This is a crucial point, as economic hardship does not discriminate by party affiliation, and voters are increasingly prioritizing candidates who offer tangible solutions to their economic woes.

The Irony of Trump’s Economic Baggage

The irony of Trump’s predicament cannot be overstated. Once hailed as a business mogul capable of translating his success into economic prosperity for the country, Trump now finds himself being held accountable for the very issues he promised to solve. This shift in perception is a significant hurdle for any campaign strategy centered around his persona.

The Unspoken Truth: Economic Realities Over Personal Branding

What Cross subtly underscores, and what the Trump team fails to grasp, is a fundamental truth in modern politics: personal branding and nostalgia cannot trump economic realities. Voters grappling with unemployment and a lack of affordable living options are unlikely to be swayed by the same tactics that worked in a pre-pandemic, pre-economic downturn era.

Conclusion: A Strategy Doomed to Fail?

In essence, Wiles’ strategy is a gamble, one that underestimates the intelligence and concerns of the American voter. The belief that Trump’s mere presence and campaigning style will overshadow the pressing economic issues facing the nation is not only misguided but potentially detrimental to the Republican Party’s hopes in the 2026 midterms.

As Cross succinctly puts it, without addressing the root causes of the economic distress that plagues the electorate, Trump’s campaign trail may well lead to a dead end. The Republican strategy needs a reality check, one that prioritizes the needs and concerns of the people over the glorification of a single individual’s political brand.
