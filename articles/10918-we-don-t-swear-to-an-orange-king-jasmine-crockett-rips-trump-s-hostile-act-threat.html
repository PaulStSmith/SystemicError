'We don't swear to an orange king': Jasmine Crockett rips Trump's 'hostile act' threat

Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) spoke out against President Donald Trump after the White House said that an effort in Congress to force the release of files on sex offender Jeffrey Epstein would be seen as a "hostile act."

During a House Judiciary Committee hearing on Wednesday, Crockett accused Trump of "weaponizing his idea of free speech to force folks and companies to become his political puppets to promote his propaganda."

She noted that Republicans had joined with the Trump administration to defund PBS and NPR.

"Trump has removed books discussing racial discrimination at U.S. military academies," Crockett said. "He's tried to strip funding to schools across the country if they don't remove materials that include discussions of racial discrimination or that promote diversity."

"It's Trump weaponizing the idea of free speech to force people, institutions, and companies to be his mouthpieces," she continued. "This ain't free speech, it's propaganda."

The lawmaker then tied the issue of free speech to the effort to obtain information about Epstein.

"And right now, as we're having these discussions, you know who's trying to shut down members from voting in the way that they want to vote?" she asked. "It's the president who literally said he would look at anyone who decided that they were going to vote to release the Epstein files as hostile."

"That sounds like a threat of — as it relates to all First Amendment protections, in addition to the fact that it's literally contravening our constitutional duty that we swore an oath to, because we don't swear to an orange king," Crockett added. "Instead, we have sworn an oath to the Constitution, and we were elected to represent the people that elected us."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Irony of "Free Speech" Weaponization

Rep. Jasmine Crockett's fiery critique of President Donald Trump's stance on free speech throws a glaring spotlight on the administration's hypocrisy. With a tone that cuts deeper than a knife, Crockett unveils a narrative that is as troubling as it is ludicrous. Trump's apparent declaration that efforts to release the files on sex offender Jeffrey Epstein would be considered a "hostile act" is the pinnacle of this absurdity. Let's dissect this farce with the precision it deserves.

Trump's Propaganda Machine

Crockett's assertion that Trump has weaponized free speech is not just an opinion; it's a cold, hard fact. By allegedly defunding PBS and NPR and removing books that discuss racial discrimination from military academies, Trump is not championing free speech. He is curating a propaganda machine that would make Orwell roll in his grave. The attempt to strip funding from schools that promote discussions of racial discrimination or diversity is another stroke of this authoritarian brush.

It's not free speech; it's selective muting.

The Epstein Files Controversy

The controversy surrounding the Epstein files is the cherry on top of this authoritarian sundae. Crockett eloquently ties the issue of free speech back to this, highlighting the administration's efforts to control the narrative by any means necessary. The threat to view efforts to release these files as hostile is nothing short of a blatant attempt to stifle democratic processes and transparency.

It's not governance; it's a gag order.

The Oath to the Constitution, Not to an "Orange King"

Crockett's closing remarks remind us of the foundational principles that govern the United States. The oath taken by elected officials is to the Constitution, not to an individual, regardless of their office. The notion that Trump's preferences could supersede constitutional duties is not just alarming; it's an affront to the very democracy the country prides itself on.

It's not loyalty; it's a constitutional crisis.

Conclusion

In a world where the lines between free speech and propaganda are increasingly blurred, Crockett's words serve as a much-needed wake-up call. The weaponization of free speech by the Trump administration, as outlined by Crockett, is a tactic designed to silence opposition and control the narrative. Such actions are antithetical to the principles of democracy and freedom that the United States purports to champion.

This isn't just about free speech. It's about the soul of American democracy.

In the face of such blatant disregard for constitutional values, one must ask: when will enough be enough? How much more of this farcical governance can the country withstand before the fabric of democracy is irreparably torn? Crockett's critique, sharp and unyielding, is a clarion call for accountability and a reminder of the duty elected officials have to uphold the Constitution, not the whims of an "orange king."
