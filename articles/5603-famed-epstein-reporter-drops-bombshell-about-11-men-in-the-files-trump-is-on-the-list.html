Famed Epstein reporter drops bombshell about '11 men' in the files: 'Trump is on the list'

A reporter whose work led to the arrests of Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell dropped a bombshell claim on Saturday, saying the DOJ has been lying and that “there really is an FBI list of other people suspected of possible wrongdoing in connection with Epstein.” Trump is on that list, she said.

Miami Herald reporter Julie K. Brown’s work on the “Perversion of Justice” published by the newspaper in 2018 spurred New York federal prosecutors to open a new criminal case into Epstein, and she has been reporting on the most recent Epstein files dump, even pointing out a prosecution memo that was immediately scrubbed after reporters started asking questions.

In a follow-up to that, Brown and the Miami Herald both dropped reports on the DOJ’s secret Epstein prosecution list.

“Contrary to what the FBI and DOJ have said over the past year, it turns out there really is an FBI list of other people suspected of possible wrongdoing in connection with Jeffrey Epstein,” according to Brown. “President Trump, who has denied any involvement with Epstein’s crimes, is on that list, as well as a number of other powerful people.”

The Herald clarifies, “After the Justice Department shut the door on releasing the Jeffrey Epstein files in July 2025, FBI agents worked on drafts of a 21-page presentation of all the evidence the FBI had gathered in the case, including a summary of allegations against 11 men. There’s no evidence that Epstein, a New York financier who sexually assaulted and trafficked hundreds of girls and young women over two decades, kept his own ‘client list’.”

The report continues:

“But documents not previously made public show the FBI had compiled its own list of possible accusations against prominent men based largely on uncorroborated tips and interviews the DOJ had compiled since Epstein’s 2019 arrest. Among the names on the list: President Donald Trump, former President Bill Clinton, Harvey Weinstein, private-equity investor Leon Black, L Brands founder Les Wexner, banker Jes Staley and others.”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Saga: A Tangled Web of Power, Deception, and Alleged Complicity

In the relentless pursuit of truth, Miami Herald reporter Julie K. Brown has once again ignited the flames of justice and accountability with her latest revelation concerning the Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). Her investigative prowess, which previously led to the arrests of Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell, has now unveiled a shocking assertion: contrary to official statements, there exists an FBI list of individuals suspected of wrongdoing in connection with Epstein’s heinous crimes. Among these names is none other than former President Donald Trump, alongside a cadre of other influential figures.

The Veil of Secrecy and Lies

The DOJ’s adamant denial and subsequent refusal to release the Epstein files in July 2025 reeks of a cover-up at the highest levels of power and influence. The FBI’s 21-page draft, summarizing evidence against notable individuals, suggests a systemic reluctance to pursue justice against the elite. This reluctance is not merely an oversight but a deliberate obfuscation of truth and accountability.

The List of Shame

The names disclosed—ranging from Trump to Harvey Weinstein, from finance mogul Leon Black to L Brands founder Les Wexner—paint a damning picture of systemic abuse shielded by power. The FBI’s reliance on “unsubstantiated tips and interviews” does not diminish the gravity of their potential involvement but rather highlights the urgent need for a thorough and unbiased investigation.

The DOJ’s Dubious Role

The DOJ’s handling of the Epstein case, particularly its decision to “shut the door” on releasing critical files, is a glaring testament to the department’s complicity in maintaining the status quo of impunity. The immediate scrubbing of a prosecution memo upon media inquiry further underscores a culture of secrecy and manipulation designed to protect the powerful at the expense of justice.

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Accountability

The revelation of the FBI’s list is not just an indictment of those named but a profound critique of the institutions meant to safeguard justice. The DOJ and FBI’s actions—or lack thereof—raise serious questions about their commitment to upholding the law when it involves individuals of substantial influence.

As the public digests the implications of Brown’s latest findings, it is imperative to demand transparency, accountability, and justice. The cloak of power should no longer serve as a shield for criminal behavior, and the pursuit of truth must prevail over the preservation of elite networks. The Epstein saga is far from over, and it is the responsibility of the media, the public, and the justice system to ensure that the full extent of these crimes and their enablers is brought to light.

In a world where power too often corrupts, Julie K. Brown’s reporting shines as a beacon of hope and a call to action. The time for silence and secrecy has passed. Now is the moment for relentless pursuit of justice, no matter how powerful the accused.
