Right-wing Newsmax is already licking their wounds

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosNovember 4, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

AlterNet reported that Newsmax guests reacted angrily as early Tuesday vote counts showed Democrats gaining ground and threatening Republican control of the House and Senate. Former White House political director Matt Schlapp claimed Democrats had “bamboozled” voters into blaming President Donald Trump’s policies for unresolved economic problems.

Former senator Rick Santorum predicted Democrats would use congressional power to investigate or impeach Trump rather than address inflation, grocery costs or gasoline prices. The article also cited election forecaster Charlie Cook predicting a severe Republican defeat, but it did not provide final results establishing which chambers or seats had changed hands.

Power was on the ballot

The central fact was not that television personalities felt wounded. Trump held the presidency, and Republicans entered the election with congressional power that Democrats were attempting to reduce or take away. Voters—not Newsmax, Biden or some unnamed socialist force—were deciding whether that arrangement would continue.

Schlapp conceded the durability of presidential power even while lamenting possible Republican losses: Trump would remain president for two more years. His reference to Senate voting rules also exposed the real concern beneath the complaints. Losing seats could constrain the administration’s legislative leverage, oversight protection and control over federal priorities.

Losing became deception

Schlapp’s explanation stripped voters of agency. If they supported Republicans, their judgment counted; if they rejected Republicans, they had been “bamboozled.” That is not analysis of an electorate. It is a permission structure for dismissing an unfavorable result without examining why people voted that way.

The maneuver also redirected responsibility toward Biden, who was not described as holding office in the article, while Trump was the sitting president whose party controlled Congress. Whatever responsibility Biden’s prior policies may bear for economic conditions, the reporting supplied no evidence establishing Schlapp’s claim that he “caused” the problem under debate. Institutional power belongs to current officeholders, and accountability follows power.

Santorum invented the alibi early

Santorum moved immediately from prospective defeat to prospective Democratic misconduct. Before the article established final control of Congress, he predicted investigations, impeachment and neglect of household costs. He also alleged that Biden-era policy deliberately increased prices, but AlterNet provided no evidence supporting that accusation.

This is preemptive blame allocation. If Democrats won, their victory supposedly reflected Trump’s usefulness as a political target rather than voter judgment about Republican governance. If economic problems persisted, Democrats would own those too—even though Trump would still control the executive branch. The argument assigns Democrats responsibility in advance while preserving Republican power from responsibility after the fact.

AlterNet blurred forecast and fact

AlterNet correctly exposed the grievance logic in the Newsmax remarks, but its own framing outran the evidence it presented. “Devastation” and a predicted “hell of a beating” are not final election results. Early tallies and an experienced forecaster’s judgment can indicate direction; they do not establish completed outcomes.

The article also declared that analysts showed Santorum was right that Democrats could thank Trump for victories and turnout. Yet the supplied text named only Cook’s prediction and did not present turnout data or analysis proving that causal claim. Mockery is not a substitute for precision, especially when the underlying story is about powerful actors trying to replace electoral accountability with narrative warfare.

The system protects power with stories

The larger pattern is familiar: officeholders exercise power, voters register consequences, and partisan media recast those consequences as fraud, manipulation or irrationality. The defeated side then treats public judgment as illegitimate while preparing to blame its opponents for governing constraints it still retains the power to impose.

That narrative does political work. It shields leaders from internal accountability, teaches audiences that adverse elections require no self-examination and turns ordinary democratic opposition into a plot against the country. The wound Newsmax displayed was not merely disappointment. It was the discovery that holding power does not guarantee protection from its consequences.
