Reading into Ruin: How the Right Crippled American Education — and How We Rebuild It

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Who Sabotaged American Education?Consequences of the Conservative AgendaRebuilding the Ruins: What Needs to ChangeA Literate Society Is a Free Society

In 1961, John F. Kennedy declared America’s intention to put a man on the moon before the decade was out. And in 1969, it happened. Not because every American had a PhD, or because every child read at grade level — but because the country, for one brief moment, believed in collective effort, public investment, and the raw power of education.

Fast-forward to today: over half the adult population can't read beyond the level of an 11-year-old, and over 50 million Americans are functionally illiterate. That’s not just a national embarrassment — it’s a controlled demolition. And yes, we can place the dynamite squarely at the feet of the Republican Party.

Who Sabotaged American Education?

Let’s call it what it is: decades of right-wing policy have gutted public education, not by accident, but by design.

  • Ronald Reagan famously said, “The nine most terrifying words in the English language are: ‘I’m from the government and I’m here to help.’” That wasn’t just anti-bureaucracy rhetoric — it was a declaration of war on public services, including schools.

  • The 1983 report A Nation at Risk, commissioned under Reagan, sounded alarms about declining test scores — but instead of investing in teachers and infrastructure, conservatives used it as an excuse to push privatization, standardization, and blame teachers.

  • Newt Gingrich’s “Contract with America” in the 1990s doubled down, slashing federal spending and vilifying public workers.

  • Bush’s No Child Left Behind (2001) was a Trojan horse: packaged as reform, it introduced high-stakes testing and punishment-based funding, which led to underfunded schools being labeled “failures” and ultimately closed.

  • Betsy DeVos, Trump’s education secretary, didn’t even try to hide her agenda: to dismantle public education and funnel public money into unregulated private and religious schools.

This wasn’t incompetence. This was ideological sabotage.

Consequences of the Conservative Agenda

The GOP’s decades-long assault on education has:

  • Starved public schools of resources.

  • Driven teachers out with low pay, lack of respect, and political scapegoating.

  • Promoted charter schools that cherry-pick students and siphon funds.

  • Turned school boards into battlegrounds for culture wars, not literacy wars.

  • Normalized ignorance, replacing expertise with “gut feelings,” and turning science into opinion.

And now? We live in a country where disinformation spreads like wildfire, conspiracy theories run rampant, and tens of millions of adults can’t comprehend basic text. How do you convince a nation that a lie is the truth? Easy — just make sure they can’t read.

Rebuilding the Ruins: What Needs to Change

Here’s how we fix it — and yes, it’s possible:

  1. Fully fund public education: Stop starving schools. Every child deserves safe classrooms, updated materials, and well-paid teachers.

  2. End privatization scams: Charter and voucher schemes are draining the system. Cut them off.

  3. Treat teachers like professionals: Raise salaries, reduce burnout, and return respect to the role.

  4. Invest in adult literacy: It’s not too late. Millions of adults want to learn — give them the tools.

  5. Ban political interference in curriculum: History isn’t “woke,” science isn’t liberal, and books aren’t the enemy.

  6. Build schools, not prisons: America spends more on incarceration than on education in many states. That’s not a budget issue. That’s a value system.

A Literate Society Is a Free Society

Republicans have sold the public on a lie — that education is broken because of “liberal indoctrination.” No. It’s broken because they broke it. On purpose. And until we reclaim the idea that an educated public is a patriotic necessity, we will remain a nation vulnerable to lies, grifts, and authoritarianism.

But it’s not too late.

We put a man on the moon with slide rules and chalkboards — not because we were a nation of geniuses, but because we invested in knowledge, in shared progress, in the belief that everyone could learn and contribute. Today, that spirit has been hijacked. While public schools crumble, a privileged few are handed the keys to elite, ideologically filtered education — not to enlighten, but to indoctrinate. We don’t need more obedient loyalists. We need critical thinkers.

If we want a future worthy of the name, we must stop hoarding education and start rebuilding it — for everyone.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
