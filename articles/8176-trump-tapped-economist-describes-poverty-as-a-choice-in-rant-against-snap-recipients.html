Trump-tapped economist describes poverty as ‘a choice’ in rant against SNAP recipients

Economist E.J. Antoni speaks on Fox Business’ “The Big Money Show,” Dec. 1, 2025. (Screengrab / Fox Business)

Economist E.J. Antoni, who President Donald Trump nominated to take charge of the Labor Department before reversing course, erupted at Americans receiving federal food assistance on Monday on Fox Business, claiming that many of them chose to live in poverty.

There are around 42 million Americans – including 16 million children – who receive food assistance through the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, or SNAP, which, due to Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act, is projected to boot at least 2.4 million people from the program, though it may affect as many as 22.3 million families over 10 years.

And, while the program provides an average monthly benefit of just under $188 per month in food assistance, with strict income caps that average out across the nation at $20,352 per year, Antoni considered it a “moral issue” for many Americans to even receive said benefits.

“Yes, you can see how many of them are in poverty, have little to no income, but for many of these people, that’s a choice!” Antoni said. “That’s not right, and this is actually a moral issue, it’s not just economic; it is morally wrong that the taxpayer is footing the bill for people who can but are simply choosing not to work. That’s not right!”

Fox Business anchor Taylor Riggs jumped into the conversation as well, and recalled how her father – who she suggested owned a business – struggled to hire workers amid the COVID-19 pandemic due to offering low wages, or, at least, wages lower than what unemployment benefits offered, which the CARES Act bolstered with an additional $600 per week for three months, later lowered to $300 before ultimately expiring.

“I firmly believe in my blood [that] there is dignity in work. Work is a moral issue; it’s good for the soul, it’s good for families, it’s good for communities, it’s good for your work ethic, it’s good for kids to see moms and dads go off to work every day,” Riggs said.

“...We’re undergoing a shift in society right now where there are some younger workers who don’t want to work, maybe where there’s sort of this entitlement, and I’m hoping that that isn’t the case. Is that changing? Is this new generation thinking ‘nah, I feel entitled, I kind of want to sit at home and collect that paycheck, I’m not sure I want revenue, I think I want handouts!’

Our Analysis:

Moralizing Poverty: A Critique of E.J. Antoni and Fox Business Commentary

In a recent eruption of controversy, Economist E.J. Antoni, previously nominated by President Donald Trump to lead the Labor Department, and Fox Business anchor Taylor Riggs have offered opinions on federal food assistance that not only misrepresent the realities of poverty in America but also dangerously moralize economic hardship. Their remarks, steeped in misunderstanding and judgment, deserve a thorough dissection.

Antoni’s Assertion: Poverty as a Choice

E.J. Antoni’s claim that many Americans choose to live in poverty and thus, by implication, deserve their hardships, is not just an oversimplification but a gross distortion of socio-economic realities. The assertion that approximately 42 million Americans, including 16 million children, opt into poverty overlooks systemic issues, wage stagnation, and the rising cost of living. To suggest that receiving an average monthly benefit of just under $188 through the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) is somehow a lucrative or desirable lifestyle choice is both ludicrous and offensive.

Antoni frames this as a “moral issue,” insinuating that it is morally reprehensible for taxpayers to support individuals who, in his view, refuse to work. This stance ignores the structural barriers to employment many Americans face, including but not limited to, lack of accessible jobs, inadequate wages, and systemic discrimination. It’s not morality at play here; it’s moralizing – a dangerous simplification of complex issues.

Riggs’ Romanticization of Work

Taylor Riggs’ contribution to the discourse is equally problematic. Her anecdote about her father’s struggle to hire workers during the COVID-19 pandemic, due to unemployment benefits supposedly outpacing wages, not only highlights the issue of low wage offers but also inadvertently points out the inadequacy of the job market to meet basic living standards. Yet, Riggs romanticizes work as inherently dignified and morally necessary, a stance that dangerously ignores the reality that many jobs do not pay livable wages or offer dignified working conditions.

Further, her speculation about a generational shift in work ethic, suggesting that younger workers feel entitled to handouts rather than earning revenue, is baseless and disparaging. It neglects the genuine desires of younger generations for meaningful work that is both sustainable and fairly compensated. The problem isn’t a lack of desire to work; it’s a lack of dignified work that can actually support a person or a family.

Conclusion: The Real Moral Issue

The real moral issue here is not the supposed choice of poverty or the alleged decline in work ethic. It’s the willingness of individuals like Antoni and Riggs to blame the economically disadvantaged for their hardships while ignoring systemic failures. To suggest that those on SNAP are there by choice is to turn a blind eye to the economic realities many Americans face. It’s a narrative that serves to justify cuts to essential services and demonize those in need, all while ignoring the root causes of poverty.

The dignity of work cannot be separated from the dignity of fair pay and humane working conditions. As long as commentators like Antoni and Riggs fail to recognize this, their arguments remain not just flawed but morally and factually bankrupt. The discourse around poverty and work needs a drastic shift towards understanding and addressing the systemic barriers that perpetuate economic hardship, rather than moralizing those who are most affected by it.
