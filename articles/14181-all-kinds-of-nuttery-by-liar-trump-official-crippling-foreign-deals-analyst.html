Speaking with MSNBC host Ali Velshi, Rubin addressed Trump's flailing tariff war that has led to Wall Street jokes that "Trump Always Chickens Out" (TACO).

That turned the conversation to Lutnick, who has become a divisive figure –– even within the Trump White House.

"We are facing really an unprecedented voluntary diminishment of our policies and the rest of the world looks at us and thinks we're nuts," she told the host. "And to some extent, we are and that's because we have a president who is completely out of control, who is ignorant, and who is surrounded himself by nuts themselves."

"When you have people like Lutnick, who is now a synonymous term for liar and is propagating all kinds of nuttery and just absolute falsehoods about the economy, about trade, the rest of the world says, 'Okay, thanks, but no thanks. We're going to park our money elsewhere. We're going to try to carry on as best we can," she remarked while laughing.

'You know, I just came back from Western Europe," she added. "It's a good thing, frankly, that we have allies that we do. Because you see in Germany, you see in the U.K. you see in France that there are responsible pro-democracy leaders who are trying their best to hold down the fort vis-à-vis Russia and Ukraine."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Credibility Crisis in Trump's Trade Policy Team

In a recent MSNBC interview, anti-Trump conservative Jennifer Rubin pulled no punches in her assessment of the current state of the U.S.'s international trade relations, particularly under the guidance of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Rubin's critique paints a picture of a diplomatic and economic strategy in disarray, largely due to what she perceives as Lutnick's lack of credibility. Let's dissect the layers of this unfolding debacle.

The Lutnick Laughingstock

Rubin's assertion that Howard Lutnick is considered a joke on the international stage is a damning indictment of the Trump administration's trade policy leadership. It's not just that Lutnick is seen as an unreliable negotiator; Rubin suggests he is actively harming America's economic interests by driving away potential investment. This is a serious accusation, implying that Lutnick's actions—or perhaps more accurately, his missteps—are contributing to a broader perception of the U.S. as an unreliable partner. In the world of international trade, where trust and credibility are currency, this is equivalent to economic sabotage.

TACO: Trump's Retreat from Trade Wars

The article mentions the Wall Street joke acronym "TACO" (Trump Always Chickens Out), highlighting skepticism about Trump's resolve in his much-touted tariff wars. This skepticism isn't just a domestic sentiment; it reverberates on the global stage, further undermining U.S. credibility. Rubin's analysis suggests that Trump's erratic approach to trade, characterized by bold threats followed by sudden withdrawals, has left the U.S. in a weaker negotiating position. It's a classic case of crying wolf: engage in bluster often enough without follow-through, and soon nobody takes you seriously.

A Surrounding Cast of "Nuts"

Rubin doesn't stop at critiquing Lutnick; she extends her criticism to the entire Trump administration, describing the president as "surrounded himself by nuts themselves." This broadside attack on the administration's competence and sanity raises questions about the systemic issues within Trump's team. When key figures in the government are broadly perceived as untrustworthy or incompetent, it is not just a personal failing but a systemic risk to national and economic security.

The Silver Lining: European Allies

Despite Rubin's scathing critique of the Trump administration's trade and foreign policy, she notes a glimmer of hope in the form of European allies. Countries like Germany, the U.K., and France are described as "responsible pro-democracy leaders" who are working to maintain stability in the face of challenges like Russia and Ukraine. This acknowledgment serves as a reminder that, while the current U.S. administration may be faltering in Rubin's view, the broader international community continues to uphold the values of democracy and open trade.

Conclusion

Jennifer Rubin's analysis, as presented in the MSNBC interview, offers a bleak assessment of the Trump administration's handling of international trade. The portrayal of Howard Lutnick as a joke and a liar is a microcosm of a larger problem: a perceived lack of credibility and competence that threatens to isolate the U.S. economically and diplomatically. The humor of "TACO" belies a serious concern that the U.S. is failing to uphold its commitments and responsibilities on the world stage. While Rubin finds solace in the strength of America's allies, her analysis is a clarion call for a reevaluation of the current administration's trade strategies and personnel.
