'2026 will be a blood bath': MAGA says 'warning signs are flashing' after blowout losses

Republicans saw wall-to-wall losses in virtually every major election on Tuesday night, and many MAGA activists are starting to fear what next year’s midterm elections may bring.

GOP candidates for governor in New Jersey and Virginia lost to their Democratic opponents by substantial margins, with Democrat Abigail Spanberger defeating Republican Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears by roughly 15 percentage points in Virginia. Democrat Mikie Sherrill defeated Republican Jack Ciattarelli in New Jersey’s gubernatorial race by more than a dozen percentage points. In New York City, Democrat Zohran Mamdani defeated former Gov. Andrew Cuomo (who ran as an independent after losing to Mamdani in the Democratic primary) by more than nine points. And in California, Prop 50 — the measure allowing for new congressional redistricting maps favorable to Democrats to temporarily replace the current maps until the 2030 Census — passed overwhelmingly despite a flood of money from GOP donors against the initiative.

In a series of posts to his Truth Social account, President Donald Trump lamented that because he wasn’t on the ballot, his party was unable to get a win. He also urged Senate Republicans to “TERMINATE THE FILIBUSTER“ and pass legislation making it harder for Americans to vote.

“Pass Voter Reform, Voter ID, No Mail-In Ballots. Save our Supreme Court from ‘Packing,’ No Two State addition, etc,” Trump wrote in a subsequent post.

MAGA’s reactions to 2025 election results suggested a dismal mood among Trump’s supporters in response to the GOP’s nationwide defeat. Turning Point USA executive Tyler Bowyer shared Trump’s frustrations about him not being on the ballot, but also chastised Republican leadership for not doing the ground work to be more competitive.

“When Trump isn’t on the ballot and you don’t make investments in chasing votes, you get voters who stay home,” Bowyer wrote.

Former Trump campaign chairman and White House chief strategist Steve Bannon told his podcast viewers that Republicans should take Tuesday night’s losses to heart, saying: “The midterms start tonight, and the warning signs are flashing.” He observed that “Democrats just flipped two Georgia commissioners,” marking “their first statewide wins in 30 years.”

Far-right influencer Mike Cernovich (who spawned the “Pizzagate” conspiracy theory) tweeted: “Ted Cruz and Mark Levin are walking Trump into impeachments and then prison. 2026 will be a blood bath.” MAGA podcaster Jack Posobiec likewise warned that “2026 will be worse if we don’t course-correct.”

“Lame duck status is going to come even faster now,” wrote conservative commentator Erick Erickson. Trump cannot turn out the vote unless he is on the ballot, and that is never happening again.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Night of Discontent: A Postmortem on the 2025 Elections

In what can only be described as a spectacular series of defeats, the Republican Party faced wall-to-wall losses in the 2025 elections, causing a seismic shift in the political landscape and igniting a firestorm of fear and speculation among MAGA activists about the impending 2026 midterm elections. The GOP’s gubernatorial candidates in New Jersey and Virginia were trounced by their Democratic opponents by substantial margins, marking a clear rejection of the MAGA agenda by the electorate.

The Stinging Defeats

Democrat Abigail Spanberger defeated Republican Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears in Virginia by a breathtaking 15 percentage points, while Democrat Mikie Sherrill handed a similar defeat to Republican Jack Ciattarelli in New Jersey. These are not just losses; they are resounding rebukes of the Republican candidates and, by extension, the MAGA movement that has gripped the GOP.

In New York City, Democrat Zohran Mamdani triumphed over former Gov. Andrew Cuomo — who attempted a political resurrection as an independent — by more than nine points. This particular race underscores the electorate’s fatigue with political dynasties and the craving for fresh, progressive leadership.

Moreover, in California, the passage of Prop 50 is a testament to the voters’ desire to ensure fair representation, despite the GOP’s substantial financial efforts to skew the process. This initiative, allowing for new congressional redistricting maps favorable to Democrats, passed overwhelmingly, showcasing a populace ready to fight against gerrymandering.

Trump’s Lament and the MAGA Meltdown

In a bizarre twist, former President Donald Trump took to Truth Social to vent his frustrations, bizarrely attributing the GOP’s failure to his absence on the ballot and calling for drastic measures that would construe a direct assault on democratic principles, such as making it harder for Americans to vote. Trump’s calls to “TERMINATE THE FILIBUSTER” and enforce restrictive voting laws like “No Mail-In Ballots” are not just anti-democratic; they’re a desperate cry from a figure watching his influence wane.

MAGA influencers and operatives, from Tyler Bowyer to Steve Bannon, echoed this sentiment of doom, each adding their own spin to the narrative of decline. Bannon’s declaration that “the midterms start tonight” amidst “warning signs flashing” is particularly telling, encapsulating the panic within the MAGA ranks at the prospect of continued electoral irrelevance.

A Republican Reckoning

What we’re witnessing is not just a series of electoral defeats but a crisis of identity within the Republican Party. The electorate’s rejection signals a profound dissatisfaction with the MAGA movement’s stranglehold on the GOP, urging a return to principles over personalities.

Erick Erickson’s commentary that “Trump cannot turn out the vote unless he is on the ballot, and that is never happening again” is a stark reminder of the GOP’s existential dilemma. It’s not just about Trump’s electoral toxicity; it’s about the sustainability of a party that has increasingly alienated moderate and independent voters by kowtowing to its most extreme elements.

Moving Forward

The 2025 elections serve as a wake-up call for the Republican Party. The path forward requires a serious introspection and a willingness to divorce itself from the cult of personality that has led it astray. The GOP must champion policies that resonate with the broader electorate and embrace a more inclusive and democratic approach to governance.

The MAGA movement’s reaction to these defeats, characterized by denial and calls for more extreme measures, only serves to highlight the urgent need for a course correction. Failure to do so could indeed make 2026 a “bloodbath” for the GOP, not because of external forces, but due to its own systemic errors and refusal to adapt to a changing America.
