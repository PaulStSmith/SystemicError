CNN anchor Audie Cornish played a devastating supercut of footage showing President Donald Trump mocking former president Joe Biden for looking drowsy at public events, followed by Tuesday’s clips of Trump himself appearing to nod off at an intense cabinet briefing covering potential war crimes by the Trump administration.

“So, the president said a lot during Tuesday’s cabinet meeting at the White House, but there were plenty of moments where he wasn’t saying anything at all,” Cornish told a CNN panel of contributors. “In fact, while his secretaries went around the table, the 79-year-old president might have looked to some as though he may have dozed off a few times, eyes closed, head nodding down. … So why are we talking about this? Well, it’s something that Trump himself made a central issue on the campaign trail a year ago.”

“He spends a day and then comes back,” Trump said last year at a campaign stop. “He falls asleep at every single event.”

“They don’t target Joe on the beach as he sleeps. He sleeps. How do you fall asleep when cameras are raging?” Trump later said.

“I wish I could do that. Sleep with the cameras [going],” he claimed at yet another campaign event.

“The White House says what you’re seeing here is the president listening attentively while running a marathon three-hour cabinet,” said Cornish, but Argument Founder Jerusalem Demsas said she wasn’t buying it.

“Obviously there’s like a level of hypocrisy here about, you know, his own ability to remain really alert and awake, as in performing his duties,” said Demsas. “… But I do think there’s a question here about why it stuck so much with Biden when it doesn’t seem to stick with Trump. There’s a there’s a lot of frustration among Democrats about why isn’t this sort of thing sticking with Trump when it’s stuck with Biden?”

“I think the reason it doesn’t stick is because Democrats don’t have a messenger who is calling him ‘Sleepy Don,’ over and over and over again,” said another panelist. “The branding is not there and they are not as ruthless about making and pushing the criticism.”

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Trump’s Drowsiness: A Political Irony

In a recent segment that can only be described as deliciously ironic, CNN anchor Audie Cornish presented a stunning juxtaposition of President Donald Trump’s past mockery of Joe Biden’s supposed drowsiness against his own recent nod-off during a critical cabinet briefing on potential war crimes by his administration.

Trump, who once reveled in the nickname “Sleepy Joe” for Biden, seems to have fallen into his own trap of drowsiness. This not only showcases a stunning level of hypocrisy but also highlights the performative nature of his criticisms.

The Art of Projection

Trump’s campaign was rife with attacks on Biden’s stamina and alertness, painting him as unfit for the presidency due to moments of apparent tiredness. Yet, here we are, witnessing Trump himself apparently struggling to stay awake during a meeting of grave importance. This isn’t just a case of “do as I say, not as I do.” It’s a glaring testament to Trump’s propensity for projection — accusing others of flaws he himself possesses.

The Double Standard

What’s particularly galling is the double standard at play. Trump’s somnolent episode during a cabinet briefing on potential war crimes should be alarming, not just for the lack of alertness but for the gravity of the topic at hand. Yet, the reaction seems muted compared to the uproar he incited over Biden’s similar moments. This disparity begs the question: why does Trump seem to Teflon-coat his way out of situations that would typically ensnare others?

CNN contributor Jerusalem Demsas hits the nail on the head by pointing out the hypocrisy in Trump’s ability to remain “alert and awake” despite his previous criticisms of Biden. The frustration among Democrats, as Demsas notes, stems from a lack of effective counter-branding against Trump, a point echoed by another panelist who lamented the absence of a “Sleepy Don” moniker.

The Brutal Reality of Political Branding

This scenario reveals an uncomfortable truth about political discourse in America: it’s less about substance and more about branding. Trump’s Teflon nature isn’t just about his personality but also about how he’s been marketed. The fact that Trump’s drowsiness hasn’t become a scandal of “Biden proportions” speaks volumes about the effectiveness of his self-crafted image and the failure of Democrats to counter it effectively.

Conclusion

The irony of Trump’s drowsiness debacle is a stark reminder of the hypocrisy and double standards that pervade American politics. It’s not just a question of alertness; it’s a matter of accountability. As long as politicians like Trump can deflect criticism and reframe narratives to their advantage, they will continue to escape the consequences of their actions, no matter how contradictory or hypocritical. This incident should serve as a wake-up call, pun intended, for those in the political arena to strive for consistency, accountability, and genuine leadership.
