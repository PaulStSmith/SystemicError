'Their careers are over': Ex-GOP operative demands targeted 'purge' after Trump's gone

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

ICE Is Not Broken. It Is Doing What Power Built It To Do

The Source of the Alarm

Steve Schmidt, a former Republican operative, said the quiet part loudly: ICE is “irredeemable,” and any serious Democratic president should treat it that way. He tied that judgment to two recent killings by ICE agents in Texas and Maine, to rushed 47-day Trump-era training, and to a chain of officials who allegedly pulled FBI investigators away from shooting probes.

That is the basic fact pattern. The more important question is what it reveals about the machinery behind the violence.

Who Has Power

ICE does not drift into brutality by accident. It is a federal force inside the Department of Homeland Security, armed by the state, staffed by people given badges and weapons, and shielded by layers of bureaucracy when accountability becomes inconvenient. The agency’s power comes from exactly that arrangement: diffuse enough to evade blame, concentrated enough to inflict harm.

Schmidt’s call for a “purge” is blunt, but the underlying point is simpler. The people who authorize, staff, and protect these institutions are not confused bystanders. They are decision-makers. If the training was rushed, someone approved it. If investigators were pulled back, someone ordered it. If the chain of custody for those memos matters, that is because power moved through it on purpose.

The Lie of Isolated Bad Apples

The standard institutional defense is already visible in the source: treat the killings as aberrations, isolate the agents, preserve the structure. That is the usual laundering operation. The agency gets a few sacrificial names, the administration gets to plead complexity, and the machinery stays intact.

But the story does not describe one rogue officer or an accidental failure of supervision. It describes an agency that can be handed weapons after 47 days of training, then defended by lawyers and managers when lethal outcomes follow. That is not a glitch. That is an operating model. When a state force is built for punishment first and accountability second, “oversight” becomes branding, not restraint.

DHS as a Shield

Schmidt’s strongest point is not the rhetoric about individual agents. It is the demand to break up the Department of Homeland Security and abolish ICE. That cuts through the usual cowardice around “reform,” which too often means preserving the institution while polishing its public image.

DHS has long functioned as a shield for coercive agencies that want the authority of law and the insulation of bureaucracy. If the parent structure exists to absorb scandal and dilute responsibility, then changing a few personnel files is cosmetic work. Abolishing ICE is not symbolic excess. It is the only response proportionate to an agency whose violence is structural, not episodic.

Democrats Cannot Tiptoe Around This

Schmidt framed ICE as a threshold issue for anyone thinking of running as a Democrat. He is right, and the bar should be higher than he stated. Democrats do not need a messaging refresh on ICE. They need to stop treating a deportation apparatus with a blood trail as a manageable policy dispute.

The political danger is not merely that Republicans defend cruelty. It is that Democrats sometimes answer institutional brutality with managerial language, as if better training or tighter procedure can civilize a system built to terrorize vulnerable people and then bury responsibility in paperwork. That is how democratic politics becomes complicit in its own dilution.

The Pattern

This story is about more than ICE. It is about what happens when the state normalizes coercion, then asks the public to mistake discipline for lawfulness. It is about how institutions create distance between decision and consequence so nobody with power has to own the result. And it is about how “reform” is often the preferred vocabulary of people who want the harm to continue at a more respectable volume.

The answer here is not to humanize ICE. It is to end it.
