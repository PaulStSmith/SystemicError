'Get us out of this!' Fed-up Trump ally now among his sharpest critics

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 15, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The MAGA Rift: Greene vs. Trump

REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

One of President Donald Trump‘s most loyal MAGA allies is now among his sharpest critics.

Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) has called out Trump and questioned whether he is still an “America First” president, MSNBC reported Friday in an exclusive interview with the Republican lawmaker.

Trump this week blasted Greene, saying, “She’s lost her way, I think.”

Greene hit back at the president’s comments, retorting: “I’m America First, America Only. Hardcore.”

The contention between the two stems from whether Trump’s presidency and focus are still on “the populist message that powered his rise. And it reflects a MAGA movement preparing for a future without Trump at the top of the ticket, with the next generation of leaders figuring out where to take the base he built.”

Greene has been critical of Trump’s attention to foreign affairs, namely his interest in ongoing conflicts, instead of looking at affordability issues in the U.S., including the high cost of living and the housing market.

“We didn’t elect the president to go out there and travel the world and end the foreign wars,” Greene told the outlet. “We elected the president to stop sending tax dollars and weapons for the foreign wars — to completely not engage anymore. Watching the foreign leaders come to the White House through a revolving door is not helping Americans.”

“One of the big campaign issues is Americans were fed up with foreign wars,” Greene said. “It’s like, get us out of this.”

Republican consultant Ryan Girdusky argued that it wasn’t a shock that Trump has tried to secure his legacy on a worldwide stage.

“When presidents don’t have to run again, they do a lot of foreign policy trips,” said Girdusky, who helped run a pro-JD Vance super PAC in the 2022 Ohio Senate election. “They do a lot of things for the legacy. And Trump’s Middle East stuff is probably the most important of any president since Nixon.”

Greene, who said she does not plan to run for president in 2028, has apparently not spoken with Trump to discuss the rift and added that her point of view is unmoved, adding “100% haven’t changed.”

She has pointed to concerns among everyday Americans, including struggles her own three adult children in their 20s are facing. Greene described “what she views as a generation facing diminishing prospects.”

“They don’t think they’re ever really going to be able to buy a home,” Greene said. “They were promised, you go to college, you’re going to get a great job. That doesn’t exist. That’s not reality.”

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Rift: Greene vs. Trump

In what seems like a dramatic turn of events, Marjorie Taylor Greene, a figure once synonymous with unwavering loyalty to Donald Trump and his MAGA doctrine, has launched into a public critique of the former president. According to a recent MSNBC report, Greene has openly questioned Trump’s adherence to his “America First” mantra, setting the stage for a significant rift within the MAGA movement. This discord underscores a crucial juncture for the movement, hinting at a future potentially devoid of Trump’s direct leadership.

Trump’s Alleged Departure from “America First”

Greene’s primary contention with Trump appears to revolve around his focus on global diplomacy and foreign affairs, which she views as a betrayal of the core “America First” values. Her criticism isn’t just a simple disagreement over policy priorities; it’s a fundamental challenge to Trump’s legacy and his current standing within the movement he helped forge. By stating she is “America First, America Only. Hardcore,” Greene positions herself as a purist, perhaps in an attempt to realign the movement’s trajectory or even to claim its leadership mantle.

A Future Without Trump?

The idea that the MAGA movement is “preparing for a future without Trump” is both intriguing and somewhat baffling. Trump, after all, has been the linchpin of the MAGA ideology. The notion that figures like Greene are beginning to envision, and indeed shape, a post-Trump MAGA world speaks volumes about the current undercurrents of dissatisfaction and the desire for a renewed focus on domestic issues.

“Americans Were Fed Up with Foreign Wars”

Greene’s critique of Trump’s foreign policy endeavors, particularly his engagement with global leaders and conflicts, taps into a sentiment that was pivotal to Trump’s initial ascendancy—war weariness and a desire for isolationism. Her remarks highlight a perceived shift away from these values, and by extension, from the needs and desires of the American electorate. This is a potent narrative, especially when paired with the struggles faced by everyday Americans, including her own adult children, in areas such as housing affordability and job prospects.

The Legacy Question

Ryan Girdusky’s comment that presidents in their latter years focus on foreign policy to cement their legacy is telling. It frames Trump’s actions as not just a deviation from “America First” but as a standard playbook maneuver. However, for MAGA purists like Greene, legacy-building exercises that detract from immediate domestic concerns are seen as unpatriotic diversions.

Conclusion: The MAGA Conundrum

Greene’s public spat with Trump is more than just political theater; it’s a manifestation of the ideological strains within the MAGA movement. As Trump seeks to secure his global legacy, figures like Greene push back, demanding a recommitment to the insular, domestic-focused principles that propelled Trump to the presidency. This rift is emblematic of a broader identity crisis within MAGA. It poses critical questions about its future direction and leadership, and more importantly, what it means to be “America First” in a post-Trump era.

As the MAGA movement grapples with these questions, the answers may well determine not just its survival, but its evolution. Will it remain tethered to Trump and his personal brand of politics, or will it morph into something new, perhaps something even more radical and uncompromisingly nationalist? Only time will tell, but the Greene-Trump rift is a clear sign that the MAGA movement is at a crossroads, with its very identity at stake.
