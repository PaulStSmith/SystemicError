The Conservative Myth of Campus Indoctrination

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 5, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Nature of Youth and ProgressThe Conservative Fear of IdeasThe Peer Effect: Not Indoctrination, But EvolutionConservatism’s Terminal DecayThe Real IndoctrinationThe Fear of TomorrowConclusion: The Backward Gaze

It’s one of the most exhausted talking points in the right-wing arsenal: that universities are leftist indoctrination camps, brainwashing America’s youth into Marxist obedience under the watchful eye of latte-sipping professors. Charlie Kirk and his Turning Point USA disciples have built entire careers around this narrative, portraying every philosophy seminar as a gulag for Republican thought. It’s nonsense — both historically and sociologically — but more importantly, it’s projection.

Let’s get something out of the way first: conservatism is, by definition, retrograde. The conservative impulse looks backward, longing for a status quo that enshrines existing hierarchies. It resists movement, change, and challenge because its power depends on the world staying precisely as it is — or reverting to some imagined golden past. The young, by contrast, look forward. They see systems decaying, rights eroding, and the planet literally burning, and they ask the most subversive question possible: Why? That question — not Marx, not Foucault, not some cabal of sociology professors — is what terrifies conservatives.

The Nature of Youth and Progress

Every generation of young people, since long before the Enlightenment — since at least Socrates’ day — has been accused of rebellion or moral decay by its elders. The Founding Fathers themselves, as university students, were condemned as radicals for daring to imagine a republic free from monarchy. The youth of the 1960s were labeled communists for opposing segregation and the Vietnam War. Even the suffragists and abolitionists of the 19th century were accused of being brainwashed, hysterical, and unpatriotic. Every movement that bent the moral arc forward started with a young generation refusing to inherit their parents’ blind obedience.

And this is the core of it: young people are not “indoctrinated” into progressivism — they arrive at it organically, through exposure to ideas, diversity, and the inescapable contradictions of conservative reality. A student growing up in a homogeneous town, isolated from difference, may encounter in college the first queer person, the first immigrant, the first Black professor. Suddenly, the simple narratives fed to them by Fox News and family dinner tables unravel. That’s not indoctrination. That’s education doing its job.

The Conservative Fear of Ideas

The conservative movement has no intellectual response to this because it’s not an intellectual movement anymore. Its engine runs on resentment. The modern right depends on anti-intellectualism — on fear of expertise, on suspicion of evidence, on hatred of nuance. When young people enter spaces where evidence matters and complexity is rewarded, conservatism loses its grip. To the reactionary mind, this feels like brainwashing. To the rest of us, it’s called learning.

That’s why conservatives attack universities, libraries, and teachers. It’s not about “balance.” It’s about control. Authoritarians throughout history have understood that education is the first battlefield. The Nazis burned books. McCarthy hunted professors. DeSantis censors curriculums. The pattern is as old as fascism itself: when your ideology can’t survive critical thought, you ban the thinkers.

Charlie Kirk’s “indoctrination” myth flips this dynamic on its head. He imagines universities as factories producing socialism, while ignoring that the only institutions actively purging dissenting thought are run by his side — from the Texas school boards rewriting history to Florida’s political takeover of New College. The right accuses the left of what it cannot help but do itself: suppress, sanitize, and sterilize the exchange of ideas.

The Peer Effect: Not Indoctrination, But Evolution

This Quora respondent nailed it: it’s not professors who shape young minds most strongly, it’s peers. Humans are social creatures, and young adults are still calibrating their moral compass. College is where they discover empathy not as a concept but as a lived experience. They meet people outside their echo chambers, and the world expands. Of course they shift left — not because they’re being told what to think, but because they’re finally thinking beyond the narrow confines of their upbringing.

The right can’t compete with that. Its worldview requires isolation. It thrives in information silos where fear of the “other” festers. But in a dorm room at 2 a.m., when a conservative freshman listens to a trans roommate describe discrimination, or a classmate from Gaza talks about war, or a woman recounts an abortion story, something changes. That’s not brainwashing. That’s humanity breaking through propaganda.

Conservatism’s Terminal Decay

There’s another reason young people abandon conservative ideology: they see where it leads. They watch the climate denial, the book bans, the cruelty toward migrants, the obsession with controlling women’s bodies, the corporate servitude. They see the hypocrisy — the family values crowd cheering for an adulterous felon, the law-and-order party rioting to overturn an election. You don’t need a lecture in critical theory to realize that rot runs deep.

Conservatism, as it exists today, offers no vision for the future. It offers fear. It offers grievance. It offers the promise that yesterday was better — for someone, somewhere, usually someone white, straight, and male. That message doesn’t resonate with a generation that’s more diverse, more connected, and more aware of systemic injustice than any in history. You can’t sell “make America great again” to kids who’ve only seen mass shootings, melting glaciers, and billionaires rocketing themselves into space while workers starve.

The Real Indoctrination

If we’re being honest, the only large-scale indoctrination project in America isn’t happening in universities; it’s happening on cable news and social media. Fox News, Daily Wire, PragerU, Turning Point — these are propaganda machines built to weaponize ignorance. They repeat simple, emotionally charged lies until their audience mistakes outrage for understanding. They thrive on the insecurity of people who feel the world moving without them and need someone to blame.

That’s the cruel irony: the people screaming about “indoctrination” are the ones doing it. They don’t want critical thinkers; they want obedient soldiers. They don’t want students learning history; they want them parroting slogans. They don’t want education; they want conditioning. And because universities, for all their flaws, still represent spaces where ideas clash freely, the right must destroy their credibility.

The Fear of Tomorrow

In the end, this hysteria about leftist campuses isn’t about students — it’s about power. The old guard knows that the future doesn’t belong to them. Every new generation that learns to question authority is a nail in the coffin of reactionary politics. Conservatives mistake the natural evolution of moral progress for conspiracy because they cannot conceive of a world where their dominance fades without someone orchestrating it.

But time itself is the great radicalizer. Exposure to truth, diversity, and lived experience turns idealism into empathy and empathy into action. That’s not indoctrination — that’s growth. And it’s unstoppable.

Conclusion: The Backward Gaze

Charlie Kirk and his ilk look at universities and see the enemy because they see mirrors — reflections of a world moving on without them. The young don’t want to conserve a dying hierarchy; they want to build something better. Conservatism cannot inspire them because it refuses to imagine tomorrow. It’s too busy embalming yesterday.

So the next time someone rants about “leftist indoctrination,” remember this: the only thing universities are truly guilty of is teaching people to think — and once people start thinking, they rarely stop. That’s what scares them. That’s why they lash out. And that’s why, no matter how many books they ban or professors they demonize, the march of progress continues.

Because the future has always belonged to those who dare to ask, “how can I make it better?”
