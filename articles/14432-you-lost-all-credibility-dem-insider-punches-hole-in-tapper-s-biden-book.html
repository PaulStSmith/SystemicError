'You lost all credibility': Dem insider punches hole in Tapper's Biden book

Jake Tapper and Jennifer Marie Brown pose on the red carpet upon arrival for the annual White House Correspondents’ Association (WHCA) Dinner in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 26, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

During an appearance on MSNBC on Sunday morning, former Democratic National Committee Chair and Jamie Harrison disputed comments attributed to him in CNN host Jake Tapper and Axios reporter Alex Thompson's controversial book about former President Joe Biden.

After discussing the internal warfare currently in play within the DNC, "The Weekend" co-host Eugene Daniels asked Harrison about the book "Original Sin," and noted that Thompson appeared on the show on Saturday.

That was when the ex-DNC chair went off.

"It says that you had an interaction with President Biden where he didn't recognize you and you said to some other Democrat that wasn't good, right? I heard that you said this didn't happen, so did it happen or not?" Daniels prompted his guest.

"It was a bald-faced lie," Harrison shot back. "I don't know where the hell they got that from and I've told that to Alex. That is a bald-faced lie, and it frankly is pissing me off, excuse my language, that they continue to push it and say it."

"I have told them –– you know, unless I need to get a cognitive test, and I just totally forget all these things, but I'm, you know, I'm 49, but maybe I'll go to my doctor and get a check-up," he continued. "So for me it provides me all of what I need to know about what this book is about."

"I've seen similar with [Biden adviser] Jake Sullivan saying that the situation that they talk about him did not happen.," he added before recalling, "I know in terms of the [George] Clooney situation, I mean, there are some things that I can also share about that Clooney situation. You know, that part where they show Barack Obama holding on to Joe Biden's arm and leading him off right in front of us? I went to my wife when I saw that that or heard about that in the book, and I said, 'Wasn't that where those three young, it was three young white men who was standing up yelling at Joe Biden about Palestine."

"They were protesting right up front, and he was trying to listen to what they were saying and Obama grabbed his arm and pulled him away," he added. "My wife said, 'Yeah, I remember that, we were sitting right there in the audience.'"

"But that book lost all credibility when they put my name in it for something that did not happen," he accused. "And then they continue to double down on that particular story."

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of Media Narratives and the Truth

In the recent whirlwind surrounding the release of Jake Tapper and Alex Thompson's book, Original Sin, concerning former President Joe Biden, the claims and counterclaims have reached a fever pitch. Notably, an instance involving Jamie Harrison, former Democratic National Committee Chair, has become a focal point of controversy. Harrison's vehement denial of an anecdote presented in the book, during his appearance on MSNBC, sheds light on the precarious balance between journalistic freedom and the responsibility towards factual accuracy. Let’s dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned cynic.

The Alleged Incident: A "Bald-Faced Lie"?

Harrison's outright dismissal of the book's account as a "bald-faced lie" is not just a denial; it's a frontal assault on the credibility of the authors and, by extension, the media outlets that peddle what he implies are fabrications. The gravity of his accusation cannot be understated. If what Harrison says holds water, we're not just talking about a minor slip-up; we're witnessing an egregious breach of journalistic ethics. But let's not leap to conclusions without examining the broader picture.

The Media's Role in Political Narratives

This scenario underscores a much larger issue at play: the role of media in shaping political narratives. Books like Original Sin are powerful tools that can influence public perception significantly. Yet, when they include potentially unfounded anecdotes, they risk undermining their integrity and, worse, misleading the public. It's critical to ask: Where is the line between reporting and sensationalism?

The Importance of Accountability

Harrison's challenge to the credibility of the book serves as a stark reminder of the importance of accountability in journalism. His readiness to undergo a cognitive test to disprove the claims is a dramatic gesture that emphasizes his stance. Moreover, his reference to other disputed anecdotes in the book, including an incident involving Jake Sullivan and a misrepresented event with George Clooney and Barack Obama, paints a picture of a publication potentially rife with inaccuracies. If these allegations of falsehood are accurate, the journalistic integrity of the authors, and the outlets that echo their claims, is in serious jeopardy.

What's at Stake

Beyond the reputations of individuals and media entities, what's truly at stake here is the public's trust in the media. In an era where the term "fake news" is thrown around with alarming casualty, incidents like this serve only to deepen public skepticism and erode trust. If journalists and authors are seen as cavalier with the truth, the very foundation of informed democracy is threatened.

Conclusion: A Call for Rigorous Scrutiny

In closing, while Harrison's vehement denial and the ensuing controversy might seem like just another day in the news cycle, they represent a critical juncture in the ongoing struggle for truth in media. It's incumbent upon journalists, authors, and media outlets to adhere to the highest standards of accuracy and integrity. Conversely, it's equally important for the public to approach such narratives with a critical eye, recognizing that the truth is often more nuanced than sensational headlines might suggest.

As we navigate these turbulent waters, let us be guided by a commitment to truth, a dedication to rigorous scrutiny, and an unwavering demand for accountability. Only then can we hope to maintain the integrity of our political discourse and the trust of the public we aim to serve.
