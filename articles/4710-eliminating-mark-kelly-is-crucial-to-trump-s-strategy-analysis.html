Eliminating Mark Kelly is crucial to Trump's strategy: analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 2, 20265 min read

U.S. Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ) speaks with members on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

MS NOW editor and writer Zeeshan Aleem says there’s a reason President Donald Trump and his persistent defense secretary can’t let Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) speak in peace without retaliation.

Trump was gravely injured by the decorated veteran’s decision to participate in a video with five other Democratic lawmakers reminding service members of their duty to disobey illegal orders. So much so that Trump’s defense secretary is now appealing a judge’s order telling the administration to back off from censuring Kelly. Trump’s people are also pursuing a reduction of Kelly’s retirement rank.

“Even though it’s true that service members swear to protect the Constitution and are only required to follow lawful orders, and even though their remarks are constitutionally protected free speech, the Trump administration responded hysterically. President Donald Trump absurdly declared the video an act of ‘sedition’ that should be ‘punishable by death.’”

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s determination “to continue fighting Kelly in court shows how fixated he is on suppressing free speech and punishing dissent against the Pentagon,” said Aleem. “His doggedness also illustrates the Trump administration’s determination to reconceptualize the military as a politicized fighting force that shouldn’t be bound by the law.”

Aleem said Trump doesn’t want a military that honors the Constitution and follows the proper chain of command with a sense of ethics. What Trump and his administration lieutenants want is a personal fighting force stretching from coast to national coast. To do that, Lt. Col. Rachel VanLandingham told MS NOW earlier that Trump needs to be able to strike just as much fear into retired veterans as active duty members.

“Hegseth’s position is that he can treat military retirees the same as active service members — who do face more stringent restrictions on their speech while serving in the military, in part to ensure the military’s need for obedience to commands,” said Aleem. The problem for Trump, however, is that courts do not impose the same First Amendment restrictions to military retirees that they impose on active members.

Despite this, the administration simply must make Kelly’s life more difficult “and rob him of his pension because he dared to question the Trump administration,”said Aleem. “… Hegseth’s vendetta against Kelly telegraphs a security vision that demands fascistic deference to political leaders. Ultimately, Hegseth’s fury that lawmakers encouraged service members to disobey illegal orders gives away the game: Why would a man who swore to support and defend the Constitution of the United States be so angry that troops are reminded that they ought to do the same?”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Trump Administration’s Clash with Senator Mark Kelly

In a recent piece by MS NOW editor and writer Zeeshan Aleem, a glaring spotlight is cast on the Trump administration’s aggressive stance against Senator Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.), a decorated veteran who dared to remind service members of their constitutional duty. This confrontation isn’t just a petty political squabble; it’s a disturbing illustration of how far the Trump administration is willing to go in its quest to reshape the military into a personal arm of power, unconstrained by legal and ethical norms.

Trump’s Absurd Accusations

The crux of the conflict lies in a video featuring Kelly and five other Democratic lawmakers, which led to Trump accusing Kelly of “sedition” and calling for his punishment by death. This hysterical response to a message advocating for the adherence to lawful orders and the Constitution is not just an overreaction; it’s a dangerous attempt to silence dissent. The idea that reminding service members of their oath constitutes sedition is not only absurd but fundamentally anti-democratic.

The Administration’s Misguided Vendetta

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s relentless pursuit of Kelly, including efforts to downgrade his retirement rank and challenge his right to free speech, is emblematic of a broader, more sinister agenda. Hegseth, acting as Trump’s loyal enforcer, is not just attacking an individual; he’s attacking the very principles that underpin military ethics and constitutional fidelity. This vendetta isn’t about maintaining discipline; it’s about enforcing a fascistic deference to political leaders, regardless of the law or morality.

The Misconception of Military Loyalty

The administration’s actions betray a fundamental misunderstanding, or perhaps a willful ignorance, of military loyalty and duty. The military swears to protect the Constitution, not to serve the whims of any particular leader. Attempting to coerce retired veterans and active-duty members into a form of politicized submission not only undermines the military’s integrity but also threatens the democratic fabric of the nation.

A Dangerous Vision of Power

What emerges from Aleem’s analysis is a portrait of a presidency obsessed with loyalty to the extent of desiring a personal fighting force. This vision of power, unbound by ethical considerations or constitutional limits, is deeply troubling. Lt. Col. Rachel VanLandingham’s insights to MS NOW emphasize the peril in Trump’s desire to instill fear not just in active service members but also in veterans who have served their country with honor.

Facing the Constitutional Reality

The courts have thus far rejected the Trump administration’s attempts to impose undue restrictions on military retirees’ speech, recognizing the fundamental rights guaranteed by the First Amendment. This legal resistance is a vital check on the administration’s overreach, but the fact that such attempts are made in the first place is alarming.

Conclusion: A Call to Defend Democratic Values

Aleem’s piece isn’t just an exposé on one administration’s overreach; it’s a warning about the fragility of democratic institutions and principles under the strain of authoritarian impulses. Hegseth’s fury and the administration’s punitive measures against Kelly reveal a contempt for the very idea of lawful dissent and a constitutional order. It’s a stark reminder that the battle for democracy often starts from within, with the defense of the principles that define and protect it. The Trump administration’s campaign against Mark Kelly isn’t just an attack on a senator; it’s an assault on the bedrock of American democracy.
