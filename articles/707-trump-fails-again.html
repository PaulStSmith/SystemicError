Trump fails again

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Trump’s “Victory” Is Just a Costly Return to the Starting Line

A Deal That Solves Nothing

Trump is selling an outcome as triumph after starting a war that, by the source’s account, produced a memo of understanding rather than a real settlement. It stops the fighting and reopens the Strait of Hormuz, but it does not address the nuclear program that Trump used to justify the conflict in the first place. That is not victory. It is a pause after wreckage, packaged as achievement.

Who Had the Power

The actual institutional power sat with Trump. He revoked the Obama-era deal in 2018, initiated the bombing, and now gets to narrate the result as success. That is the core asymmetry here: one actor broke the prior constraint, chose escalation, and then claimed credit when the damage was partially reversed. The source is right to frame this as a failure of decision-making, not an unavoidable muddle.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The rhetoric of “victory” does what political spin always does: it shifts attention away from responsibility and toward spectacle. The source makes clear that the Strait of Hormuz was open before Trump started bombing, and that Iran had already accepted limits on nuclear-grade material under the Obama deal. So the supposed win is largely restoration of conditions that existed before Trump created the crisis. Calling that accomplishment is propaganda with better lighting.

The Real Consequences

The costs are concrete. The source says Iran is now under a more extremist regime than before the war. Oil prices have spiked. The United States has become more dependent on fossil fuels again. Vladimir Putin’s regime has benefited from higher oil prices. The war has cost the United States an estimated $90 billion, caused widespread suffering across the Middle East, and left Israel more precarious. These are not abstract geopolitical ripples. They are the bill.

Netanyahu, War, and the Blame Game

The source also notes that Benjamin Netanyahu is not a party to the agreement and did not approve it, even though Israel is now in a worse position. That matters because it exposes how war propaganda launders responsibility across multiple actors while shielding the one who actually made the decisive call. Trump launched the war. Trump owns the escalation. Any attempt to disperse blame into a fog of regional complexity is a dodge.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

This is the familiar architecture of authoritarian politics: break the system, inflict damage, then declare success when the damage becomes too expensive to ignore. The public is handed a slogan; the powerful keep their leverage; the rest absorb the consequences. A “victory” that merely restores the status quo after catastrophe is not a victory at all. It is a confession that power can manufacture disorder, rename retreat, and still demand applause.
