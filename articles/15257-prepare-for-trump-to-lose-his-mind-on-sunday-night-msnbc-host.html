Following a debate on MSNBC's "The Weekend" on whether former Vice President Mike Pence deserves to be the recipient of the John F. Kennedy Profile in Courage Award for his actions on Jan. 6th, co-host Jonathan Capehart stated he was more interested in how Donald Trump will react.

Pence will receive the award for “putting his life and career on the line” when he oversaw the certification of the 2020 presidential election results despite threats and pressure from Trump combined with an assault on the Capitol that forced lawmakers from both parties to flee.

Noting the award will be presented at the John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum Sunday night in Boston, co-host Elise Jordan defended Pence, explaining, "I think the award is a good move just because it shows grace for anything –– what happened in the past, I'm not going to say I agreed with everything Mike Pence did as vice president certainly –– but the human capacity for change and how it is powerful when people step up and they do their job, which sometimes it is simply doing their job and not taking orders, and he could have taken orders and it could have been disastrous."

Our Analysis:

The Profile in Courage Award or A Profile in Convenience?

In a recent episode of MSNBC's "The Weekend," the discourse veered into the territory of political theater with the announcement that former Vice President Mike Pence is to be the recipient of the John F. Kennedy Profile in Courage Award. This decision, ostensibly for his role on January 6, 2021, when he certified the 2020 presidential election results amid chaos and pressure from then-President Donald Trump, has stirred not just a political debate but a moral one.

Let's Unpack the Irony

The award, named after John F. Kennedy's 1957 book, "Profiles in Courage," is meant to honor public officials who have made courageous decisions without regard for the personal or professional risks involved. The irony here is as thick as the book itself. Mike Pence, hailed for doing the bare minimum required by his oath to the Constitution, is now being celebrated as a beacon of courage. The action for which Pence is being celebrated—certifying the election results—is, in fact, a fundamental duty of his office, not an extraordinary act of bravery.

The Real Courageous Acts Overlooked

What's glaringly absent in this narrative are the countless individuals who faced real danger, who stood up against misinformation, voter suppression, and threats to their lives, long before the events of January 6 and without the protection of a vice presidential title. Where is their award for courage? It seems in our current political climate, the bar for what constitutes courage has been set so low that basic job requirements now qualify one for high honors.

The Spectacle of Reactions

The co-hosts’ focus on Donald Trump's anticipated reaction to Pence's award is a vivid illustration of the media's obsession with spectacle over substance. Jonathan Capehart's eagerness for Trump's "head to explode" on True Social is a testament to the never-ending cycle of reaction and counter-reaction that fuels the political entertainment complex. It's a distraction from the systemic issues at play, reducing what should be a moment of reflection on true courage to just another episode in the Trump reality show.

A Missed Opportunity for Genuine Discourse

Elise Jordan's defense of the decision to award Pence, citing it as a nod to "the human capacity for change," misses the mark. It's not about denying individuals the possibility of change but questioning the criteria by which we judge such change to be courageous. By focusing on a single decision of Pence's that aligns with constitutional duty, we overlook a broader history of actions and policies that many would argue lack courage, compassion, or even basic decency.

In Conclusion: A Profile in Convenience

The decision to honor Mike Pence with the Profile in Courage Award is less about acknowledging genuine bravery and more about the convenience of selecting a figure who fits a particular narrative—one that simplifies the complex and tumultuous reality of American politics into digestible, headline-friendly bites. It is a missed opportunity to celebrate the unsung heroes who embody the true spirit of courage John F. Kennedy envisioned. In the end, this award does not illuminate the path of bravery but rather highlights the shadows of our collective political memory, where convenience often overshadows courage.
