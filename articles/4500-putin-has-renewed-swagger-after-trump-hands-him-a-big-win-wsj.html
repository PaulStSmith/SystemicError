Putin has 'renewed swagger' after Trump hands him a big win: WSJ

By Paulo Santos

FILE PHOTO: Russia’s President Vladimir Putin applauds during a plenary session of the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum (SPIEF) in Saint Petersburg, Russia, June 20, 2025. REUTERS/Anton Vaganov/File Photo

Kremlin strongman Vladimir Putin’s declining fortunes have taken a major turn for the better because Donald Trump’s war on Iran has given the staggering Russian economy a shot in the arm as oil prices lurch upward.

According to the Wall Street Journal, Russian crude that was languishing unsold just days ago has become a hot commodity. The U.S. has eased sanctions on Russian oil, opening the door for key buyers to increase purchases. With oil and natural gas prices surging, Russian producers are reaping substantial profits.

The shift is already visible in global markets. In India, traders who previously demanded steep discounts to purchase Russian oil are now attempting to sell at prices above global benchmarks—a dramatic reversal.

“The longer that this conflict goes on, the world will increasingly rely on both Russian crude oil and Russian refined products,” said Naveen Das, senior crude analyst at Kpler.

Emboldened by his suddenly improved fortunes, Putin has grown more aggressive with “renewed swagger’ on the global energy stage. He has threatened to cut off remaining energy supplies to Europe before the continent’s deadline to eliminate Russian LNG and pipeline gas imports by 2027.

“Other markets are opening now,” Putin declared on state television Wednesday. “If they shut us down in a month or two, wouldn’t it be better to stop now and move to those countries that are reliable partners?”

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed Friday that the Iran conflict has directly fueled demand for Russian energy products.

Global benchmark Brent crude has surged nearly 30 percent since the attacks began. While higher prices normally benefit all oil producers, disruption in the Gulf has crippled Russia’s primary competitors in that region, leaving Moscow uniquely positioned to capitalize on the crisis.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Putin’s Profit Surge Amidst Global Turmoil

The recent article sheds light on a rather disturbing trend where geopolitical conflicts are manipulated to prop up the flailing economies of authoritarian regimes. At the heart of this narrative is Vladimir Putin’s Russia, which has seemingly found a lifeline in the escalating conflict between the United States and Iran. Let’s dissect the layers of this development with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

The Windfall from Conflict

First and foremost, the notion that Donald Trump’s war on Iran has inadvertently become the economic boon for Putin’s Russia is as ironic as it is unsettling. The U.S.’s easing of sanctions on Russian oil not only highlights a stark contradiction in policy but also underscores a deep-seated flaw in international diplomacy. The surge in oil prices, as a result of this conflict, directly benefits Russia, an adversary on numerous fronts, including electoral interference and geopolitical aggression.

Market Dynamics at Play

The shift in market dynamics, as detailed in the article, is nothing short of dramatic. Russian crude, once struggling to find buyers, is now being sold at a premium. This scenario is not merely a stroke of luck but a calculated outcome stemming from geopolitical unrest. The fact that traders in India, a key player in the global energy market, are now hustling to sell Russian oil above global benchmarks speaks volumes about the perverse incentives at play.

Putin’s Emboldened Stance

Riding the wave of economic fortune, Putin’s “renewed swagger” on the global stage is both predictable and alarming. His threats to cut off energy supplies to Europe signal a dangerous game of brinkmanship that not only endangers global energy security but also emboldens Russia’s aggressive posture in other arenas. This bravado, fueled by newfound economic leverage, represents a significant escalation in the global power play.

The Indirect Beneficiaries of Conflict

Kremlin’s acknowledgment, through spokesman Dmitry Peskov, that the Iran conflict has directly fueled demand for Russian energy, unveils the macabre reality of modern geopolitics where conflicts serve as economic lifelines for regimes like Putin’s. The surge in Brent crude prices, while crippling Russia’s competitors, positions Moscow in a uniquely advantageous spot to capitalize on the crisis.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

The situation presents a cautionary tale about the unintended consequences of foreign interventions and sanctions. While intended to isolate and penalize rogue states, these measures can inadvertently create economic windfalls for the very adversaries they seek to contain. The case of Putin’s Russia thriving amidst the U.S.-Iran conflict is a testament to the complex and often counterproductive web of international relations and economic dependencies.

In conclusion, the unfolding scenario is a stark reminder of the need for a more cohesive and nuanced approach to international policy and diplomacy. The global community must recognize the inherent risks of enabling authoritarian regimes through conflict and seek alternative strategies that mitigate these unintended boons to their economies. The current trajectory, as elucidated in the article, is not just unsustainable but perilously counterintuitive.
