Trump’s Fox News throw down proves 'the wheels are coming off' his regime

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosOctober 21, 20263 min read

What happened

AlterNet reports that President Donald Trump has intensified his public attacks on Fox News while promoting the pro-Trump Right Side Broadcasting Network. Conservative commentator Jim Geraghty argued that the dispute shows an increasingly strained relationship between Trump and the network as the midterms approach.

Trump praised RSBN’s coverage of a Kentucky appearance and accused Fox of alienating “MAGA” supporters by occasionally featuring Democrats. AlterNet also reports that he attacked Fox over its decision to fire Maria Bartiromo, who reportedly told the White House that anchors had been instructed to stop platforming his false claims.

Geraghty described Fox as more willing to criticize Trump after its $787 million settlement with Dominion Voting Systems over false claims about the 2020 election. He concluded that the relationship had become colder, though he did not establish that a complete break was imminent.

The power is still concentrated

Trump holds the presidency, controls the executive branch and commands a political movement whose attention can materially affect conservative media audiences. Fox controls a powerful distribution system capable of validating, minimizing or challenging what that administration says. RSBN offers Trump something narrower but more reliable: coverage without meaningful editorial friction.

This is not a fight between equal victims of misunderstanding. It is a struggle between a president seeking favorable treatment and media institutions deciding how much reputational and legal exposure they will accept in exchange for access, audience loyalty and political influence.

Fox helped build the expectation

Trump’s indignation makes sense within the system Fox helped create. Years of highly favorable coverage taught him that conservative media existed not merely to cover his movement, but to protect and amplify it. An occasional Democratic guest or critical segment can therefore be cast as betrayal rather than ordinary journalism.

That does not make Fox an independent counterweight. The shift described by AlterNet appears bounded and self-protective. The Dominion settlement gave the company a concrete financial reason to reconsider broadcasting election falsehoods. A corporation responding to a $787 million liability is managing risk, not necessarily recovering principle.

“Tantrum” is too small a frame

Geraghty’s language emphasizes Trump’s “whining” and “self-pity.” Those descriptions may capture his rhetoric, but they reduce an institutional pressure campaign to a personality defect.

The consequential act is not that Trump feels aggrieved. It is that a sitting president is publicly signaling which outlets deserve his movement’s loyalty according to how faithfully they serve him. Promoting RSBN while denouncing Fox pressures conservative media to compete on obedience. Calling that merely a tantrum obscures the leverage behind it.

Loyalty has a price

Fox’s reported distancing also exposes the transactional foundation of the relationship. Trump benefited from sympathetic coverage. Fox benefited from an audience organized around Trump. The arrangement became less attractive when carrying his false election claims produced enormous legal costs.

That incentive structure remains intact even if the participants change. A compliant outlet can gain attention by eliminating scrutiny; a political leader can reward it with promotion and access. The immediate consequence is a media market in which ideological loyalty is measured by willingness to repeat a leader’s preferred account of reality.

The system manufactures replacements

The larger pattern is not simply that Trump and Fox may be breaking up. It is that partisan media ecosystems can discard insufficiently compliant institutions and elevate more obedient ones. Fox need not become hostile to Trump for RSBN to benefit; it only needs to impose limits that Trump considers unacceptable.

Institutional power is visible in who can punish whom. Fox can withhold a platform, but Trump can direct supporters toward competitors and redefine routine scrutiny as treason. The result is not healthy media pluralism. It is a selection process that rewards outlets for surrendering editorial judgment before legal liability forces them to rediscover it.
