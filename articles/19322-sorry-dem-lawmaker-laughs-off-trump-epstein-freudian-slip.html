Rep. Melanie Stansbury (D-NM), an active leader in the House Oversight Committee’s ongoing probe into Jeffrey Epstein and his potential co-conspirators, laughed off a seemingly unintentional “Freudian slip” Saturday after mistakenly referring to the disgraced financier as Donald Trump.

Stansbury spoke with the progressive media company MediasTouch in a video published Saturday about the importance of her committee reaching out to individuals previously associated with Epstein to obtain depositions. The interview was briefly derailed, however, after Stansbury appeared to refer to Epstein as Trump before quickly correcting herself.

“It’s clear that even in the lead up to Epstein’s trial in 2019, they had prepared a list of co-conspirators, and there were only two men that were listed as co-conspirators,” Stansbury said, speaking with MediasTouch’s Katie Phang.

“But all of the other men around Donald – sorry, a Freudian slip there! Around Jeffrey Epstein who were perpetrators were not being investigated.”

President Donald Trump has continued to be plagued by his past ties to Epstein during his second term in office, and to such an extent that a majority of Americans believe that he authorized the U.S. attack on Iran, at least in part, to “distract” from the “Epstein scandal,” a recent Data for Progress poll found.

Trump’s past ties with Epstein have also been the subject of ridicule among many of his critics, some of whom have recently erected a statue in Washington, D.C. to memorialize Trump and Epstein’s past friendship, and in the style of the 1997 hit film “Titanic.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Article’s Political Framing and Responsibility

The article in question presents an interesting case of political framing, the potential for misdirection in assigning responsibility, and the intertwining of political narratives with public perception. It covers Rep. Melanie Stansbury’s (D-NM) slip of the tongue during an interview, an ongoing probe into Jeffrey Epstein and his connections, President Donald Trump’s association with Epstein, and public opinion influenced by these narratives. Let’s dissect each element critically.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

1. House Oversight Committee’s Role:
The House Oversight Committee, where Rep. Melanie Stansbury plays an active role, holds significant institutional power in investigating matters of public concern, including the Epstein case. Their decisions on whom to reach out to, what to investigate, and how to publicize their findings are pivotal. In this scenario, Stansbury and the committee possess the institutional power to shape the narrative around Epstein and his associations.

2. Public Opinion and Media Influence:
While not a traditional form of institutional power, media outlets and public opinion play a critical role in political narratives. The mention of a Data for Progress poll and the erection of a statue to memorialize Trump and Epstein’s past friendship highlight how public sentiment and media portrayal can influence perceptions and potentially policy or investigatory focus.

Decision-Makers and Their Impact

Rep. Melanie Stansbury’s Comments:
Stansbury’s Freudian slip, referring to Epstein as Trump, can be seen as an unintentional error. However, the decision to discuss the Epstein case and the emphasis on co-conspirators directly impacts public perception, especially when such slips occur. It inadvertently ties Trump more closely to Epstein in the public’s mind, regardless of the factual basis of their relationship.

Media’s Role and Responsibility:
The media, represented here by MediasTouch and the article itself, decides how to frame these incidents. The decision to highlight the Freudian slip and to connect it with Trump’s past ties to Epstein, alongside public opinion polls and symbolic gestures like the statue, reinforces a specific narrative. This framing can direct public attention and sentiment in ways that may not fully align with the complexities of the situation or the actual institutional responsibilities.

Misdirection and Framing

Misdirecting Responsibility:
The article potentially misdirects responsibility and focus. By emphasizing the Freudian slip and its implications, alongside Trump’s past ties to Epstein, there’s a risk of overshadowing the substantial issues at play—namely, the need for a thorough investigation into Epstein’s activities and connections. The real story, the pursuit of justice and accountability for Epstein’s actions, risks being sidelined by political spectacle.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating:
Linking Trump to Epstein primarily through a slip of the tongue and past associations, without presenting concrete evidence from the investigation, leans towards creating a false equivalence. It’s crucial to differentiate between political associations and legal culpability. While Trump’s past ties with Epstein are a matter of public record and deserving of scrutiny, they should not overshadow the broader investigation or serve as a scapegoat for Epstein’s crimes.

Conclusion

In dissecting the article’s framing, it’s evident that while institutional power lies with the House Oversight Committee and the media, the decision-making highlighted involves both the explicit actions of the committee and the implicit decisions by the media on how to frame these actions. The framing, unfortunately, risks misdirecting responsibility toward Trump based on associations and public perception, rather than focusing on the concrete actions and decisions that led to Epstein’s ability to evade justice for so long.

Criticism, where due, must be levelled accurately and based on facts rather than insinuations or political rivalry. The emphasis should remain on ensuring a thorough and unbiased investigation into all of Epstein’s connections, irrespective of their political affiliations, to uphold justice and accountability.
