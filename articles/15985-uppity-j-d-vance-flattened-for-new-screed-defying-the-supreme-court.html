'Uppity' J.D. Vance flattened for new screed defying the Supreme Court

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Vance's Misguided Due Process Commentary: A Scathing Review

U.S. Vice President JD Vance looks on during a meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump (not pictured) and El Salvador President Nayib Bukele (not pictured) in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 14, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Vice President J.D. Vance was raked over the coals on Wednesday morning for a series of social media posts on X on Tuesday where he continued to defend the Donald Trump administration for wrongfully shipping a Maryland man to El Salvador despite an admonition from the Supreme Court.

With the battle over the deportation and imprisonment of Kilmar Abrego Garcia at a notorious Salvadoran prison camp reaching the point where even the conservative Wall Street Journal editorial board is stepping in and deploring the lack of due process, Vance has doubled down and blown off concerns.

In a long post on X, Vance argued, "To say the administration must observe 'due process' is to beg the question: what process is due is a function of our resources, the public interest, the status of the accused, the proposed punishment, and so many other factors. To put it in concrete terms, imposing the death penalty on an American citizen requires more legal process than deporting an illegal alien to their country of origin."

He then added, "Here's a useful test: ask the people weeping over the lack of due process what precisely they propose for dealing with Biden's millions and millions of illegals. And with reasonable resource and administrative judge constraints, does their solution allow us to deport at least a few million people per year?"

That led MSNBC's Joe Scarborough to question Vance's legal education at what he mocked as "uppity" Yale Law School.

On "Morning Joe," Scarborough stated, "Well, you know, I'm just a simple country lawyer, and I didn't go to Yale or wherever he went; Yale, Harvard, whatever uppity schools he went to. I went to University of Alabama, roll tide, and University of Florida go gators, but I can tell you, I don't know what they taught at Yale. I can tell you in southern state schools, they taught something called due process."

Noting a viral clip of voters in Iowa getting in the face of Sen. Chuck Grassley (R) in defense of Abrego Garcia, Scarborough added, "So you see those people out in Iowa, maybe they did not go to the law school that J.D. went to, the vice president went to," before moving on to, "I guess I should thank Jesus on holy week that I went to a law school that actually taught due process because the Supreme Court has actually followed the Constitution of the United States."

For good measure he noted, "They also taught us that if the Supreme Court rules on something nine to nothing, nine to nothing, that's the Constitution, that's the law of the land. And so to tweet that after the Supreme Court ruled nine to nothing, that due process still existed in America and existed for people that that that the administration wanted to grab up and whisk away and take away on an airplane, right? For a guy that went to Yale Law School, I think he went to Yale Law School, that's kind of unbelievable."

Our Analysis:

Vance's Misguided Due Process Commentary: A Scathing Review

In a move that can only be described as a brazen defiance of basic legal principles, Vice President J.D. Vance recently ignited a firestorm of criticism with his controversial remarks on X (formerly known as Twitter). Vance, in a bewildering attempt to defend the indefensible actions of the Donald Trump administration - specifically, the wrongful deportation of Maryland man Kilmar Abrego Garcia to El Salvador - has showcased a disturbing disregard for due process.

Vance's Dubious Legal Philosophy

Vance's argument, as presented on X, is a masterclass in obfuscation and legal relativism. He states, "To say the administration must observe 'due process' is to beg the question: what process is due is a function of our resources, the public interest, the status of the accused, the proposed punishment, and so many other factors." This is not just a slippery slope; it's a freefall into legal anarchy. The Vice President is essentially arguing that due process - a cornerstone of American jurisprudence and civil liberties - is a malleable concept, subject to arbitrary factors including the government's current resource allocation and the whims of public interest.

Scarborough's Righteous Rebuke

Joe Scarborough's response, dripping with sarcasm and incredulity, highlights the absurdity of Vance's position. The MSNBC host rightly mocks Vance's elite education, questioning what, if anything, was taught at Yale Law School regarding due process. Scarborough's "simple country lawyer" persona not only serves to underscore the universal understanding of due process but also highlights the dangerous elitism that Vance's argument reeks of. When even the conservative Wall Street Journal editorial board acknowledges the gross lack of due process in Garcia's deportation, Vance's stance becomes not just untenable, but laughable.

The Supreme Court's Unanimous Decision

Scarborough's reference to the Supreme Court's unanimous decision affirming the importance of due process, even for those the administration seeks to "grab up and whisk away," cannot be overstated. This 9-0 ruling isn't just a legal rebuke; it's a thunderous declaration that due process isn't optional. For Vance to continue his defense post this ruling is not only "unbelievable" but a stark illustration of his disconnect from the very Constitution he is sworn to protect.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Vance's comments do not exist in a vacuum. They reflect a growing trend among certain political factions to undermine the legal safeguards that protect all individuals within the United States, citizen or not. His attempt to parse due process into a negotiable term based on subjective criteria sets a dangerous precedent. It suggests that rights are not inalienable but are instead contingent upon one's status, public opinion, or the current administration's policy goals.

This isn't just a systemic error; it's a systemic threat. Vance's stance, if left unchallenged, risks eroding the foundation of American democracy and the rule of law. It is imperative that we, as a society, remain vigilant against such distortions of our legal principles and continue to advocate for the universal application of due process, regardless of the individual or the circumstances involved.
