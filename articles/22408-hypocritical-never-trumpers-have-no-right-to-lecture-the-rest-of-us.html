Hypocritical Never-Trumpers have no right to lecture the rest of us

Based on an article by D. Earl Stephens

Paulo SantosAugust 23, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real StoryWho Had PowerThe Conversion Is CheapMedia As LaundromatWhat They HideThe Pattern

The People Who Lit the Match Want Credit for the Fire

The Real Story

The source is a tirade against Never Trump Republicans, with David Frum as the named example, and against the media platforms that keep recycling these men as wise elders. Its central complaint is simple: people who helped build the Bush-era wreckage, backed the Tea Party, and enabled the political culture that produced Trump now want to posture as sober critics of the Left.

Who Had Power

The people being denounced here did not merely hold opinions. They held institutional power through the Bush White House, the Republican coalition, corporate media, elite universities, and the donor class that bankrolled the whole arrangement. That is the point the piece gets right, even if it buries it under rage: these are not outsiders who got history wrong from the sidelines. They were inside the machinery when it mattered.

The Conversion Is Cheap

The late-stage anti-Trump pose is not repentance. It is a reputational reset. A man like Frum can present himself as a principled dissenter only after the worst damage is already done, after the wars, after the inequality, after the court capture, after the party’s descent into open reaction. That is not courage. It is a way to launder a record without paying for it.

Media As Laundromat

The piece is also dead right about the media ecosystem that keeps these people in rotation. The Atlantic, cable news, and similar prestige outlets love a Republican who can scold the Left in polished sentences while pretending his own side was merely misled. That performance gives elite media what it wants: insider drama without structural accountability. It turns a political arsonist into a commentator on smoke.

What They Hide

The deeper fraud is blame shifting. These figures want to make Trump look like an accident that happened to respectable conservatism, instead of the most visible result of decades of anti-democratic politics, white grievance, war making, Christian-right radicalism, and corporate obedience. The source correctly points to Bush, McConnell, the wars, Citizens United, and the corrosion of civil rights. That is the actual lineage. Trump did not emerge from nowhere. He walked through a door these people kept open.

The Pattern

This story is not about one columnist or one wounded ego. It is about a familiar elite maneuver: break the country, then ask for moral authority because you found the damage embarrassing. The same class that normalized catastrophe now demands deference for denouncing the final monster it helped create. That is how political malpractice survives in plain sight. It renames itself adulthood, calls itself prudence, and insists the people who were right all along still need a lecture.
