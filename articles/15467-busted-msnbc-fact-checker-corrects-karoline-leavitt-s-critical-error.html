Busted: MSNBC fact-checker corrects Karoline Leavitt's critical error

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 30, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Tariff Tango: A Dance of Deflection and Distortion

REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

MSNBC contributor Jeremy Peters fact-checked White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt on MSNBC Reports over the principles of inflation.

Reacting to a report that Amazon is placing a “tariff price” next to every item they sell, indicating how much of the price of that item results from President Donald Trump’s tariffs, White House Leavitt described it as a "hostile and political act."

“Why didn't Amazon do this when the Biden administration hiked inflation to the highest level in 40 years? And I would also add that it's not a surprise because, as Reuters recently wrote, Amazon has partnered with a Chinese propaganda arm,” said Leavitt, holding a news clip for the press room. “So, this is another reason why Americans should buy American. It's another reason why we are onshoring critical supply chains here at home to shore up our own critical supply chain and boost our own manufacturing here.”

Peters took umbrage at Leavitt suggesting Biden “hiked inflation.”

“First of all, a president doesn't choose to hike inflation, right? There's not a policy he can set into place today [to do that.] Yeah, it's not an interest rate, right? It's something that's determined by other forces, but Trumpis choosing to raise tariffs on Chinese goods and all sorts of other goods. So, this is, in effect, a tax.”

But Republicans are not appreciating any reference to the “T-word” in connection with the GOP or Trump, Peters told MSNBC Reports anchor Chris Jansing. “Republicans don't want you saying that. They certainly don't want to get blamed—if these costs do indeed get passed along to consumers for any extended period of time—to be associated with that,”

Inflation, he added, was something Republicans “complained about to no end during the Biden administration,” and he said he found the sudden role reversal in 2025 “interesting.”

“Remember during the 2024 campaign, you had the Democrats being rightfully criticized for saying ‘why is everyone so worried about the economy? The economy is good. Look at the Dow. Look at the gdp.’ And people weren't feeling that, and Biden was almost insisting, ‘no, what's wrong with you? Snap out of it. You should feel good about this. But now Trump is in. Republicans are basically saying the same thing right now, telling people, ‘don't believe what you read in your 401k statement, your brokerage account, or the prices that you're seeing at the grocery store or on the shelf at Best Buy. Everything is fine.’”

Peters predicted that role reversal likely extending to a similar voter reaction in the next election.

“At the end of the day, the American people did not take Joe Biden's word for it. There's not really much of a reason to think they’ll take Trump’s either.”

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Tango: A Dance of Deflection and Distortion

In a recent segment on MSNBC Reports, the conversation twirled around White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt's commentary, which, frankly, could be mistaken for a page ripped out of the Big Book of Political Deflection. Leavitt's reaction to Amazon's decision to label products with a "tariff price" is a classic spin, accusing the move of being a "hostile and political act." This assertion is as bold as it is baffling, suggesting that transparency in pricing—and by extension, accountability in policy—somehow constitutes hostility.

Leavitt's attempt to shift blame to the Biden administration for hiking inflation to the highest level in 40 years is a maneuver so transparent it would make a ghost blush. MSNBC contributor Jeremy Peters rightly called out this flagrant attempt at misdirection. Peters' rebuttal is grounded in the reality that presidents do not hike inflation as if they were adjusting the thermostat on a chilly day. Inflation is a complex economic phenomenon influenced by a myriad of factors far beyond the immediate reach of presidential decrees.

The commentary spirals further into the realm of the absurd with the insinuation that Amazon's partnership with a Chinese propaganda arm is somehow relevant to the discussion. This leap in logic is as graceful as a brick in a ballet, serving only to muddy the waters and distract from the core issue at hand: the impact of tariffs on consumer prices.

Peters hits the nail on the head when highlighting the Republican aversion to the "T-word" - tariffs. Tariffs are, in effect, taxes imposed on imported goods. Their cost invariably trickles down to consumers, manifesting as higher prices on the shelf. This simple economic fact seems to be an inconvenient truth for those who championed these tariffs, only to recoil at the prospect of being associated with the resultant price hikes.

The observed role reversal Peters mentions, with Republicans now downplaying economic anxieties they once amplified under the Biden administration, is a study in political hypocrisy. The same voices that once fanned the flames of inflation fears are now urging the public to ignore the smoke filling the room. This sudden change of tune is as disingenuous as it is predictable, revealing a party more concerned with maintaining power than addressing the tangible repercussions of its policies on the average American.

Peters' prediction of voter reaction is a poignant reminder of the broader implications of these political gymnastics. The American public, having once turned their backs on Biden's reassurances, are unlikely to be swayed by Trump's assurances that "everything is fine." This skepticism towards political platitudes is a healthy sign of a democracy still capable of critical thought, despite the best efforts of its leaders to cloud the waters.

In conclusion, the segment on MSNBC Reports serves as a microcosm of the larger political discourse, one where accountability is dodged like a bullet, and transparency is treated as an adversary. Peters' analysis cuts through the smoke and mirrors, offering a clear-eyed view of the situation. The "tariff price" debacle is not just about tariffs; it's about the integrity of our political discourse and the urgent need for leaders who can face economic realities without resorting to spin.
