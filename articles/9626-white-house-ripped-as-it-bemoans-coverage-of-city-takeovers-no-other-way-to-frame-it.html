Describing the President’s deployment of the National Guard and U.S. military, ostensibly to protect ICE agents, federal buildings, and patrol city streets, as a “proven formula” to curb crime — which FBI data show was already declining nationwide — Leavitt rebuked the press for “framing this like the president wants to take over the American cities with the military.”

Asked at Monday’s briefing if Democrats have “reason to be concerned that there are long-term plans by this administration to keep U.S. military or the National Guard in American cities, like what we’re seeing here in Washington,” White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters, “Why should they be concerned about the federal government offering help to make their cities a safer place?”

Aside from Washington, D.C., no mayor who has been notified of possible federal troops on their streets has supported the idea. Mayor Bowser has taken a reserved stance, given the District’s federal constraints. Federal law, in most cases, requires the governor to ask the President for troops.

“They should be concerned about the fact that people in their cities right now are being gunned down every single night, and the president, all he’s trying to do is fix it,” Leavitt insisted, despite troops largely being unwelcome.

She decried what she alleged was a “complete smear campaign by the Democrats and the media, quite frankly.”

“I was reading Politico Playbook this morning — one of the most inside the Beltway newsletters you can find — and you guys are framing this, like the president wants to take over the American cities with the military. The president wants to help these local leaders who have been completely ineffective in securing their own cities,” Leavitt claimed, before suggesting plans for further militarization.

“And we have already seen, there’s a positive formula for that. Look at Washington, D.C. The murder rate has declined significantly. Our streets are safer. People who live here feel safer. All of you in this room, I know you do. You just won’t admit it.”

“And it’s a proven system that works, and the president wants to make America’s cities safe again,” Leavitt added. “And I don’t think there’s anything wrong with that.”

Critics blasted Leavitt’s remarks.

“Troops are being deployed to US cities where the crime rate has plummeted—against the wishes of states, cities, and local residents. It IS a takeover. No other way to frame it,” wrote political strategist Sawyer Hackett.

Democratic activist and strategist Olivia Julianna pointed to a headline that read: “Trump says military should use U.S. cities as ‘training grounds’.” She commented, “This was LAST WEEK btw.”

Author and political commentator Brian Tylor Cohen remarked: “Probably because that is *quite literally* what’s happening.”

Our Analysis:

The Militarization Masquerade: Trump’s Urban Takeover

In what can only be described as a brazen display of authoritarian ambition, the White House, under President Donald Trump, is signaling a plan to expand the domestic deployment of military forces across U.S. cities. This move, audaciously defying opposition from local leaders, is cloaked in the guise of public safety. Yet, it’s nothing short of a power grab, leveraging the military against American citizens.

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt’s comments are a masterclass in Orwellian doublespeak. Describing the deployment of the National Guard and U.S. military ostensibly to protect ICE agents, federal buildings, and patrol city streets as a “proven formula” to curb crime, Leavitt blatantly ignores FBI data showing a nationwide decline in crime. This isn’t about making cities safer; it’s about political theater—a show of force to intimidate and suppress.

Leavitt’s rebuke of the press for “framing this like the president wants to take over the American cities with the military” is as laughable as it is terrifying. When asked if Democrats have reason to be concerned about long-term military presence in American cities, her retort, “Why should they be concerned about the federal government offering help to make their cities a safer place?” misses the mark entirely. It’s not about the offer of help; it’s about the imposition of military force in defiance of local governance and wishes.

The assertion that all the president is trying to do is “fix” the problem of city crime is a gross simplification of complex societal issues that cannot be solved with soldiers and tanks. It’s a narrative that seeks to exploit genuine concerns for safety to justify an expansion of federal military power onto the streets of America’s cities.

Leavitt’s attack on the Democrats and the media as part of a “complete smear campaign” is a tactic as old as time—blame the messenger to distract from the message. Her claim that the militarization of D.C. has made the streets safer and that people feel safer is anecdotal at best, and at worst, a deliberate misrepresentation of the true impact and public sentiment.

Moreover, the notion that the military should use U.S. cities as “training grounds,” as Trump reportedly suggested last week, is not only a chilling proposition but also a clear indication of how this administration views the rights and lives of its citizens—as expendable in the pursuit of power and control.

Critics like political strategist Sawyer Hackett, Democratic activist and strategist Olivia Julianna, and author and political commentator Brian Tylor Cohen have rightly blasted Leavitt’s remarks. The deployment of troops against the wishes of states, cities, and local residents, where crime rates have plummeted, is indeed “a takeover. No other way to frame it.”

In sum, the Trump administration’s plan to deploy military forces in American cities is a dangerous overreach that threatens the very fabric of our democracy. It’s not about safety; it’s about control. And in the face of such an intolerable abuse of power, it’s imperative for all of us to stand up, speak out, and hold those in power accountable. The streets of America’s cities are not battlefields, and its citizens are not the enemy. It’s high time the administration learned that lesson.
