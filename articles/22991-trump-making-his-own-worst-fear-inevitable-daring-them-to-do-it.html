Trump making his own worst fear inevitable: 'Daring them to do it'

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Impeachment Is Not the Story. Power Is.

The Source Confuses Drama for Leverage

The Hill piece treats a possible third impeachment as if it were mainly a question of political appetite, timing, and whether Democrats want to “go after” Trump again. That framing is too small. The real issue is institutional power: who can compel documents, force testimony, freeze the legislative agenda, and make the costs of lawbreaking harder to hide.

Impeachment is not a morality play. It is one of the few mechanisms Congress has to expose abuse when the executive branch is acting like it is above the law.

Trump Is Daring Congress Because He Can

The source is right to note that Trump has been openly stacking up conduct that invites confrontation, from the “crypto billions” to the gifted jumbo jet, the drug boat kill teams, and budget impoundment. That is not confusion. It is accumulation. It is a president behaving as though legal and constitutional restraints are negotiable if he is brazen enough.

This is the central fact: Trump is not merely risking impeachment. He is testing whether Congress will absorb misconduct as background noise.

The Senate Is the Shield

The article gets closer to the truth when it admits that a Republican-controlled Senate turns impeachment into a short, dead process. That is not a procedural accident. It is institutional protection. The 2020 trial lasted 20 days. The 2021 trial lasted five. Those were not serious reckonings; they were containment exercises.

Republicans are not passive observers here. They are the enclosure that keeps presidential abuse from becoming consequence. As long as they control the Senate, impeachment can be reduced to a partisan spectacle that helps Trump rally his base while the record gets buried.

What Democrats Should Use the House For

The piece implies Democrats may hesitate because a Senate conviction is unlikely. That is too timid. A House majority should not treat impeachment as a verdict that must end in removal. It should treat impeachment as a forcing mechanism: a way to drag abuses into daylight, lock the story into the congressional record, and make obstruction harder.

If Democrats regain the House and possibly the Senate, the point is not ceremonial indignation. The point is to make Trump’s conduct expensive, traceable, and politically corrosive. The question is not whether impeachment “succeeds” in the narrow sense. The question is whether Democrats are willing to use congressional power like they mean it.

Not a Stunt, Not a Ritual

Stirewalt’s best line is also the most revealing: the real question is whether impeachment becomes “another stunt or something that actually draws blood.” That is the right framework, even if the article mostly circles it. The danger is not overuse of impeachment. The danger is a political system so hollowed out that repeated presidential abuse can be processed as content.

If Congress cannot do more than stage a failed rebuke while the executive branch piles up illegalities, then the problem is not that impeachment is too partisan. The problem is that impunity has become the default operating system.

The Systemic Pattern

This story is about a familiar American failure: accountability only becomes imaginable when partisan control shifts, and even then it depends on whether the opposition is willing to act like an institution with teeth. Trump understands that. Republicans understand that. The article only half-says it.

The real lesson is uglier than impeachment theater. Authoritarian politics does not always announce itself as a break with the system. Sometimes it is just the system, in public, counting on exhaustion, delay, and a compliant Senate to make lawbreaking feel normal.
