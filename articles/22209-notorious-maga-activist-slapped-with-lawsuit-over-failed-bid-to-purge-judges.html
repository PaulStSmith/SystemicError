Notorious MAGA activist slapped with lawsuit over failed bid to purge judges

Based on an article by Matthew Chapman

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Quiet Election Rigging of the Loudman

What Happened

Scott Presler, through his PAC Early Vote Action, is alleged to have run an undisclosed campaign to push Pennsylvania voters to reject the retention of three state Supreme Court justices. Public Citizen says that campaign money was spent to influence a judicial election without the registration and disclosures Pennsylvania law requires.

Who Actually Had Power

The power here was not some diffuse “activist energy.” It was the combination of money, media access, and a political action committee trying to shape control of a state Supreme Court in the country’s largest swing state. Presler and EVA made the decision to intervene. They are the actors alleged to have chosen opacity as a tactic, because opacity is how you buy influence while dodging accountability.

The judges were the target, not the problem. The institution at stake was the court itself. This was an attempt to steer judicial power by manipulating a retention election and hiding the financial machinery behind it.

The Point Was Secrecy

Pennsylvania’s disclosure rules exist for a reason: if someone is spending to sway an election, the public is supposed to know who is paying. According to the complaint, Presler and his PAC ignored that requirement even while their campaign reached national and local media. That is not an administrative footnote. It is the method.

The pattern is familiar. First comes the performance of populist outrage. Then comes the anonymous money. Then comes the claim that no one can really prove what happened. The point of the concealment is to make organized political interference look like spontaneous civic sentiment.

The Framing Is Too Small

This story should not be reduced to Presler’s personal controversies, his conspiracy-mongering, or his long history as a right-wing provocateur. Those details matter, but they are not the center of gravity. The center is an organized effort to influence a court without the transparency the law demands.

That is where the misdirection lives. Attention drifts toward the most chaotic figure in the room, while the larger operation stays intact: a networked campaign to weaken democratic institutions through procedural abuse, media amplification, and selective ignorance of the rules. That is not confusion. It is strategy.

What This Reveals

Right-wing politics keeps trying to launder institutional sabotage through the language of activism. It wants the public to see loud personalities, not deliberate power grabs. It wants outrage without disclosure, mobilization without accountability, and court capture without the embarrassment of admitting that courts are the target.

The democratic response is not reverence for procedure as an abstraction. It is insisting that the rules apply to the people trying to break the system while pretending to defend it. When a PAC tries to influence a state judiciary in the dark, the story is not about one activist’s theatrics. It is about the machinery of authoritarian politics learning how to hide in plain sight.
