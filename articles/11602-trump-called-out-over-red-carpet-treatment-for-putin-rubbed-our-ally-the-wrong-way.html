Trump called out over 'red carpet' treatment for Putin: 'Rubbed our ally the wrong way'

U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hand with Russian President Vladimir Putin, as they meet to negotiate for an end to the war in Ukraine, at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S., August 15, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

A CNN anchor called out President Donald Trump on Friday afternoon as he rolled out the red carpet for Russian President Vladimir Putin at the start of a high-stakes summit in Alaska.

Trump and Putin were meeting on Friday to discuss ending the ongoing invasion in Ukraine. The discussions were taking place at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson.

The two deplaned Friday afternoon and shook hands on a red carpet before departing in The Beast.

The stunning image caught the attentin of CNN anchor Abby Phillip.

"Just a note on the red carpet of it all. I mean, I think it's a visual that, you know, our reporters in the region say is just rubbing Ukraine the wrong way," she said. "To roll out the red carpet for a man who is wanted for war crimes against Ukraine in what might be a negotiation is something that is not going to be received well there."

She added that Trump does these sorts of things partly to make Putin feel welcomed and make him feel like they're "buddies again."

"Whether or not he uses that to his strategic advantage, we will find out. But it's one that, you know, if Ukraine is our ally in this conflict, is rubbing our ally the wrong way," she warned.

Fellow anchor John King said Putin is "winning the early moments."

"He's getting a picture—greeted by the president of the United States on U.S. soil after more than three years of being a pariah in the world for invading a democracy in Europe," said King. "The question is, it'll be judged by substance? If they get substance, if there's legitimate progress toward a ceasefire or some progress toward ending the war in Ukraine that Ukraine can accept, then that's how the summit will be judged."

He concluded, "These pictures are being shown all across Russia right now, all across Europe right now, and a leader who has been a pariah has just been greeted on U.S. soil by the American president. Again, if Trump can get for what he's giving, we'll see at the end. But so far, he's giving well."

Our Analysis:

The Red Carpet Controversy: Trump, Putin, and Ukraine

In a move that can only be described as audaciously tone-deaf, President Donald Trump has once again demonstrated his flair for the controversial by rolling out the literal red carpet for Russian President Vladimir Putin amidst the ongoing invasion of Ukraine. The imagery of such a gesture, set against the backdrop of a high-stakes summit in Alaska, is not just a slap in the face to Ukraine—it's a stark illustration of the complex and often problematic relationship between Trump and Putin.

A Visual Metaphor for Diplomatic Insensitivity

CNN anchor Abby Phillip's observations cut to the heart of the matter. By extending such a grandiose welcome to Putin, Trump isn't just making a statement about his relationship with the Russian leader; he's broadcasting a message about his disregard for Ukraine's plight. Phillip rightly notes that this "visual" is "rubbing Ukraine the wrong way," a country still reeling from the consequences of Russia's aggression. It's a bold reminder of how optics in international relations can sometimes speak louder than actions or intentions.

Trump's Strategy: Friendship or Folly?

Phillip suggests that Trump's motivations may stem from a desire to rekindle the "buddies again" dynamic with Putin. However, this approach raises serious questions about Trump's strategic acumen. Is the welcoming of a leader accused of war crimes against an ally a masterstroke of diplomacy, or is it a blunder of historic proportions? The notion that rolling out the red carpet for Putin could somehow lead to a strategic advantage is hopeful at best and dangerously naive at worst.

The Putin Paradox: Pariah Turned Guest of Honor

Fellow CNN anchor John King hits the nail on the head by pointing out the early symbolic "win" for Putin. Being greeted by the President of the United States on American soil, after years of international isolation for invading a democratic neighbor, is a significant image win for Putin. These visuals, as King notes, are not just for the American audience but are being broadcast across Russia and Europe, rehabilitating Putin's image on the world stage.

The Judgement of History: Optics vs. Outcomes

King suggests that the ultimate judgement of this summit will hinge on its substance and outcomes, particularly regarding progress toward ending the war in Ukraine. While this is undoubtedly true, the optics of the summit's commencement cannot be overlooked. The images of Trump welcoming Putin with open arms (and a plush red carpet) have already been seared into the global consciousness, underscoring a narrative of American acquiescence to Russian aggression.

Conclusion: A Misstep of Monumental Proportions

As the world watches, the unfolding drama of Trump's summit with Putin in Alaska is a vivid reminder of the precarious nature of international diplomacy and the power of visual symbolism. By choosing to honor Putin in such a manner, Trump has not only alienated Ukraine but also sent a chilling message to democratic nations everywhere. The implications of this gesture are far-reaching, and its repercussions may be felt long after the red carpet is rolled back up.

In the grand chessboard of global politics, Trump's decision to roll out the red carpet for Putin may be remembered as a move that sacrificed much for the sake of very little. Whether this will lead to any substantive progress remains to be seen, but the initial optics are undeniably disconcerting. As Phillip and King have adeptly highlighted, the summit's success should not only be measured by its tangible outcomes but also by its adherence to the principles of dignity, respect, and solidarity with those fighting for their sovereignty and democratic integrity.
