'He's a phony': Tucker Carlson insults fly as fight erupts between GOP pundits

Former White House Press Secretary Ari Fleischer, a Fox News contributor, attacked right-wing influencer Tucker Carlson for hypocrisy on a potential war with Iran.

In a Sunday post on X, Fleischer shared a video of Carlson calling for military action against Iran in 2006 and 2012. The post contrasted that with Carlson's opposition to an attack in 2025.

"I've known Tucker for 25 years," Fleischer wrote. "Tucker doesn't have a consistent thought in his head, except self-promotion. He's a phony."

"Watch him here advocate to destroy Iran. Now he says that's a warmonger," he added. "He's a modern carnival barker."

Former Rep. Matt Gaetz (R-FL) came to Carlson's defense by noting that Fleischer helped to sell former President George W. Bush's war in Iraq.

"Thank goodness America still has Bush Administration officials around to tell us how Middle East Regime Change Wars turn out," Gaetz wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Fleischer-Carlson Feud: A Window into Right-Wing Hypocrisy

In a recent and rather spicy exchange on X (formerly Twitter), Ari Fleischer, the former White House Press Secretary under George W. Bush, and now a Fox News contributor, launched a scathing attack on Tucker Carlson, a prominent right-wing influencer and Fox News host. The bone of contention? Carlson's flip-flopping stance on military action against Iran.

The Pot Calling the Kettle Black

Fleischer, in a move dripping with irony, accused Carlson of hypocrisy. He unearthed videos from 2006 and 2012 showing Carlson ardently advocating for military action against Iran. Fast forward to 2025, and Carlson's tune has dramatically changed, positioning him against the very war cries he once led. Fleischer didn't mince words, labeling Carlson a "phony" and a "modern carnival barker," focused solely on "self-promotion."

This accusation would have carried more weight if it didn't come from Fleischer, a figure synonymous with the Bush administration's misleading rhetoric on weapons of mass destruction — rhetoric that led to the disastrous Iraq War. The pot isn't just calling the kettle black; it's doing so from a stove that set the house on fire.

Enter Matt Gaetz: The Unlikely Voice of Reason?

Complicating the narrative is former Rep. Matt Gaetz (R-FL), who jumped into the fray in defense of Carlson. Gaetz, not typically known as a paragon of wisdom, hit a nerve by calling out the disastrous outcomes of Middle East regime change wars, a legacy of the Bush administration in which Fleischer played a pivotal role.

Gaetz's retort, "Thank goodness America still has Bush Administration officials around to tell us how Middle East Regime Change Wars turn out," is a masterclass in sarcasm, highlighting the catastrophic and well-documented failures of the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. It's a poignant reminder of the dangers of heedlessly diving into conflicts based on flimsy pretexts or, worse, outright fabrications.

The Bigger Picture

This squabble between Fleischer and Carlson, with Gaetz chiming in, is emblematic of a broader issue within the right-wing ecosystem: a glaring lack of consistency and principle. The readiness to flip-flop on critical issues based on political convenience or media ratings underscores a deeper, systemic problem. It's not just about Iran or the specter of war; it's about the integrity of political discourse and the responsibility of those with a platform to advocate for policies that impact millions of lives.

Carlson's transformation from a hawk to a dove (and vice versa) based on the prevailing political winds is troubling. Yet, Fleischer's attempt to castigate him reeks of hypocrisy given his own complicity in one of the most disastrous foreign policy blunders of the 21st century.

Concluding Thoughts

The Fleischer-Carlson spat is more than petty infighting; it's a microcosm of the broader ideological and moral bankruptcy plaguing certain segments of the political right. When the architects of past disasters feel emboldened to critique others for inconsistencies, it's clear that self-awareness is in short supply.

As observers, we must demand better. The stakes are too high for anything less. War is not a game of political ping-pong to be played on cable news or social media. It demands sober reflection, consistent principles, and, above all, a commitment to the truth — qualities sorely missing in this latest episode of right-wing theater.
