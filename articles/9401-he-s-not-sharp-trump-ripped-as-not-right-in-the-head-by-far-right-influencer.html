'He's not sharp': Trump ripped as 'not right in the head' by far-right influencer

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures onstage as he speaks at the American Cornerstone Institute’s Fourth Annual Founders Dinner at George Washington’s Mount Vernon Estate in Mount Vernon, V.A., U.S., September 20, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

White nationalist influencer Nick Fuentes, who infamously once dined with President Donald Trump and Kanye West (Ye) at Mar-a-Lago, blasted the president last week as a “weird guy” who is “not right in the head.”

“Look, something’s wrong with him, man,” Fuentes told a viewer on his online streaming show, as Mediaite reported. “And I’m not saying that to be nasty, but like, he’s a weird guy.”

“And some of the stuff that he does, just the way that he’s always glazing himself, and repeats himself.”

“Something’s, he’s not right in the head, and I don’t know if that’s his psychology or if it’s just his age, but there’s something not right there, and anyone that’s been around him will tell you that,” Fuentes charged.

“He’s a weird dude,” he added. “Like, he’s an odd cat, and maybe you need to be to be as tough as he is, but he’s not sharp. If there was something wrong with him 10 years ago, it was a lot less apparent, because 10 years ago, he’s a lot more sharp, and, I think, compelling, and general, and now, he’s just slow and monotonous, and repeats himself, and seems to really not know what’s going on.”

Fuentes was responding to a viewer who had written, “Looks like your assessment of Trump as a tired old man is correct.”

“Saw a report today that lots of his view of Chicago or NYC is from old BLM riot clips people show him. He just wants to play golf, fly on AF1, have fancy dinner parties, sign Oval Office EOs, watch 80s movie and get told he is getting revenge on [John] Bolton.”

Our Analysis:

The Ironic Critique: Nick Fuentes on Donald Trump

In a somewhat surreal twist of political theater, Nick Fuentes, a figure synonymous with white nationalist rhetoric and infamous for dining with former President Donald Trump and Kanye West (Ye) at Mar-a-Lago, has turned his critical eye towards Trump, branding him a “weird guy” and suggesting he is “not right in the head.” This critique, coming from Fuentes, is not just rich with irony but also laden with hypocrisy and a glaring testament to the chaotic nature of extremist political alliances.

Fuentes, during his online streaming show, embarked on a diatribe against Trump, citing his behaviors and mental acuity as points of concern. “Look, something’s wrong with him, man,” Fuentes stated, painting a picture of a former president who has supposedly lost his sharpness and become monotonous and repetitive. This assessment, while it may hold a grain of truth regarding Trump’s public demeanor and speaking patterns over the years, is dripping with irony coming from Fuentes, a figure who has built his platform on far-right extremism and misinformation.

The critique delves into the realms of personal attack and speculative diagnosis, areas Fuentes is arguably ill-equipped to navigate with any credibility. He mentions, “Something’s, he’s not right in the head,” a statement that not only lacks empathy but also professionalism, considering Fuentes is not a mental health expert. Moreover, the comments about Trump’s psychological state or age-related decline are speculative at best and malicious at worst, reflecting a personal vendetta rather than a constructive critique.

Fuentes’ remarks were in response to a viewer’s comment that echoed a sentiment of disillusionment with Trump, describing him as a “tired old man” overly fixated on the past. This narrative, that Trump is living in a bygone era, obsessed with golf, fancy dinners, and revenge fantasies, while ignoring the present realities, is a compelling critique of Trump’s leadership and priorities. Yet, when this critique is levied by Fuentes, it loses its potency, mired as it is in the murky waters of Fuentes’ own controversial and extremist views.

This entire episode is emblematic of the fractures within the far-right ecosystem, where allegiances are as fickle as they are fervent. Fuentes’ about-face on Trump reveals not just personal grievances but also the inherent instability within extremist political circles. It’s a reminder that these alliances are often built not on mutual respect or shared ideological foundations, but on the shifting sands of convenience, power, and publicity.

In conclusion, while Fuentes’ criticisms of Trump might find resonance with some observers, the source of these critiques significantly undermines their validity. Coming from Fuentes, these remarks are not a poignant political analysis but rather an ironic sideshow, highlighting the disarray and disillusionment within the far-right factions that once hailed Trump as their champion. The real story here isn’t just about Trump’s alleged decline, but about the fracturing and infighting within the extremist political ranks that once stood united under his banner.
