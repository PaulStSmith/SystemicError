Trump’s War on Reality: The Strongman Who Calls Domestic Violence a ‘Little Fight’

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 2, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump has always treated facts the way a pickpocket treats pockets: opportunities to be violated, emptied, and rewritten. But his recent remarks at the White House Liberty Commission event at the Museum of the Bible reveal something darker than his usual carnival of lies. This time, he openly minimized domestic violence, waving it away as a nuisance statistic that ruins his fantasy of an 87% crime drop in Washington, D.C. — a figure he appears to have pulled from the same imaginary vault where he stores his election victories and divine mandates.

Trump’s words weren’t an off-the-cuff stumble. They were a window into a worldview where abuse is trivial, numbers are malleable, and state violence is noble. His speech reads like the fever dream of a man who demands total obedience not just from people, but from reality itself.

The Mask Slips: Domestic Violence as ‘A Little Fight’

There’s a particular kind of cruelty in how Trump treats harm done inside the home. For him, domestic violence isn’t a crisis. It isn’t trauma. It isn’t structural oppression or a national failure to protect vulnerable people. No — it’s merely an accounting inconvenience.

“If a man has a little fight with the wife, they say, this was a crime.”

A little fight. That phrase lands like a slap across the country. It reduces battered spouses — overwhelmingly women — to characters in a sitcom spat. It trivializes the broken bones, the police calls, the shelters, the funerals. It reframes domestic violence as some bureaucratic scam that unfairly pads crime statistics.

This is the logic of authoritarians: if the data makes you look bad, redefine the data. If people are harmed, then change what ‘harm’ means. If you can’t claim 100% safety, call those last stubborn crimes fake.

For Trump, the issue isn’t violence. It’s optics.

The Imaginary ‘Safe Zone City’

Trump’s entire narrative of crime is built on invented numbers and imaginary victories. He claims Washington, D.C. went from “the most violent city” to a “totally safe zone” under his guidance. The facts disagree — violently — but Trump doesn’t dwell on details. He simply asserts a new reality and expects the room to kneel before it.

He brags that crime is down “87%” and then — with breathtaking indifference — says the only reason he can’t claim 100% is because domestic violence is still being counted.

The message is unmistakable: victims are in the way. Their suffering disrupts the myth of Trump the omnipotent crime-fighter. Abuse becomes an impurity in the numbers, something to be dismissed rather than addressed. This isn’t policy. This is erasure.

Militarized Fantasy: The President as Warlord

From there, Trump shifts into another favorite delusion: portraying himself as a strongman who personally sent “troops” into Los Angeles to crush crime. He claims he “saved Los Angeles” by deploying federal forces — something that simply did not happen. It’s historical fanfiction, but it serves a purpose.

He wants to paint Democratic leaders as helpless, weak, and reliant on him. He wants his audience to imagine American cities as chaotic wastelands saved only by his courage. And most importantly, he wants domestic military force to seem normal, even heroic.

This is not a man describing governance. It’s a man describing conquest.

He uses the language of violence without hesitation:

“We killed it. And it was nasty. But we killed it immediately.”

‘It,’ of course, meaning civil unrest, political opposition, or whatever group he projects his resentment onto at any given moment. When Trump boasts about ‘killing’ problems, it’s a tell. Strongmen never hide their appetite for force; they advertise it as public service.

The Religious Seal of Approval

As always, Trump baptizes his fantasies in God’s name:

“Amazing the way God works, isn’t it?”

He wraps himself in religious imagery to make authoritarianism look like divine destiny. He invokes America’s 250th anniversary as though it exists to honor him. He claims credit for the Olympics and the World Cup, folding global events into his personal mythology.

Trump is not content to be a political figure. He wants to be a figure of worship — ordained, chosen, sanctified. By invoking God, he excuses his own excesses and elevates his whims above scrutiny.

This is a dangerous formula: violence plus divine mandate equals theocratic strongman.

Obama, Envy, and the Pathological Need for Credit

Trump’s tirade against Barack Obama in this speech is equal parts petty and revealing. He mocks Obama for failing to secure the Olympics and brags about personally negotiating with the IOC. The story is almost certainly fabricated, but the emotional truth is transparent: Trump cannot stand a world where anything good happens without his fingerprints on it.

Even the country’s semiquincentennial — 250 years of American history — becomes something he longs to claim credit for. He jokes about ‘creating 250 years,’ and for a moment, the mask slips again. The thought excites him.

He’s not kidding. He really does believe the nation’s major narratives orbit around him.

The Authoritarian Throughline

Across this entire speech, one consistent theme repeats:

Trump believes suffering does not matter unless it flatters him.

Domestic violence? A trivial bookkeeping annoyance.
Crime statistics? Raw clay to shape into propaganda.
Los Angeles? A conquered territory in his imaginary war.
God? A tool of political branding.
History? A vehicle for his personal legend.

This is not political rhetoric. It’s the worldview of a dictator in waiting — or worse, a dictator in action.

Minimizing domestic violence is not just misogyny. It’s a policy signal. It tells police, prosecutors, and bureaucrats to deprioritize cases. It tells survivors their trauma won’t ‘count.’ It creates an atmosphere where abuse can flourish in the shadows because the man on top wants cleaner numbers, not safer families.

Domestic abusers hear permission.
Victims hear invisibility.
Trump hears applause.

The Reality We Cannot Ignore

Trump’s remarks are part of a long pattern: dehumanizing women, dismissing violence, attacking democratic institutions, and wielding faith as a political cudgel. Each line in this speech is a blow to the idea of a government that serves its people.

We’re not dealing with a man who sees crime as something to solve. We’re dealing with a man who sees crime statistics as something to conquer, rewrite, or ignore based on what makes him look strongest.

When survivors of domestic violence are reduced to statistical clutter, when cities become war zones in the president’s imagination, when God becomes a stage prop, and when history itself bows to a single ego, the result isn’t just a political speech. It’s a threat.

Trump told us exactly who he is — again.
He told us what he values — again.
And he told us whom he’s willing to sacrifice.

Believe him.
