Members of the National Guard walk inside the Lincoln Memorial, after U.S. President Donald Trump deployed the force and ordered an increased presence of federal law enforcement to assist in crime prevention, in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 18, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

President Donald Trump announced on a radio show that he would go out in Washington, D.C. with the National Guard, federal agents, and Secret Service as part of his ongoing occupation of the District. The idea brought jeers from those who view it as nothing more than performative.

Former Republican Party chairman Michael Steele called it nothing more than a continuation of "performative BS from Trump."

"It's not about crime in DC (violent crime fell 30% last yr) it is about the domestic deployment of the military and targeting DC residents--it has nothing to do with safety. This is about Trump #DramaQueen," he added.

Charles Johnson, guitarist with George Duke and "Little Green Footballs" co-founder, commented on BlueSky, "Trump says he’s going out in DC tonight with the National Guard. SANDWICH MAKERS TO THE RAMPARTS!"

The post harkens back to the lawyer who chucked a foot-long submarine sandwich into the chest of a federal agent. His minor protest has become part of the mockery of the agents standing around without much to do. One encouraged DC residents to close down and turn off lights and "make it look like a ghost town."

One person questioned why Trump has been so afraid to go out in Washington, given that former President Joe Biden and his wife, Dr. Jill Biden, were frequently seen at DC restaurants and other events in the city.

One famous Biden Washington story is that Biden went to a movie with his granddaughter. When they came out, they spotted a homeless veteran. While the couple was in President Barack Obama's administration, Dr. Biden would attend frequent barre classes and get her hair cut by a local Georgetown stylist.

Former first lady Michelle Obama would regularly cover herself in a hat and dark glasses to sneak into Target for some light shopping. Today, the Obamas continue to live part-time in a D.C. home. Mrs. Obama has even been spotted attending SoulCycle spin classes and the couple regularly dines out.

"I guess those bone spurs only hurt when you're in the prime of your life(?)" questioned rapper N.E. Boddie.

New Hampshire Democrat Chip Moynihan posted an AI image of Trump as a "mall cop."

Our Analysis:

The Theatrics of Power: Trump's Latest Stunt

In a move that could only originate from the playbook of Donald Trump, the former president has declared his intention to parade through Washington, D.C., flanked by the National Guard, federal agents, and the Secret Service. This announcement, ostensibly under the guise of addressing crime in the capital, has been met with justifiable skepticism and outright ridicule. Let's dissect this spectacle and the reactions it has provoked, slicing through the performative veil to expose the true motivations and implications.

The Masquerade of Safety

Trump's declaration, as per usual, comes wrapped in the flag of public safety. Yet, the narrative crumbles under the slightest scrutiny. Michael Steele, the former Republican Party chairman, cuts to the heart of the matter, highlighting that this maneuver is not about combating crime—which, by the way, fell by 30% last year—but rather about the "domestic deployment of the military and targeting DC residents." Steele's analysis strips the initiative of any pretense of altruism, branding it as "performative BS" and casting Trump in the role of a #DramaQueen.

The Absurdity of the Spectacle

Amidst the criticism, there's a layer of mockery that cannot be ignored. Charles Johnson, a musician and co-founder of "Little Green Footballs," quips about "sandwich makers to the ramparts!"—a reference to a bizarre incident involving a submarine sandwich and a federal agent. This satirical take underscores the farcical nature of Trump’s announcement, likening it to an overblown production lacking substance and purpose.

The Contrast with Reality

The article cleverly juxtaposes Trump's fear-mongering and self-aggrandizement with the everyday normalcy of former President Joe Biden and his wife, Dr. Jill Biden. The Bidens, unlike Trump, are depicted engaging with the city in mundane, human activities—dining out, attending classes, and even interacting with homeless veterans. This stark contrast serves not only to humanize the Bidens but to highlight Trump's isolation and detachment from the very people he claims to protect.

The Bone Spurs Jab

Rapper N.E. Boddie’s jab about Trump’s "bone spurs" is a scathing reminder of Trump's dubious draft deferment during the Vietnam War. This comment, while humorous, is a cutting indictment of Trump's history of avoiding personal inconvenience or discomfort, while now posturing as a figure of law and order.

The Mall Cop Metaphor

Finally, the AI-generated image of Trump as a "mall cop" shared by Democrat Chip Moynihan encapsulates the essence of Trump's current role: a caricature of authority, wielding power without purpose or legitimacy. This image serves as a biting conclusion to the circus that Trump continues to orchestrate, long after his presidency has ended.

Conclusion

Trump's latest announcement is a masterclass in performative politics, designed not to serve the public interest but to stoke fear, assert control, and feed his insatiable ego. It's a reminder of the dangers of allowing theatrical displays of power to substitute for genuine leadership and empathy. As the criticisms and satirical jabs reveal, Trump's actions are seen by many as nothing more than a desperate attempt to remain relevant in a world that has moved on from his brand of chaos and division. Let's not be distracted by the spectacle but focus on the systemic issues that continue to plague our society.
