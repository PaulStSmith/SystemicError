George Conway warns Trump’s 'delusions' sending GOP 'off the deep end'

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 30, 20262 min read

Trump Owns the Money, the GOP Owns the Humiliation

Republicans are reportedly begging Donald Trump to open up the roughly $400 million sitting in his MAGA.inc PAC for their midterm races, and he is not rushing to help. George Conway, speaking on MS NOW, used the moment to argue that Trump’s greed and fixation on himself make him unreliable to his own party, pointing to Trump’s contemptuous response when Ted Cruz warned of disaster. Whatever one thinks of Conway’s diagnosis, the political fact is simpler: the Republican Party is dependent on a man who treats it like an accessory.

The Power Is Not Hidden

The central question is not whether Trump is emotionally healthy. The central question is who controls the resources. Trump does. That is the whole story. He has the money, the attention, and the authority that Republican senators and candidates now have to petition for like supplicants outside a private office.

This is not a party with an internal strategy. It is a personality cult with a campaign account.

Dependence Is the Mechanism

Republicans did not stumble into this position by accident. They built it. They spent years turning the party into a vehicle for Trump’s personal power, then acted shocked when that same structure produced personal rule instead of collective discipline.

Now the bill is due. Candidates need money. Party leaders need coordination. Trump needs only himself. The asymmetry is not a bug in the system. It is the system working exactly as designed.

Pathology As Cover

Conway’s language tries to explain Trump through illness, delusion, and narcissism. Maybe that makes for television. It does not answer the more important political question: what institutions allowed one man to accumulate this much leverage over an entire major party?

Pathologizing Trump can become a convenient escape hatch. It turns deliberate domination into a psychological case study. It invites the audience to gape at the man while ignoring the Republicans who enabled him, normalized him, and then tethered their own survival to his whim.

The Party Wants Patronage, Not Principles

The panic over Trump’s PAC is revealing because it strips away the mythology. Republicans are not asking for shared purpose or a coherent agenda. They are asking their patron whether he will pay for the machine. That is not party politics in any serious democratic sense. It is feudal bargaining with donor lists.

When Ted Cruz reportedly warned of disaster and Trump answered with contempt, that was not a private flare-up. It was a demonstration of hierarchy. Trump does not owe loyalty to the party because the party already surrendered loyalty to him.

What This Story Actually Shows

The real scandal is not that Trump is selfish. The real scandal is that a major political party built itself around a man whose most reliable political skill is extracting obedience while offering nothing durable in return. The result is a hollowed-out institution that cannot govern, cannot plan, and cannot even reliably finance its own candidates without begging the same figure who helped wreck its credibility.

That is the larger pattern: personalized power destroys collective accountability, then demands to be treated as leadership. Republicans keep mistaking dependence for strength. It is the opposite.
