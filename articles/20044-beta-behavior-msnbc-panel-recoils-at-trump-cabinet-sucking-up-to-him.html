'Beta behavior': MSNBC panel recoils at Trump Cabinet sucking up to him

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theatre of Absurdity: Trump's Cabinet Praise Fest

Mika Brzezinski, Joe Scarborough (MSNBC screenshot)

After watching a supercut of members of Donald Trump’s Cabinet lavishing praise on the president during the three-hour-plus televised meeting on Tuesday, the entire panel on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” made no effort to hide their disgust and embarrassment for the administration officials.

In the clip, one after another official expressed their joy working for Trump, with hedge fund millionaire Scott Bessent, now the treasury secretary, gushing, “Economic security is national security and our country has never been so secure… Your Administration has made a meaningful dent in the budget deficit. The average budget deficit during this term is 26% less than the last twelve months under Biden.”

Not to be outdone, Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer chimed in with, “If you all haven’t stopped by the Department of Labor, Mr. President, I invite you to see your big, beautiful face on a banner in front of the Department of Labor, because you are really the transformational president of the American worker.”

Middle East envoy, Steve Witkoff, added, “Mr. President, working for this government, for you, is the greatest honor of my life. I tell it to everybody, and I really do feel that way, and I thank you because it’s a privilege to go out there and represent you in your humanitarian effort, in your goals of solving conflicts all over the world.”

After running the clip, the cameras caught MSNBC co-hosts Mike Brzezinski and Joe Scarborough with their faces in their hands.

Noting Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick proclaimed he was “having the time of his life,” Scarborough repeated the phrase and added, “... saying things that degrade me as a man.”

As Mika Brzezinski groaned, Scarborough turned to co-host Willie Geist and pointed out, “It continues, this is like part two.”

“You know, at what point does personal pride come in?” Geist remarked. “None of that –– a lot of the guys we just heard from there are these sort of self-described masters of the universe, many of them on Wall Street. The MAGA ethos of masculinity, right? Alpha male, all that; you just don't find more beta behavior.”

“Aren't they the ones that talk about beta males?” Scarborough observed.

Our Analysis:

The Theatre of Absurdity: Trump's Cabinet Praise Fest

In an era where political theatrics often overshadow substantive policy discussions, the latest spectacle from Donald Trump's administration serves as a stark reminder of the depths to which sycophancy can plunge. The recent three-hour-plus televised meeting, as dissected with evident distaste by MSNBC's "Morning Joe" hosts, was less a display of governmental efficiency and more a cringe-inducing tableau of obsequiousness.

The Supercut of Sycophancy: A series of Trump's Cabinet members, including hedge fund millionaire turned Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, took turns at the altar of Trump's ego, offering praise so effusive it bordered on the absurd. Bessent's claim that "Economic security is national security and our country has never been so secure… Your Administration has made a meaningful dent in the budget deficit," flies in the face of readily available data that suggests otherwise. The assertion of a 26% reduction in the average budget deficit during Trump's term compared to the last twelve months under Biden not only lacks context but ignores the broader economic challenges and the fiscal stimulus necessitated by the pandemic.

The Cult of Personality: Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer's invitation to Trump to admire his "big, beautiful face on a banner" at the Department of Labor is a striking example of the personality cult that seems to have engulfed Trump's Cabinet. It's not just about praising the president's policies or leadership; it's a bizarre form of hero worship that would be out of place in most mature democracies.

The Irony of 'Alpha' Posturing: Steve Witkoff's declaration of it being the "greatest honor of my life" to work for Trump, particularly in representing him in "humanitarian effort" and conflict resolution, is almost laughable when one considers the administration's track record in these areas. The Middle East envoy's comments, like those of his colleagues, underscore a disconnect from the realities of Trump's foreign policy, which has often been criticized for its aggressive, isolationist, and transactional approach.

MSNBC co-hosts Mike Brzezinski and Joe Scarborough's reactions—faces in hands, expressions of disbelief and embarrassment—mirror what many viewers likely felt watching the clip. Scarborough's lamentation of the degradation to his manhood by parroting such sycophantic lines and Willie Geist's critique of the "beta behavior" from supposed "masters of the universe" captures the absurdity of the situation perfectly.

The MAGA Ethos and Its Contradictions: The irony of these "alpha" figures from the world of business and finance debasing themselves in a display of unadulterated fealty to Trump highlights a fundamental contradiction within the MAGA movement. The ethos of rugged individualism and masculine bravado is utterly at odds with the reality of these performances of submission and adulation.

In conclusion, this episode is emblematic of a broader issue within American politics where loyalty to personality often trumps adherence to principle or policy. The sycophantic display by Trump's Cabinet members is not just a testament to his cult of personality but a disturbing indication of the erosion of democratic norms where critical thinking and independent judgment are sacrificed at the altar of political expediency and personal ambition. The "Morning Joe" panel's reaction is a necessary voice of reason in an age where such displays of unthinking loyalty are becoming all too common.
