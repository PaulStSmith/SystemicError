'Should not have opened that box': House Republicans condemn own party's 'debacle'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20255 min read

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump and Speaker of the House Mike Johnson shake hands during a House Republican members conference meeting in Trump National Doral resort, in Miami, Florida, U.S. January 27, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz/File Photo

When President Donald Trump called on the Republican-controlled Texas legislature to redraw new U.S. House of Representatives district maps earlier this year, he expressly hoped GOP lawmakers could find five additional Republican seats by carving up Democratic districts. But after federal judges in Texas ruled those maps illegal, some House Republicans are railing against the entire effort.

Politico reported Tuesday evening that the recent 2-1 decision by the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas has some Republicans bemoaning the apparent failed effort in the Lone Star State to give Republicans an advantage ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-Calif.) told reporters that House Speaker Mike Johnson’s (R-La.) refusal to denounce the redistricting push was a “total failure of leadership.” Other Republicans are also speaking out against GOP-led gerrymandering efforts.

“I was never in favor of doing all this redistricting stuff anyway,” Rep. Dan Newhouse (R-Wash.) told Politico. “Should not have opened that box.”

Kiley’s California district is likely to flip Democrat in the wake of https://www.alternet.org/newsom-texas/, which Golden State voters approved by massive margins earlier this month. The Texas decision only applies to the Lone Star State’s new maps and will not impact the new temporary maps created by Prop 50, where Democrats are expected to flip five total seats next year. California-based Republican strategist Rob Stutzman called the Texas gerrymandering gambit a “possible debacle” if the Supreme Court decides to uphold the lower court’s ruling.

“This will have been a ready, shoot, aim exercise by Trump,” Stutzman said.

Should the Texas decision be upheld, the Lone Star State will revert to its 2021 maps, meaning Reps. Greg Casar (D-Texas) and Lloyd Doggett (D-Texas) will no longer be pitted against one another to remain in office. Doggett quoted author Mark Twain on Tuesday, saying “the reports of my death, politically, are greatly exaggerated.” Other Texas Democrats like Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-Texas) — who has been contemplating a U.S. Senate run — will also be able to run for another term in their respective districts.

Trump’s effort to gerrymander Democrats out of their seats in red states has already been running into significant obstacles, as Indiana Senate president pro tempore Rodric Bray said recently he did not have the votes to pass gerrymandered maps through his chamber. New Hampshire Gov. Kelly Ayotte (R) has also poured cold water on Trump’s redistricting campaign despite her state having a Republican trifecta government, saying earlier this year it was “not on the top of [voters’] priority list.”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Gerrymandering Fiasco: Texas and Beyond

In a recent political maneuver that could only be described as audaciously partisan, President Donald Trump called upon the Texas legislature, a bastion of Republican control, to redraw the U.S. House of Representatives district maps. His barely veiled ambition? To dismantle Democratic districts and conjure up five additional Republican seats from the political ether. However, this attempt at political alchemy has not only failed but backfired spectacularly, thanks to a decisive 2-1 ruling by the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas.

Judicial Rebuff and Republican Rifts

The court’s ruling slices through the GOP’s redistricting ambitions like a hot knife through butter, marking a significant setback for Republicans hoping to tilt the playing field in their favor ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. The fallout has been swift and severe, with House Republicans, notably Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-Calif.), lambasting House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) for his leadership—or the glaring lack thereof. Kiley’s criticism is not just a lone voice in the wilderness; other Republicans have echoed his sentiments, expressing regret over opening Pandora’s box of redistricting.

Rep. Dan Newhouse (R-Wash.) succinctly summed up the sentiment, indicating a clear discomfort with the gerrymandering efforts. This internal dissent within the Republican ranks highlights a crisis of conscience and strategy, suggesting a rift between those who still believe in fair play and others who seem all too willing to distort democratic boundaries for political gain.

The California Counterpoint

The article also touches upon the repercussions of these gerrymandering efforts beyond Texas, particularly in California. Here, voters approved Prop 50 by overwhelming margins, a move likely to see the state’s political landscape shift significantly, with Democrats expected to gain five seats. This serves as a stark contrast to the GOP’s failed redistricting efforts in Texas, showcasing a potential backlash against overt partisan manipulation of electoral boundaries.

Republican strategist Rob Stutzman’s remarks encapsulate the debacle, framing it as a haphazard exercise led by Trump. Stutzman’s analysis points to a broader issue within the GOP: a readiness to engage in political gambits without fully considering the consequences or viability of such actions.

A Ray of Hope in Texas

Despite the turmoil, the decision to revert to the 2021 maps in Texas offers a lifeline to Democrats like Reps. Greg Casar and Lloyd Doggett, who were previously set to clash due to the now-nullified redistricting efforts. Doggett’s invocation of Mark Twain to describe his political resurgence is both apt and telling, symbolizing not just his personal victory but also a broader triumph for democratic principles over partisan gerrymandering.

A Pattern of Resistance

Texas isn’t an isolated incident. Trump’s gerrymandering crusade has encountered resistance in other states like Indiana and New Hampshire, where local Republican leaders have expressed skepticism or outright opposition to redrawing maps for partisan advantage. This pattern of resistance underscores a growing realization within parts of the GOP that democracy’s integrity must not be sacrificed on the altar of political expediency.

Conclusion: A Democracy Tested

The entire episode is a stark reminder of the dangers posed by gerrymandering to the fabric of American democracy. It’s not just about partisan advantage; it’s about the fundamental principles of fair representation and electoral integrity. As the GOP grapples with the backlash from its own ranks and the judiciary, one thing becomes clear: the battle for the soul of American democracy is far from over, and it will require vigilance, integrity, and a commitment to fairness from all sides of the political spectrum.

In the end, the gerrymandering saga, with its twists and turns, serves as a critical lesson in political hubris and the enduring power of democratic institutions to correct course—even in the face of seemingly insurmountable odds.
