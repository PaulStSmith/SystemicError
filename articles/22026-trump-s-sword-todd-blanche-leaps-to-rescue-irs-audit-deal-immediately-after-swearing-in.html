'Trump's sword': Todd Blanche leaps to rescue IRS audit deal immediately after swearing-in

Based on an article by David Edwards

Trump Got the Justice Department He Paid For

Power on Display

The key fact is simple: the Justice Department is now acting to protect Donald Trump’s private legal bargain from judicial scrutiny. Todd Blanche, sworn in as attorney general on Monday, moved the next day to help rescue a settlement that shielded Trump, his family, and his businesses from IRS audits after his side dropped a $10 billion lawsuit against the agency.

That is not an abstract legal controversy. It is the federal law-enforcement apparatus being turned toward the defense of the president’s personal interests.

The Settlement Was the Story

The source material lays out the arrangement plainly enough: Trump’s settlement bought audit protection in exchange for ending a massive claim against the IRS. Judge Kathleen Williams called enough attention to the deal that she later issued sanctions against Trump’s legal team and barred anyone from referring to the agreement in official proceedings.

That matters because the fight is not really over paperwork. It is over whether a powerful political figure can convert public institutions into instruments of private immunity, then relabel the result as ordinary litigation.

Blanche’s Job Was Obvious

Blanche’s move was not a surprise, and that should be the scandal, not the excuse. Cory Booker said during the confirmation fight that Blanche would become Trump’s “sword” and “shield.” Adam Schiff said he would never do what is best for the American people, only what is best for Donald Trump. The timing vindicates the warning.

Blanche also gave written assurances to Senators John Cornyn and Thom Tillis before confirmation, including promises to limit the scope of the audit deal and kill the so-called “anti-weaponization fund.” Those guarantees bought him the vote. Then, once in office, his department filed to overturn the judge’s order anyway. That is not institutional confusion. That is political compliance with a paper trail.

The Real Enablers

It is tempting to treat this as a story about Blanche alone. That would be too narrow. The Senate confirmed him 50-49. Cornyn and Tillis extracted promises, then moved on after getting the formal language they wanted. Republican resistance stopped where Trump’s personnel power began.

Even the filing’s language performs the same trick the settlement did: it recasts a politically loaded arrangement as a sincere legal dispute. Acting Deputy Attorney General R. Trent McCotter argues that Trump “indeed did suffer significant harm” and that the case could not have taken 109 days to settle if it were collusive. That is argument by procedural vibes, not by innocence. A fast settlement is not proof of wrongdoing, but a settlement that hands a president and his family audit immunity after a $10 billion lawsuit is not normal either.

The Pattern Is Corruption With Better Stationery

This is the governing model: use public power to launder private advantage, then wrap the result in legal language and call it process. The complaint about “fraud-on-the-court” sanctions is not a technical dispute over civility. It is a test of whether courts will tolerate a settlement that looks, in the judge’s view, like a deal hidden in plain sight.

The larger pattern is ugly and familiar. Trump’s operation relies on loyalists inside institutions, legalistic camouflage, and enough partisan discipline to keep obvious conflicts moving under procedural cover. The point is not to persuade everyone the arrangement is fair. The point is to make accountability feel slow, specialized, and optional.

The Lesson

The story is not that the system is confused. The system is being used.

Trump’s Justice Department is protecting a settlement that protected Trump. Senate Republicans got reassurances, then helped clear the path. A judge noticed the shape of the deal and tried to interrupt it. That is the conflict. Everything else is cover.
