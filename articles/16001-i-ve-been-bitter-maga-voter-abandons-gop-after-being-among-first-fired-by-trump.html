'I've been bitter': MAGA voter abandons GOP after being among first fired by Trump

A Republican voter in Northern Virginia who backed President Donald Trump since 2016 says she's finished with the GOP, the Huffington Post reported.

The story highlighted a number of Trump voters who regretted their votes for a whole host of reasons, from his mismanagement of the pandemic in 2020, to the 2019 Uvalde school shooting in Texas, to one voter who came to see his presidency as an affront to Christian values.

But a 58-year-old voter identified as Denise in Virginia had a personal reason for the turnabout: Trump, and tech billionaire Elon Musk, eliminated her veteran healthcare position.

"Two days after I celebrated him taking office in 2025, he cut my position within the federal government," said Denise. "I am a 21-year federal employee who took a position last year in the Office of Equity Assurance at the VA (Veterans Affairs). In that capacity, we were working to assist underserved veteran populations within the U.S. and its territories: rural and tribal veterans, women veterans, minorities, senior veterans, homeless vets. We were working to figure out what disparities these groups face because of where they live, and trying to offer them a more equal chance at obtaining VA benefits."

Unfortunately, Denise said, this was classified as a "diversity, equity, and inclusion" position, "and Trump signed an executive order following his inauguration which eliminated all DEI offices within the federal government. His EO technically ended our positions, but because of our tenure, veterans status, longevity, and performance status within VA, our senior leadership was planning to reassign each of us to other areas of the Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) ― but Elon Musk and DOGE canceled those reassignment plans and terminated us via Reduction in Force (RIF)."

"I’ve been pretty bitter ever since," Denise added. "I will be moving towards becoming an Independent from now on."

Denise's story is hardly unique — a number of other Trump voters working within the federal government have ended up fired as a result of Trump's policies and come forward to tell their stories, including a disabled veteran who provided IT services for the Federal Emergency Management Administration (FEMA), and an IRS agent who warned the president is "destroying people's lives."

Even some outspoken Trump-supporting celebrities have grown uncomfortable with this situation; in February, "Shazam!" lead actor Zachary Levi, a longtime MAGA booster, begged the president on Fox News to show some restraint with the layoffs: “There are a lot of people that are very afraid right now because there are truly good working people that work for the government, that are getting lost in the cracks.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fallout: Trump, Musk, and the GOP's Alienated Base

In a recent exposé by the Huffington Post, a narrative unfolds that is as predictable as it is tragic. Among the disillusioned ranks of former President Donald Trump's supporters, personal stories of regret and betrayal have begun to surface, painting a somber picture of a base abandoned. But none so poignant, perhaps, as that of Denise, a 58-year-old veteran healthcare worker from Northern Virginia, whose testimony serves as a damning indictment of Trump's administration and its allies, including tech mogul Elon Musk.

Denise's account is a stark reminder of the human cost of political decisions, a detail often lost in the broader discussions of policy and ideology. After dedicating 21 years to federal service, her career was abruptly cut short by an executive order signed by Trump, eliminating all diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) offices within the federal government—a move that, in no uncertain terms, speaks volumes about the administration's priorities. The subsequent cancellation of her reassignment by Musk and DOGE, leading to her termination, is a further blow, highlighting a capricious disregard for the livelihoods of those deemed expendable in the pursuit of a narrower vision for America.

Denise's disillusionment and her decision to become an Independent is symptomatic of a larger trend within the GOP's base. The stories of a disabled veteran providing IT services for FEMA, an IRS agent, and even celebrity supporters like Zachary Levi, all echo a similar sentiment of abandonment and disillusionment. These are individuals who believed in a promise—a promise of a better America, safeguarded by its leaders. Instead, they found themselves victims of the very administration they supported, casualties of policies that seemed more focused on ideological purity than on the welfare of its citizens.

The Fallout

The repercussions of such policies and decisions are manifold. First and foremost, they underscore the human cost of political maneuvering, a cost too often overlooked. The lives disrupted and careers terminated are not mere collateral damage; they are a testament to the real-world impact of governance—or the lack thereof.

Moreover, these narratives highlight a growing rift within the Republican Party, a rift between its leadership and its base. When lifelong supporters, especially those who have dedicated their careers to public service, find themselves cast aside, the message is clear: loyalty to the party does not guarantee protection or reciprocation. This disillusionment could have significant electoral ramifications, as more individuals like Denise reconsider their political affiliations and allegiances.

A Cautionary Tale

Denise's story, and those like it, should serve as a cautionary tale for the GOP. The party's embrace of divisive policies and figures has not only alienated a segment of its base but has also sown seeds of dissent among those who once were its staunchest defenders. As the GOP looks to the future, it must confront this reality and reckon with the consequences of its actions.

In the end, the saga of Denise and her fellow disillusioned Trump supporters is a microcosm of a larger political and cultural malaise. It's a reminder that behind every policy, every executive order, there are real people, with real lives, aspirations, and contributions to society—people who deserve better than to be treated as expendable pawns in a game of political chess. The GOP, and indeed all political leaders, would do well to remember this.
