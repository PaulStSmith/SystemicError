When holiday celebrations are done President Donald Trump will be facing “daunting challenges in 2026,” said USA Today writer Bart Jansen.

The lingering impasse over government spending is brewing in the House and Senate and Jansen said Americans will be facing a health care cost spike in 2026 now that Republicans and Trump have let Obamacare subsidies expire.

“The political disputes about spending − and the possibility of another federal government shutdown − will play out while waiting for a Supreme Court decision that could overturn tariffs central to the president’s economic agenda that are projected to generate more than $3.3 trillion over the next decade,” said Jansen.

Foreign affairs are also volatile for Trump next year, said Jansen with Trump shuffling toward a peace agreement in the Russian war with Ukraine that’s heading into its fourth year. He’s also struggling to keep the ceasefire in Gaza alive amid isolated skirmishes and bloodshed between Israel and Hamas.

Add to this the Justice Department’s maddening trickle of documents concerning the investigations into convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein , which is dominating the political debate.

Jansen said Trump’s thoroughly dominated Congress Republican Party only approved 61 laws in 2025, reflecting harsh party polarization. Generally, lawmakers approve more than 300 bills in each two-year Congress.

“[But] Republicans have little room to maneuver with majorities of 53-47 in the Senate and 220-213 in the House,” Jansen said. “The House margin will narrow when Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, R-Georgia, leaves Jan. 5. Two seats previously held by Democrats will be filled with special elections in Texas on Jan. 31 and in New Jersey on April 16.”

“And midterm elections — when the president’s party traditionally loses seats — loom in November for the entire House and one-third of the Senate.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Article on Trump Facing Challenges in 2026

The article from USA Today outlines a series of significant challenges awaiting President Donald Trump as he navigates through 2026. The narrative weaves through a labyrinth of domestic and international dilemmas, each casting a long shadow over Trump’s administration. Let’s dissect the key points with the precision of a political surgeon, shall we?

The Domestic Arena: A Fiscal Fiasco and Health Care Havoc

The article kicks off with a dire warning: Trump will be greeted by “daunting challenges” in 2026, primary among them being the “lingering impasse over government spending.” This isn’t just a small hiccup in the grand scheme of things. It’s a symptom of a diseased political system where brinkmanship trumps (pun intended) rational policymaking. The potential for another federal government shutdown looms large, a scenario that would further erode public trust in government efficiency and effectiveness.

Health care costs, meanwhile, are set to spike, thanks to the expiration of Obamacare subsidies. This isn’t just a policy oversight; it’s a failure of governance that directly affects the wallets and well-being of millions of Americans. The Republicans, with Trump at the helm, have effectively pulled the rug out from under the feet of those reliant on these subsidies for affordable health care. The consequences? Likely catastrophic for countless families.

The International Stage: Tariffs, Tensions, and Turmoil

Jansen moves on to describe a volatile foreign affairs landscape, highlighting Trump’s struggles on multiple fronts. The Supreme Court decision on tariffs is a ticking time bomb that could dismantle key aspects of Trump’s economic agenda. Over $3.3 trillion is at stake here. Let that sink in for a moment. This isn’t just about numbers on a balance sheet; it’s about the livelihood of Americans and the stability of global markets.

Then there’s the peace agreement in the Russian war with Ukraine and the ceasefire in Gaza. Trump is depicted as barely keeping these situations from spiraling out of control. These aren’t just diplomatic chess games; they’re real conflicts with real human casualties. The article paints a picture of an administration scrambling to maintain peace, with mixed results.

The Political Landscape: A Congress in Crisis

The article doesn’t pull punches when it comes to Trump’s challenges on the home front, particularly with Congress. The Republican Party, described as “thoroughly dominated” by Trump, only managed to pass 61 laws in 2025. This isn’t just unproductive; it’s historically dismal. It underscores a profound level of dysfunction and polarization that cripples the legislative process.

The narrow majorities in both the Senate and the House are a recipe for legislative gridlock. The upcoming departure of Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene and the special elections in Texas and New Jersey add layers of uncertainty. And with midterm elections on the horizon, the traditional loss of seats by the president’s party could further complicate Trump’s political calculus.

Conclusion: A Presidency at a Crossroads

This article from USA Today provides a grim forecast for President Trump in 2026, marked by domestic policy failures, international instability, and legislative lethargy. It’s a stark reminder of the complex web of challenges that await national leaders and the profound impact their decisions (or lack thereof) have on the lives of everyday people.

In essence, Trump’s presidency appears to be at a crossroads, with the potential for crisis looming on every front. Whether he can navigate these treacherous waters remains to be seen, but one thing is clear: the stakes could not be higher.
