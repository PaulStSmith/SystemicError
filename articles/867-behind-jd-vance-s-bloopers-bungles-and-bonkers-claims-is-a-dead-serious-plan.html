Behind JD Vance's bloopers, bungles and bonkers claims is a dead serious plan

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

JD Vance and the Politics of Useful Bigotry

Power Is the Point

The story is not really about an especially erratic vice president. It is about how power gets built, packaged, and laundered. JD Vance sits close to the center of the Trump regime, but the article makes clear that his real function is not policy craftsmanship or legislative brokerage. It is amplification: he gives elite racism and anti-democratic ambition a younger face, a cleaner sentence, and a veneer of intellectual seriousness.

That matters because the people directing this machine are not confused. Peter Thiel, Elon Musk, Curtis Yarvin, and their circle are described here as committed to a politics that treats democracy as an obstacle to wealth, not a principle to preserve. Vance is not wandering into this project by accident. He is being cultivated for it.

The Lie Is the Feature

The source gives the clearest evidence of Vance’s method in Springfield and Southampton. In both cases, he took real violence or real hardship and converted it into a preexisting racial story. In Springfield, he repeated the claim that Haitian immigrants were eating pets even after local officials contradicted it. In Britain, he blamed “migrants” for a murder committed by a man who was born and raised in Britain.

That is not misunderstanding. It is political arson with a talking point attached.

The article is right to treat these episodes as more than sloppy rhetoric. Vance is not merely wrong. He is using grotesque falsehoods to direct public anger downward, toward people with less power and less institutional protection. The lie is not a side effect of the message. The lie is the message.

Who Benefits

The source’s strongest argument is that Vance’s bigotry is functional. It distracts attention from the people who actually hold leverage: billionaires, political patrons, and the authoritarian intellectuals who want state power without democratic restraint. When Vance talks about “civilizational decline,” he is not identifying the cause of social breakdown. He is selling a scapegoat that protects the architects of inequality.

This is the old bargain of reactionary politics with a tech-age upgrade. Rage gets directed at migrants, minorities, bureaucrats, judges, and the press. Meanwhile, the people funding the operation move wealth upward and concentrate decision-making downward, away from public accountability and toward private command.

Misdirection as Strategy

The article frames Vance as a mouthpiece for “Western civilization” panic, but that language itself is part of the fraud. Civilization talk is doing the work of displacement. It turns a political project of domination into a defense of order. It makes oligarchic ambition sound like cultural preservation.

The more useful frame is simpler: this is elite misdirection. The attack on migrants and “self-hatred” is not mainly about migrants. It is about teaching the public to misread who has power and who uses it. It is about making mass resentment available for authoritarian use while keeping its real sponsors sheltered behind philanthropy, venture capital, and nationalist slogans.

The Anti-Democratic Coalition

The source connects Vance to a broader network of anti-democratic ideologues and financiers who want a “Caesar-like” executive and a state run like a startup. That is not a metaphor to admire. It is a blueprint for rule by owners who prefer obedience to deliberation.

The article is strongest when it stops pretending this is fringe theater. It is a coalition with money, media reach, and access to state power. It wants bureaucrats purged, courts overridden, and democratic constraints recast as decadence. Vance’s role is to translate that project into grievance politics for a mass audience that will never see the spreadsheet.

The Pattern

The recurring pattern is not chaos. It is authoritarian cruelty disguised as cultural diagnosis. First the story invents a social enemy. Then it assigns blame to the weak. Then it protects the powerful by insisting the real problem is decay, not capture.

That is the larger political meaning here. Vance is not just a crude liar; he is a bridge between billionaire anti-democrats and the public resentments they need to govern. The danger is not that he misunderstands reality. The danger is that he understands exactly how to weaponize it.
