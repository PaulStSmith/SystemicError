'Quite a claim': Observers stunned by MAGA's 'delusional' claim about Charlie Kirk's life

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Martyrdom of Charlie Kirk: A MAGA Manufactured Myth

Roses and candles are placed next to a picture of Charlie Kirk during a vigil in Berlin, Germany. REUTERS/Annegret Hilse

Observers slammed the claim that slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk is a martyr, a claim that multiple MAGA politicians repeated during Kirk's memorial service on Sunday in Glendale, Arizona.

Several high-profile MAGA members spoke at Kirk's memorial, such as President Donald Trump, Vice President JD Vance, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, and MAGA podcaster Benny Johnson. Some of the speakers compared Kirk to Stephen, who is the Christian religion's first martyr.

The claims stirred intense debate on social media.

"Benny Johnson, who has a history of plagiarizing & passing along fake news, compares Kirk to Stephen the 1st Christian martyr by claiming Kirk & Stephen were the same age," Baptist minister Brian Kaylor posted on Bluesky. "Quite a claim since we don't actually know Stephen's age."

"It’s perfect that the first MAGA martyr is a ghoulish mediocrity comprised of nothing but hate and grift," journalist Dorian Lynskey posted on Bluesky. "Instead of trying to invent a noble, decent Kirk that didn’t exist, they should lean into that quintessence of MAGA."

"Did Saint Stephen call for executing a political foe, as Charlie Kirk did with President Biden in 2023?" journalist David Corn posted on Bluesky.

"These people have never heard Kirk's abusive attacks on others or they are people with very bad values and characters," Victoria Johnson, professor emerita of politics and history, posted on X.

"The normies, the majority, can spot a dangerous cult," leftwing writer Wajahat Ali posted on X. "That's what MAGA has become."

"Charlie Kirk was killed by a white man from a Christian family deep in MAGA land," consultant Hashim Mteuzi posted on X. "And somehow these folks call him a martyr. These people are delusional."

Our Analysis:

The Martyrdom of Charlie Kirk: A MAGA Manufactured Myth

In a spectacle that could only emerge from the depths of MAGA's ever-twisting narrative, the memorial service for Charlie Kirk unfolded in Glendale, Arizona, with a parade of high-profile MAGA politicians elevating Kirk to a status that has stirred both ire and incredulity across the political spectrum. Among them, notable figures such as President Donald Trump, Vice President JD Vance, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, and MAGA podcaster Benny Johnson have not just eulogized Kirk; they've attempted to canonize him in the annals of conservative martyrdom, drawing parallels to Stephen, the first Christian martyr. This comparison not only stretches credulity but also exposes the MAGA movement's penchant for myth-making at the expense of factual integrity and decency.

The Absurdity of the Comparison

Benny Johnson, a figure already marred by a history of plagiarism and dissemination of misinformation, makes a particularly eye-roll-inducing leap in logic by likening Kirk to Stephen, despite the glaring lack of historical evidence regarding Stephen's age—or, more significantly, the context of their respective 'martyrdoms'. This analogy is as flawed as it is revealing, shedding light on the MAGA movement's desperate scramble for a narrative that could cloak their ideology in the garb of religious and historical martyrdom.

MAGA's Ghoulish Mediocrity

Journalist Dorian Lynskey's characterization of Charlie Kirk as a "ghoulish mediocrity comprised of nothing but hate and grift" slices through the memorial's sanctimonious veneer to reveal the essence of MAGA's rallying around Kirk. The attempt to posthumously polish Kirk's image into that of a noble figure not only fails to align with his public persona and statements but also underscores MAGA's broader strategy of championing divisive and hateful figures as heroes — so long as they serve the movement's agenda.

The Hypocrisy of Martyrdom

David Corn's pointed question regarding Kirk's call for executing political foes juxtaposes the stark moral dissonance in MAGA's narrative. The leap from advocating violence against political adversaries to being honored as a martyr not only stretches the bounds of hypocrisy but also blatantly ignores the fundamental teachings of forgiveness and peace central to Christian martyrdom narratives.

MAGA's Dangerous Cult of Personality

The voices of critique, from Baptist minister Brian Kaylor to leftwing writer Wajahat Ali, converge on a crucial point: the MAGA movement's transformation into what many perceive as a dangerous cult. This cult thrives on revisionist history, conspiracy theories, and a disconcerting willingness to overlook the vitriolic and divisive rhetoric of its champions, as long as it serves their ideological ends. Hashim Mteuzi's observation that Kirk was killed by an individual deeply embedded in MAGA's own demographic yet is still hailed as a martyr by the movement highlights the delusional lengths to which MAGA adherents will go to maintain their narrative.

In sum, the attempt to cast Charlie Kirk as a martyr is not merely a misstep in judgment but a calculated move in MAGA's ongoing effort to mythologize and sanctify its political agenda. This narrative, fraught with hypocrisy, misinformation, and a glaring disregard for historical and moral truths, further cements MAGA's legacy as one of division and deceit. In their quest for a martyr, MAGA politicians have instead unveiled the movement's true quintessence: a dangerous blend of willful ignorance and ideological fanaticism.
