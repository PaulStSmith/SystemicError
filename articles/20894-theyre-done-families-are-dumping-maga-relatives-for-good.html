They’re done: Families are dumping MAGA relatives for good

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Family Rift Is Not the Story. The Vote Is.

This Was a Decision, Not a Mystery

The source article starts with holiday estrangement and ends where it should: at power. The real rupture is not that relatives have become "too political." The rupture is that people voted for a machine built to target the people around them, and now want the social consequences to disappear on command.

The Fox-host framing of "progressive cruelty" is itself a dodge. It pretends the injury began when queer kids, immigrant families, and people with working memories stopped playing nice. It did not. The injury began when voters chose a candidate and a coalition that had already announced, in public, exactly what they intended to do.

Trump Did Not Hide the Project

This is not a case of voters being misled by a vague promise about groceries. The source lays out the record plainly: Trump ran on racism in 2015, repeated it in 2024, and brought Stephen Miller's deportation fantasy with him. Miller did not whisper this agenda. He advertised it for years. The Heritage Foundation did not draft a neutral policy memo. It published a blueprint for ethnic and political reengineering, and the administration is executing it.

That matters because responsibility sits at the top. Trump made the decision. Miller engineered the apparatus. JD Vance helped launder the transition from public disgust to open collaboration. The voters who now claim surprise were not denied the terms. They were offered the terms and accepted them.

The Lie of "Border Security"

The administration's favorite alibi is economic necessity, followed by criminal panic. Both are thin. The source notes undocumented immigrants paid $96.7 billion in taxes in 2022, and in 40 states they paid a higher effective state and local tax rate than the top 1 percent. So the "they drain us" line is not analysis. It is propaganda for people who want cruelty to sound like budgeting.

The crime story is just as hollow. ICE's own figures, as cited in the source, show a tiny number of sexual offense arrests compared with the scale of sexual violence in the United States. The point of the racial panic was never accuracy. It was permission. When a regime wants to build fear around brown bodies, statistics are only there to slow the audience down long enough to miss the knife.

ICE Is the Point

The article comes close to saying the quiet part out loud: ICE is not a broken agency that needs better training. It is the instrument. More than 675,000 deportations, a swollen detention system, rising deaths in custody, suicides, medical neglect, and "very large staging facilities" are not accidental side effects. They are the operating logic of a deportation state.

That is why "oversight" is a fake answer. Oversight does not civilize a structure built to disappear people, then rationalize the deaths with a percentage point. If an agency doubles the detained population without the staff to keep people alive, that is not dysfunction in a liberal sense. It is institutionalized indifference with a badge. ICE should be abolished.

The Real Misdirection

The most useful lie in this story is the idea that the offended families are being petty. They are not. They are responding to a material fact: someone in the family voted for the people now trying to erase their neighbors, their spouses, their children, and in many cases them. Refusing intimacy with that choice is not emotional excess. It is moral clarity.

What the right calls "Trump Derangement Syndrome" is actually a demand that other people absorb the consequences of your vote without protest. It asks the targeted to perform warmth toward their own persecution so the persecutor can keep dinner plans. That is not a social misunderstanding. It is a pressure campaign against conscience.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple. Authoritarian politics does not begin with camps and mass detention. It begins with normalization, with people insisting that the brutal thing is not really what it looks like, that the targets are overreacting, and that everyone should just get along. Then the institutions move. Then the deaths accumulate. Then the same people who voted for the machinery act shocked that anyone took them seriously.

The source article is strongest when it stops pretending this is about awkward family gatherings and names the system underneath. That is the only honest frame: power chose racial punishment, institutions carried it out, and ordinary people are now deciding whether to keep pretending that consequence is rudeness.
