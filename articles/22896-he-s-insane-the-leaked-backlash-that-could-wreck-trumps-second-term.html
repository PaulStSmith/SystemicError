He's insane: The leaked backlash that could wreck Trump’s second term

Based on an article by John Stoehr

Paulo SantosSeptember 2, 20263 min read

A Presidency Built on Noise, Not Government

The Performance Is the Point

The source is about Donald Trump flooding the feed with AI fantasies: “Lake America,” machine-gun geese, desert explosions, and Kharg Island “blown to smithereens.” That matters less as content than as method. The president is using state-adjacent spectacle to replace governing with attention capture, while the country absorbs the costs in war, inflation, and institutional fatigue.

Who Actually Has Power

Trump holds the institutional power here. He is the one posting the propaganda, bragging about the economy, dismissing critics as “backwards and poor,” and driving the public frame. But he is not acting alone. The vice president tells him what he wants to hear. The “secretary of war” plays along with jokes and posture. Pentagon leaders issue a “non-concur” and then comply anyway. That is the machinery of power in the piece: not a single madman, but an executive system that keeps executing even as it loses confidence in itself.

Misdirection as Governance

The article’s strongest evidence of misdirection is how Trump tries to turn obvious damage into performance art. He posts fantasy war clips while real war grinds on. He claims food costs are coming down while the article cites warning signs of rising food inflation. He boasts about record employment with a claim that collapses under basic arithmetic. This is not confusion. It is an attempt to bury consequences under noise, to make repetition do the work of reality.

That tactic only works because the media environment rewards it. Each absurd post becomes a new object for outrage, which drains attention from the underlying failures: a war that is “too much for too long,” a weak economic story, and a government that cannot manage its own story without turning it into propaganda.

The War Is the Test

The most serious material in the source is not the Lake Ontario clowning. It is the Pentagon memo saying the war against Iran is unsustainable, that it weakens the ability to confront China, and that multiple commands and the Navy’s top admiral issued a “non-concur” while still carrying out the order. That is not a healthy command structure. It is a chain of obedience detached from judgment.

The article is right to note that military leaders have little reason to trust the commander-in-chief. But the larger problem is not simply Trump’s erratic behavior. It is that the state can be driven into a strategic dead end and still keep moving because the bureaucracy is built to obey first and explain later. That is how disastrous wars continue after their political rationale has collapsed.

The Economy as Backdrop, Not Constraint

The article also places Trump’s propaganda against a deteriorating economic picture: job losses over nine months, weak labor growth relative to population needs, and analysts warning of more food inflation ahead. That matters because authoritarian politics thrives when material pain is denied or rerouted into fantasy.

Trump’s answer is not policy. It is insult, denial, and cheerleading for data centers as if “Data Reign” were an economic program. The point is not that he is lying badly. The point is that his coalition no longer needs economic truth to stay intact so long as the spectacle remains loud enough.

The Pattern Behind the Farce

This is the broader pattern: a president who uses absurdity to cover failure, a cabinet that flatters instead of constrains, and institutions that obey while privately admitting the mission is broken. The result is not just incompetence. It is a political order that converts crisis into content and calls that leadership.

The Democratic response cannot be to treat this as merely weird or embarrassing. It is a power arrangement. The country is being governed through intimidation, delusion, and managed surrender, while the people with formal authority keep handing the microphone back to the man wrecking the room.
