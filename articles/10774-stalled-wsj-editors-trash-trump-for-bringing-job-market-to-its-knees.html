'Stalled': WSJ editors trash Trump for bringing job market to its knees

The conservative Wall Street Journal editorial board took stock of the latest dismal jobs report on Friday — and laid a key part of the blame for it at the feet of President Donald Trump.

"Friday’s monthly report for August confirms that job creation has stalled amid his tariff barrage," wrote the board, a frequent critic of Trump's trade policy. "Employers added a mere 22,000 jobs last month while the numbers were revised down for the previous two by a combined 21,000. This means only 107,000 new jobs were created in the last four months — an average of 27,000. Monthly job gains averaged 167,000 last year."

Even worse, the board noted, "Nearly all of the new jobs last month were in social assistance and healthcare (46,800), which rely on government spending," while manufacturing saw a loss of 38,000 jobs — meaning the public sector is propping up the Trump economy from total freefall.

It's not a mystery why any of this is happening, the board continued.

"The Occam’s razor explanation is the uncertainty and additional costs from Mr. Trump’s border taxes," they wrote. "Caterpillar estimates that tariffs will cost the equipment maker $1.8 billion this year. Deere projects a tariff hit of about $600 million, mainly from higher steel and aluminum costs. Deere is also hurting because soybean farmers have seen their market share in China shrink after its trade retaliation. Tariffs are slamming U.S. auto makers like Ford ($2 billion tariff cost this year)."

And even though Trump has done everything in his power to open up federal regulations for an oil drilling spree, "oil and gas producers say the tariffs have increased prices for materials and caused them to pull back on drilling."

The only real hope for the economy at this point, the board wrote, is if Trump loses his appeal to the Supreme Court after lower courts found his tariff scheme unconstitutional.

"What Mr. Trump needs is a broad revival in business confidence of the kind that accompanied his November victory and appeared before his tax on imports and willy-nilly interventions in private business decisions," the board concluded. "Repeat after us: Tariffs are taxes, and taxes hurt economic growth."

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Tangle: Unraveling Trump's Economic Missteps

In a recent scathing critique, the Wall Street Journal editorial board has attributed a significant portion of the blame for the latest abysmal jobs report to none other than President Donald Trump. Their analysis, grounded in the harsh realities of economic figures and corporate testimonies, paints a vivid picture of a presidency mired in misguided trade policies. Let's dissect their arguments and the broader implications for the American economy.

Job Creation Stagnation: The Stark Numbers

The editorial board doesn't mince words when it describes job creation under Trump's tenure as "stalled." With a mere 22,000 jobs added in August and revisions that paint an even bleaker picture for the preceding months, the figures are indeed alarming. The average of 27,000 new jobs in the last four months starkly contrasts with the healthier 167,000 monthly job gains of the previous year. This slowdown is especially concerning given the board's attribution of nearly all new jobs to sectors like social assistance and healthcare, which are buoyed by government spending, not a robust private sector.

The Culprit: Tariffs

"Tariffs are taxes, and taxes hurt economic growth." This succinct summation by the editorial board cuts to the heart of the issue. Trump's tariffs, described as a "barrage," have introduced uncertainty and inflicted additional costs on American businesses, from giants like Caterpillar and Ford to soybean farmers feeling the crunch of lost market share in China. The specific figures cited—$1.8 billion in costs for Caterpillar, $600 million for Deere, and $2 billion for Ford this year alone—underscore the tangible, detrimental impact of these tariffs on the economy.

Misguided Energy Policies

While Trump has aggressively deregulated to spur an oil drilling frenzy, the tariffs have paradoxically increased prices for materials, leading to a reduction in drilling activities. This contradiction highlights a fundamental flaw in Trump's economic strategy: the left hand undermines what the right hand seeks to achieve.

The Hope for a Turnaround

The editorial board posits that the only real hope for the economy may lie in the Supreme Court's hands, which is considering the constitutionality of Trump's tariff scheme. This reliance on judicial intervention to correct executive missteps is a damning indictment of the current administration's economic policies.

Conclusion: A Call for Economic Rationality

The Wall Street Journal editorial board's critique is a clarion call for a return to economic sanity. The evidence is clear: Trump's tariff policies have not only stalled job growth but also placed an undue burden on both consumers and producers. The appeal to basic economic principles—highlighted by the board's reminder that tariffs are effectively taxes—should resonate with anyone concerned about the health of the American economy.

In sum, the editorial serves not just as a rebuke of Trump's trade policies but as a broader warning against the perils of economic nationalism. As history has repeatedly shown, protectionism is a precarious path that often leads to more harm than good. The hope now is that this message resonates beyond the pages of the Wall Street Journal and into the policy formulations of the current and future administrations.
