‘Didn’t happen’: Trump’s head-scratching claim smacked down by governor's staff

President Donald Trump trashed Baltimore, Maryland, during the signing of an executive order on Monday, claiming that Gov. Wes Moore had given him glowing praise. The reality, however, is something very different.

"It's a deathbed," Trump said of Baltimore. "I know Gov. Moore said, 'Oh, he wants to take a walk with me.' He meant it in a derogatory tone. I said, no, no, no, I'm the president of the United States. Clean up your crime and I'll walk with you. But there's — it's a tremendous crime. But he was trying to be derogatory. He doesn't have what it takes, but he's trying to be derogatory."

He recalled meeting Moore at the Army-Navy game and claimed that at the time, the governor gushed with praise.

"They said, 'Oh, there's Gov. Moore, he'd love to see you,'" recalled Trump from the Oval Office. "He came over to me. He hugged me, shook my hand. You were there. He said, 'Sir, you're the greatest president in my lifetime.' I said, 'It's really nice that you say that, I'd love you to say it publicly. But I don't think you can do that.' So, it's OK. But no. 'Sir, you're doing a fantastic job. I want to just shake your hand.'"

"Thank you very much, governor," Trump said he told Moore.

"And then, uh, every time I see him on television, he's knocking the hell out of me," Trump continued. "So, but that's alright. That's called politics."

Raw Story reached out to Moore's campaign about the incident to ask if that was the way the interaction unfolded.

"Didn't happen," communications director Carter Elliott said about Trump's rant.

"There's literally a video of the whole interaction," he added.

Trump and Moore have spent the past week feuding on social media and on television. At one point, Moore invited Trump to take a walk with him in Baltimore. But twice, so far, Trump has refused, indicating it was too dangerous for him.

"President Bone Spurs will do anything to get out of walking," Moore quipped in response to Trump on X over the weekend. Trump turned down his offer to take a walk in Baltimore. "Even if that means spouting off more lies about the progress we're making on public safety in Maryland. Hey Donald, we can get you a golf cart if that makes things easier. Just let my team know."

The governor served 16 years in the U.S. Army, including service in combat, and he has participated as governor in the morning PT routine with the U.S. Naval Academy, personal training on the basketball court, and showing off his pull-ups.

Our Analysis:

Parsing the Hyperbole: Trump's Takedown of Baltimore and Feud with Gov. Moore

In a spectacle that feels more like a scene out of a political drama than the sobering reality of American governance, President Donald Trump has launched a verbal barrage against Baltimore, Maryland, and its governor, Wes Moore. The occasion? The signing of an executive order, a moment that somehow spiraled into a critique of Baltimore's crime rates and a personal anecdote about a supposed interaction with Moore that, according to Moore's camp, never happened.

The Alleged Praise from Gov. Moore

Trump claims that during a meeting at the Army-Navy game, Moore showered him with adulation, labeling him the greatest president of his lifetime and lauding his performance in office. Yet, Moore's communications director, Carter Elliott, cuts through this narrative with a terse, "Didn't happen." This contradiction lays bare a recurring motif in Trump's tenure: a distortion of reality to suit personal narratives, often unanchored by facts or corroborative testimony. It's a classic Trump maneuver—asserting private accolades that stand in stark contrast to public criticisms, insinuating a cowardice or duplicity among his detractors.

The Baltimore Conundrum

Trump's depiction of Baltimore as a "deathbed" reeks of the hyperbolic disdain he often reserves for cities and states under Democratic leadership. His refusal to entertain Moore's invitation to walk through Baltimore, citing safety concerns, juxtaposes sharply with his self-styled image as a fearless leader. Instead, it paints a picture of a president who not only disengages from the complexities of urban governance but also uses these difficulties as fodder for political point-scoring.

The Physical Fitness Jab

Moore's invitation to Trump, suggesting a walk through Baltimore, and his subsequent quip about providing a golf cart, strikes a chord deeper than a simple retort. It's a masterful undermining of Trump's excuses, highlighting Moore's own physical engagement and military service—credentials that silently rebuke Trump's deferments during the Vietnam War era, colloquially referred to as "President Bone Spurs." Moore's record stands in stark contrast to Trump's, emphasizing a life of active service against one marred by evasions and excuses.

The Bigger Picture

This incident encapsulates more than just a personal feud; it's emblematic of the deeper systemic issues plaguing American politics. Trump's narrative strategy, focusing on division and derision, sidesteps the urgent needs of places like Baltimore. It's a misdirection—blame shifting from political leadership's failure to address crime, poverty, and systemic inequality, to personal attacks and political theater.

In essence, Trump's comments and the ensuing back-and-forth with Gov. Moore underscore a politics of distraction—where personal vendettas overshadow substantive policy discussions and where the realities of governance are buried under layers of hyperbole and misinformation. Baltimore, like many American cities, deserves more than to be used as a prop in political sparring. It demands focused attention, resources, and genuine, sustained engagement from its leaders—qualities seemingly in short supply in the current political climate.
