When the Water Hits Their Asses: Trades, Power, and 50 Years of Voting Against Reality

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

For years now, social media has been flooded with earnest posts praising “the trades.” Plumbers. Electricians. Welders. Mechanics. Builders. Cooks. The message is always the same: these jobs can’t be exported, and they won’t be replaced by AI. There’s truth there—real truth. And then there’s a yawning silence about the political choices that helped make life in the trades harder, more precarious, and more brutal than it ever needed to be.

This article is about holding both truths at once. The trades matter. They are indispensable. And a large portion of tradespeople, over decades, have repeatedly voted against their own material interests—then acted shocked when the consequences arrived.

The Part Everyone Gets Right: You Can’t Offshore a Pipe

Let’s start with the obvious. You can’t export a burst pipe to Bangalore. You can’t outsource a downed power line to a call center. You can’t fix a commercial HVAC failure with a Slack channel and a JIRA ticket.

Trades are physically local by definition. They are embedded in geography, infrastructure, and time. When something breaks, someone has to show up—often early, often tired, often dirty, often under pressure.

That reality isn’t changing.

AI will not crawl under a house to replace corroded plumbing. It will not climb utility poles in a storm. It will not repair a restaurant’s gas line while the owner panics about losing the dinner rush. These jobs exist because the physical world exists—and the physical world is messy, stubborn, and resistant to abstraction.

That part of the meme is correct.

Automation Will Touch the Edges—Not the Core

What will happen—already is happening—is partial automation:

• Prefabrication in construction
• Robotic welding in controlled factory environments
• AI-assisted diagnostics
• Smarter tools and sensors
• Better scheduling and logistics

None of that eliminates the trades. It changes them.

The day-to-day work—the unpredictable calls, the bespoke fixes, the human judgment under imperfect conditions—remains stubbornly human. Trades are resilient not because they’re “old-fashioned,” but because they deal with non-repeatable reality.

So yes: the trades will endure.

Which makes what comes next all the more infuriating.

Fifty Years of a Bad Deal

For more than half a century, the Republican Party has sold tradespeople a story:

• Regulations are the enemy
• Unions are corrupt
• Government is the problem
• Taxes are theft
• Healthcare should be earned
• Safety rules are “red tape”

Wrapped in that story was identity bait:

• You’re a “real American”
• You work with your hands
• You’re not one of those people
• College is for elites and fools

And it worked.

Despite being objectively working-class, despite relying on physical labor, despite bodies that wear out early, tradespeople—in aggregate—voted Republican again and again.

They voted for:

• Union busting
• Deregulation
• Weak OSHA enforcement
• Employer-controlled healthcare
• Attacks on pensions
• Tax cuts that never reached them

They were warned. Repeatedly.

The Lie Beneath the Pride

The most corrosive part of this bargain wasn’t economic. It was psychological.

Trades were told that dignity came from endurance, not protection. That pain was proof of worth. That needing help was weakness. That solidarity was suspect.

So long as someone else—immigrants, minorities, academics, journalists—was being hurt, it was tolerable. Even enjoyable. The cruelty was the point.

Until it wasn’t.

When the Bill Comes Due

Now the FAFO posts are everywhere.

• “Why are my material costs exploding?”
• “Why did my healthcare premiums skyrocket?”
• “Why is my pension gone?”
• “Why is my job less safe?”
• “Why doesn’t anyone care about workers anymore?”

Because the system you voted for was never designed to care.

The Republican Party does not respect trades. It uses them.

Praise is cheap. Policy is what matters.

Respect Isn’t a Meme

If America truly respected the trades, we’d already have:

• Universal healthcare
• Strong unions without demonization
• Enforced safety standards
• Publicly funded apprenticeships
• Retirement security for worn-out bodies
• Disability protections that don’t require ruin

Instead, we have Instagram graphics and hollow slogans—after the damage is done.

Is This Unfair to Tradespeople?

To individuals? Sometimes.

To the pattern? No.

Not everyone voted this way. Not everyone deserves what’s happening. Many were raised inside this ideology long before they could interrogate it.

But collective political behavior has collective consequences.

Solidarity doesn’t mean pretending this didn’t happen.

The Water Is High Because Swimming Was Mocked

For decades, tradespeople were told swimming lessons were socialism. Lifeboats were tyranny. That anyone warning about storms was a liar.

Now the water is at their necks, and suddenly fairness matters.

It’s not that sympathy is impossible.

It’s that reckoning is unavoidable.

You can’t spend fifty years dismantling the systems that protect workers and then act shocked when there’s nothing left to catch you.

The Final Truth

The trades will survive AI.

What they may not survive—unless something changes—is the political fantasy that strength means standing alone while being exploited.

The water isn’t unfair.

It’s rising exactly as warned.

And knowing how to fix a pipe doesn’t help much if you spent a lifetime voting to drain the pool.
