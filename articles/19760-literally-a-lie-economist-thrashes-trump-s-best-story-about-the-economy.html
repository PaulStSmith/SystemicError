'Literally a lie': Economist thrashes Trump's 'best story' about the economy

Donald Trump gestures at the end of an event in Rome, Georgia. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

A prominent economist thrashed President Donald Trump’s “best story” about the U.S. economy during a new interview on Sunday.

Last week, Trump claimed that he had “won affordability” during a speech in Rome, Georgia. But that claim appears to contradict the latest economic data released by his administration, which showed that U.S. economic growth slowed to 2.2% in 2025 from 2.8% in 2024, according to the Bureau of Economic Analysis. Meanwhile, inflation increased more than expected in December, Reuters reported.

Justin Wolfers, economics and public policy professor at the University of Michigan, said during an interview on MS NOW’s “Alex Witt Reports” that the data proved Trump’s story about the economy is “literally a lie.”

“We all know it’s a lie,” Wolfers said.

“There are people in his White House who are printing signs with lies for people to hold up, because a lie is the best story that they’ve got to tell right now,” he continued, referring to signs Trump supporters have held up at rallies declaring that prices for consumers are coming down.

“Morally, I find lying disgusting,” Wolfers added. “Economically, I think the answer here is very simple, which is he’s losing on affordability. He always declares victory. I’m not going to give the man’s words any credence, but the reality is utterly clear to every one of us who goes to the store.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Fairy Tale: A Critical Analysis

In a recent interview, President Donald Trump boasted about conquering “affordability” in the U.S. economy, a claim that not only stretches the truth but outright defies it, according to the latest economic indicators and expert opinion. Economist Justin Wolfers delivered a scathing rebuttal to Trump’s assertions, exposing the chasm between political rhetoric and economic reality.

The Contradiction Between Claims and Data

Trump’s victory lap in Rome, Georgia, where he proudly claimed to have “won affordability,” stands in stark contrast to the cold, hard figures released by his own administration. Economic growth in the U.S. has decelerated, dropping from 2.8% in 2024 to a more sluggish 2.2% in 2025, as reported by the Bureau of Economic Analysis. Furthermore, December witnessed an inflation surge that outpaced forecasts, a development that doesn’t exactly scream “affordability”.

Wolfers’ Unvarnished Truth

Justin Wolfers, a respected voice in the field of economics, did not mince words when addressing Trump’s narrative. Describing the president’s story as “literally a lie”, Wolfers highlighted the dissonance between Trump’s narrative and the tangible experiences of Americans. The mention of Trump supporters waving signs proclaiming a fall in consumer prices at rallies, as Wolfers pointed out, is nothing short of a theatrical display of misinformation. This imagery serves as a potent symbol of the administration’s strategy: fabricate a palatable narrative, regardless of its detachment from reality.

The Moral and Economic Implications of Lying

Wolfers’ critique extended beyond mere economic analysis, venturing into the realm of morality. The economist expressed disgust over the administration’s inclination to lie, a sentiment likely shared by many Americans who value integrity in public office. The moral degradation that lies bring to the political sphere is alarming, but their economic ramifications are equally concerning. By misleading the public about the state of affordability and economic well-being, the administration not only erodes trust but also hampers informed decision-making among consumers and investors alike.

Conclusion: A Clear-Eyed View of Affordability

As Wolfers poignantly remarked, the reality of affordability—or the lack thereof—is evident to anyone engaging with the economy, from the grocery store shopper to the financial analyst. Trump’s declarations of victory in the face of contradicting evidence are a disservice to the American people. The administration’s attempt to craft a narrative of economic success through falsehoods is a telling sign of its priorities and its relationship with the truth.

In sum, the disconnect between President Trump’s “best story” and the lived economic experiences of Americans is not just a matter of differing perspectives; it is a stark illustration of a leadership style grounded in fabrication and denial. The economic narrative, as framed by Trump, is a fairy tale at best, one that the American public, guided by facts and figures, must recognize as fiction.
