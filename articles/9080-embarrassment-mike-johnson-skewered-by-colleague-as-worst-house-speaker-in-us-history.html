'Embarrassment': Mike Johnson skewered by colleague as 'worst House speaker in US history'

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) tore into House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) on Friday’s edition of MSNBC’s “All In” for continuing to keep the House shuttered for business, accusing him of “surrendering” the authority of Congress to President Donald Trump.

Johnson continues to hold the House closed because he wants the Senate to vote on a clean resolution to open the government the Republican majority has already passed. However, some observers have also accused him of engineering the closure of Congress as a ploy to prevent any action on a bipartisan discharge petition to compel the White House to release the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files, which are expected to further embarrass President Donald Trump for his role in the scandal.

“I think I just saw that the Speaker of the House, Mike Johnson, has notified that next week will also be recess, which means I think that’s a sixth, is that right?” said Hayes. “That’s a sixth consecutive week that he’s keeping the House out.”

“Yeah,” confirmed Raskin. “I mean, he has completely surrendered the legislative power of the people. This is the people’s representatives. And so he’s just lied, lying down and capitulating completely to Donald Trump and whatever he wants to do. It’s a disgrace and an embarrassment that means he will go down in history as the worst speaker in the history of our country for doing this.”

“But in any event, we’ve got emergency conditions in the country because, just like we need to restore food, and not just for November, but going forward, we need to restore the health care of the people,” noted Raskin, referencing the shutdown standoff over extending Affordable Care Act subsidies that are vanishing for millions of people.

“So we say to the Republicans, no, don’t take a sixth week of paid vacation where you don’t come to Washington, you don’t come to your offices in the Rayburn Building or the Longworth Building or the Cannon Building, but you stay back home, but you’re not even meeting with your constituents because you can’t dare to face them because of the shame and the scandal of what the Republicans are doing to America right now,” said Raskin.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Abdication: How Mike Johnson’s Leadership Embodies Congressional Surrender

In a recent spectacle of political theater, Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) unleashed a scathing critique on MSNBC’s “All In,” directed at House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) for his continued inaction and the subsequent paralysis of the House. Raskin’s accusations lay bare a stark reality: Johnson’s leadership—or lack thereof—has morphed into a full-blown surrender of congressional authority, seemingly at the behest of President Donald Trump.

The Engineered Shutdown: A Ploy to Shield Trump?

The closure of Congress is not just an administrative hiccup; it’s a calculated maneuver. Observers have suggested that Johnson is not merely trying to force a Senate vote on a clean resolution to reopen the government—a resolution the Republican majority has already passed—but is also attempting to stymie any action on a bipartisan discharge petition. The goal? To keep the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files under wraps, files that are expected to further tarnish Trump’s already questionable reputation.

Six weeks of silence. This isn’t a mere delay; it’s an unprecedented abdication of responsibility. The legislative branch, designed to be a check on executive power, has been reduced to a spectator in its own arena, with Johnson as the facilitator of this historic capitulation.

The Cost of Inaction

Raskin doesn’t mince words when he describes Johnson’s tenure as potentially the “worst speaker in the history of our country.” But this isn’t just about poor leadership; it’s about the tangible impact on American lives. The shutdown standoff—ostensibly over extending Affordable Care Act subsidies—threatens the health care of millions. Yet, as Raskin poignantly notes, Republicans seem more inclined to take a “paid vacation” than address the urgent needs of their constituents.

The refusal to face the public—to engage with the very individuals they’re elected to serve—speaks volumes. It’s not merely avoidance; it’s an admission of guilt. The “shame and scandal” Raskin references isn’t just about the potential fallout from the Epstein case or the Affordable Care Act subsidies. It’s about the broader scandal of abdication, of a Congress rendered impotent by its own leadership.

Conclusion: A Call to Accountability

Mike Johnson’s leadership—or the glaring lack thereof—represents a critical juncture for American democracy. It’s a moment that demands not just scrutiny but action. The continued closure of the House, the engineered inaction, and the blatant disregard for the pressing needs of the American people are not just political maneuvers; they’re a betrayal of the fundamental principles upon which the United States was founded.

As we dissect Raskin’s accusations and the broader implications of Johnson’s actions, one thing becomes clear: this isn’t just about partisan politics. It’s about the integrity of our democratic institutions. And if Johnson’s tenure as Speaker is to be remembered for anything, let it be as a cautionary tale—an example of how not to lead, and a stark reminder of the costs of surrendering congressional authority.
