'They sound exactly the same': Trump plot to rename ICE torn apart by critics

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Acronym Game Is Not a Joke. It Is a Cover Story.

The Stunt

Trump’s Saturday Truth Social post proposing to rename Immigration and Customs Enforcement to “National Immigration and Customs Enforcement,” or “NICE,” is being mocked for its childishness. That misses the point. The post was not policy. It was a propaganda exercise: a president using branding to distract from the fact that his administration controls the machinery doing the work.

Who Has Power

The real power here sits with the presidency, not with the acronym. ICE exists because executive authority gives it force, budget, mandate, and cover. Trump did not discover a naming problem; he revealed an obsession with controlling perception while keeping the coercive state intact. Renaming the agency would not change its role. It would only put a cleaner label on the same apparatus.

The Misdirection

The target of the joke in Trump’s post was the press, which he framed as “dishonest” and “unpatriotic.” That is classic misdirection: turn attention away from the institution that acts and toward the people reporting on it. The trick is familiar because it works on a simple premise. If you can make the debate about media mockery, you don’t have to defend the agency’s conduct or explain the politics behind it.

Optics Over Substance

This is not accidental stupidity. It is governing by acronym, nickname, and distraction. Trump’s fixation on whether “ICE” sounds like “NICE” is a way of reducing state violence to a branding problem. The administration keeps the power, keeps the enforcement, keeps the threat, and asks the public to laugh at the label instead of examining the agency. That is not confusion. It is an attempt to substitute theater for accountability.

What the Mockery Reveals

The ridicule from commentators lands because the stunt is transparently unserious. But the deeper fact is that Trump’s camp knows image management is often easier than policy defense. A silly renaming proposal can soak up attention, generate cable chatter, and bury harder questions about who orders enforcement, who benefits from it, and who pays the cost. The absurdity is a tool.

The Pattern

This is the political pattern in miniature: consolidate power, personalize institutions, then wrap the whole thing in contempt for the press so the abuse can proceed under noise. The point is never the name. The point is to control the story while the machinery of the state keeps moving.
