'Crisis': Trump has business owners in key swing state feeling 'out of control'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Gage Skidmore.

Once deep red, Georgia has evolved into a volatile swing state that — like Pennsylvania, Michigan or Arizona — can go either Democratic or GOP in statewide races.

Georgia has a conservative two-term Republican governor, Brian Kemp, as well as two Democratic U.S. senators (Jon Ossoff and the Rev. Raphael Warnock). And President Donald Trump, after losing the Peach State to Joe Biden in 2020, won it by roughly 2 percent in 2024.

It remains to be seen how Georgia will affect the 2026 midterms, but one issue that Georgia Democrats will likely be campaigning on next year is the effect of Trump's new tariffs.

READ MORE: 'Part of you dies a little': MAGA-supporting FBI official admits he hates his job

In an article published on May 31, The Guardian's George Chidi examines the effect that tariffs are having in Georgia — where many businessowners, according to Chidi, are struggling to make decisions.

"If you want a bellwether to measure the broad impact of Donald Trump's tariffs on the economy," Chidi explains, "look south to Georgia. The political swing state has a $900bn economy — somewhere between the GDPs of Taiwan and Switzerland. The hospitality industry is facing an existential crisis. Wine merchants wonder aloud if they will survive the year. But others, like those in industrial manufacturing, will carefully argue that well-positioned businesses will profit."

Chidi adds, "Some say they're insulated from international competition by the nature of their industry. Others, like the movie industry, are simply confused by the proposals that have been raised, and are looking for entirely different answers. So far, it’s a mixed bag…. Unpredictability is driving volatility, and volatility is poisonous to businesses built for stable markets and stable prices. "

Georgia-based wine distributor Carson Demmond, according to Chidi, "put a hold on orders after Donald Trump enacted sky-high tariffs on European goods last month."

Demmond told The Guardian, "It means that as strategic as I’m trying to be with regards to timing my orders so I don’t get hit with lots of tariff bills at the same time, I feel like now, all of that is out of my control. I never want to face a situation where I have too many orders that all sail and land at the same time, and then getting hit with really large tariff bills in one fell swoop."

Our Analysis:

Georgia: The Swing State's Economic Conundrum Under Trump's Tariffs

The state of Georgia, once a steadfast bastion of deep red Republican dominance, has morphed into a battleground of unpredictability and political volatility, mirroring the oscillating fortunes of states like Pennsylvania, Michigan, or Arizona. This shift is emblematic of a broader national trend, where staunch political allegiances are increasingly giving way to a more nuanced and fragmented electoral landscape. Yet, the recent imposition of tariffs by the Trump administration has thrown a wrench into this already complex political machinery, casting a long shadow over Georgia's economic stability and, by extension, its political future.

The Bipartisan Paradox

Georgia's political dichotomy is as fascinating as it is perplexing. On one hand, it boasts a conservative, two-term Republican governor in Brian Kemp. On the other, it is represented in the U.S. Senate by two Democrats, Jon Ossoff and the Rev. Raphael Warnock. This paradox underscores the state's volatile political identity, further complicated by President Donald Trump's narrow victory in 2024 after a loss in 2020. Such a backdrop sets the stage for an intense and unpredictable battle in the 2026 midterms, with Trump's tariffs poised to become a central issue.

The Economic Fallout

The Guardian's George Chidi offers a sobering glimpse into the tangible impact of Trump's tariffs on Georgia's economy, a mixed bag of uncertainty, volatility, and existential crises. The state, with an economy rivaling that of entire nations, finds itself at a crossroads. The hospitality and wine industries face dire straits, while sectors like industrial manufacturing adopt a cautiously optimistic outlook, betting on their strategic positioning to weather the storm.

Chidi's narrative is not just about numbers; it's about people. Take Carson Demmond, a Georgia-based wine distributor, who encapsulates the dilemma faced by many business owners. Trump's "sky-high tariffs" on European goods have forced Demmond into a corner, stripping him of control and leaving her at the mercy of unpredictable tariff bills. This story is a microcosm of the broader economic volatility plaguing the state, driving home the point that unpredictability is not just an abstract economic concept but a real and pressing concern for businesses striving for stability.

The Political Implications

The economic volatility wrought by Trump's tariffs is not merely a matter of fiscal concern; it is a ticking time bomb with far-reaching political ramifications. As Georgia Democrats gear up for the 2026 midterms, the tariffs issue offers fertile ground for campaigning, potentially rallying a diverse coalition of voters adversely affected by the policy. The question remains, however, whether this strategy will be enough to sway the state's electorate, which has shown itself to be as unpredictable as the economic forces buffeting the state.

In Conclusion

Georgia stands at the forefront of a national reckoning, grappling with the complexities of a shifting political landscape and the harsh realities of an economy under strain from Trump's tariffs. The state's journey from a dependable red stronghold to a volatile swing state serves as a microcosm of the broader American political evolution, marked by fragmentation, polarization, and an ever-present uncertainty about the future.

As the 2026 midterms approach, all eyes will be on Georgia—not just as a bellwether for national political trends, but as a litmus test for the resilience of its economy and the tenacity of its electorate in the face of challenges that are both deeply local and profoundly global. The stakes could not be higher, and the outcomes are anything but certain.
