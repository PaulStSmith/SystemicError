'He's breaking it': Despair as renegade GOP senator sabotages his own party's agenda

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 3, 20254 min read

Republican U.S. Senator John Thune (R-SD), who was elected to become the next Senate Majority Leader, speaks to the media after a U.S. Senate Republicans meeting to vote on leadership positions for the 119th Congress, on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., November 13, 2024. REUTERS/Leah Millis

Louisiana Republican Sen. John Kennedy is torching his own party's spending agenda in an act of political sabotage that has left fellow GOP senators scrambling to contain the damage, according to a report Saturday.

The Senate veteran has declared bipartisan spending talks "dead as Jimmy Hoffa" and accused his colleagues of "playacting" through the trillion-dollar government funding process. His scorched-earth approach is infuriating Republicans and Democrats alike as they desperately try to keep basic government operations on track.

"He's breaking it," fumed Sen. Patty Murray (WA), the top Appropriations Democrat, when asked about Kennedy's destructive tactics.

Kennedy's reign of chaos includes blocking his own energy and water spending bill—which directs nearly $60 billion annually—while demanding deeper cuts that Democrats consider a betrayal of bipartisan agreements. He's also threatened to derail Interior Department funding and forced leadership into embarrassing delays over his demand to vote against Congress's own budget.

"What we're seeing is different, and I don't know why," said Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK), lamenting the loss of collegiality that once defined the appropriations process. "We don't have that right now, which is unfortunate."

Even Senate Majority Leader John Thune seemed baffled by Kennedy's antics, awkwardly promising to "do what we can to get the appropriations process moving again."

Kennedy remains unrepentant, dismissing his colleagues' pleas for cooperation with trademark folksy contempt. When asked by Politico about bipartisan deal-making, he scoffed about the chances of success.

"Donkeys may fly someday, too," he said.

"I love 'We Are the World,' it's a beautiful song," Kennedy added mockingly. "But it's not reality."

Our Analysis:

The Saboteur in the Senate: Analyzing John Kennedy's Fiscal Frenzy

In what can only be described as an astonishing display of political pyrotechnics, Louisiana Republican Sen. John Kennedy has taken a flamethrower to the bridges of bipartisanship—leaving his own party in the lurch and both sides of the aisle in an uproar. His recent actions suggest a man not just on a mission but on a warpath, with the trillion-dollar government funding process in his crosshairs. Kennedy's declaration that bipartisan spending talks are "dead as Jimmy Hoffa" is not just a colorful turn of phrase; it's a stark illustration of the current state of fiscal negotiations in the Senate.

The Fallout of Fiscal Firestarting

Kennedy’s approach, which can be aptly described as scorched-earth, has not only infuriated colleagues but has thrown a wrench into the gears of basic government operations. The senator's blockade of his own energy and water spending bill, a crucial piece of legislation directing nearly $60 billion annually, is a bold move. But it's his demands for deeper cuts—a betrayal of previously agreed-upon bipartisan deals—that reveal a more troubling trend.

When Sen. Patty Murray (WA), the top Appropriations Democrat, says, "He's breaking it," she's not merely expressing frustration. She's highlighting the erosion of a foundational process that, despite its flaws, has kept the government running. Kennedy's tactics aren't just an annoyance; they're a threat to the stability of American governance.

The Loss of Legislative Collegiality

What's perhaps most disconcerting is the reaction from Kennedy's colleagues. Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) notes the departure from a once collegial appropriations process, hinting at a deeper rot within the Senate's halls. This isn't just about partisan politics; it's about the abandonment of procedural respect and the norms that ensure legislative functionality. Senate Majority Leader John Thune's befuddled response only underscores the chaos Kennedy has sown within his own ranks.

Kennedy's Mocking Defiance

Kennedy's response to calls for cooperation is as telling as it is contemptuous. His rejection of bipartisan deal-making, couched in folksy derision, speaks volumes about his stance on governance. To liken the possibility of bipartisan success to "donkeys flying" is not just to reject the current efforts at negotiation; it's to mock the very idea of political compromise. His quip about "We Are the World" being a "beautiful song" but "not reality" further cements his position as not just a fiscal conservative, but a senator seemingly reveling in the discord he’s creating.

The Consequences of Political Pyrotechnics

Kennedy's actions raise critical questions about the future of fiscal policy and governance in the Senate. By torching the bridges of bipartisanship and cooperation, Kennedy isn't just making a statement about spending; he's challenging the foundational principles that enable a functioning government. This isn’t about fiscal responsibility; it's about political sabotage.

As observers, we must ask: what is the endgame for Kennedy and those who might follow in his footsteps? Are the principles of governance and compromise to be casualties in the pursuit of ideological purity? Kennedy’s strategy, while bold, risks not just fiscal fallout but the erosion of legislative integrity itself.

In the theater of political spectacle, Kennedy has chosen a role more akin to a saboteur than a senator. The consequences of such a performance are yet to be fully realized, but one thing is clear: in the scorched earth left behind, finding common ground will be all the more difficult.
