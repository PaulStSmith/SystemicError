​'Full panic mode': New report confirms Trump-related 'Epstein cover-up at FBI'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump attends a press conference at Trump National Golf Club, in Rancho Palos Verdes, U.S., September 13, 2024. REUTERS/David Swanson/File Photo

Reports that federal workers were tasked with reviewing Jeffrey Epstein files to flag any mention of Donald Trump are indeed true, according to a legal expert on Sunday.

Legal analyst Allison Gill, better known as Mueller, She Wrote, did a little investigating of her own after it was reported that FBI agents were instructed to "flag" any mention of Donald Trump in the Jeffrey Epstein files.

"When I saw that over one thousand people had been put to work reviewing the Epstein files, I put a call out on my BlueSky account," the legal expert said as she explained her request for anonymous details from members of the FBI who were tasked with the assignment.

She adds, "In the 24 hours since, I’ve received several messages, including from a former analyst that was assigned to review the files, and a few things stood out to me."

According to Gill, "First, approximately 1,000 personnel in the Information Management Division (IMD) and the FBI New York Field Office were assigned to this task, confirming the whistleblower account made to Senator Durbin’s office. I can also confirm that a log exists tracking the mentions of Donald Trump in the files, and that there were approximately 100,000 files containing roughly 300,000 pages. Individual analysts were told to flag mentions of Trump by document and page number by logging them in an Excel spreadsheet, then they’d hand in their spreadsheet at the end of their (sometimes 24 or even 48-hour) shift. But it’s important to note that the agents were not told to flag Trump until later in a process that began mid-March."

Going further, she reports, "The process of reviewing the Epstein and Maxwell files was chaotic, and the orders were constantly changing - sometimes daily."

"Regarding the ever-changing nature of the instructions, which one source described as 'full panic mode,' there were at least four different review instructions," she wrote on Sunday.

She went on to describe what could be a major vulnerability in the federal operation.

"One person I spoke to on the condition of anonymity said that many agents spent more time waiting for new instructions than they did processing files. But here’s what caught my attention: the files were stored on a shared drive that anyone in the division could access. Normally, access is only granted to those working on a project, but because of the hurried nature of the exercise, the usual permission restrictions were not in place," according to the expert. "Additionally, the internal SharePoint site the bureau ended up using to distribute the files toward the end did not have the usual restricted permissions. This left the Epstein and Maxwell files open to viewing by a much larger group of people than previously thought."

Because of video training programs, she noted, "That means that video exists of trainers explaining the process of flagging instances of Donald Trump appearing in the files, and those videos went out on unclassified networks within the bureau."

"It’s also of note that the trainers toward the end were folks from the Department of Justice, and not the FBI," she said. "As far as content, there was one confirmed mention of Donald Trump in the files reviewed by an analyst who again spoke on the condition of anonymity. Beyond that, there were other instances of Trump appearing in the files, but the number of times and to what extent is unknown."

She added:

"But the log exists."

Our Analysis:

The Epstein-Trump Connection: A Closer Look at the FBI's Dubious Task

In a recent revelation that sounds more like a plot from a political thriller than a procedural reality, reports have confirmed that federal workers were indeed assigned the monumental task of sifting through Jeffrey Epstein's files for any mention of Donald Trump. Legal analyst Allison Gill, also known by her moniker Mueller, She Wrote, has thrown a glaring spotlight on this operation, revealing a series of concerning details that demand our scrutiny.

The Scale of the Operation

First and foremost, the sheer volume of manpower and resources allocated to this task is staggering. Gill's investigation uncovered that approximately 1,000 personnel were deployed to comb through an estimated 300,000 pages across 100,000 files. This revelation not only speaks volumes about the perceived importance of the task but also raises questions about the allocation of federal resources. Why such a vast deployment for this specific endeavor?

The Trump Flagging Protocol

The instructions given to the agents are particularly noteworthy. The directive to flag any mention of Trump by document and page number, and to log these instances in an Excel spreadsheet, suggests a highly systematic approach to this task. However, the fact that this directive was not implemented until midway through the operation raises eyebrows. Was this an afterthought, or a calculated move to ensure a certain level of progress before drawing attention to Trump's potential involvement? The latter could suggest a strategic, if not manipulative, approach to managing the narrative surrounding the Epstein-Trump connection.

Operational Chaos and Security Concerns

Gill's report paints a picture of an operation marred by chaos and constant changes in instructions. Described by one source as being in "full panic mode," the process was anything but smooth, with agents reportedly spending more time waiting for new instructions than actually reviewing files. This chaotic approach not only undermines the efficiency of the task but also highlights a potential mismanagement within the operation.

Moreover, the decision to store sensitive files on a shared drive accessible to a broader range of personnel than necessary is a glaring security lapse. In a time when data privacy and security are of paramount importance, such an oversight is both shocking and unacceptable. The use of an internal SharePoint site without the usual restricted permissions further exacerbates this vulnerability, leaving the Epstein and Maxwell files exposed to unnecessary risk.

Training Videos on Unclassified Networks

The distribution of training videos detailing the process of flagging Trump mentions on unclassified networks within the bureau is another questionable decision. This practice not only risks the confidentiality of the operation but also potentially compromises the integrity of the investigation.

The Department of Justice's Involvement

The late-stage involvement of trainers from the Department of Justice, as opposed to the FBI, adds another layer of complexity to the operation. This shift in oversight could indicate internal disagreements or a lack of confidence in the FBI's handling of the matter. It begs the question: what prompted this change, and what implications does it have for the investigation's objectivity?

A Log Exists, But Questions Remain

Perhaps the most tantalizing detail of all is the existence of a log tracking mentions of Donald Trump within the Epstein files. While Gill confirms the presence of at least one mention, the full extent of Trump's appearances and the context of these mentions remain shrouded in mystery.

In Conclusion

The Epstein-Trump file review operation, as detailed by Allison Gill, highlights a series of operational missteps, security lapses, and potentially manipulative tactics. The chaotic nature of the review process, combined with the strategic flagging of Trump mentions and the broadened access to sensitive files, paints a picture of an investigation fraught with vulnerabilities.

As we continue to dissect the implications of this operation, one thing is clear: the need for transparency, accountability, and a rigorous examination of the facts has never been more crucial. The public deserves answers, and the integrity of our federal agencies hangs in the balance.
