New Trump 'sabotage' rule will make it 'much easier' to fire workers for political beliefs

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 6, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Trump administration is clearing the way to discipline or fire senior career officials across government, according to a new report.

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management plans to issue a rule Thursday that creates a new category for high-ranking career employees tasked with executing administration policies, which the Wall Street Journal reported would remove their ability to appeal firings or disciplinary action to prevent them from standing in the way of President Donald Trump’s policies.

“People can’t be conscientious objectors in the workforce in a way where it interferes with their ability to carry out their mission,” OPM Director Scott Kupor told the newspaper. “When conscientious objection becomes sabotage or trying to find ways to thwart the objectives of the administration — that is not allowed.”

OPM said the new rule was aimed at disciplining federal workers deemed insufficiently supportive of the Trump agenda and is part of the administration’s broad effort to reduce the government workforce, but federal worker unions fear that the president’s advisers could use the new rule as a pretext to remove employees over their political views.

The rule change would affect about 50,000 employees, according to OPM officials, but union leaders believe the administration could attempt to significantly expand that new category to include many more and reverse civil service protections in place since the 19th century to insulate the professional workforce from partisan influence.

OPM officials will complete the full list within 30 days or positions eligible for the new category and Trump is then expected to issue an executive order recategorizing specific positions, and the final rule makes clear that employees could be disciplined for leaks to the media and other actions seen as impediments to the president’s agenda.

“If you’ve announced to all of your co-workers that you hate the administration,” Kupor said, “and then you perform badly at your job and you don’t do anything, the fact that you said you’re going to resist to all of your co-workers is something that could be taken into account.”

The Government Accountability Project and National Active and Retired Federal Employees Association, which has sued the administration over an executive order that led to the new rule, said the new category would violate a 1978 law that establishes job protections to career federal employees.

“It will be much easier for the administration to squelch dissent or to thwart the work of civil servants who are trying to ensure that they are performing due diligence when it comes to policy development,” said Love Rutledge, a coach for federal employees.

Our Analysis:

The Thin Veil of Authoritarianism: Unpacking the Trump Administration’s Latest Move

In a move that reeks of authoritarianism thinly veiled as bureaucratic restructuring, the Trump administration is poised to dismantle over a century of civil service protections through the introduction of a rule that will make it significantly easier to discipline or fire senior career officials who are deemed insufficiently loyal to Trump’s agenda. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) is at the forefront of this controversial shift, which ostensibly aims to create a new category for high-ranking career employees tasked with executing administration policies. However, the implications of this move are far-reaching and deeply concerning.

The Guise of Efficiency Overriding Democratic Norms

At first glance, the administration’s rationale, as stated by OPM Director Scott Kupor, is to prevent “conscientious objectors” from sabotaging the Trump administration’s objectives. Kupor’s assertion that “conscientious objection becomes sabotage or trying to find ways to thwart the objectives of the administration — that is not allowed,” is a chilling reminder of the lengths to which this administration will go to suppress dissent within the federal workforce. This statement alone should raise alarm bells about the erosion of democratic norms and the suppression of protected speech within the government.

A Direct Assault on Civil Service Protections

The rule change, which could potentially affect about 50,000 employees, is not just an administrative adjustment. It is a direct assault on civil service protections that have been in place since the 19th century, designed to insulate the professional workforce from partisan influence. The notion that the administration could significantly expand this new category to include more employees is a testament to its broader effort to politicize and reduce the government workforce, undermining the very foundations of a nonpartisan, professional civil service.

The Specter of Political Persecution

Federal worker unions and advocacy groups are right to fear that this new rule could be used as a pretext to remove employees based on their political views. The targeting of employees for disciplinary action or termination based on perceived loyalty to the Trump agenda is tantamount to political persecution. It is a blatant attempt to purge the federal government of dissenting voices, thereby weakening the checks and balances essential for a functioning democracy.

Legal and Ethical Implications

The move by the Trump administration to recategorize specific positions and discipline employees for actions seen as impediments to the president’s agenda, including leaks to the media, raises serious legal and ethical questions. It violates a 1978 law that establishes job protections for career federal employees, threatening the integrity and independence of the civil service. It is a stark reminder of the administration’s disregard for legal norms and its penchant for authoritarian governance practices.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The introduction of this rule by the Trump administration is not just an attack on federal workers; it is an attack on the very principles of democracy and good governance. It is imperative that this move is met with rigorous scrutiny, legal challenges, and public outcry. The professional federal workforce must be protected from partisan purges to ensure that it can continue to serve the American people effectively, without undue influence from transient political agendas. The specter of authoritarianism looms large over this latest policy change, and it must be resisted with all the vigor that the defenders of democracy can muster.
