'Significant math issue': Expert warns House GOP will soon be crippled by absences

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has navigated a difficult year with a razor-thin majority where he had to wrangle every last vote on key issues, and frequently had to juggle unexpected vacancies. But things are about to get a whole lot worse for him, one congressional staffer predicted Tuesday on X.

That’s because no fewer than 18 members of the House Republican caucus are running for other offices next year, and can’t be counted on to always be in Washington for critical votes.

Aaron Fritschner, a top staffer for Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA), flagged this in response to a Punchbowl News analysis of Johnson’s “brutal” 11 months ahead, which includes yet another shutdown fight looming over the next two months, and rising discontent from the GOP rank and file and even other House GOP leaders about the direction of the caucus.

“Punchbowl’s rundown here is good, and there’s another problem for Johnson that’ll creep up next year: attendance,” wrote Fritschner. “Republicans have 18 members running for other offices next year, compared to just 8 Dems. Campaigning will keep them out of DC, a significant vote math issue.”

Among those 18 members are a number of gubernatorial campaigns, including Reps. Andy Biggs (R-AZ), Byron Donalds (R-FL), John James (R-MI), Tom Tiffany (R-WI), Nancy Mace and Ralph Norman (R-SC), and Elise Stefanik (R-NY); Senate campaigns, including Reps. Andy Barr (R-KY), Barry Moore (R-AL), Ashley Hinson (R-IA), and Buddy Carter (R-GA); and Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX), who is seeking the nomination for state attorney general.

All of this comes as both Democrats and Republicans await Tuesday night’s results in a highly-watched special election for a vacant congressional seat in Tennessee, which polls have flagged as neck and neck despite President Donald Trump having carried the district by over 20 points.

Our Analysis:

House Speaker Mike Johnson’s Upcoming ‘Brutal’ Year

House Speaker Mike Johnson’s tenure can be likened to navigating a ship through a storm with a crew half inclined to jump overboard at the first sight of land. This analogy becomes particularly apt when considering the 18 members of the House Republican caucus running for other offices next year, as highlighted in a recent article. This political escapade not only signals a tumultuous time ahead for Johnson but also lays bare the fractious nature of the current political landscape.

The Attendance Crisis

The crux of the issue, as Aaron Fritschner astutely points out, is attendance. With a “razor-thin majority,” every vote counts, and the absence of these 18 Republicans, due to their campaigns elsewhere, will significantly impact Johnson’s ability to marshal votes for critical decisions. This isn’t just a minor hiccup; it’s a gaping wound in the side of Johnson’s majority, threatening to bleed out at every major legislative turn.

Comparative Neglect

It’s telling that the GOP faces this issue to a far greater extent than their Democratic counterparts, with the latter having only 8 members running for other offices. This disparity not only emphasizes the ambition within the Republican ranks but also highlights a potential lack of commitment to the legislative process. One can’t help but wonder if the allure of higher or different offices is distracting from the duties they currently hold.

The Electoral Wild Card

Adding to Johnson’s migraine is the upcoming special election in Tennessee, a battleground that, despite being a stronghold in the past, is now ominously “neck and neck.” This isn’t just a local skirmish; it’s a barometer for the broader national mood, and a potential loss here could signal a devastating blow to an already beleaguered House majority.

A Caucus in Discontent

The article also whispers of rising discontent within the GOP ranks, with even other House GOP leaders questioning the direction of the caucus. This isn’t just a matter of herding cats; it’s trying to lead a pack of lions who have tasted blood and found they like it. Johnson’s leadership will not only be tested by external forces but from within his ranks, a challenge that could define his tenure.

Conclusion

In sum, Mike Johnson’s path ahead is fraught with peril, both seen and unforeseen. The attendance issue is not merely a logistical problem but a symptom of a deeper malaise within the GOP, one that threatens to undermine its legislative effectiveness. As the political landscape continues to shift, Johnson’s ability to navigate these treacherous waters will be the ultimate test of his leadership and the cohesion of the Republican caucus. Let’s watch closely, for the outcome of this saga will not only impact the immediate political battles but also the future direction of the Republican Party.
