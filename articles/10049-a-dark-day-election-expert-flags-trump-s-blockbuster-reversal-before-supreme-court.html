A prominent voting rights lawyer warned on Thursday that President Donald Trump’s Department of Justice is taking a new position in “dozens and dozens” of voting rights cases, and that decision could harm many voters ahead of the 2026 midterms.

Marc Elias, who founded the Elias Law Group, discussed a recent court filing from the DOJ on a new episode of progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen’s show “Democracy Docket.” In the filing, the DOJ argued that a test in Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act that has been used to protect district that are predominantly made up of voters of color should be thrown out, Elias said.

“This is a dark day for democracy,” Elias said.

“In this blockbuster filing, the DOJ itself has reversed its position,” Elias continued. “Not just its position in this case, its position in literally dozens upon dozens of cases where both Democratic and Republican attorneys general and their solicitor generals have defended the Voting Rights Act.”

The case concerns Louisiana’s voting map, which state lawmakers were required to redraw in 2024 after federal judges found it violated the Voting Rights Act by not having enough majority-minority districts. It is currently being reviewed by the Supreme Court and a decision could be handed down as early as next term.

The DOJ argued that the test used to determine whether Louisiana’s map was unconstitutional is illegal itself. Instead of the current test, Elias said the DOJ wants to create a different test that disentangles race and party. He described it as “nonsensical.”

“There are places where Black voters vote overwhelmingly for Democratic candidates; you’re never going to be able to disentangle race from party,” Elias said. “So, what they’re basically saying is if the state of Louisiana discriminates against black voters, too bad, unless those black voters can show that they can pass a map that will still favor Republicans while vindicating the right of black voters. It’s nonsensical.”

Our Analysis:

The DOJ’s Disturbing Shift in Voting Rights Stance: A Closer Look

In a revelatory and frankly alarming turn of events, the Department of Justice (DOJ) under President Donald Trump’s administration has evidently decided to pivot sharply from its historical stance on voting rights protection, as indicated by Marc Elias, a titan in the realm of voting rights law. This shift, as elucidated during Elias’s conversation on Brian Tyler Cohen’s show “Democracy Docket,” signals not just a change but a potential upheaval in the protection mechanisms for voters of color ahead of the 2026 midterms.

The DOJ’s recent court filing, which Elias brings to light, advocates for the dismantling of a critical test under Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act. This test, historically a bulwark against discriminatory practices in district drawing, particularly in safeguarding districts predominantly inhabited by voters of color, is now on the chopping block. The DOJ’s rationale? To replace it with a test that purportedly disentangles race from party—a concept Elias rightfully deems “nonsensical.”

A “Dark Day for Democracy”

Elias does not mince words in describing the gravity of the DOJ’s reversal, labeling it a “dark day for democracy.” This isn’t merely about altering a stance in a single case; it’s about overturning decades of bipartisan consensus on the importance of the Voting Rights Act. The implications are profound, affecting “dozens upon dozens” of cases and, by extension, the very fabric of American electoral integrity.

The Core of the Controversy: Louisiana’s Voting Map

The controversy stems from a case concerning Louisiana’s voting map, mandated to be redrawn by 2024 following a federal judgment against its violation of the Voting Rights Act due to a lack of majority-minority districts. The Supreme Court’s pending decision on this could set a precedent with far-reaching consequences.

The DOJ’s “Nonsensical” Proposal

The DOJ’s proposed test—to create a clear demarcation between race and party affiliation in voting rights considerations—is where logic seems to fray. Elias points out an unavoidable truth: in many areas, particularly where Black voters are a significant demographic, voting patterns overwhelmingly favor the Democratic Party. The DOJ’s proposal, thus, places an unrealistic burden on these voters: to devise a map that caters to Republican favor while also advocating for Black voters’ rights. The inherent contradiction in this proposal is not just “nonsensical”; it’s perilously close to being outright disenfranchising.

The Bigger Picture

What stands out in this development is not just the potential for immediate harm to voters of color but the signal it sends about the DOJ’s trajectory under Trump’s administration. It’s a stark departure from a long-held, bipartisan commitment to uphold the Voting Rights Act, a cornerstone of American civil rights legislation. This move doesn’t just undermine the Act itself; it threatens to erode the very foundations of equitable voting rights protection in the United States.

In essence, the DOJ’s filing is not merely a legal position; it’s a political statement, one that could have dire consequences for the sanctity of the vote in America. As the 2026 midterms loom, the need for vigilance and advocacy for voters’ rights has never been more critical. The DOJ’s stance is not just a departure from tradition; it’s a clarion call for those concerned with democracy and equality to stand up and demand accountability. This “dark day for democracy,” as Elias aptly describes it, should not just be a moment of critique but a catalyst for action.
