Trump tries to shrug off No Kings protests — but is hit by astounding turnout

By Paulo Santos

The Real Story Behind the No Kings Protests

In a sweeping wave of national dissent, millions of Americans took to the streets this past Saturday, marking the third large-scale No Kings protest against what many perceive as President Donald Trump’s authoritarian tendencies. From the bustling streets of Minneapolis to the sandy shores of San Francisco, the message was clear and unified: “Trump must go now! No ICE, no wars, no lies, no kings.”

The Power of Numbers

Organized by the activist group Indivisible, expectations were set high with projected attendance numbers suggesting more than 9 million participants across the nation. While these numbers are still being tallied, the visual evidence from aerial footage across cities like Washington, DC, New York City, Boston, and beyond showcased massive turnouts. This growing momentum, up from 5 million in June and 7 million in October of last year, underscores a significant escalation in public engagement and concern.

The Voices of the Movement

The flagship event in Minneapolis not only drew over 200,000 demonstrators but also featured a roster of high-profile speakers and performers who lent their voices and status to amplify the protest’s message. Minnesota Governor Tim Walz, U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders, Representative Ilhan Omar, actress Jane Fonda, and rock legend Bruce Springsteen, who paid tribute through song to slain protesters, all stood in solidarity with the movement’s goals.

However, the presence of these political figures raises critical questions about the framing of power and responsibility in this scenario. While their support brings attention to the cause, it is Indivisible, the organizing body, and the millions of unnamed participants who truly drive the movement. These protests are not merely extensions of political patronage but rather a grassroots expression of widespread dissent.

Misdirection and Response

In a dismissive retort, White House spokesperson Abigail Jackson labeled the protests as “Trump Derangement Therapy Sessions,” a statement that appears to diminish the validity of the demonstrators’ concerns. This response not only sidesteps addressing the substantive issues raised by the protests but also attempts to redirect the narrative towards trivializing the dissent itself. It’s essential to scrutinize such responses that aim to delegitimize genuine civic engagement by painting it as irrational or inconsequential.

Nationwide and Beyond

It’s not just in the U.S. where these sentiments are echoed. Indivisible’s co-founder Leah Greenberg highlighted international solidarity with No Kings events also held in cities like London and Madrid. This global dimension emphasizes the broader implications of what is framed as a national issue, suggesting a widespread critique of not just President Trump’s policies but of rising authoritarian tendencies in global politics.

Conclusion

While the No Kings rallies are a response to immediate political figures and policies, attributing the movement solely to anti-Trump sentiment simplifies and misdirects. These protests reflect deeper systemic frustrations that extend beyond any single administration. As such, they should be viewed not just as reactions to a particular leader’s actions but as a call for broader systemic change. In the narrative of power and protest, the real story often lies not with those who hold formal power, but with the masses that rally for change. The true measure of these protests’ impact will be in how they shape policy and public opinion moving forward, not in the fleeting comments of current officeholders.
