Trump's 'haphazard moves' leave judges begging for help to fix dilapidated courthouses

The fallout from President Donald Trump’s Department of Government Efficiency has created serious concerns among judges, who issued an urgent plea to lawmakers Tuesday over crumbling courthouses, according to a new report.

Falling ceilings, contaminated water and malfunctioning elevators are among the problems facing federal judges and courts, Politico reported Tuesday. A top federal courts official has asked for courts to have the right to build or manage their own facilities. Courthouses are currently operated under the General Services Administration.

“Federal courthouses are in crisis. Without immediate action, the problems will continue to worsen,” Judge Robert J. Conrad Jr., director of the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts, wrote in a letter to Congress. “Action is needed now to reverse a downward spiral of critical-system failures, long-term underfunding of repairs, security risks, and climbing costs.”

This is not the first time judges have aired grievances.

“While judges have groused for decades about neglect by their government landlord, complaints about GSA reached a fever pitch last year after haphazard moves by President Donald Trump’s Department of Government Efficiency initiative radically downsized the agency and targeted some courthouses for sale without any input from the judges who work there,” Politico reported.

Judges could lay some of the blame at Trump’s DOGE cuts.

“The urgent missive the judiciary sent to Congress Tuesday doesn’t make direct mention of DOGE, but it notes that GSA eliminated almost half its staff in recent months, creating security and safety risks by leaving no one on site at many courthouses to address hazards and urgent repairs,” according to Politico. “Legislation the judges are backing would gradually transition existing courthouses from GSA to a new Judiciary Buildings Service.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Fallout of Trump’s Efficiency Measures on Federal Courthouses

The recent Politico report unveiling the dire straits of federal courthouses under the Trump administration’s Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) initiative is a stark reminder of the perils of misplaced priorities and misguided efficiency drives. Judges have issued an urgent plea to lawmakers over conditions that sound less like halls of justice and more like scenes from a dystopian novel: falling ceilings, contaminated water, and malfunctioning elevators. This isn’t just an unfortunate situation; it’s a screaming indictment of the administration’s cavalier approach to essential public infrastructure.

A Crisis of Neglect and Mismanagement

Judge Robert J. Conrad Jr.’s letter to Congress paints a grim picture of the federal judiciary’s working conditions, emphasizing a crisis point reached due to “critical-system failures, long-term underfunding of repairs, security risks, and climbing costs.” The request for courts to have the right to build or manage their own facilities highlights a desperate need for autonomy in addressing these gross inadequacies, a need born out of the General Services Administration’s (GSA) apparent abdication of its responsibilities.

The DOGE Debacle

The Politico report traces the roots of this crisis back to the DOGE initiative, which sought to radically downsize the GSA, the very agency responsible for maintaining these courthouses. The initiative not only reduced the GSA’s workforce by almost half but also “targeted some courthouses for sale without any input from the judges who work there.” This haphazard approach to government efficiency—prioritizing cost-cutting over the functionality and safety of critical infrastructure—has evidently backfired, leaving the judiciary in a state of peril.

Security and Safety at Risk

The consequences of these actions—or lack thereof—are far-reaching. The reduction in GSA staff has left many courthouses without onsite personnel to address hazards and urgent repairs, creating significant security and safety risks. It’s a scenario that beggars belief: the very institutions that uphold the law and ensure justice are themselves left in conditions that wouldn’t be out of place in a negligence lawsuit.

A Call for Legislative Action

The judges’ backing of legislation to transition existing courthouses from the GSA to a new Judiciary Buildings Service is a clear signal that the status quo is untenable. It’s a recognition that when it comes to the administration of justice, efficiency cannot come at the expense of efficacy and safety. The proposed Judiciary Buildings Service is not just a logistical change; it’s a necessary step towards reclaiming the dignity and functionality of these vital institutions.

Conclusion: A Stark Reminder

This situation serves as a cautionary tale about the limits and dangers of efficiency initiatives that fail to consider the broader implications of their actions. It’s a reminder that government cannot be run like a business, where costs are cut without regard to the impact on essential services. As this report makes painfully clear, the fallout from such an approach is not just a matter of inconvenience but a serious threat to the administration of justice itself. The urgent plea from federal judges is more than a request for funding or autonomy; it’s a demand for respect for the judiciary and the critical role it plays in our democracy.
