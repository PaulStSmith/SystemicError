Republicans once opposed red tape — but now it's their favorite weapon

Based on an article by Bridgette Dunlap, Missouri Independent

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Red Tape Is the Point

The Trick

The source story is straightforward: Missouri’s Medicaid system is already dysfunctional, and Republicans have now layered on federal work requirements that force eligible people to repeatedly prove they deserve coverage. The result is not efficiency. It is attrition by paperwork. The same logic shows up in abortion restrictions and voter ID schemes: make the process so exhausting, expensive, and document-heavy that rights exist mostly for people with the time, money, and institutional literacy to survive the maze.

Who Has Power

The people imposing this system hold the power. Republican lawmakers wrote the federal Medicaid changes. The Trump administration sharpened the rules further. Missouri Republicans then tried to bake work requirements into the state constitution instead of confronting the cost and administrative collapse their own policy would trigger. That is the governing coalition here: not confused administrators, not unlucky beneficiaries, but officeholders using state machinery to narrow access.

The Administrative Violence

The article gets one thing exactly right: the burden is the mechanism. Republicans claim the savings come from “fraud, waste, and abuse,” but the text makes clear what the policy is designed to do. It is designed to make people fall off coverage because they cannot keep up with the forms, deadlines, office visits, missing letters, closed offices, and incomprehensible instructions. This is not a side effect. It is the business model.

That matters because bureaucratic cruelty often arrives in the language of neutral administration. But the power relationship is obvious. Poor people are not being asked to do something trivial. They are being forced to perform labor simply to keep health insurance they already qualify for.

The Same Playbook Everywhere

The article also connects the dots across policy areas: abortion, voting, Medicaid. The pattern is consistent. When Republicans cannot ban something outright, they convert access into a paperwork contest. When direct power is politically costly or legally blocked, they reach for red tape. That is not small government. It is managerial repression.

In the abortion context, the article points to medically inappropriate regulations that made clinics nearly impossible to operate. In the voting context, it points to the SAVE Act and its document-heavy eligibility rules, which would burden people less likely to have easy access to birth certificates, passports, or consistent name records. The target is not random inconvenience. The target is participation by people least able to absorb administrative punishment.

Missouri as a Test Case

Missouri is not just a local story; it is a stress test for how punitive bureaucracy scales. The state already lacks the staff and computer systems to carry out the new federal requirements efficiently. Missouri estimated the added administrative costs at $294.6 million before the Trump administration added more rules for sick people. That detail should end any fantasy that this is disciplined governance. It is expensive sabotage dressed up as fiscal restraint.

And because the system is already broken, the burden lands twice: on residents who lose coverage and on state government that must burn public money to implement a policy built to fail.

What This Reveals

The deeper political pattern is simple. Republicans do not merely tolerate red tape when it serves them; they weaponize it. They condemn bureaucracy in speeches, then deploy it aggressively when direct restriction is harder to sell. The rhetoric is anti-government. The practice is government used as an obstacle course.

That is the real lesson here. Administrative burden is not a bug in these policies. It is the intended enforcement mechanism. If a policy only works by exhausting the people it governs, then the policy is not about order, efficiency, or integrity. It is about power choosing friction as its preferred form of cruelty.
