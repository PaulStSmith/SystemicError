HuffPost journalist Jennifer Bendery sought comment from the agency after she reported that HUD Secretary Scott Turner allowed employees to leave work early on Friday in honor of Easter and Passover in what the publication dubbed “a brazenly religious message.”

But instead of responding to the reporter’s request for comment on the story, a HUD spokesperson instead slammed the HuffPost in a statement Bendery said was “so bonkers I laughed out loud.”

“Huffington Post’s Trump Derangement Syndrome is so severe it is standing up for criminal gang members while standing against the Judeo-Christian values our country was founded on,” the spokesperson said in the statement. “Our constitution says that we were endowed by our creator. Every coin minted and dollar printed is minted with America’s motto ‘In God We Trust.’ HUD will continue to follow President Trump’s leadership in protecting our first amendment right to freedom of religion.”

A confused – but amused — Bendery quipped on X: “Criminal gangs, coins, what?” the HuffPost reporter wrote, followed by a laughing emoji. “(The Constitution definitely does not say we were endowed by our creator,)” she added.

HuffPost’s senior White House correspondent SV Dáte also got in on the action.

“The Constitution says we were endowed by our creator?” Maybe the very snotty spokesperson means the Declaration of Independence,” Dáte wrote in an X post.

Bendery added in her report on Friday that Turner’s email was so “overtly religious” that a group of HUD employees reached out to the Freedom From Religion Foundation, which prompted the group to file a complaint on Thursday urging him to take back the message.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the HUD Spokesperson's "Bonkers" Statement

In a recent debacle that reads more like a satire than a piece of professional communication, a spokesperson for the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) has unleashed a statement so astonishingly absurd that it merits a closer examination. The HUD was called out by HuffPost journalist Jennifer Bendery for allowing employees to leave work early in honor of Easter and Passover, a move Bendery labeled as “a brazenly religious message.”

The HUD's Unhinged Counterattack

Rather than addressing the issue at hand, the HUD spokesperson opted for a wildly off-topic tirade, bizarrely accusing the HuffPost of suffering from "Trump Derangement Syndrome" and, even more ludicrously, of "standing up for criminal gang members while standing against the Judeo-Christian values our country was founded on." This statement is not just a non sequitur; it's a head-first dive into the deep end of irrationality.

Misquoting the Constitution

The spokesperson's claim that "Our constitution says that we were endowed by our creator" is a glaring misrepresentation. This phrase, as HuffPost’s senior White House correspondent SV Dáte correctly pointed out, comes from the Declaration of Independence, not the Constitution. This mix-up is not just a minor slip; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of American foundational texts, which is alarming coming from a federal department spokesperson.

"In God We Trust" and the First Amendment

The invocation of the motto "In God We Trust" and the assertion that HUD is simply following President Trump’s leadership in protecting the first amendment right to freedom of religion misconstrues the secular nature of the U.S. government. The First Amendment protects freedom of religion just as much as it protects freedom from religion, ensuring that government entities do not favor one religion over another—or religion over nonreligion.

The Actual Issue: Promoting Religion in a Federal Agency

The core issue here—HUD Secretary Scott Turner’s decision to send employees home early for religious observance—seems to have been lost in the spokesperson's histrionic response. This action raises significant concerns about the separation of church and state, a cornerstone of American democracy. Moreover, the fact that a group of HUD employees felt compelled to reach out to the Freedom From Religion Foundation indicates that this was not merely a generous gesture but one that uncomfortably blurred the lines between church and state.

Conclusion: A Reflection of a Broader Problem

This incident is emblematic of a broader problem within certain factions of the government: a disregard for professionalism, an inability to engage with criticism constructively, and a disturbing tendency to misrepresent foundational American principles to suit their narrative. The HUD spokesperson's response was not just "bonkers," as Bendery put it; it was a stark manifestation of the systemic issues plaguing our political discourse. In a time when clarity, accuracy, and respect for foundational American values are more critical than ever, such responses are not just laughable—they're deeply concerning.
