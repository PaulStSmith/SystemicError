Trump accidentally disproves his own claim about call with Gavin Newsom

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 11, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Misdirection: Trump's Fumble with FactsThe Illusion of CommunicationThe Reality of MisleadingThe Larger ImplicationConclusion

President Donald Trump mistakenly proved that he had falsely claimed that he called California Gov. Gavin Newsom "a day ago" on June 9 by providing a log that showed the conversation took place on June 7.

While speaking to reporters in the Oval Office on Tuesday, Trump was asked when the last time he talked to Newsom about the anti-ICE protests in Los Angeles.

"A day ago," Trump replied. "Called him up to tell him got to do a better job, he's doing a bad job. Causing a lot of death and potential death."

"There was no call. Not even a voicemail," Newsom later insisted. "Americans should be alarmed that a President deploying Marines onto our streets doesn't even know who he's talking to."

Trump responded by sending Fox News anchor John Roberts a screenshot of his call log, which showed the call occurred three days earlier.

Elon Musk's Grok AI explained the confusion.

"June 7th was not one day ago from June 10th, 2025," Grok said. "It was three days prior. The confusion may stem from conflicting claims about a call between President Trump and Governor Newsom, with a call log showing contact on June 7th, not June 9th as Trump suggested."

The Art of Misdirection: Trump's Fumble with Facts

In a spectacular display of what can only be described as a blend of misinformation and outright denial, President Donald Trump has once again found himself entangled in a web of his own making. This time, the debacle centers around his claim of having spoken to California Governor Gavin Newsom "a day ago" on June 9, regarding the anti-ICE protests in Los Angeles. However, the truth, as it often does, paints a starkly different picture.

Trump's assertion crumbled under the weight of evidence when he himself provided a log showing the conversation took place on June 7. This isn't just a minor discrepancy; it's a glaring testament to the President's cavalier attitude towards facts, timelines, and the significance of communication in the midst of a crisis.

The Illusion of Communication

Trump's claim to have reached out to Newsom to express his concerns over the handling of the protests is, at best, a misguided attempt to showcase leadership. At worst, it's a fabrication designed to mislead the public. The assertion that he called Newsom "a day ago" was not just incorrect; it was an attempt to create a narrative of proactive engagement where there was none. Governor Newsom's outright denial of such a call, stating, "There was no call. Not even a voicemail," adds layers to this saga of miscommunication and misinformation.

The Reality of Misleading

The irony of Trump responding by sending a screenshot of his call log to a Fox News anchor, intending to validate his claim, only to further incriminate himself, cannot be overstated. It's a vivid illustration of the chaos that ensues when the leader of the free world cannot keep track of his own statements. The intervention of Elon Musk's Grok AI to clarify the confusion only adds a touch of surrealism to the whole episode, highlighting the absurdity of needing artificial intelligence to fact-check the timeline of presidential communications.

The Larger Implication

What stands out most in this incident is not just the factual inaccuracy but the alarming indifference to the truth. Trump's casual approach to detailing his interaction with a state governor during a period of civil unrest speaks volumes about his disregard for the responsibilities of his office. Moreover, Newsom's pointed critique, "Americans should be alarmed that a President deploying Marines onto our streets doesn't even know who he's talking to," underscores the dangerous implications of such cavalier misinformation.

Conclusion

It underlines the critical need for accountability, transparency, and respect for the truth, especially when the stakes involve the deployment of military forces on American soil.
