'Hit a nerve': Insider reveals why Trump is 'even more frustrated' by nickname

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of the Nickname: Trump's TACO Tantrum

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., February 18, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Donald Trump recently lashed out at a reporter who the president said asked a "nasty" question, and another reporter on Saturday explained exactly why the commander-in-chief was "even more frustrated" by the inquiry.

Semafor's Shelby Talcott, who recently was in the Oval Office with Trump, appeared on MSNBC on Saturday to talk about Trump's recent actions.

After a segment on an Iran report that "frustrated' the Trump administration, the host asked Talcott about the question, which centered around TACO, or Trump Always Chickens Out, which is a derogatory nicknamed Wall Street investors are reportedly using to trade off of Trump's purported tendency to cave to tariff pressure.

EXCLUSIVE: Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

The host, Alex Witt, said Trump was "really lashing out at the insinuation that he keeps chickening out when it comes to implementing his tariff plan," and played a video of the reporter asking the question. That query "rubbed him the wrong way," Witt said.

"I mean, he normally loves a nickname or some kind of a quippy acronym," she added.

After experiencing a minor audio issue, Talcott said, "This is something that, you know, President Trump sort of touts his deal-making abilities, and so to be called a chicken, and in this sense, it's not surprising that he got frustrated."

Then she explained what her sources have been telling her regarding Trump's reply.

"But I'm also told, you know, he sort of alluded to this in his answer. He wasn't aware of this term before this reporter asked him, and so that sort of caught him off guard and frustrated him even more," she said.

Witt added, "TACO, it's, you know, kind of quippy. I mean, he's used to dishing it out. I'm just saying."

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Nickname: Trump's TACO Tantrum

In a spectacle that has become all too familiar, Donald Trump once again exhibited his notoriously thin skin when confronted with a moniker that did not bask in the glow of flattery. Semafor's Shelby Talcott's recount of Trump's reaction to being called out on his tariff timidity—courtesy of the acronym TACO (Trump Always Chickens Out)—is a case study in the former president's inability to take what he so freely dishes out.

The Irony of Trump's Displeasure

One cannot help but marvel at the irony of Donald Trump, a man who has built a significant portion of his political identity on the creation and propagation of derisive nicknames for his adversaries, finding himself at a loss when the linguistic sword is pointed in his direction. The term TACO, reported to be a creation of Wall Street investors critiquing Trump's habit of folding under tariff pressure, evidently hit a nerve. This incident underscores a critical flaw in Trump's armor: his profound aversion to being perceived as weak or indecisive, especially in the realm of deal-making, where he fancies himself a master.

The Response: A Predictable Pattern

Trump's reaction to the TACO query—lashing out at the reporter and expressing frustration—fits a well-established pattern. Throughout his presidency and subsequent public life, Trump has reacted with vitriol to any semblance of criticism or challenge. This instance was no different, except that it involved an acronym that not only mocked but also succinctly summarized a perceived policy failing. What makes this situation particularly delicious is Trump's reported ignorance of the term prior to the confrontation, a factor that reportedly "frustrated him even more."

A Missed Opportunity for Reflection

Rather than taking the criticism in stride, or even using it as a moment for self-reflection or policy clarification, Trump chose the path of least resistance: outright denial and deflection. This reaction is emblematic of Trump's broader inability to engage constructively with criticism. It's a missed opportunity to address the substance of the critique—that his approach to tariffs and trade negotiations has often been characterized by bluster followed by retreat.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is a microcosm of a larger pattern of behavior that has defined Trump's political and public life. His penchant for personal attacks, coupled with a glaring intolerance for receiving them, paints a picture of a leader more concerned with image than substance. The TACO incident, while seemingly trivial, is a telling insight into Trump's leadership style and his approach to governance. It also serves as a reminder of the importance of accountability and the power of a well-crafted nickname to reveal truths that long-winded critiques might not.

In conclusion, Trump's TACO tantrum offers a stark reminder of the former president's fragility in the face of criticism and his unwavering commitment to a persona that cannot admit fault or tolerate dissent. As Trump continues to navigate the turbulent waters of American politics, one can only wonder how many more acronyms, critiques, and nicknames will send him into a frenzy of frustration. In the end, the TACO episode is not just about a nickname; it's about the revealing nature of Trump's reactions to opposition, a fundamental flaw that continues to define his legacy.
