'Colossal waste of time': Retired Army general disgusted after Trump parade

Donald Trump's Saturday parade honoring the 250th anniversary of the establishment of the U.S. Army, which coincidentally fell on his 79th birthday, fell flat with one former Army general who expressed his disgust on MSNBC on Sunday morning.

The parade and following speech by the president, which was seen by a diminished crowd that reportedly started streaming out early, was panned on Saturday night and opinions did not change overnight.

Speaking with the hosts of MSNBC's "The Weekend," U.S. Army Brig. Gen Steve Anderson (RET.) stated that the event was a failure on multiple levels.

After praising the Army service members who were pressed into service for the celebration, Anderson lashed out at the president for his wastefulness.

"It was a colossal waste of time, effort and money we don't need," he bluntly stated. "That's not who we are; we don't do these kind of things. We don't march down the streets like that. We prove ourselves and our value as an army, our strength as an army through our actions, not parades. That's something that dictators do."

"And I think the money that was spent, I mean, it's colossal when you think about it: $45 million is what the White House admits to. But this town was shut down for three days –– it's still shut down out there! You can't get across the 14th Street Bridge, for instance, and you shut down the airport for 3.5 hours. You know, the tens of millions of dollars of inconveniences that were created in the town, Uber drivers are complaining, you know, the hotels have been shut down. I mean, you can't get around. And so there's a tremendous cost associated with that. We're going to have to fix all the roads, by the way."

"And think about what we could do with that money if we had it instead," he pointed out. "I mean, the soldiers, sailors, airmen, marines out there, they need support. Donald Trump already stole $1 billion from MILCON, for military construction, for the for the southern border. I mean, so there's barracks that are not being renovated, there's child-care centers that are not being built. There are perks that that soldiers and families and their families deserve. and they're not going to be able to get because Donald Trump has stolen this money, essentially taken it and used it."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Parade: A Masterclass in Misplaced Priorities

Donald Trump's recent parade, ostensibly to honor the 250th anniversary of the establishment of the U.S. Army—which conveniently coincided with his 79th birthday—has been met with the kind of enthusiasm one might reserve for a root canal. Despite the fanfare, the event was less a grandiose celebration of military might and more an exercise in ego-stroking, as evidenced by the sparse crowd and the early exodus of attendees.

A Waste of Resources

U.S. Army Brig. Gen Steve Anderson (RET.) didn't mince words when he lambasted the event on MSNBC's "The Weekend," calling it a "colossal waste of time, effort, and money." Anderson's criticism cuts to the heart of the issue: the grotesque expenditure on a parade of pomp and circumstance at a time when the U.S. faces pressing fiscal challenges. The general's disdain for the spectacle is palpable, and rightly so. The United States Army proves its mettle through deeds, not displays—a sentiment echoed by military leaders and personnel who value substance over showmanship.

The Cost of Vanity

The financial toll of Trump's parade is nothing short of staggering. With the White House admitting to a cost of $45 million, one shudders at the thought of the actual price tag once all ancillary expenses are tallied. The logistical nightmare created by this event—a three-day shutdown of significant parts of the city, including major bridges and the airport—speaks volumes about the misplaced priorities of the administration. The disruption to local businesses and the inconvenience to residents are just collateral damage in the grand scheme of Trump's vanity project.

Robbing Peter to Pay...Trump?

Perhaps the most egregious aspect of this debacle is the diversion of funds from critical military needs to finance the parade. Anderson highlights the bitter irony of Trump siphoning off $1 billion from military construction (MILCON) for his border wall, depriving service members and their families of essential services and infrastructure improvements. Barracks remain unrenovated, childcare centers unbuilt, and soldiers' families underserved—all in service of a misguided display of military pageantry.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Leadership

Trump's parade is emblematic of his tenure: a spectacle over substance approach that prioritizes personal aggrandizement over the public good. The event was not just a missed opportunity to genuinely honor the U.S. Army's storied history; it was a blatant misuse of taxpayer dollars and a slap in the face to the very principles of military service. In a world fraught with challenges, both domestic and international, the U.S. cannot afford leaders who mistake leadership for showmanship. The soldiers, sailors, airmen, and marines deserve better. America deserves better.
