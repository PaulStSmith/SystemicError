Alleged Epstein co-conspirators named on House floor as lawmaker overrides redactions

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Representative Ro Khanna (D‑CA) speaks to the media after viewing unredacted Jeffrey Epstein files at the Department of Justice office building in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 9, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

An animated Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) took to the House floor Tuesday to publicly reveal the names of six “powerful men” alleged to be co-conspirators of Jeffrey Epstein, reading their names as a matter of public record.

“There were six wealthy, powerful men that the DOJ hid for no apparent reason!” said Khanna, who alongside Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) had filed the bill that ultimately forced the DOJ to release its files on Epstein. “When Congressman Massie and I pointed this out to the DOJ, they acknowledged their mistake, and now they have revealed the identity of these six powerful men.”

Both Khanna and Massie alleged on Monday that the DOJ was actively protecting the identities of the aforementioned six men, who were identified in a DOJ document as potential co-conspirators of Epstein’s. Their pressure on the DOJ, Khanna said, ultimately forced the agency to un-redact the alleged co-conspirators’ identities, prompting even more questions from the California lawmaker.

“My question is, why did it take Thomas Massie and I going to the Justice Department to get these six men’s identities to become public?” Khanna said. “And if we found six men that they were hiding in two hours, imagine how many men they are covering up for in those 3 million files!”

The names Khanna gave are Salvatore Nuara, Zurab Mikeladze, Leonic Leonov, Nicola Caputo, Sultan Ahmed Bin Sulayem, and billionaire businessman Leslie Wexner

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling: A Glimpse into the Abyss

In a bold and animated display of political defiance, Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) has thrust into the public sphere the names of six “powerful men” linked to Jeffrey Epstein, an act that pierces the veil of secrecy maintained by the Department of Justice (DOJ). This revelation, made from the floors of the House, isn’t just a routine announcement; it’s a seismic jolt to the foundations of power and privilege that have long shielded the elite.

The Curious Case of DOJ’s “Oversight”

Khanna, alongside Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), has been at the forefront of a legislative push that resulted in the DOJ releasing files on Epstein, a move that uncovered the identities of six men previously hidden by what can only be described as a bureaucratic sleight of hand. The question that burns through the fog of this revelation is as pointed as it is profound: Why was the DOJ, an institution tasked with upholding justice, engaged in the active concealment of these names?

Khanna’s outrage is not just justified, it’s essential. The act of unmasking these individuals - Salvatore Nuara, Zurab Mikeladze, Leonic Leonov, Nicola Caputo, Sultan Ahmed Bin Sulayem, and billionaire businessman Leslie Wexner - serves as a stark reminder of the opaque and often incestuous relationship between power, wealth, and the mechanisms of justice.

The Tip of the Iceberg

Khanna’s rhetorical query to the DOJ underscores a chilling possibility: if a mere legislative nudge was all it took to unearth these names, what other secrets lie buried within the DOJ’s 3 million files on Epstein? The implications are staggering and point to a systemic rot that transcends individual actors.

The bipartisan effort by Khanna and Massie illuminates a rare but vital unity in the face of a corrupt nexus of power. It’s a reminder that the fight against the machinations of the elite cannot be pigeonholed into neat political categories. The Epstein saga, with its labyrinthine network of influencers, moguls, and power brokers, transcends political divides, implicating a global elite in a web of complicity and cover-up.

A Moment of Reckoning

This moment on the House floor transcends mere political theater; it’s a clarion call for accountability and transparency. It challenges not only the DOJ but also the societal structures that perpetuate silence and complicity in the face of egregious abuses of power. Khanna and Massie’s actions represent not just a quest for answers but a bold step towards dismantling the protective barriers that shield the elite from scrutiny.

The unmasking of these “powerful men” is more than a revelation; it’s a testament to the persistence of those willing to challenge the status quo. But let us not be naive. This is but an opening salvo in a much larger battle against a deeply entrenched system that prioritizes power over justice, wealth over accountability.

In this context, the actions of Khanna and Massie are not just commendable; they are a beacon of resistance in an era marked by obfuscation and impunity. The road ahead is fraught with challenges, but the unveiling of these names serves as a powerful reminder that no one is above the law, and that the pursuit of justice, however arduous, is a fight worth waging.
