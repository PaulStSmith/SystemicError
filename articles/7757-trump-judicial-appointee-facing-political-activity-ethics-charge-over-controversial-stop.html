Trump judicial appointee facing 'political activity' ethics charge over controversial stop

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unsettling Trajectory of Emil Bove: From Trump’s Lawyer to Federal Judge

Donald Trump, Emil Bove (Photo via Curtis Means for Reuters)

A former Donald Trump lawyer who leapfrogged from that position to an FBI job before being appointed to a lifetime position on the federal bench could be headed for a court-ordered investigation after an ethics complaint was filed late Wednesday.

Controversial attorney Emil Bove, who is still under a cloud over his involvement in ignoring another federal judge’s order to halt the forcible deportation of immigrants, is now being scrutinized for attending a Trump rally in a tiny Pennsylvania resort town on Tuesday.

According to a report from the New York Times, Bove was photographed in the crowd at the casino in the Poconos where the president gave a rambling speech, which set in motion the ethics complaint from advocacy group Fix the Court that was filed with the chief judge of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.

The report notes that Fix the Court’s Gabe Roth is accusing Judge Bove of defying the rules about the “appearance of impropriety” and engaging in “political activity.”

According to Roth, “This was a highly charged, highly political event that no federal judge should have been within shouting distance of,” which led conservative critic, and former Justice Antonin Scalia clerk, Ed Whelan, to tell the Times, “I can’t understand how he could possibly think it appropriate to go there. You can argue about whether the rules clearly prohibit what he did, but he showed terrible judgment.”

On her Substack platform, former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance piled on and noted that any other federal judge would have avoided the event like it was “the plague.”

As for the possibility of an investigation, she explained, ”Most complaints end with a dismissal, but in some cases, there is an investigation, and it can be significant. When it’s concluded, the special committee submits a report of its findings and recommendations to the judicial council. The council can dismiss a case at this state, or conclude it because corrective action has been taken.”

She added that no matter what happens to Bove — which could include censure or being suspended from hearing cases — federal appointees, “... get life tenure. In exchange, they should refrain from behavior that is arguably permissible if it could damage the integrity of the judiciary or create even an appearance of impropriety.”

Echoing comments Bove reportedly made in the deportation imbroglio that is still going on, Vance wrote, “ That’s the job. If you don’t want it, then don’t become a federal judge. Otherwise, you might just as well say ‘F--- the courts.’”

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Trajectory of Emil Bove: From Trump’s Lawyer to Federal Judge

In a troubling yet unsurprising turn of events, Emil Bove, a former attorney for Donald Trump, has found himself at the center of an ethics storm. His journey from Trump’s legal team to an FBI role, and then astonishingly, to a lifetime appointment on the federal bench, reads more like a cautionary tale of political patronage gone awry than a merit-based ascension to one of the judiciary’s most esteemed positions.

Bove’s latest escapade, attending a Trump rally in a small resort town in Pennsylvania, has triggered an ethics complaint spearheaded by the advocacy group Fix the Court. The complaint underscores a glaring “appearance of impropriety” and accuses Bove of engaging in political activity, which is a clear violation of the ethical standards expected of federal judges.

It’s disheartening to note that this is not Bove’s first brush with controversy. His past is marred by his involvement in the forcible deportation of immigrants, blatantly ignoring another federal judge’s order to halt such actions. This previous ethical lapse should have served as a red flag, yet here we are again, witnessing another chapter of his questionable judgment unfold.

The presence of a federal judge at a politically charged event, especially one associated with a figure as divisive as Donald Trump, is not only inappropriate but also deeply concerning. It raises questions about impartiality, integrity, and the very essence of judicial conduct. As pointed out by Gabe Roth of Fix the Court, no federal judge should have been “within shouting distance” of such an event. This sentiment is echoed by conservative critic Ed Whelan, who expressed disbelief at Bove’s appalling judgment.

Former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance’s commentary on the situation further amplifies the severity of Bove’s actions. Her analogy, likening other federal judges’ avoidance of such politically charged events to avoiding “the plague,” starkly highlights the breach of decorum Bove engaged in. Vance’s insights into the potential outcomes of the ethics complaint process—ranging from dismissal to significant investigations and possible censure—shed light on the mechanisms in place to address such breaches. Yet, her conclusion is a stark reminder of the lifelong tenure federal appointees enjoy, underscoring the expectation of exemplary behavior in exchange for such a privilege.

Bove’s actions, and the subsequent outcry, underscore a deeper, systemic issue within the judiciary and political appointments. The expectation that federal judges maintain an unwavering commitment to impartiality and avoid any “appearance of impropriety” is not merely a guideline but a foundational principle of the judiciary’s integrity. Emil Bove’s decision to attend a Trump rally, therefore, is not just a lapse in judgment but a flagrant disregard for the principles that underpin the judiciary’s role in democracy.

As this situation unfolds, one cannot help but ponder the long-term implications of such conduct on the judiciary’s credibility and the broader implications for the rule of law. The case of Emil Bove is a glaring example of the challenges facing the judicial system in an era of heightened political polarization. It begs the question: how can the judiciary maintain its integrity when its members flout the very rules designed to protect its impartiality?

In conclusion, Emil Bove’s attendance at a Trump rally is not just a personal misstep; it’s a systemic error that reflects poorly on the judiciary’s current state. It’s a wake-up call for a thorough examination of the processes that allowed someone with Bove’s controversial past to ascend to the federal bench. For the sake of the judiciary’s integrity, let’s hope this incident prompts a much-needed introspection and reform. After all, as Joyce Vance aptly put it, if one cannot adhere to the ethical standards expected of a federal judge, perhaps they should reconsider their suitability for the role altogether. Otherwise, they’re essentially saying, “F--- the courts.” And that, dear readers, is something our democracy can ill afford.
