'More egg on the faces of prosecutors': Expert blasts Trump DOJ's new prosecution threat

Based on an article by Nicole Charky-Chami

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Power Is the PointThe Target Is Deliberately Weaker“Misled Congress” Is Doing a Lot of WorkThe Framing Is MisdirectionProsecutorial Revenge as Party DisciplineWhat This Reveals

Trump and Jordan Are Not Pursuing Justice. They Are Prosecuting the Record.

Power Is the Point

The immediate story is simple: Rep. Jim Jordan asked the Justice Department to consider criminal charges against Jack Smith, claiming Smith misled Congress about phone records obtained during the 2020 election investigation. Donald Trump then amplified the request with his own “Justice at last?” post. That is the context. The meaning is uglier.

This is not a neutral demand for legal clarity. It is a test of whether Republican power can turn an investigation into a weapon against the investigator.

The Target Is Deliberately Weaker

Jack Smith is no longer the special counsel prosecuting Trump. That matters. The people pushing this are not confronting an equal force; they are aiming at a former prosecutor who is now exposed to the machinery they control or influence. Jim Jordan chairs the House Judiciary Committee. Trump commands the party line. Together they can convert institutional stature into a campaign of retaliation.

That is the actual structure here: power above, vulnerability below, and a legal complaint stretched to fit the revenge.

“Misled Congress” Is Doing a Lot of Work

The allegation is that Smith misled Congress about phone records gathered from members of Congress. But the source material already shows the gap between accusation and crime. David Aronberg, a former state attorney, said Smith did not lie and called the episode “a political game, not a serious matter for the courts.” He also drew the obvious line: if the complaint is that Smith did not volunteer more information in advance of what senators and House members were thinking, that is not criminal conduct.

That distinction matters because the accusation is being dressed up as law while functioning like punishment. The point is not to establish a clean legal theory. The point is to make the process itself the spectacle.

The Framing Is Misdirection

Republicans are trying to recast accountability as misconduct by the person doing the accounting. That is a familiar move. It shifts attention away from the underlying investigation into Trump and toward procedural grumbling about the prosecutor. It tells the public to focus on whether Smith was sufficiently deferential to lawmakers who were under scrutiny, instead of asking why the people now invoking “justice” are so eager to criminalize the work that once threatened them.

Aronberg’s warning that this is political retribution is the right frame. The weak legal claim is the cover story. The real goal is to scare investigators, punish independent enforcement, and advertise that the party in power can reach backward through the system and strike at anyone who touched Trump.

Prosecutorial Revenge as Party Discipline

Trump’s “Justice at last?” post gives the game away. This is not a search for a cleaner justice system. It is a loyalty ritual. The message to Republicans is that the justice system is legitimate when it protects Trump and illegitimate when it scrutinizes him. That is not conservatism; it is a hostile takeover of law by grievance.

The mention of Letitia James and James Comey in the source makes the pattern harder to deny. The same apparatus that complains endlessly about weaponization is busy using state power to settle scores. It is revenge with a flag draped over it.

What This Reveals

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics does not merely evade accountability. It attacks the people and institutions that generate it. When the facts become inconvenient, the remedy is not self-correction. It is to turn prosecutors into defendants, investigations into offenses, and public power into a private shield.

That is the real meaning of this episode. The issue is not whether Jack Smith was perfectly generous to Congress. The issue is that Trump and his allies are trying to make political retaliation look like legal seriousness.
