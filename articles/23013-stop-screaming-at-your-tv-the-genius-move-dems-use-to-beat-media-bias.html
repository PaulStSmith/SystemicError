Stop screaming at your TV: The genius move Dems use to beat media bias

Based on an article by John Stoehr

The Press Keeps Handing Republicans the Center

The Source of the Problem

The article is right about the basic damage: political journalism keeps treating Republicans as the default measure of reality, then asking Democrats to prove they deserve to exist within it. The result is not balance. It is a legitimacy machine for power that already has the state, the courts, the donor class, and most of the institutional habit of being taken seriously.

That is the real story here. Not that liberals are too emotional about media coverage. Not that they need better messaging. The problem is that the press keeps assigning Republicans the role of natural authority and forcing Democrats to enter the frame as defendants.

Who Gets to Be “Normal”

The source correctly identifies a central media trick: Republicans are treated as the party of presumed legitimacy, while Democrats are treated as a party that must constantly justify itself. That is not neutrality. It is a structural preference dressed up as professionalism.

The article goes further, and the point is stronger than the prose gives it credit for. When reporters center Republican outrage, Republican definitions of sexism, Republican standards for what counts as acceptable political speech, they are not merely covering the story. They are deciding which worldview sets the terms. The audience is then asked to react inside that box.

That is how misdirection works. The press presents the Republican frame as the neutral one, then calls it objectivity when everyone else is forced to respond to it.

The Double Standard Is the Mechanism

The article’s best claim is that Democrats are treated as suspicious by default because they are multiracial, multi-ethnic, and multi-religious in a political culture that still confers automatic seriousness on whiteness. That is an inference, but it is not a random one. It explains a lot of what the source describes: why Republican standards travel as common sense, why Democratic legitimacy is treated as contingent, and why journalists keep acting as if the GOP owns the center of American life.

This is not just a matter of tone. It shapes whose misconduct gets normalized and whose response gets pathologized. A Republican can behave like an open sexist, and the coverage becomes a debate about political strategy. A Democrat can refuse the premise of the question, and suddenly the issue is whether they are cooperative enough, disciplined enough, or appropriately apologetic.

That asymmetry is the system. The system is the story.

Ossoff and El-Sayed Refuse the Script

The article points to Jon Ossoff and Abdul El-Sayed as examples of Democrats refusing to kneel to the press’s preferred choreography. Ossoff does not spend his energy flattering the mythology around Trump. He names Trump as weak, insulated, ornamental, and unserious, which is closer to the truth than the solemn deference the media usually awards any occupant of the Oval Office.

El-Sayed’s exchange is even more revealing. He declines to let a campaign be defined by someone else’s stream, someone else’s bad faith, or the press’s demand that he perform ritual separation before he can speak about his own race. That demand is familiar because it is not really about antisemitism, coalition politics, or responsibility. It is about control. The media wants the right to decide which associations count, which explanations are valid, and which candidates are allowed to frame their own work.

When El-Sayed pushes back, he is not just sparring with a host. He is rejecting the premise that journalists are entitled to stand above politics while assigning everyone else a subordinate role inside it.

What the Framing Still Misses

The source is strongest when it attacks media gatekeeping, but it still softens the political stakes by making the struggle sound like a matter of strategy and self-confidence. It is more than that. The press is not merely over-centering Republicans out of habit. It is participating in a hierarchy that protects reactionary power by treating it as the baseline.

That is why the Republican Party keeps getting the benefit of the doubt even when it behaves like a machine for domination. Its agenda is laundered through the language of seriousness. Its misogyny gets called politics. Its racial comfort is treated as normal. Its cruelty is reframed as debate. Meanwhile, Democrats are pushed into endless self-explanation, as if legitimacy is a privilege they must earn from institutions that are already inclined to deny it.

That is not an accident. It is how power preserves itself without having to announce its name.

The Larger Pattern

The deeper lesson is simple: if Democrats let Republicans define the center, they will keep losing before the argument even starts. A political movement cannot win by asking permission from institutions built to mistrust it. It has to move the frame, not plead inside it.

The source is right to celebrate Democrats who refuse to accept the press’s rules. But the larger lesson is harsher. The media’s habit of centering Republicans is not a neutral error. It is one of the ways American political legitimacy gets rationed upward to the right. Until Democrats stop performing for that system, they will keep being treated as guests in their own democracy.
