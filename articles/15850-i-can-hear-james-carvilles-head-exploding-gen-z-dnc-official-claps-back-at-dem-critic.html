'I can hear James Carville’s head exploding': Gen Z DNC official claps back at Dem critic

By Paulo Santos

(Screengrab via CNN)

Democratic National Committee Vice Chair David Hogg escalated the firestorm he created this week when he announced a new multimillion-dollar campaign targeting incumbents in his party – an effort he made clear that he isn’t backing down from.

Hogg on Friday jabbed critics inside his party who have slammed his new campaign, including longtime Democratic strategist James Carville, who called it “the most insane thing I ever heard.”

“Aren’t we supposed to run against Republicans?" Carville said in remarks critical of Hogg on Wednesday. The DNC official responded directly to Carville with his own question before ripping open the internal party feud even further.

“What’s his plan to deal with our 27% approval rating? That’s my question to him,” Hogg told CNN’s Jake Tapper during an appearance on his show, “The Lead.”

“The fact of the matter is, right now, our party is in a crisis,” he said. Hogg went on to say that the Democratic Party should be propping up a message that encourages people to support them, “not simply because we're not Donald Trump, but because we have a better message.”

But he wasn’t done dressing down Carville, who shot to legendary status among Democrats after leading Bill Clinton’s successful 1992 presidential campaign as chief strategist.

“Carville believes in a politics of being timid, of hiding,” Hogg said. “I believe in fighting, and that is what people want to see right now from the Democratic Party.”

He mentioned as an example Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) traveling to El Salvador in defense of the wrongly deported Maryland father Kilmar Abrego Garcia.

"We can't simply just hide as Carville repeatedly promotes,” Hogg added. “Not to mention the fact that, frankly, he has not won an election since before I was born. I think it's time for some new voices in our party.”

The 25-year-old told Tapper he was born in 2000.

“I can hear James Carville’s head exploding all the way from New Orleans when you called him timid,” Tapper quipped.

The CNN host let Carville know he’s welcome back on his show next week.

Our Analysis:

The Democratic Schism: David Hogg vs. The Old Guard

In a move that has sent shockwaves through the Democratic establishment, David Hogg, the Vice Chair of the Democratic National Committee, has ignited a firestorm within his own party. His announcement of a multimillion-dollar campaign aimed at unseating incumbents within the Democratic Party is nothing short of a political earthquake. Hogg's audacious strategy has not only drawn the ire of party stalwarts but has also laid bare the simmering tensions between the old guard and the new wave of Democratic activists.

The Carville Conundrum

James Carville, a figure synonymous with Democratic strategy and success in the 90s, finds himself at the epicenter of Hogg's critique. Carville's incredulous response to Hogg's campaign, labeling it “the most insane thing I ever heard,” encapsulates a generational and ideological rift within the party. Carville's question, “Aren’t we supposed to run against Republicans?” reveals a fundamental disagreement on the direction and strategy of the Democratic Party.

Hogg's counter is both pointed and poignant. He questions Carville's strategy in the face of a 27% approval rating for the party. This is not just a retort; it's a clarion call for introspection within the Democratic ranks. Hogg challenges the party's complacency and the inefficacy of a strategy that leans too heavily on anti-Trump sentiment rather than promoting a compelling, positive message. His critique of Carville as a symbol of “politics of being timid” strikes at the heart of the matter - the need for a Democratic Party that fights rather than hides.

A Party in Crisis

Hogg's declaration that the party is in a crisis and his insistence on a more assertive stance reflects a broader existential question facing Democrats: What does the party stand for? The reference to Senator Chris Van Hollen's proactive defense of Kilmar Abrego Garcia in El Salvador serves as a case in point for Hogg's vision of a party that takes bold actions for justice, a stark contrast to the perceived timidity Carville represents.

The Call for New Voices

Perhaps the most scathing of Hogg's criticisms is his allusion to Carville's age and the bygone era of his electoral successes. The assertion that Carville "has not won an election since before I was born" is not just a jab at Carville's age; it's a damning indictment of a party leadership disconnected from the current socio-political landscape and the younger generation's aspirations. Hogg's campaign is more than a political maneuver; it's a demand for generational and ideological renewal within the Democratic Party.

Conclusion: A Party at Crossroads

The feud between Hogg and Carville symbolizes a party at a crossroads. On one side stands the old guard, steeped in the victories and strategies of the past; on the other, a new generation of Democrats, like Hogg, who are impatient for change and unafraid to challenge the status quo. This internal battle is not just about personal disagreements; it's about the soul of the Democratic Party and its path forward. Will the party heed Hogg's call for a bolder, more assertive approach, or will it retreat to the familiar comfort of the strategies of yesteryears? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the Democratic Party cannot afford to ignore the systemic error within its ranks.
