Several recent steps taken by President Donald Trump have been criticized as being undemocratic and "authoritarian" in nature. Earlier this month, a survey of more than 500 political scientists found that most think the United States is transitioning from liberal democracy toward some form of authoritarianism.

An article by Gregg Barak published in Salon on Wednesday said that "The Turner Diaries," a 1978 novel by William Luther Pierce, the founder of an American white nationalist group, is what drives the Make America Great Again (MAGA) worldview, which is seen the factor behind these "authoritarian" moves by the administration.

The article called the novel "modern America’s original manual of hatred."

"First published in 1978 and recently banned by Jeff Bezos’ Amazon following the assault on the US Capitol on January 6, thanks to the combined minds of Steve Bannon and Steve Miller, this racist dystopian novel about a white supremacist insurrection undergirds the Trumpian worldview. In a nutshell, the book is an apocalyptic tale of genocide against racial minorities set in a near-future America," Barak wrote in the article.

"This narrative successfully captured 49.8% of the voting electorate in November 2024. First introduced in 2015 after Donald and Melania Trump came down the Trump Tower escalator to announce his bid for the Republican nomination, the premise was always focal to his three political campaigns and his first term of abuse, lawlessness and corruption. Soon after the failed coup d’état on Jan. 6, 2021, this narrative became the core message of Trumpism," he added.

"Perhaps nothing captures Trump’s authoritarian agenda better than ICE’s illegal kidnapping and disappearing of hundreds of people or DOGE’s firings or dismissals of some 250,000 federal workers – all without any due process of law. All of which makes perfect sense in the Trumpian schemes to dismantle and emasculate USAID worldwide and to the Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s rationale for the proposed 'redesign' of the State Department to diminish or do away with human rights programs and others targeting war crimes or the strengthening of freedom and democracy," Barak said.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Trump Administration's Authoritarian Tendencies Through the Lens of 'The Turner Diaries'

In an era where the line between political maneuvering and outright authoritarianism becomes increasingly blurred, recent actions by President Donald Trump’s administration have sparked a fierce debate regarding the democratic health of the United States. A comprehensive review of the situation reveals a disturbing trend, one that suggests a shift towards an undemocratic ethos that is antithetical to the fundamental principles upon which the country was founded.

Authoritarianism in the Guise of MAGA: A Dangerous Precedent

The heart of the matter lies in the administration's apparent embrace of ideologies that are starkly authoritarian, if not outright dictatorial. This is not merely speculative fear-mongering but a conclusion drawn by a significant body of over 500 political scientists. Their consensus paints a grim picture of the U.S.'s trajectory away from liberal democracy towards a more authoritarian model. This shift is deeply concerning, not least because of the historical consequences that follow such transitions in governance.

The Disturbing Influence of 'The Turner Diaries'

Central to understanding the MAGA movement's authoritarian tilt is its ideological foundation, which, as Gregg Barak astutely points out in his Salon article, finds inspiration in "The Turner Diaries," a notorious novel that has long been a touchstone for white nationalist and extremist thought in America. The novel's themes of racial hatred, insurrection, and genocide are not merely abstract fears but have found echoes in the policies and rhetoric emanating from the Trump administration.

Barak’s analysis is not to be taken lightly. The novel's influence on the MAGA worldview is a stark reminder of the dangerous undercurrents of extremism that threaten the fabric of American society. The fact that this narrative captured a significant portion of the electorate in 2024 is a testament to the chilling effectiveness of this ideology in shaping political discourse and action.

The Reality of Trumpian Authoritarianism

Perhaps most damning is the administration's actions, which bear the hallmark characteristics of authoritarian regimes. The illegal detainment and disappearance of individuals by ICE, the mass firings and dismissals of federal workers by DOGE, and the systematic dismantling of USAID, among other actions, are not isolated incidents but part of a broader agenda to undermine the very institutions designed to uphold democracy and human rights.

The proposed "redesign" of the State Department by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, aimed at diminishing programs focused on human rights and war crimes, is particularly egregious. It represents a clear intent to erode the mechanisms of accountability and transparency that are essential for a healthy democracy.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The implications of the Trump administration's authoritarian bent are far-reaching. The influence of "The Turner Diaries" and the MAGA movement's adoption of its hateful thesis are not just political curiosities but real and present dangers to the American democratic experiment. As Barak rightly points out, the right wing’s fascination with this dystopian narrative has evolved from a fringe conspiracy theory to a driving force behind the current administration's authoritarian actions.

It’s crucial that these developments are not viewed in isolation but as part of a concerted effort to reshape the United States into a model that is fundamentally at odds with democracy. The fight against this tide is not merely political; it is a fight for the soul of the nation itself.
