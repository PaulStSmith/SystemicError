As political commentator Matthew Yglesias writes, Trump's allies in the business community learned that Trump "is crazier than they thought" thanks to his erratic tariff policies, and he makes the case that they should understand that Trump is behaving just as destructively in other policy areas.

Among other things, Yglesias points to the fact that Trump recently fired several national security officials at the behest of Laura Loomer, a right-wing influencer with a long history of pushing racist conspiracy theories whom even many Republicans regard as being akin to "radioactive waste from Godzilla."

Yglesias goes on to document Trump policy blunders with dissolving USAID, which he said has led to the deaths of children in Africa; his slashing of scientific research and education funding; and his decision to let Elon Musk and his staff at the Department of Government Efficiency make cuts to staffing and regional offices at the Social Security Administration.

"My point is that far from seeing Trump’s reversal as a positive sign, it ought to be an eye-opening moment for sane people on the center-right about the fact that these guys really will do things that are incredibly crazy and stupid," he warns. "They are, in fact, doing many such things."

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Business Confidence in Trump’s America

The recent "Liberation Day" tariffs introduced by President Donald Trump have not only sent shockwaves through the stock market but have also shattered the illusion that Trump was the pro-business president many CEOs had hoped for. This development serves as a stark wakeup call, revealing Trump’s governance as not just unpredictable but dangerously erratic.

The Illusion of Stability Shattered

For those in the business community who once thought aligning with Trump would be beneficial, the reality has proven to be a bitter pill. The dramatic downturn in the stock market following the announcement of the tariffs is just the tip of the iceberg. Political commentator Matthew Yglesias rightly points out that Trump's behavior should not only be seen as a deviation but as a consistent pattern of destructive governance.

The Alarm Bells Ignored

The dismissal of several national security officials at the suggestion of Laura Loomer, a figure so controversial that even her own political allies regard her as toxic, should have served as a clear warning. Yet, it was largely overlooked, buried under the avalanche of daily controversies. This episode is emblematic of the Trump administration’s penchant for prioritizing fringe voices over seasoned expertise, a practice that has far-reaching consequences beyond the immediate shock value.

A Trail of Policy Blunders

The dissolution of USAID and the slashing of scientific research and education funding are not mere policy shifts; they are indicative of a government that has turned its back on global responsibilities and the future of innovation. The decision to let Elon Musk spearhead cuts in the Social Security Administration under the guise of government efficiency is particularly jarring. It represents a stark departure from the administration’s duty to protect its most vulnerable citizens.

The Bigger Picture

Yglesias’s warning to the sane elements within the center-right is a dire one: the erratic and destructive policies of Trump are not outliers but part of a broader pattern of governance that is antithetical to rational, responsible leadership. The business community, once hopeful for a pro-business administration, now finds itself navigating a landscape of volatility and unpredictability.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call for the Business Community

The unveiling of the "Liberation Day" tariffs is more than just an economic blip; it is a clarion call for those who had harbored any remaining illusions about the Trump administration’s compatibility with sound business practices. The hope that Trump would champion the business community has been dashed, replaced by the stark reality of an administration governed by caprice rather than strategy. It’s an eye-opening moment that should prompt serious reflection on the part of those who once thought a Trump presidency would be good for business. In Trump’s America, the only certainty is uncertainty, and the cost of this unpredictability is borne by everyone.
