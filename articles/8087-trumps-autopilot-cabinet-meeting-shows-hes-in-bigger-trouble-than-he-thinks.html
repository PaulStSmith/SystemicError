Trump’s 'autopilot' Cabinet meeting shows he’s 'in bigger trouble than he thinks'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 4, 20254 min read

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 26, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst/File Photo

President Donald Trump held one of his regular cabinet meetings on Tuesday afternoon, and amid a growing list of heavily publicized scandals and his own plummeting popularity, one Democratic strategist argued that the affair showed that he his “in bigger trouble than he thinks.”

Writing for The Hill on Wednesday, “veteran Democratic strategist” Max Burns analyzed the “directionless” cabinet meeting in unsparing terms. He lambasted the various secretaries as “so preoccupied with propping up Trump’s fragile ego that they have lost even a passing interest in actually governing” and concluded that “absolutely no one” is “really running the country.”

There are various reasons, Burns explained, why this meeting came “at a tough psychological moment for the president.” Just last week, Trump’s national approval rating fell by five points to 36%, the lowest ever recording by the long-running Gallup poll. The poll also found that Trump’s approval ratings on some of the key issue that have propelled his election victories -- like immigration, the economy and foreign policy -- are also now underwater, signaling an voter base that is no longer happy about anything he is doing, as consumers prices continue to skyrocket, jobs dry up and ICE deportation raids leave a bad taste in the mouths of many onlookers.

The meeting also comes on the heels of a ballooning scandal surrounding Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, whom a Washington Post report found to have ordered a second strike on survivors of an initial boat strike in the Caribbean Sea, an move that many experts believe would amount to a war crime or simply outright murder. After initially denying the story, the White House ultimately acknowledged that the strike played out largely as detailed in the report, and Hegseth himself boasted about the boat strike campaign at the meeting.

“We’ve only just begun striking narco boats and putting narco terrorists at the bottom of the ocean,” Hegseth said, though as Burns noted, the Pentagon has acknowledged that the identities of many of the people on the boats are unknown, and evidence of criminality has not been presented to the public.

Again, Burns explained, these strikes are another issue the Trump administration is digging its heels in on, despite only 29% of Americans approving of them.

“You people are crazy,” Trump said to reporters at the meeting, addressing questions about the mounting concerns surrounding his presidency. “I’ll let you know when something is wrong.”

“Really?” Burns wrote in response. “If this is what the country looks like when things are going well, Trump is in bigger trouble than he thinks.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Cabinet Circus: Governance or Ego-Stroking Session?

In a recent spectacle that President Donald Trump called a cabinet meeting, it became glaringly obvious that his administration is more concerned with massaging the president’s ego than addressing the critical issues facing the United States. Veteran Democratic strategist Max Burns, in his analysis for The Hill, doesn’t mince words when he describes the gathering as “directionless,” a term that could easily serve as the motto for Trump’s tenure in the Oval Office.

Burns’s critique highlights a disturbing trend: secretaries and advisers seem more invested in being sycophants rather than stewards of the nation. This is not just an embarrassing display of loyalty over logic; it’s a dangerous abdication of responsibility. When the focus shifts from governing to ego-boosting, the country suffers.

A Psychological Low Point

This cabinet meeting coincided with a nosedive in Trump’s approval ratings, now at a dismal 36% according to Gallup. But it’s not just the numbers that are alarming; it’s what they signify. Trump’s presidency, fueled by promises of strong immigration policies, economic growth, and foreign policy prowess, is failing on all fronts. Skyrocketing consumer prices, job scarcity, and the bitter aftertaste of ICE raids are just the tip of the iceberg.

A Scandal Too Far?

The meeting also shed light on a burgeoning scandal involving Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and a second strike on survivors of a boat strike in the Caribbean. Hegseth’s boast about “putting narco terrorists at the bottom of the ocean” would be laughable if it weren’t so horrifying. The Pentagon’s admission that many of the boat’s occupants’ identities and criminal affiliations are unknown raises grave ethical concerns. This isn’t just about poor decision-making; it’s a potential war crime.

Burns rightly points out the administration’s stubbornness in the face of overwhelming disapproval. With only 29% of Americans in favor of the strikes, it’s clear that Trump and his cabinet are out of step with the nation’s values and concerns.

Governance or Madness?

Trump’s dismissive response to reporters’ questions — “You people are crazy. I’ll let you know when something is wrong” — is emblematic of his presidency’s disconnect from reality. If this is Trump’s idea of “things going well,” then his presidency is not just in trouble; it’s in freefall.

Max Burns’s analysis is a stark reminder of the consequences of prioritizing presidential egos over effective governance. The cabinet meeting wasn’t just directionless; it was a vivid illustration of an administration that has lost its moral compass and strategic direction. The Trump administration needs a reality check, and it needs it now. The health of the nation depends on it.
