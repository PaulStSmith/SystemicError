President Donald Trump was named in an explosive tip to the FBI regarding the 2019 death of Jeffrey Epstein, the Justice Department’s recent release of around 3.5 million files has revealed.

“Pretty major allegation,” wrote journalist and former MSNBC host Mehdi Hasan Friday in a social media post on X, responding to the discovery of the FBI tip. “Weird that mainstream media journalists haven’t asked Trump about it, right?”

The tip was submitted in February of 2023 by Epstein’s own brother, Mark Epstein, and directly names Trump as potentially being involved in the death of his brother, which was ultimately ruled a suicide.

“Jeffrey Epstein was murdered in his jail cell,” Mark Epstein wrote in the tip. “I have reason to believe he was killed because he was about to name names. I believe [President] Trump authorized [his] murder.”

Epstein, the disgraced financier and convicted child sex offender who was awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, was found dead in a Manhattan city jail in August of 2019, his death immediately sparking questions alongside a federal investigation into the circumstances surrounding his death.

Jeffrey Epstein’s death was controversially ruled a suicide, but Mark Epstein started alleging that his brother was murdered in 2025. It wasn’t until the Justice Department’s release of around 3.5 million Epstein files last week, however, that Mark Epstein’s tip to the FBI was revealed.

In the tip, Mark Epstein encourages the FBI to contact him for further details. It was submitted to the FBI National Threat Operations Center, and passed along to a “special agent” whose name was redacted.

Trump had a long and well-documented friendship with Jeffrey Epstein in the 1990s to early 2000s. In 2002, Trump called Jeffrey Epstein a “terrific guy” who “likes beautiful women,” many of whom “are on the younger side.” In 2017, Jeffrey Epstein said he was Trump’s “closest friend for ten years.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein-Trump Allegation: A Deep Dive

The latest bombshell to hit the headlines from the Justice Department’s release of an astonishing 3.5 million files related to Jeffrey Epstein’s controversial death is nothing short of seismic. Among these files, a tip submitted by Mark Epstein, Jeffrey’s brother, directly implicates former President Donald Trump in the 2019 death of the disgraced financier, which was officially ruled a suicide. This revelation begs for a closer examination, not just of the allegation itself but of the broader implications it holds for media integrity and the tangled web of power and privilege in America.

The Allegation

Mark Epstein’s claim is explosive: he alleges that Trump authorized his brother Jeffrey’s murder to prevent him from naming influential figures involved in his crimes. This isn’t a minor accusation; it’s a direct charge of orchestrating a murder against a former U.S. President, made all the more shocking by its source—Epstein’s own brother.

The Context

Jeffrey Epstein’s death in a Manhattan jail cell has been a subject of controversy and conspiracy theories since the moment it was reported. Officially ruled a suicide, the circumstances of his death—occurring while he awaited trial on sex-trafficking charges—immediately raised questions about the potential involvement of powerful individuals who stood to be exposed by Epstein’s testimony.

Adding to the intrigue is Trump’s well-documented association with Epstein, highlighted by their mutual admiration in the early 2000s. Trump’s 2002 characterization of Epstein as a “terrific guy” who “likes beautiful women...on the younger side” is particularly damning in hindsight, painting a picture of two men deeply entwined in a world of privilege and predation.

Media Silence: A Deafening Void

Mehdi Hasan’s observation about the mainstream media’s silence on this matter is as perplexing as it is concerning. The lack of coverage or inquiry into Trump regarding this tip is not just an oversight; it’s a glaring omission that speaks volumes about the state of journalistic integrity and the potentially selective nature of investigative vigor. The question isn’t just why Trump hasn’t been asked about this—but why the media isn’t clamoring for answers on an allegation of this magnitude.

The Bigger Picture

This situation transcends the specifics of the allegation itself, touching on the broader issues of accountability and transparency among the elite. The Epstein saga has continually peeled back layers on the extent to which wealth and power can shield individuals from the consequences of their actions. The revelation of Mark Epstein’s tip to the FBI only adds another layer of complexity to this narrative, suggesting that the web of silence and protection may extend even further than previously thought.

The Importance of Skepticism

While the allegation is shocking, it’s crucial to approach it with a critical eye. The claim needs thorough investigation, not just because of who it involves, but because of what it represents: a potential miscarriage of justice at the highest levels. Blind acceptance is as dangerous as outright dismissal; the truth, whatever it may be, demands diligence, not sensationalism.

Conclusion

The revelation of Mark Epstein’s tip to the FBI is a bombshell that should provoke outrage, inquiry, and reflection. It’s a stark reminder of the power dynamics that govern our society and the often insidious relationship between wealth, power, and justice. The media’s role in questioning, investigating, and holding individuals accountable is more critical than ever. As this story unfolds, one can only hope it leads to a reckoning with the truths we’ve been too complacent or fearful to confront.
