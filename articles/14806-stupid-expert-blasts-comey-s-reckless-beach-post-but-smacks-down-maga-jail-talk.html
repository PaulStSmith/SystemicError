Pushing back on MAGA allies calling for Comey to be locked up over the photo, Honig, a former federal prosecutor, dismissed the outrage as simply political speech, not a prosecutable threat.

“It’s ridiculous,” Honig said Thursday as he slapped down Trump administration officials calling for Comey to be federally investigated over his Instagram post, which included a photo of shells arranged to spell “86 47.” While Honig made clear the photo was “not criminal,” he still delivered critical words for the former FBI director.

“I am no fan of Jim Comey's,” Honig said bluntly. “I think posting this was extremely reckless, to use a phrase that he once used, and stupid.”

Comey deleted the post shortly after it went viral and sparked outrage throughout MAGA world, saying he hadn’t realized the number “86” could be interpreted as a violent message, CNN reported.

“He has acknowledged that. He took it down. He said, ‘I should not have posted this,’” Honig told host Kaitlan Collins.

He added: “This is not a criminally chargeable threat against the president. It's political speech. It’s way too broad. It's stupid, it's reckless. It's not criminal. That's just hyperbole that you're hearing from the cabinet members.”

Our Analysis:

Comey's Controversial Post: A Deep Dive into the Repercussions and Hypocrisy

Former FBI Director James Comey's social media escapade, involving a now-deleted post that sparked a firestorm of controversy, underscores the chaotic circus that American political discourse has devolved into. CNN legal analyst Elie Honig was quick to jump into the fray, branding Comey's actions as “extremely reckless,” yet he was adamant that the post fell squarely within the realm of political speech, far removed from any criminal implications. This episode reveals not just the impulsiveness of Comey but also the predictable overreaction of MAGA allies, showcasing a broader issue of how political factions weaponize the legal system against each other.

The Post in Question: Comey's Instagram post, which featured shells arranged to spell “86 47,” was interpreted by many as a veiled threat against President Donald Trump, given the colloquial use of "86" to mean getting rid of something or someone. The fact that Comey, a former FBI Director with thorough knowledge of the weight words carry, especially in today’s politically charged environment, chose to post such a message is baffling. It's a stark reminder of the lack of foresight and responsibility that plagues our political discourse. Honig's characterization of the post as "stupid" and "reckless" hits the nail on the head, but perhaps doesn't go far enough in condemning the irresponsibility at play.

The Reaction: The outcry from Trump’s camp, calling for Comey to be “locked up,” is a glaring testament to the hyperbolic and vindictive nature of political retaliation today. It's a disturbing echo of the "lock her up" chants directed at Hillary Clinton during the 2016 presidential campaign, illustrating a dangerous trend towards criminalizing political adversaries. Honig's dismissal of these calls as mere political speech is correct, yet it highlights a deeper issue: the weaponization of legal threats for political gains. This tactic undermines the legal system and contributes to the erosion of democratic norms. The irony is palpable – those who once decried the FBI's investigation into Trump's campaign ties to Russia as a "witch hunt" are now all too eager to see their political foes behind bars over a social media post.

The Hypocrisy: What’s particularly striking is the hypocrisy on display. Comey, once revered by some for his stoic representation of law and order, has descended into the mire of petty political bickering, reducing his contributions to public discourse to that of a provocateur rather than a figure of moral authority. Similarly, the response from MAGA allies, quick to call for federal investigations over a post while simultaneously decrying the politicization of justice when it suits them, is emblematic of the selective outrage that plagues American politics.

In conclusion, Comey’s Instagram saga is a microcosm of the larger dysfunction within American political and social discourse. Elie Honig’s analysis, while on point regarding the legal non-issue here, perhaps misses the opportunity to critique the broader societal implications. The incident is a stark reminder of the responsibility that comes with public platforms and the need for a return to a more thoughtful, less reactionary public discourse. As we navigate these tumultuous waters, it's imperative that we demand better from our public figures and ourselves, lest we continue to be embroiled in endless cycles of outrage and retribution that serve only to divide us further.
