'Do better!' Pam Bondi gets earful from furious Dem for 'covering up' right-wing terrorism

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi, then as U.S. President-elect Donald Trump’s nominee, testifies at a Senate Judiciary Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 15, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz/File Photo/File Photo

Attorney General Pam Bondi faced harsh grilling on Capitol Hill Wednesday from Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove (D-CA), who demanded to know, among other things, why her Justice Department pulled down a report on the dangers of violent right-wing extremists — and the congresswoman grew visibly frustrated as Bondi sidestepped the question.

“I want to go back to what [President Donald Trump] said about [Minnesota Gov.] Tim Walz,” said Kamlager-Dove, referring to the president’s promotion of conspiracy theories that Walz had a hand in the assassination of former Minnesota House Speaker Melissa Hortman and her husband. “Do you agree that President Trump undermined public trust in law enforcement by suggesting that Governor Walz was involved in the murders?”

“I am not familiar with that statement,” said Bondi.

“Well, that is a disappointing response,” said Kamlager-Dove. “And it is also why your office and Donald Trump have zero credibility, because I know and you know, it was a right-wing extremist who murdered the Hortmans. And you and the president are being dishonest with the American people. Last fall, after the horrific shooting of Charlie Kirk, President Trump was asked if there were extremists on both the left and the right, and his exact words were, ‘I’ll tell you something that’s going to get me in trouble, but I couldn’t care less. The radicals on the right oftentimes are radical because they don’t want to see crime. The radicals on the left are the problem, and they’re vicious and they’re horrible and they’re politically savvy.’ Do you agree with this statement?”

Bondi took a deep breath, but said nothing for several seconds.

“You’re taking too long, Madam Attorney General,” said Kamlager-Dove. “But you know the answer, because your own department has addressed the question. In 2024, the DOJ research arm issued a report on domestic terrorism, stating that the number of far-right attacks continues to outpace all other types of terrorism and domestic violence extremists ... and after the president made those comments, the DOJ took the report down. And I sent you a letter asking you a simple question: Why did the Justice Department remove the data that shows right-wing extremists have killed more innocent people than left-wing extremists?”

“Did you refer to immigration enforcement as domestic terrorism?” said Bondi.

“You should answer my question,” said Kamlager-Dove. “You’re on my time, but you won’t. And what I am asking you to do is put the report back, restore the data, stop scrubbing important data from your website. Stop taking down reports that you know the American people need to know about.”

“There are violent, dangerous people out here with real threats, and the dishonesty from your agency comes from the covering up of these threats that could hurt American people and put our lives at risk,” said Kamlager-Dove. “Do better.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Evasion: Attorney General Pam Bondi’s Capitol Hill Charade

In what can only be described as a political theater of the absurd, Attorney General Pam Bondi found herself in the hot seat on Capitol Hill, subjected to a grilling by Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove (D-CA) that laid bare the administration’s uncomfortable relationship with the truth regarding violent right-wing extremism. The congresswoman demanded answers on why the Justice Department pulled a critical report on the dangers posed by these extremists, only to be met with evasion and deflection—a tactic that has become all too familiar in today’s political landscape.

Bondi’s performance was a case study in the art of sidestepping. When confronted with President Trump’s baseless allegation against Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz, involving him in a murder conspiracy, Bondi’s response was to feign ignorance. This tactic, though predictable, is deeply troubling. It highlights a willingness to overlook the president’s penchant for conspiracy theories, even when they undermine public trust in law enforcement and the safety of political figures.

Kamlager-Dove’s frustration was palpable and justifiably so. Her pointed questioning aimed to uncover the rationale behind the Justice Department’s decision to remove a report that detailed the stark reality of far-right violence—a reality that the current administration seems determined to obscure. The report, issued in 2024, unequivocally stated that far-right attacks continue to outpace all other forms of terrorism and domestic violence extremism. Yet, in the wake of Trump’s remarks downplaying the threat posed by the right while vilifying left-wing activists, the DOJ saw fit to retract this crucial piece of research.

The exchange between Bondi and Kamlager-Dove was more than just an argument over a report; it was a microcosm of the administration’s broader strategy of misinformation and denial. By questioning Bondi’s decision to label immigration enforcement as domestic terrorism, we see a classic diversion tactic, aimed at shifting the conversation away from the uncomfortable truths about right-wing extremism.

Kamlager-Dove’s demands were simple: restore the data, stop scrubbing the website of important information, and acknowledge the real threats that Americans face from violent, right-wing extremists. Yet, these demands were met with silence and evasion.

This incident is emblematic of a larger, more insidious problem: the systematic undermining of public trust and the deliberate manipulation of facts to serve a political agenda. The administration’s refusal to confront the reality of right-wing extremism not only endangers lives but also erodes the very foundations of our democracy.

In her closing remarks, Kamlager-Dove implored the Attorney General to “do better.” Yet, as we’ve seen time and again, this administration seems fundamentally incapable of doing so. Their allegiance lies not with the truth, nor with the American people, but with their own distorted narrative—a narrative that threatens to unravel the fabric of our society.

The Capitol Hill confrontation may have ended, but the battle for truth and accountability rages on. As citizens, we must continue to demand transparency and integrity from our leaders. Anything less is a disservice to the principles upon which our nation was built.
