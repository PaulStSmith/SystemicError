Expert warns Trump’s ‘mendacious goons’ are killing trust in America

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

The Lie Is the Instrument

Not a Gaffe, a Governing Method

David Rothkopf is right about the scale of the lying, and his examples are damning: false claims about economic data, casualty counts, policy, climate, DOGE, health, and the machinery around Trump. That is the surface. The deeper fact is that this is not an administration that lies around policy. It uses lying as policy.

Power Sits at the Top

The people with actual institutional power are not anonymous rumor merchants. They are Trump, his press shop, his communications staff, his Cabinet, and the Republican leaders repeating the script. When the president lies, the apparatus does not correct him. It amplifies him. When the White House lies, the rest of the party treats it as discipline. That is not confusion. That is coordination.

They Are Breaking Shared Reality

Rothkopf gets closest to the real injury when he points to government data itself. If economic figures, military casualty counts, climate information, and public health guidance cannot be trusted, then the state is no longer a source of fact. It becomes a machine for selective reality. That is the point. A government that controls the levers of information can make harm harder to name and slower to resist.

The GOP Is Laundering the Damage

The article names the Speaker, the caucus, Scott Bessent, Marco Rubio, and the broader party chorus promising prosperity and peace on command. That matters because the lie does not stay inside Trump’s mouth. It gets laundered through institutions that should be checking him. Republicans are not passive victims of presidential volatility. They are active enablers who turn deliberate falsehood into a partisan product.

The Article Stops Short of the System

Rothkopf is strongest when he identifies corruption, incompetence, and malevolent intent. He is weaker when he frames the answer as mostly individual skepticism and personal truth-seeking. That is necessary, but insufficient. The problem is not that citizens forgot to be careful readers. The problem is that a political movement captured institutions and then used those institutions to manufacture distrust, conceal agenda, and degrade accountability.

The Pattern

This is the authoritarian pattern in plain clothes: attack the press, poison the data, flood the zone with contradiction, and then ask people to treat the resulting fog as normal politics. The lie is not a bug in the system. It is how power protects itself while doing damage.
