'No invasion': Court deals major blow to Trump's deportation plans in new ruling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump arrives at Philadelphia International Airport in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S., March 22, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump faced a major legal setback late Tuesday night after a federal appeals court rejected the White House’s use of an 18th-century law to speed up deportations of Venezuelan nationals.

In its efforts to swiftly deport Venezuelan migrants suspected of being members of international crime syndicates, the Trump administration has invoked the 1798 Alien Enemies Act, which allows for the swift deportation of citizens of a hostile nation the United States is at war with. Trump has routinely justified mass deportations that deny migrants due process under the guise of the United States being subjected to an “invasion.”

In a 2-1 decision late Tuesday night, however, the 5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals rejected the Trump administration’s argument, and issued an injunction prohibiting the administration from using the law as the basis for deportations.

“Our analysis leads us to grant a preliminary injunction to prevent removal because we find no invasion or predatory incursion, conclude on the current record that the updated notice satisfies due process, and remand for further proceedings,” the ruling read.

The lone judge to dissent from the majority opinion was Circuit Court Judge Andrew Oldham, who was nominated and appointed by Trump in 2018. The two judges rebuking the Trump administration’s use of the 1798 law were former President George W. Bush-appointee Judge Leslie Southwick and former President Joe Biden-appointee Irma Carrillo Ramirez.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Legal Quagmire: The Misuse of an 18th-Century Law

In a bold yet unsurprising maneuver, President Donald Trump's administration attempted to fast-track the deportation of Venezuelan nationals through the antiquated lens of the 1798 Alien Enemies Act. This law, a relic of a bygone era, was designed for situations where the United States was at war with a foreign nation. Trump, in his characteristic fashion, stretched the interpretation of this law to the breaking point, aiming to expedite the removal of migrants under the alarmist pretext of an "invasion".

A Dubious Justification for Mass Deportations

Trump's justification for these mass deportations hinges on a gross misrepresentation of the situation at the U.S. border. Labeling the influx of Venezuelan migrants as an "invasion" to invoke the Alien Enemies Act is not only a legal stretch but a moral travesty. It's a transparent attempt to sidestep due process and human rights under the guise of national security. The administration's actions are a blatant disregard for the principles of justice and fairness that are supposed to underpin the American legal system.

The Court Pushes Back

In a late-night ruling, the 5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals delivered a decisive blow to the Trump administration's strategy. By a 2-1 decision, the court rejected the use of the 1798 law as a foundation for the deportations, with the majority opinion striking down the administration's arguments as unfounded. The court stated, "Our analysis leads us to grant a preliminary injunction to prevent removal because we find no invasion or predatory incursion", effectively putting a halt to Trump's expedited deportation plans.

The dissenting voice in this decision was Judge Andrew Oldham, a Trump appointee, which might not come as a surprise. However, the rebuke from Judges Leslie Southwick, a George W. Bush appointee, and Irma Carrillo Ramirez, a Joe Biden appointee, underscores a broader judicial resistance to the Trump administration's often questionable legal tactics.

The Broader Implications

This ruling is a significant setback for Trump, but more importantly, it's a win for the rule of law and due process in the United States. It sends a clear message that even in the face of politically motivated legal gymnastics, the judiciary can serve as a bulwark against the misuse of power. The Trump administration's attempt to weaponize an 18th-century law against modern-day migrants was a disturbing effort to circumvent the protections afforded to individuals under U.S. law, and thankfully, it was recognized as such by the courts.

Looking Ahead

As this case proceeds, it will be crucial to monitor how the Trump administration responds and whether it seeks other avenues to impose its harsh immigration policies. This ruling may set a precedent for future legal challenges against the administration's actions on immigration and beyond. For now, at least, the courts have shown they will not stand idly by as the administration attempts to bend the law to fit its exclusionary agenda.

In a time when the fabric of American democracy seems increasingly under strain, decisions like this serve as a reminder of the resilience of its institutions. The rejection of the Trump administration's use of the 1798 Alien Enemies Act is a testament to the enduring power of checks and balances in the United States. It's a sharp rebuke of an administration that too often appears to believe it operates above the law.
