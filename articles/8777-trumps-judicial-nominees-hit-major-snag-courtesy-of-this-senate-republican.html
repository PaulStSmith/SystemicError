North Carolina Sen. Tillis blocking Trump’s Mississippi nominees

by Taylor Vance and Christa Dutton, NOTUS, Mississippi Today
November 12, 2025

A North Carolina senator is holding up Mississippi’s nominations for federal judgeships and U.S. attorneys because he wants Sen. Roger Wicker to help an indigenous group in his state gain federal recognition as a tribe.

Republican Sen. Thom Tillis told a reporter from NOTUS that his block on four Mississippi nominees is due to negotiations with Wicker, Mississippi’s senior senator, over federal recognition of the Lumbee and other issues unrelated to the nominees themselves. Wicker serves as chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, which has enormous sway over the legislation in which the Lumbee tribe would be recognized.

“Roger’s one of my favorite people here, and, you know, it’s just a matter of using the leverage people use every day here,” Tillis said.

Wicker’s office did not respond to a request for comment.

The Lumbee is a group of indigenous people in North Carolina that has been seeking federal recognition as a tribe for over a century. But other federally recognized tribes have opposed the effort.

Tillis has been a vocal supporter of federal recognition for the Lumbee. He is not running for reelection in 2026, so next year will be his final opportunity to secure a bipartisan bill for the indigenous people.

President Donald Trump has also supported federal recognition for the Lumbee.

Most of North Carolina’s congressional delegation supports federal recognition of the Lumbee. Language granting them federal recognition was added to the House version of the Pentagon’s annual spending blueprint.

But language about the Lumbee was not included in the Senate’s version of the blueprint.

Tillis also said he is negotiating with Wicker over other issues, but he declined to say what the issues were.

In August, Trump nominated Robert Chamberlin and James Maxwell, both justices on the Mississippi Supreme Court, to vacant federal judgeships in northern Mississippi. Trump in July nominated Scott Leary and Baxter Kruger, his choices for U.S. attorney for the Northern and Southern districts of Mississippi, respectively.

None of the four nominees responded to requests for comment. Both Wicker and Mississippi’s other U.S. senator, Cindy Hyde-Smith, support the nominees, though neither is a member of the Judiciary Committee.

Our Analysis:

The Political Puppetry of Senator Thom Tillis: A Masterclass in Leverage and Obstruction

In an audacious move that encapsulates the Machiavellian machinations of Capitol Hill, North Carolina Sen. Thom Tillis has taken a controversial stance by holding Mississippi’s federal nominations hostage. His demands? For Sen. Roger Wicker, Mississippi’s senior senator, to assist in the recognition of an indigenous group, the Lumbee, as a federally recognized tribe. This maneuver, as reported by Taylor Vance and Christa Dutton of NOTUS, Mississippi Today, isn’t just a small-scale political skirmish; it’s a glaring example of how individual agendas can gridlock the gears of governance.

Tillis’s Tactics: Leverage or Blackmail?

At first glance, Tillis’s actions may seem like a noble fight for indigenous rights. But scratch the surface, and you find a more complex narrative of political leverage and personal legacy. Tillis, a Republican, openly admits to using the block on four Mississippi nominees as a negotiation tool with Wicker. “It’s just a matter of using the leverage people use every day here,” Tillis stated, shedding light on the transactional nature of political dealings. But let’s call it what it is: a quid pro quo that places personal and political ambitions over the effective functioning of the judiciary and law enforcement in Mississippi.

The Lumbee Recognition: A Centuries-Old Struggle

The Lumbee Tribe’s quest for federal recognition is a legitimate and heart-wrenching saga of a community fighting for its rights, identity, and benefits that come with federal recognition. While Tillis’s advocacy for the Lumbee Tribe could be commended, leveraging unrelated judicial nominations to push through this agenda raises ethical questions. It’s a stark reminder that in the political arena, the ends often justify the means, no matter the collateral damage.

Wicker’s Silence and Trump’s Involvement

The silence from Sen. Roger Wicker’s office in response to this controversy is deafening. As chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, Wicker has significant influence, yet his lack of public engagement on this issue speaks volumes about the opaque nature of political negotiations. Additionally, the involvement of President Donald Trump in supporting the Lumbee’s federal recognition adds another layer of complexity and intrigue, hinting at the convoluted alliances and agendas that permeate our political landscape.

The Nominees: Caught in the Crossfire

The four nominees, Robert Chamberlin, James Maxwell, Scott Leary, and Baxter Kruger, find themselves unwitting pawns in a power play that overshadows their qualifications and the needs of the Mississippi judicial system. This situation underscores a troubling trend where individual careers and the efficient administration of justice are secondary to political maneuvering.

Conclusion: The Need for Transparency and Integrity

This episode is a glaring illustration of the systemic dysfunction within our political system, where political leverage trumps governance and public service. Tillis’s actions, while not uncommon, should not be normalized. They represent a broader issue of political obstructionism that prioritizes personal agendas over the collective good.

As observers and constituents, we must demand greater transparency and integrity from our elected officials. The process of federal nominations and the recognition of indigenous tribes are too important to be left to the whims of political strategizing. It’s high time for a systemic overhaul that puts the needs of the people first, untangling the complex web of political leverage that hinders progress and equity in our society.
