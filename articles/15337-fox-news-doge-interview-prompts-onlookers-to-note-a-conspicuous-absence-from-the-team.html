Fox News DOGE interview prompts onlookers to note a conspicuous absence from the team

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20255 min read

DOGE employees including Edward Coristine (pictured left), (Fox News screenshot via YouTube)

The Fox News Jesse Watters Primetime special featuring Elon Musk's controversial DOGE team touched off a social media firestorm about who was, and who wasn't included in the roundtable discussion.

"Elon Musk was nice enough to invite us into the Eisenhower Building last night to sit in on his weekly 10 p.m. DOGE meeting," Watters said by way of introduction. "We met the whole crew, even 'Big Balls.'"

The 19-year old who gave himself the unfortunate nickname is Edward Coristine, a first-year student at Northeastern University, with a dubious work history that included getting fired from an internship for allegedly "leaking proprietary company information."

Social media users took aim at Coristine, but also wanted to know why the interview didn't really feature the "whole crew," since Amy Gleason, DOGE's "acting administrator" per the White House, was conspicuously absent.

Tech journalist Mike Masnick posted, "Fox News' Jesse Watters met with 'the DOGE boys' for their 'nightly meeting,' including... Big Balls. The meeting was led by Elon Musk, who the DOJ has insisted in court does not lead DOGE. I did not see Amy Gleason at the meeting. cc: @annabower.bsky.social"

Bower is the senior editor of Lawfare, who wrote an exposé on Gleason being named "purported administrator" for legal purposes after the Trump administration claimed "that neither DOGE nor its de facto leader, Elon Musk, have real decision-making authority."

BlueSky poster covie93.bsky.social, with more than 53,000 followers, also asked, "Why was Amy Gleason, the person the WH said is in charge of DOGE, not in that fox 'news' interview with the DOGE team???"

The Sunday Mirror's Mikey Smith made fun of Coristine's cleaned-up appearance, writing, "Don’t know what you expected the DOGE kid nicknamed 'Big Balls' to look like, but I wouldn’t have guessed 'family wedding Jedward.'"

Political commentator Family-guy @eswat.bsky.social, with more than 10,000 followers, posted, "I watched an interview, last night, of Elon Mush and all the DOGE area leaders by Fox News. I was sickened by the lies and ridiculous accusations. '40% of the phone calls that come in EVERY DAY are fraudsters trying to steal from the system.'”

Journalist Kim Zetter remarked how "Musk makes sure to praise Trump and his cabinet, with whom he has tangled over DOGE activity."

Over on Elon Musk's X, the commentary was far more complimentary.

MAGA Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) called the DOGE crew "patriots" and "heroes," while Michael Reagan, adopted son of Ronald Reagan and president of the Reagan Legacy Foundation wrote, "I challenge @Abc @NBC @CBS and @Cnn to put Elon Musk and his Doge team on for a sit down interview as did FOX. Do they have a pair or not??"

Analyzing the Fox News Special with Elon Musk and the Controversial DOGE Team

The recent Fox News Jesse Watters Primetime special featuring Elon Musk and his so-called DOGE team has been nothing short of a media circus, sparking a social media firestorm. The inclusion and exclusion of certain figures in this roundtable discussion have raised more questions than answers, highlighting a bizarre mix of politics, technology, and media manipulation.

First off, let's address the elephant in the room: Edward Coristine, aka 'Big Balls.' A 19-year-old with a dubious work history, including getting fired for allegedly "leaking proprietary company information," somehow becomes a centerpiece of a primetime news special. The choice of spotlighting Coristine not only cheapens the discourse but also distracts from the more pressing issues at hand. The fixation on such a character is a red herring, diverting attention from the true nature of the DOGE project and its implications.

More troubling, however, is the absence of Amy Gleason, the "acting administrator" of DOGE, per the White House. Her conspicuous absence raises significant questions about the transparency and authenticity of the discussion. Tech journalist Mike Masnick and others have pointed out this glaring omission, emphasizing the dissonance between the public portrayal of DOGE's leadership and the legal realities behind the scenes. The Trump administration's claim that "neither DOGE nor its de facto leader, Elon Musk, have real decision-making authority" is a farce, contradicted by Musk's very public and prominent role in the project.

The social media reaction further underscores the polarization and confusion surrounding the DOGE team and its activities. While some, like MAGA Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) and Michael Reagan, laud Musk and his team as "patriots" and "heroes," others are rightly skeptical of the narrative being pushed by Fox News and Musk himself. The claim made during the interview, that "40% of the phone calls that come in EVERY DAY are fraudsters trying to steal from the system," is a baseless and dangerous accusation, designed to stoke fear and detract from the systemic issues at play.

Furthermore, Musk's effort to praise Trump and his cabinet amidst the DOGE activities is a calculated move to align with political power, further muddying the waters of technological innovation and governance. It's a tactic that seeks to shield the project from criticism by wrapping it in the flag and calling it patriotism.

The challenge issued by Michael Reagan to other major news networks to feature Musk and his DOGE team is a disingenuous dare that plays into the spectacle rather than encouraging a serious examination of the facts. It's a classic misdirection, a call for more noise instead of a substantive conversation.

In conclusion, the Fox News special with Elon Musk and the DOGE team is a microcosm of the larger issues plaguing our media and political landscape: spectacle over substance, personalities over policies, and misinformation over meaningful dialogue. As observers and participants in this ongoing saga, it is crucial to cut through the noise and demand transparency, accountability, and integrity in our discussions about technology and governance. The DOGE project, Musk's involvement, and the media's portrayal thereof deserve a critical, factual, and nuanced analysis, not a primetime puff piece.
