The toxic Republican coincidence that happened the day Gloria Steinem died

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

The Rebuild of Female Dependency

This Is Not Culture War

The source article is about Gloria Steinem, the Equal Rights Amendment, and the resurgence of open misogyny on the right. That is the surface. The real story is power: who can still write rules that make women economically dependent, politically subordinate, and legally vulnerable. The article’s examples are not random eruptions of bad behavior. They are evidence of a movement that has moved from commentary into office, from grievance into administration.

The Institutions Doing the Work

The people with actual power here are not anonymous internet trolls. They are officeholders, judges, agency heads, and political operatives who can turn ideology into policy. The article names them plainly: Pete Hegseth at Defense, Robert F. Kennedy Jr. at HHS, Trump at the top of the ticket and still shaping the governing culture, Republican legislators in Missouri, the Texas attorney general, the Supreme Court’s Republican majority, and the Archivist of the United States. That is the machinery.

The point is not that a few men said ugly things. The point is that men with institutional authority are using the state to normalize female dependency, block reproductive autonomy, and treat women’s equality as an unfinished crime scene to be reopened.

Misdirection as Strategy

The right’s favorite trick is to turn domination into “debate.” Women are told the problem is tone, tradition, family values, or confusing cultural change. The article cuts through that fog. When a bank once required a man’s signature for a woman’s credit, that was not etiquette. It was power enforcing dependency. When Republicans now fight the Equal Rights Amendment by hiding behind procedural dates, that is the same impulse in legal drag.

The misdirection matters because it softens deliberate harm into administrative confusion. The people doing the damage know exactly what they are doing. They are not failing to understand women’s equality. They are trying to contain it.

The Coalition Behind the Backlash

This is not just one movement of loud men. It is a network. Pastor Joel Webbon, Doug Wilson’s orbit, Nick Fuentes, the TheoBros ecosystem, and Republican-linked officeholders all reinforce the same hierarchy: male rule, female submission, household authority translated into political authority. The article shows how this worldview travels from sermon to conference stage to Pentagon worship service.

That is the larger pattern: reactionary politics does not begin with law and end with culture. It moves both ways at once. It builds a religious, media, and institutional pipeline that makes subordination sound natural, then uses state power to make it durable.

Bodily Control Is the Core Issue

The abortion restrictions, Texas sepsis data, the Missouri registry proposal, and the prosecution of a Houston midwife are not separate stories. They are the operational side of the same project. Once women’s autonomy is framed as a problem to be managed, pregnancy becomes a site of surveillance, punishment, and coercion. Once that logic is normalized, deaths and injuries can be described as unfortunate side effects instead of policy outcomes.

That is why the article’s examples matter: the cruelty is not incidental. It is the mechanism.

The Systemic Pattern

The deepest political fact here is that women’s equality remains dependent on institutions that can still be captured, delayed, or reversed. The Equal Rights Amendment already has the necessary state ratifications, yet Republicans keep fighting it through procedural sabotage and captured institutions. That is the governing model: never defeat equality honestly, just bury it under paperwork, appointments, and judicial refusal.

The broader lesson is simple. Patriarchy does not survive only through attitudes. It survives through officeholders, agencies, courts, and party infrastructure that know exactly how to convert resentment into rulemaking. The backlash against women is not a side story in American politics. It is one of its organizing principles.
