Conservative admits surprise at GOP's willingness to 'humiliate themselves'

The Republican Convention Was an Obedience Ritual

A Pledge to Trump, Not a Political Program

At an unprecedented midterm convention, Donald Trump instructed Republican attendees to raise their hands and repeat a pledge that included trying to “cheat like hell,” while accusing Democrats of cheating without evidence. The gathering reportedly became a celebration of Trump despite expectations of severe Republican losses and widespread voter anger.

That is enough context. The central fact is not that Republicans embarrassed themselves. It is that the leader of the party used its convention to make public submission a condition of belonging.

Humiliation Is a Tool of Control

Charlie Sykes describes Republican officials’ conduct as self-humiliation and wonders why people in power would willingly degrade themselves. The surprise is misplaced.

Forced absurdity can be politically useful. When officials repeat a claim they cannot substantiate—or pledge themselves to conduct they would condemn in an opponent—they demonstrate that loyalty outranks truth, consistency, and reputation. Their humiliation is not incidental to the ritual. It is the proof that the hierarchy holds.

Trump had the microphone, the stage, and the authority to dictate the words. Party officials supplied the audience and complied. They were not confused about who controlled the event.

The Party Chose Submission

The Republican Party is not a helpless institution being mysteriously transformed from the outside. Its officials organized the convention. Its delegates participated. Its political apparatus elevated Trump’s grievance campaign when it could have presented candidates, policies, or an explanation for its anticipated electoral losses.

Calling this merely a “dark element” of the public shifts attention downward, toward ordinary voters, and away from the professionals who possess institutional authority. Voters did not schedule the convention or script its defining spectacle. Party leaders did.

Those leaders may fear Trump’s supporters, value his fundraising power, share his objectives, or calculate that dissent would end their careers. Whatever the mixture, their repeated decisions—not an atmospheric national darkness—keep his dominance operational.

The Cheating Claim Is Preemptive Propaganda

Trump’s unsupported accusation that Democrats are unprecedented cheaters performs two functions at once. It delegitimizes an unfavorable result before ballots are counted, and it converts threatened misconduct by his own side into supposed retaliation.

That is the structure of authoritarian propaganda: invent pervasive enemy corruption, then portray norm-breaking as defensive necessity. The allegation does not require evidence because its purpose is not to establish what happened. Its purpose is to authorize what comes next.

The convention amplified that message instead of rejecting it. Institutional complicity turned one politician’s accusation into a party ritual.

Surprise Conceals the Record

Sykes’s astonishment also exposes a recurring weakness among anti-Trump conservatives. They recognize the degradation but continue treating it as an inexplicable collapse of character rather than a rational response to incentives their party built and preserves.

Republican officials have learned that submission can bring protection, attention, and continued access to power. Resistance can bring a primary challenge, public abuse, and political exile. Under those conditions, humiliation is not evidence that self-interest disappeared. It is evidence that the institution now rewards abasement.

The Founders’ presumed faith in elite pride offers little protection when careers depend on demonstrating obedience.

The System Rewards the Kneeling

The larger pattern is institutional cowardice disguised as personal weakness. The spectacle looks ridiculous, but ridicule alone understates its political function. A party capable of making officials rehearse baseless claims in unison is training them to accept a leader’s account of reality over observable facts and electoral outcomes.

That discipline matters beyond one convention. It prepares the organization to excuse future abuses, discredit losses, and punish anyone who refuses the script. The danger lies not in politicians surrendering their dignity. It lies in what disciplined officials will surrender after dignity no longer costs them anything.
