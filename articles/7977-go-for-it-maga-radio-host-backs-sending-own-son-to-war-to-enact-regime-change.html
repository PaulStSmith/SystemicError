‘Go for it!’ MAGA radio host backs sending own son to war to enact regime change

Lourdes Ubieta, a conservative radio host from Miami, Florida and major backer of President Donald Trump, said recently that if it took sending U.S. troops – which may include her own son and cousin – to Venezuela to enact regime change, then the Trump administration should “go for it.”

“If to free Venezuela and for the best interest of the national security of the U.S., you need to do it, you go for it,” Ubieta told The Washington Post in its report published on Saturday, telling the outlet that she would support a full U.S. invasion of the South American nation as a last resort.

The Trump administration has aggressively escalated military threats against Venezuela in the past several months, starting with deadly strikes in September on suspected drug-carrying vessels that have killed at least 83 people.

Trump has also reportedly considered assassinating Venezuelan President Nicholas Maduro, has illegally closed the nation’s airspace, deployed an aircraft carrier strike group to its coast, and said recently that land operations would begin “very soon.”

For Ubieta, her pro-interventionist views were at odds with many in the MAGA base, a significant number of whom outright reject the idea of pursuing regime change in other nations. Republican strategist Steve Cortes argued that supporting U.S. military intervention in Venezuela wasn’t only not aligned with “America First” principles, it was directly anti-thetical to it.

“The most important pillar of America First is nonintervention. That’s the glue that holds the coalition together,” Cortes told the Post. “While Maduro creates instability for America, for all of the Americas, by no means is this some imminent threat that we have to take serious, tangible action to depose him.”

Other prominent Republicans who’ve long been strong supporters of Trump have shared Cortes’ sentiment, including Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who explicitly spoke out against enacting regime change in Venezuela this week. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) spoke out against regime change in Venezuela as well, arguing that the America First “movement will dissolve” should Trump pursue military action in the South American nation.

But for Ubieta, nothing was more “America First” than toppling the administration of Maduro, even if it meant sending her own son and cousin to war.

“When you think of America First, you need to think of national security first, and the Venezuelan regime is a threat to our national security,” Ubieta said, speaking with the Post.. “There’s a lot of ignorance regarding the Venezuela issue.”

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Zealotry of Lourdes Ubieta

In a display of alarming militaristic fervor, conservative radio host Lourdes Ubieta has recently endorsed the notion of a U.S. invasion of Venezuela, a stance that starkly contrasts the growing chorus of voices within her own political camp. Despite the grave implications of her advocacy – which extends to the potential deployment of her own family members – Ubieta’s position illuminates a disturbing trend of interventionist zeal that disregards the catastrophic consequences of such actions.

The Trump Administration’s Aggressive Posture

The Trump administration’s escalation of military threats against Venezuela, as reported, is not merely a speculative concern but a harrowing reality. From deadly strikes claiming at least 83 lives under the guise of targeting drug-carrying vessels to the consideration of assassinating President Nicholas Maduro, these actions represent a flagrant violation of international norms and human decency. The deployment of an aircraft carrier strike group and the closure of Venezuela’s airspace are not only acts of aggression but also signal a dangerous precedent in the conduct of foreign policy.

A Stark Contradiction within the MAGA Base

Ubieta’s pro-interventionist stance starkly contradicts the “America First” principles, a cornerstone of the MAGA base’s ideology that emphasizes nonintervention. Republican strategist Steve Cortes succinctly captures this contradiction by highlighting the foundational role of nonintervention in the MAGA coalition. The opposition from prominent Republicans, including Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene and Sen. Rand Paul, underscores a significant rift within the party, challenging the unity and ideological coherence of the “America First” movement.

The Misplaced Prioritization of National Security

Ubieta’s justification of regime change in Venezuela under the guise of national security is a perilous oversimplification of a complex geopolitical issue. Labeling the Venezuelan regime as a threat to U.S. national security without a nuanced understanding of the situation not only reveals “a lot of ignorance regarding the Venezuela issue” but also perpetuates a myopic worldview where military intervention is seen as a panacea for international disputes. The assertion that toppling Maduro’s administration epitomizes “America First” policies dangerously conflates national interest with aggressive, unilateral military action.

The Cost of Interventionist Policies

The human cost of Ubieta’s advocated policies – potentially involving the lives of her own son and cousin – underscores the personal stakes entangled in such hawkish rhetoric. Yet, the broader implications of such an intervention, potentially resulting in widespread devastation, destabilization, and loss of life, are far-reaching. The history of U.S. military interventions offers a somber testimony to the long-term consequences of such actions, often leaving countries in a state of turmoil, conflict, and suffering.

Conclusion

Lourdes Ubieta’s endorsement of a U.S. invasion of Venezuela as a means to enact regime change is not only at odds with the “America First” principle of nonintervention but also represents a dangerous flirtation with military adventurism. The casual acceptance of war and its consequences, as evidenced by Ubieta’s willingness to sacrifice her own family members, reflects a troubling zealotry that prioritizes misguided militant strategies over diplomatic solutions. As the MAGA base grapples with its ideological direction, it must confront the inherent contradictions and moral quandaries posed by such interventionist stances.
