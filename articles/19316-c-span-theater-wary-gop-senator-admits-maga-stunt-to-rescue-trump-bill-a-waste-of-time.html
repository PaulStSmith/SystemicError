'C-SPAN theater': Wary GOP senator admits MAGA stunt to rescue Trump bill a waste of time

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 15, 20265 min read

WASHINGTON — Senate Republicans aren’t keen on using a talking filibuster to jam through President Donald Trump’s SAVE America Act, which doesn’t have the votes to pass the chamber.

Trump and allies, including Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT), have pushed Republicans to force a marathon talking filibuster to try to break Democratic resistance and pass the legislation, which has faced fierce criticism, as it would block large numbers of eligible citizens from voting to address a virtually nonexistent problem. The bill would require proof of U.S. citizenship to register or re‑register to vote in federal elections and impose a nationwide voter ID requirement with strict documentation rules.

Trump‑aligned conservatives want to force Democrats into an old‑school, around‑the‑clock talking filibuster if they want to block the bill, rather than letting them quietly sustain a 60‑vote blockade.

But in candid hallway remarks this week, Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY) dismissed the prospect of weaponizing the talking filibuster as logistically unworkable. She told reporters she’d “talk till the cows come home” if her conference demanded it, but conceded it would “chew up the rest of the year” and produce few results.

“The practicality of doing it just is not apparent to me,” she said.

When asked about a Thursday vote in which the Senate again failed to secure enough votes to advance a Homeland Security funding bill, Lummis lamented, “Everything for the last month has been C-SPAN theater.”

While she heard that Trump sent an offer to Democrats to fund DHS, her party hasn’t seen it.

“They’re not showing it to Republicans,” she insisted.

Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-WV) said the Senate may drag out floor time next week.

Meanwhile, Democrats are pointing fingers back at Republicans over the shutdown of DHS, which includes TSA, FEMA, and the Coast Guard. Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-NM) said Republicans blocked unanimous consent to fund those agencies on two separate occasions last week and this week, calling GOP claims that Democrats are obstructing the process “baloney.”

“That’s on them,” he declared to Raw Story.

Lujan said Trump admitted “out loud” what the SAVE Act is really about.

“Republicans believe the SAVE Act wins them the midterms,” he said. “This is not about fraud or this one thing or another. They wanna cheat and steal these elections in November because they see what’s really happening.

Luján added that the bill would be “devastating” to Native American communities and tribal elders.

“It would throw them off the rolls. They take away their rights. It’s just ridiculous,” he said.

Analysis of the SAVE America Act Debate and Senate Dynamics

Institutional Power and Decision Makers

The article outlines a political standoff in the U.S. Senate over President Donald Trump’s SAVE America Act. Institutional power in this context lies with the Senate Republicans and Democrats, with Republicans attempting to navigate a legislative path for a bill that lacks sufficient support to overcome a filibuster. The decision to pursue a talking filibuster, a tactic to force a bill through despite opposition, is attributed to Trump and his allies, notably Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT). However, resistance within the Republican ranks, exemplified by Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY), highlights a division on strategy rather than objective.

Sen. Lummis’s dismissal of the talking filibuster due to logistical impracticality underscores a recognition of the limits of their power. Meanwhile, Democrats, represented in the article by Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-NM), position themselves against the bill, citing its potential effects on voter suppression, particularly among Native American communities.

The Framing of Responsibility

The article juxtaposes Republican efforts to push through the SAVE America Act with Democratic opposition, framing the debate around the mechanism of the filibuster and accusations of obstruction. This framing risks misdirecting responsibility towards Democrats for blocking the legislation, without sufficiently highlighting the controversial nature of the bill itself—namely, its implications for voter suppression.

The responsibility for the Homeland Security funding deadlock further complicates the narrative, with Sen. Luján accusing Republicans of obstructing funding processes, a claim that the article suggests has merit given the context of the broader legislative stalemate.

Misdirection and Accountability

The article suggests that the SAVE America Act is a strategic play by Republicans, led by Trump, to influence midterm election outcomes under the guise of addressing voter fraud—a problem that is described as “virtually nonexistent.” This narrative is critical since it identifies a potential misdirection of legislative focus and an attempt to manipulate electoral conditions rather than address genuine security or integrity issues within the electoral system.

Sen. Luján’s comments bring to light the targeted impact of the SAVE America Act on specific communities, emphasizing the bill’s potential to disenfranchise Native American voters. This aspect of the debate highlights a significant ethical and political concern that transcends procedural wrangling over filibusters and legislative tactics.

Conclusion

In analyzing the situation, it is evident that Senate Republicans, particularly those aligned with Trump, hold the institutional power to influence the debate around the SAVE America Act. However, internal division and the strategic impracticality of a talking filibuster as outlined by Sen. Lummis indicate a lack of unified will to use that power to its fullest extent. Meanwhile, Democrats, through opposition and public criticism, are employing their institutional power to block a bill they view as ethically and democratically detrimental.

The article’s framing, which focuses on legislative tactics and inter-party accusations, partially misdirects responsibility away from the substantive issues at the heart of the SAVE America Act debate—voter suppression and the integrity of democratic processes. It is crucial to scrutinize not just the procedural aspects of this legislative battle but the underlying motivations and the potential consequences of the legislation in question.

In this context, criticism of the Republican strategy to push through the SAVE America Act is well-placed, given the lack of evidence for widespread voter fraud and the clear implications for voter suppression. Conversely, the framing of Democrats as obstructive overlooks the legitimate concerns about the bill’s impact on voting rights, particularly for marginalized communities. The narrative around the Homeland Security funding impasse further demonstrates the complexity of accountability, suggesting that both strategic decisions and ethical considerations are at play in this multifaceted political confrontation.
