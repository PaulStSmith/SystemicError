'Why are we not talking about his mental acuity?' MSNBC host nails growing Trump problem

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unraveling of Reason: Trump's Mental Acuity in Question

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

During an MSNBC panel on recent proclamations from Donald Trump that have left observers baffled, "The Weekend" co-host Jonathan Capehart expressed dismay that there is not more attention being paid to the president's state of mind.

With former Rep. Barbara Comstock (R-VA) commenting on Trump's nonsensical tariff claims, Capehart couldn't contain himself and exclaimed, "I wrote down this note immediately: why are we not talking about his mental acuity? How?"

Co-host and former GOP speechwriter Elise Jordan offered, "The last interview he did with [NBC's] Kristen Welker, he did not look as sharp and he looked, at least at a minimum, tired. At worst case, he is not as sharp as he used to be."

Comstock then jumped in with, "I mean he's talking about how it's great that those, right the [container] ships coming over are empty, that means we're saving money, it is literally economically illiterate."

"His own people –– look at Steve Moore, Heritage Foundation, a guy who opposes tariffs has been saying to people behind the scenes, actually, he's been saying it on Fox News, this is insane," she added. "

"You can tell –– Ken Griffin who gave money, huge money to Republicans last year, including Trump, has said this has made us 20 percent poorer in a month done permanent damage to the American brand and this isn't going to be something we can turn around from now. You know, and what he [Trump] does is he burns down the house and then he comes in and says, 'Oh, the market went up.' Well, no."

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Reason: Trump's Mental Acuity in Question

In an era where political discourse has often descended into the realms of the absurd and the bizarre, the recent observations from an MSNBC panel regarding Donald Trump's latest proclamations have ignited a much-needed conversation about the former president's mental acuity. Jonathan Capehart's exasperation is not just a mere reflection of personal dismay but a mirror to a broader concern that seems to be woefully under-discussed: Why is there not more attention being paid to Trump's state of mind?

It's not every day that you hear a panel on a major news network openly questioning the mental sharpness of a former president. Yet, here we are, with Capehart and his co-hosts, including former GOP speechwriter Elise Jordan, expressing concerns that Trump did not appear as sharp in his last interview. The choice of words here ranges from "tired" to questioning his mental sharpness outright. This is not mere political opposition speaking; this is an alarm bell being rung, loud and clear.

Barbara Comstock, a former Republican representative, added fuel to the fire by labeling Trump's comments on tariffs as "economically illiterate." This isn't just a critique of policy; it's a damning indictment of Trump's understanding of basic economic principles. When you have figures within your own party baffled by your statements, it's not just a red flag; it's a blazing inferno signaling a crisis of competence.

The commentary from GOP circles doesn’t stop there. Steve Moore of the Heritage Foundation, a conservative think tank, and Ken Griffin, a major Republican donor, have both expressed their incredulity and concern over Trump's actions and statements. Griffin's assertion that Trump's policies have made the U.S. "20 percent poorer in a month" and inflicted "permanent damage to the American brand" is a scathing critique from within the ranks. It's particularly telling when Moore, an opponent of tariffs, is publicly decrying Trump’s stance as "insane" on Fox News, a network known for its generally favorable coverage of Trump.

What we're witnessing is not just political disagreement but a fundamental questioning of Trump's cognitive capabilities. This isn't about ideology; it's about the basic ability to comprehend and navigate complex issues and the implications of one's decisions. The panel's discussion brings to light an uncomfortable truth that many have whispered but few have dared to voice with such clarity: the possibility that the former leader of the free world may not be mentally equipped to handle the weight of the office he once held.

This conversation transcends political boundaries. It's a matter of national and global concern. When those who have served with or supported Trump in the past are raising alarms, it's time to listen. The reluctance of the media and public figures to directly address the issue of Trump's mental fitness is not just an oversight; it's a dereliction of duty. As the panelists on MSNBC have shown, it’s possible and necessary to question the mental acuity of public figures, especially when their actions and statements have far-reaching consequences.

In closing, the discourse surrounding Trump's mental state is not just political fodder; it's a grave concern that requires serious attention. The whispers and concerns about his cognitive abilities need to be addressed openly, not just for the sake of American politics but for the sake of rational governance. The MSNBC panel has cracked open a door. It's high time we all walked through it and faced the issue head-on.
