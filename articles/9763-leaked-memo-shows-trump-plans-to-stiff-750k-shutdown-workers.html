Leaked memo shows Trump plans to stiff 750k shutdown workers

A leaked White House draft memo suggests that the Trump administration is actively considering stiffing as many as 750,000 federal workers and denying them compensation while furloughed amid the ongoing government shutdown, according to an Axios report published Tuesday.

As many as 750,000 federal workers are projected to be furloughed for every day of the shutdown – now in its seventh day – according to estimates from the Congressional Budget Office.

While a 2019 law guarantees furloughed federal employees retroactive pay, the leaked White House draft memo – described to Axios by three insiders – reveals that the Trump administration has a different interpretation of the law that may allow furloughed federal workers to be denied backpay.

“Does this law cover all these furloughed employees automatically?” said a senior White House official, speaking with Axios on the condition of anonymity. “The conventional wisdom is: yes, it does. Our view is: no, it doesn’t.”

The 2019 law is known as the Government Employee Fair Treatment Act (GEFTA), and was signed into law during the previous government shutdown in 2018-19, the longest shutdown in United States’ history.

What White House officials have zeroed in on, however, is a provision in the amended version of the law, which stipulated that retroactive pay for furloughed federal workers would be provided “subject to the enactment of appropriations Acts ending the lapse,” which officials are considering interpreting as meaning backpay would have to be appropriated by Congress.

“If it [GEFTA] was self-executing, why did Congress do that?” the White House official said, referring to Congress’ amendment to the law. “It’s precedent, [and any other interpretation is] ridiculous.”

Critics, however, say the White House’s new interpretation was off base, including Nekeisha Campbell, a labor attorney with a Washington, D.C.-based law firm.

“There is no legal authority to support that interpretation of the statute,” Campbell said, speaking with Axios.

“When the language of a statute is plain, courts must apply it except in the rare circumstance when there is a clearly expressed legislative intent to the contrary, or when a literal application would frustrate the statute’s purpose or lead to an absurd result. The law here is quite clear. The caveat is, if you follow the law.”

Still, White House officials were adamant that the law remained unclear, and another official — described by Axios as a “senior administration official” — was quick to blame Democrats for the issue even materializing.

“This would not have happened if Democrats voted for the clean [continuing resolution],” the senior administration official told Axios.

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misinterpretation: The Trump Administration’s Furlough Fiasco

In what can only be described as a bold maneuver in legal gymnastics, the Trump administration appears to be toying with the idea of financially stranding as many as 750,000 federal workers amid an already painful government shutdown. According to a leaked draft memo reported by Axios, the administration is considering a creative interpretation of the 2019 Government Employee Fair Treatment Act (GEFTA) that could deny furloughed workers their deserved compensation. This act, mind you, was specifically designed to protect federal employees from the fallout of governmental gridlock, a noble intent that seems to be lost in translation—or perhaps deliberately ignored.

The Dubious Distinction of Legal Interpretation

The leaked memo suggests that the administration sees a loophole in the language of GEFTA, particularly in a provision that ties retroactive pay to the “enactment of appropriations Acts ending the lapse.” This interpretation, as per the administration’s contorted logic, implies that Congress must specifically appropriate funds for backpay—despite the law’s clear intention to ensure furloughed workers are paid regardless of Congress’s ability to end a shutdown expediently.

This interpretation is not just innovative in its legal acrobatics; it’s downright destructive. It prioritizes political maneuvering over the livelihoods of hundreds of thousands of federal employees, treating their financial security as mere collateral in a broader game of legislative chicken.

A Convenient Scapegoat

In a move that surprises no one, the administration was quick to point fingers at Democrats, blaming them for the shutdown and the subsequent pay issue. This blame game is a tired tactic, one that sidesteps responsibility and ignores the administration’s role in both the shutdown and the current debacle over federal worker pay. It’s a classic case of political deflection—if you can’t dazzle them with brilliance, baffle them with bull.

Legal Experts Disagree

Legal experts, including labor attorney Nekeisha Campbell, have called out the administration’s interpretation as baseless. The law, according to Campbell and surely any rational reading, clearly intends for furloughed workers to receive backpay. The administration’s stance not only flies in the face of legal precedent but also common decency. It’s a stark reminder of how far some will go to twist the law to fit their needs, at the expense of those who serve our nation.

The Real Victims

Let’s not forget the real victims in this legal and political quagmire: the federal workers and their families, who face an uncertain financial future because of a shutdown they did not cause and a legal interpretation they did not ask for. These individuals, who serve our country in capacities ranging from national security to environmental protection, deserve better than to be used as pawns in a political game.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

This entire episode is emblematic of a larger, systemic problem within our current political climate: the willingness to compromise the well-being of American workers for political gain. The Trump administration’s attempt to reinterpret the GEFTA is not just a legal misstep; it’s a moral failure, showcasing a stunning disregard for the people who keep our country running.

As this situation unfolds, it’s crucial that we keep the spotlight on those affected most and hold those in power accountable for their actions—or, in this case, their misinterpretations. The furloughed workers deserve their due pay, and the administration owes them not just compensation, but an apology.
