MAGA senator slams illegal immigrants as 'criminals' — then steps in to free ICE detainee

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An officer with the U.S. Department of Homeland Security stands as people protest against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) outside the U.S. immigration court in, New York City, U.S., July 28, 2025. REUTERS/Shannon Stapleton

MAGA Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA), who has been a fervent supporter of Immigration and Customs Enforcement efforts to deport undocumented migrants, reversed course when it came to one of his constituents, according to The New York Times.

Kennedy told Fox News on July 17, “If you’re in our country illegally, you’re a criminal. Illegal immigration is illegal, duh.”

And yet, the senator's office recently asked the Department of Homeland Security to release 25-year-old Paola Clouatre after two months in a Louisiana detention center, the report said.

Clouatre is a Mexican citizen and mother of two young children who is married to a U.S. Marine Corps veteran.

"In an email on Tuesday morning, Christy Tate, the constituent services representative in Mr. Kennedy’s office, wrote to Mr. Clouatre to confirm that his wife had been released from ICE custody after her office had made a formal request to the federal agency," the report said.

The email read in part, “I am so happy for you and your family. We will continue to keep you, your family and others that are experiencing the same issues in our prayers.”

Reporter Pooja Salhotra wrote that Clouatre came to the United States as a teenager "to seek asylum with her mother and brother." After her mother failed to show up for a court hearing in California in 2018, a judge issued a deportation order against the teen.

Now estranged from her mother, Clouatre said she didn't learn of the deportation order until earlier this year "when she was already in the process of applying for a green card," the report said.

She was detained by ICE agents on May 27 shortly after giving birth, and while attending a "routine appointment in New Orleans related to her application for a green card and permanent resident status," according to the report.

During her incarceration, Clouatre's husband brought the couple's 9-week-old baby to the jail so she could breastfeed. She is currently wearing an ankle monitor and awaiting a court date for her immigration proceedings.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Hypocrisy of Senator John Kennedy's Immigration Stance

The recent actions of MAGA Senator John Kennedy (R-LA) provide a glaring exhibition of the hypocrisy that often pervades the political discourse around immigration in the United States. Kennedy, who has been a vocal advocate for the aggressive deportation of undocumented migrants, suddenly shifted his stance when the issue hit closer to home—a constituent's predicament tugged at his heartstrings, or perhaps more cynically, at his political interests.

"Illegal immigration is illegal, duh," Kennedy proclaimed on Fox News on July 17, painting with a broad brush the complex and nuanced issue of undocumented migration. Yet, in a stark departure from his public hardline rhetoric, Kennedy's office intervened on behalf of Paola Clouatre, a 25-year-old Mexican citizen and mother of two young United States citizens, who found herself ensnared by the very immigration enforcement policies Kennedy has championed. Clouatre, who came to the U.S. as a teenager seeking asylum, was detained by ICE shortly after giving birth, a situation that would seem to perfectly encapsulate the 'criminal' element Kennedy so fervently decries.

However, the senator's office contacted the Department of Homeland Security, requesting Clouatre's release from a Louisiana detention center, showcasing a remarkable double standard. It's telling that Kennedy's office not only facilitated her release but also followed up with an email stating, “I am so happy for you and your family. We will continue to keep you, your family, and others that are experiencing the same issues in our prayers.” This sudden bout of empathy is commendable yet glaringly inconsistent with Kennedy's public stance.

The narrative of Paola Clouatre, who faced deportation after her mother failed to appear at a court hearing, reveals the often Kafkaesque nature of the U.S. immigration system. The fact that she was detained while applying for a green card and shortly after giving birth adds layers of cruelty to her ordeal. Kennedy's selective intervention underscores a broader issue within the political landscape: the willingness to exploit immigration as a wedge issue for political gain, while making exceptions when politically expedient or personally convenient.

Kennedy's actions beg the question: Why should compassion and common decency be reserved for constituents with a direct line to a senator's office? This episode reveals not just the hypocrisy of one politician but the systemic failings of U.S. immigration policy, which all too often lacks humanity and fairness. It's a stark reminder that behind the political grandstanding and sound bites are real human beings—mothers, children, families—caught in the crosshairs of a deeply flawed system.

In the end, Senator Kennedy's case is a microcosm of a larger issue: politicians who are quick to demonize undocumented immigrants for political points, yet are equally quick to abandon their principles when it's convenient. This incident should not just fade into the next news cycle but serve as a catalyst for a deeper examination of the inconsistencies and inhumanities of current immigration policies and rhetoric. The question remains: will it? Or will the political establishment continue to play a cynical game with people's lives at stake?
