Trump loves the poorly educated —and America's IQ decline explains why

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

The Stupid Voter Story Is Doing Cleanup for Power

What the source actually says

The piece argues that Americans are getting less cognitively sharp, points to falling test scores and IQ measures, blames phones, social media, noise, and overstimulation, and then folds all of that into Trump’s political rise. It uses education data, a stalled bipartisan cellphone study bill, and district-level voting patterns to suggest that declining attention and comprehension are reshaping U.S. politics.

The blame is pointed in the wrong direction

The article keeps calling the electorate stupid, but the more revealing fact is who profits from that stupidity narrative. Trump is not an accidental beneficiary of confusion. He is the political operator who turned ignorance into a business model, and the source says so plainly when it describes him as a conman who manipulates ignorance. Fox News, wealthy donors, and Republican machinery are not passive observers of a cultural decline. They are active participants in packaging grievance, flattening reality, and selling voters a politics built on self-harm.

The problem is not that millions of people woke up one day and became idiots. The problem is that organized political actors found a durable use for anti-knowledge politics and kept feeding it.

Technology is not the whole explanation

Phones, social media, loud media environments, and sensory overload may contribute to shorter attention spans and weaker comprehension. The source cites studies linking early fast-paced TV exposure to attentional deficits and notes broader research on noise pollution and cognitive stress. Fine. But that is still only part of the story.

The more important question is who built the incentives that reward constant stimulation, emotional overload, and dumbed-down spectacle. That is not a natural force. It is a political economy. The article gestures at this and then retreats into a hand-wringing cultural diagnosis, as if the nation were merely drifting toward stupidity instead of being managed into distraction by institutions that monetize it.

Education is where the damage becomes political

The source is right that education matters. It notes that the most educated congressional districts vote heavily Democratic while the least educated districts lean Republican, and it points out that Democrats deliver concrete benefits like jobs, infrastructure, broadband, healthcare, and safety regulation. That matters.

But the article still underplays the political scandal: a democracy can only survive if learning is treated as public infrastructure, not a decorative ideal. Falling reading and math scores, collapsing comprehension, and the inability to sustain complex thought are not just cultural trivia. They shape who can evaluate claims, follow consequences, and resist fraud.

Democrats cannot just be the party of better outcomes while leaving the information environment to rot. If the coalition against authoritarian politics wants to win, it has to fight over literacy, media power, and civic capacity as aggressively as it fights over wages and infrastructure. Otherwise the party keeps delivering material gains while the country gets trained to ignore them.

The useful lie is that this is confusion

The source’s sharpest instinct is its recognition that Trump thrives by exploiting ignorance. That is the real pattern. Authoritarian politics does not need a fully informed public; it needs a public too overloaded, undereducated, or demoralized to trace cause and effect. The system works when people are taught to feel, not think; to cheer, not inspect; to blame themselves, not the people extracting from them.

Calling that “stupidity” is emotionally satisfying, but it is also lazy. The harder truth is worse: a large part of the damage is deliberate, profitable, and political.

The larger pattern

This story is not about Americans becoming naturally dim. It is about power learning how to govern through distraction, contempt, and managed ignorance. Trump is the loudest expression of that method, not an exception to it. The real contest is over whether democratic politics can rebuild the conditions for clear thinking before the anti-knowledge machine finishes the job.
