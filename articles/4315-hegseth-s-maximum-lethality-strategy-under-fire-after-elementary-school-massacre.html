Hegseth's 'maximum lethality' strategy under fire after elementary school massacre

A group of more than 120 Democrats in the US House on Thursday pressed Pentagon Secretary Pete Hegseth on whether American forces used artificial intelligence in the deadly bombing of an elementary school in southern Iran.

“What is the role of artificial intelligence, if any, in selecting targets, assessing intelligence, and making legal determinations during Operation Epic Fury?” the Democratic lawmakers, led by Rep. Sara Jacobs (D-Calif.), asked in a letter to Hegseth. “If AI is used, is it subject to human review and at what point? Was artificial intelligence, including the use of the Maven Smart System, used to identify the Shajareh Tayyebeh school as a target? If so, did a human verify the accuracy of this target?”

The letter to Hegseth was sent a day after The New York Times reported that Pentagon investigators preliminarily concluded that US forces were responsible for the bombing of the girls’ school in Minab, Iran—a strike that killed at least 175 people, mostly children.

The Democratic lawmakers cited the Times’ reporting in their letter, writing that they “are particularly disturbed” by the school bombing, which President Donald Trump initially—and without a shred of evidence—tried to pin on Iran before later saying he didn’t “know enough about it” to assign blame.

According to the Times, the school strike “was the result of a targeting mistake by the US military, which was conducting strikes on an adjacent Iranian base of which the school building was formerly a part.”

The US military has confirmed using AI tools in its illegal war on Iran, which is being carried out in partnership with Israeli forces that have used artificial intelligence extensively in their genocidal assault on the Gaza Strip.

“Our war fighters are leveraging a variety of advanced AI tools,” Brad Cooper, the head of the US Central Command, said in a video message released Wednesday. “These systems help us sift through vast amounts of data in seconds so our leaders can cut through the noise and make smarter decisions faster than the enemy can react.”

NBC News reported earlier this week that the US military is “using AI systems from data analytics company Palantir to identify potential targets in the ongoing attacks.”

“The use of Palantir’s software, which relies in part on Anthropic’s Claude AI systems, comes as Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth aims to put artificial intelligence at the heart of America’s combat operations,” the outlet noted.

During his tenure as head of the Pentagon, Hegseth has worked to dismantle initiatives aimed at reducing civilian killings, scoffed at “stupid rules of engagement,” and touted “maximum lethality” as a top priority for the US military.

In their letter on Thursday, the House Democrats wrote that mass civilian deaths in the US-Israeli war on Iran are “alarming yet unsurprising” given Hegseth and Trump’s open contempt for legal constraints on American forces.

“The US and Israel have reportedly struck or impacted numerous civilian sites—including schools, hospitals, gymnasiums, public gathering spaces, and a UNESCO heritage site,” the lawmakers wrote. “Civilians and civilian infrastructure may under no circumstances be the object of attack and must at all times be respected and protected by all parties.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Article’s Claims and Assignments of Responsibility

Institutional Power and Decision Making

Institutional Power: The article implicates several key actors in the situation surrounding the bombing of an elementary school in southern Iran, notably the Pentagon, under the leadership of Secretary Pete Hegseth, and the broader U.S. military apparatus. It also mentions President Donald Trump, who initially tried to deflect blame onto Iran. The involvement of Israeli forces alongside the U.S. military suggests a coalition effort.

Decision Making: The direct decision to use artificial intelligence (AI) systems, including the Maven Smart System, in targeting operations during “Operation Epic Fury” seems to fall under the Pentagon’s purview, specifically under the leadership of both Secretary Pete Hegseth and Brad Cooper, the head of the US Central Command. The decision to target the Shajareh Tayyebeh school, as reported, was a result of a “targeting mistake” by the U.S. military.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article does not appear to misdirect responsibility but rather points to a systemic issue within the U.S. military’s operational command and its leadership’s decision-making processes regarding AI and target selection. However, it’s critical to assess whether the framing around AI’s role might deflect from broader issues of military accountability and the ethical implications of “partnership” operations with Israeli forces.

Critical Analysis

AI and Human Oversight: Questions raised by the Democratic lawmakers about the role of AI in military operations and the extent of human oversight are crucial. The deployment of AI in warfare, especially in target identification and decision-making without substantial human review, presents significant ethical and legal concerns. The article suggests that while AI tools are being used to sift through data rapidly, the specifics of human oversight, particularly in the case of the school bombing, remain unclear.

Secretary Hegseth’s Role: As head of the Pentagon, Hegseth’s policies and priorities—including dismantling initiatives aimed at reducing civilian casualties and promoting “maximum lethality”—are directly relevant to the incident. His leadership and the policies enacted under his tenure are critical factors in assessing both the specific incident and the broader conduct of the U.S.-Israeli operations in Iran.

President Trump’s Statements: Trump’s initial attempt to blame Iran, followed by an admission of ignorance, reflects a troubling approach to accountability at the highest levels of government. While Trump’s role in the specific targeting decision is likely indirect, his leadership and statements as President have a significant impact on the public narrative and institutional priorities.

Israeli Partnership: The collaboration with Israeli forces, particularly in the context of their use of AI in Gaza, raises broader questions about the coalition’s adherence to international laws regarding civilian protection. The reported widespread impact on civilian infrastructure underlines this concern.

Conclusion

The tragedy of the Shajareh Tayyebeh school bombing, as reported, underscores a profound failure in the military’s targeting processes and a disregard for civilian protection, potentially exacerbated by an over-reliance on AI without sufficient oversight. While Secretary Hegseth and the U.S. military’s leadership bear direct responsibility for the operational decisions leading to this incident, the systemic issues at play, including the role of AI in modern warfare and the ethical implications of military partnerships, demand broader scrutiny. President Trump’s deflective and uninformed responses further highlight a lack of accountability that permeates from individual incidents to overarching military strategy. The criticisms levied by the Democratic lawmakers, therefore, are not misplaced but perhaps do not go far enough in addressing the systemic origins of such tragedies.
