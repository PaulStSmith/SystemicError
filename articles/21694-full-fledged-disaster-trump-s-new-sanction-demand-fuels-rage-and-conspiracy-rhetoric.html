'Full-fledged disaster': Trump's new sanction demand fuels rage and conspiracy rhetoric

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The SetupWho Actually Holds PowerThe Real Decision-MakerMisdirection as MethodConspiracy Is Not A Side EffectThe Pattern

Trump’s Sanctions Theater Is Not Confusion. It Is Control.

The Setup

Trump’s Sunday Truth Social post urged Republicans to “add Iran to the Russian Sanctions Bill” after two U.S. service members were killed in Jordan, and the comment immediately spun out into outrage, speculation, and the usual sludge of online conspiracy. The reporting also shows the basic political fact underneath the noise: this is about a sanctions bill, Trump’s past resistance to it, and a faction of Republicans still waiting for permission to do the obvious.

Who Actually Holds Power

The power in this story sits with Trump. Not because he wrote the bill, but because he still has the ability to freeze it, redirect it, and make Republican lawmakers chase his latest rewrite of reality. Politico’s report makes that plain: Graham believed he had talked Trump into backing the sanctions measure, only for it to be put on ice as Trump tried to negotiate with Vladimir Putin. That is not accidental drift. That is presidential leverage used to stall policy.

Sen. Kevin Cramer’s defense of the amendment also exposes the imbalance. He says adding Iran would be “the most obvious and logical” tribute to Graham because “it’s his bill.” But the point is not tribute. The point is that Senate Republicans are still organizing their own legislative agenda around Trump’s approval and whatever emotional garnish he slaps on top of it.

The Real Decision-Maker

The source text makes Trump look verbally sloppy, but the more important story is that he keeps making foreign policy by impulse and retreat. He praised Putin, reportedly shelved the sanctions effort, then turned around and demanded a change that reads like an after-the-fact correction. That is not coherence. It is executive prerogative without discipline.

And the damage is concrete. The article cites recent reporting that Iran may have pocketed up to $6 billion from oil shipments after sanctions were lifted, money now being linked in the source to attacks on U.S. troops. Whether every downstream claim there is fully established or not, the political pattern is clear: sanctions are not trivia. They are leverage, and Trump has been willing to trade that leverage away while pretending he is still the tough guy in the room.

Misdirection as Method

The weakest part of the reporting is how easily the conversation slides from institutional failure into personality chatter. Trump’s typo-like incoherence, Graham’s memory, Loomer’s deranged fantasy about a “sudden death,” the online pile-on around a dead senator: all of it creates motion without accountability. The noise becomes the story, while the actual decision-making gets a pass.

That is the trick. Deliberate policy reversal gets treated like confusion. A sanctions bill gets treated like a loyalty ritual. A dead senator gets turned into a prop for internet abuse. Meanwhile, the people with the power to set terms are the ones benefiting from the fog.

Conspiracy Is Not A Side Effect

Laura Loomer’s reaction is not merely grotesque; it is politically useful to the same ecosystem that normalizes Trump’s behavior. Her claim that Graham’s death was orchestrated by Russian and Iranian operatives, despite his office confirming cardiovascular disease, is not an eccentric add-on. It is a machine for replacing evidence with allegiance.

This is how authoritarian politics works in practice. It does not only lie. It floods the zone with accusations so unhinged that ordinary causality starts to look optional. Once that happens, actual statecraft can be laundered through paranoia, and every failure can be blamed on shadowy enemies instead of the people making the decisions.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple: Trump preserves power by keeping Republican governance unserious, reactive, and dependent on his moods. The sanctions bill matters because it shows how even basic tools of state power get subordinated to personal vanity, diplomatic flirtation with strongmen, and the need to perform toughness after the fact.

That is the real story here. Not one post, not one senator’s legacy, not one conspiracist’s fever dream. The story is a ruling faction that treats policy as theater, truth as optional, and consequences as something to blame on somebody weaker once the damage is done.
