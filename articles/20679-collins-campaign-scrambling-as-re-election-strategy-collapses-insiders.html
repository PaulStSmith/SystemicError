Collins campaign scrambling as re-election strategy collapses: insiders

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosJuly 10, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Lost ShieldWho Actually Held PowerThe Trap in the FramingTrump Is Still the AnchorWhat the Polls Actually SayThe Bigger Pattern

Collins Built a Race Around a Damaged Man. That Is Not Strategy. It Is Moral Evasion.

The Lost Shield

Politico’s report is simple enough: Susan Collins’ campaign had banked on Graham Platner as the convenient wreckage in the race, and his suspension after credible allegations of sexual misconduct and assault blows up that plan. The GOP calculation was not to persuade voters on Collins’ record. It was to bury her under a deliberately uglier opponent and hope the comparison did the work.

Who Actually Held Power

The real power in this story sat with Collins and the Republican operation around her. They were not passive observers of a messy race; they were active beneficiaries of a political bet built around opposition research and reputational contamination. That matters. The story is not that Collins was unlucky. It is that her campaign treated another person’s alleged misconduct as a campaign asset.

The Trap in the Framing

The weak framing is obvious: a narrative about “scrambling” and “strategy” softens what is really going on. This was not an innocent collapse of plans. It was a dependence on candidate degradation as a substitute for democratic accountability. Collins was trying to avoid the central fact of her own politics, which the report states plainly: she is a Republican running in a state increasingly hostile to Trump and his party.

Trump Is Still the Anchor

The report also exposes the deeper problem Republicans cannot spin away. Trump remains the political weight around Collins’ neck, and around every Republican on the ballot. Her team wanted the race centered on Platner’s personal scandal because that would displace the more politically dangerous issue: Republican alignment with Trump’s authoritarian brand. Once the foil weakens, the party’s own record comes back into view.

What the Polls Actually Say

The internal polling in the report is the most revealing detail. Platner trailing Collins helped the GOP story. But alternative Democratic candidates either led her or ran close, which suggests Collins was not protected by strength so much as by the convenience of a weakened opponent. That is not a mandate. It is a hostage situation with better branding.

The Bigger Pattern

This is a familiar Republican pattern: use scandal as camouflage, use personalization to obscure institutional responsibility, and use the worst behavior available on the field to make your own politics seem normal by comparison. Democrats do not need to pretend Collins is uniquely clever to understand the lesson. The point is simpler and uglier. The Republican Party is still running on misdirection, and when the distraction fails, the underlying problem returns intact: a party tied to Trump, dependent on his damage, and willing to exploit anything that keeps voters from looking straight at that fact.
