Former federal prosecutor Preet Bharara has signed on as counsel for Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) to fight the new criminal investigation the Trump administration has launched into him.

The investigation, led by Ed Martin, will examine whether Schiff committed mortgage fraud by holding primary residences in California and Maryland at the same time — a common practice for members of Congress that experts say, as long as all applicable state laws are followed, is not illegal. A spokesman for Schiff clarified he did not mislead any mortgage lender about his use of either property.

New York Attorney General Letitia James is reportedly being investigated as part of the same probe.

Bharara issued a scathing statement against the investigation, Politico's Kyle Cheney reported.

"The allegations against Senator Schiff are transparently false, stale, and long debunked," said Bharara. "Now Ed Martin, the most brazenly partisan and politically compromised person possible for the task, has been picked to investigate a political adversary. The bias here is glaring. Mr. Martin's nomination to be U.S. Attorney of the District of Columbia was derailed not only by his partisanship and unfitness, but also by the hold Senator Schiff appropriately placed on the nomination."

"Mr. Martin is a January 6-defending lawyer who has repeatedly pursued baseless and politically-motivated investigations to fulfill demands to investigate and prosecute perceived enemies. Any supposed investigation led by him would be the very definition of weaponization of the justice process."

Martin was previously appointed as Trump's acting U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia, but after a string of high-profile and baseless legal threats against people and groups Trump was annoyed at, GOP senators made clear they would not provide the votes to confirm him. Trump moved Martin into the U.S. pardon attorney's office to keep him in the DOJ.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Shadows of Justice: The Bharara-Schiff Saga

In a move that reeks of political vendettas more than the pursuit of justice, the Trump administration has launched a criminal investigation into Senator Adam Schiff (D-CA), with former federal prosecutor Preet Bharara stepping into the ring as Schiff’s legal counsel. This case isn't just about alleged mortgage fraud; it's a glaring spotlight on the weaponization of legal systems against political adversaries, a tactic as old as politics itself but no less disturbing with each iteration.

The Allegations: As Thin as Air

At the heart of this investigation, led by Ed Martin, is the claim that Schiff committed mortgage fraud by having primary residences in both California and Maryland—a common practice among members of Congress and hardly a scandalous revelation. Schiff's spokesman has already thrown cold water on the flames, stating unequivocally that Schiff did not mislead any mortgage lenders regarding his use of the properties. This begs the question: why pursue such a flimsy case? The answer, unsurprisingly, lies not in the pursuit of justice but in the pursuit of political gain.

Ed Martin: The Fox Guarding the Henhouse

Preet Bharara's statement, as reported by Politico's Kyle Cheney, pulls no punches in describing the investigation as baseless, motivated by stale allegations that have long been debunked. Bharara's criticism of Ed Martin as "the most brazenly partisan and politically compromised person possible for the task" hits the nail on the head. Martin, whose nomination as U.S. Attorney of the District of Columbia was blocked due to his partisanship and unfitness (not to mention a hold placed by Schiff himself), is a figure who has defended the indefensible actions of January 6 and pursued baseless investigations to please his political masters. This isn't just the pot calling the kettle black; it's the pot launching a full-scale investigation into the kettle's coloration based on dubious grounds.

Weaponization of the Justice Process

The most damning aspect of this saga is the clear weaponization of the justice process against political adversaries. Bharara's assertion that any investigation led by Martin would be "the very definition of weaponization of the justice process" is a dire warning about the path we're treading. When legal tools are wielded not in the service of justice but as blunt instruments of political warfare, the very foundations of democracy are at risk.

A Dangerous Precedent

This investigation sets a dangerous precedent. If political figures can leverage the justice system to launch investigations based on thin allegations against their opponents, what stops the system from becoming a circus of endless retribution? Today it's Schiff; tomorrow, it could be anyone deemed a political threat. This isn't justice; it's a mockery of the principles that the justice system is supposed to uphold.

Conclusion: A Call for Sanity

The investigation into Adam Schiff, spearheaded by a figure as compromised as Ed Martin, is a stark reminder of the lengths to which political vendettas will go. It's a wake-up call for those who care about the integrity of the justice system and the principles of democracy. As this saga unfolds, it will serve as a litmus test for the health of our political and legal institutions. Will they succumb to the disease of partisan warfare, or will they rise above, reaffirming their commitment to justice, fairness, and the rule of law? Only time will tell, but if history is any guide, optimism must be tempered with a heavy dose of realism.
