Pam Bondi accused of using DOJ to spy on Congress: 'It's astounding'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi attends a press conference, as she unveils actions against the state of Maine, which is locked in a dispute with the Trump administration over transgender policy, at the Justice Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 16, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis/File Photo

During contentious testimony on Wednesday, some congressional members suspected Attorney General Pam Bondi of abusing the Justice Department to spy on how they planned to question her.

In an exclusive conversation with Raw Story, Rep. Hank Johnson (D-GA) outlined precisely why he believes this is the case — and what it says about the Trump administration.

“Pam Bondi came prepared to mislead, to defend, to insult, to refuse to answer,” said Johnson. “It is an exercise that she prepared for.”

Beyond that, he added, “I understand that she may also have had a list of items that each member of Congress who presented themselves to the Department of Justice to review the Epstein files unredacted, the exact and precise documents that those Congresspeople looked at. That’s what I heard. And you have to request specific ones.”

“So what we have is the possibility of the DOJ under Pam Bondi, and Pam Bondi herself, having the activity in DOJ computers — in other words, you track what members of Congress are looking at on your systems. That should not be. And then you come to Congress today armed with that information ready to, ready to defend yourself. Or use in whatever way you decide might be most appropriate.”

“That smacks of, you know, there being a surveillance state,” he added.

Johnson further agreed that Bondi was perpetrating a double standard, given the Trump administration, and its allies in Congress, expressing fury over former special counsel Jack Smith investigating the phone records of lawmakers like Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC), who had spoken with Trump in the leadup to the Jan. 6 attack and the plot to overturn the 2020 election. Senate Republicans even slipped a provision into a recent funding bill that would let them sue the Justice Department for this.

“The duplicity is astounding,” said Johnson.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Bondi’s Testimony Unveiled

In a scenario that reads like a Kafkaesque drama, Attorney General Pam Bondi’s recent congressional testimony spirals into a chilling narrative of alleged surveillance and manipulation. The event underlines a farcical yet disturbing spectacle of power play within the Trump administration, as dissected by Rep. Hank Johnson (D-GA) in his revelations to Raw Story.

The Allegations: Bondi is accused of utilizing the Justice Department’s resources to spy on congressional members’ preparations and strategies for questioning her. If true, this not only smacks of a blatant abuse of power but also morphs the department into an Orwellian tool of surveillance against its governmental brethren.

The Preparation: Rep. Johnson’s description of Bondi’s approach to the testimony—”prepared to mislead, to defend, to insult, to refuse to answer”—paints a vivid picture of a political theater where deceit and evasion are the main acts. It’s as if Bondi entered the stage armed with a script designed to deflect and counter, rather than enlighten and clarify.

The Surveillance State: The heart of the matter lies in the alleged monitoring of congress members’ access to sensitive documents. This operation, if confirmed, represents a grave intrusion into the legislative branch’s oversight functions—a fundamental pillar of democracy. It’s an act that reeks of authoritarianism, suggesting a surveillance state where power is maintained through monitoring and intimidation.

The Duplicity: Johnson’s assertion of a “double standard” highlights the hypocrisy at play. The Trump administration and its allies, who were incensed by investigations into their own dealings—pointedly, the probing into phone records related to the Jan. 6 attack—now find themselves embroiled in a potentially more invasive act of surveillance. This juxtaposition is not just ironic; it’s a glaring testament to the administration’s selective outrage and moral flexibility.

The Conclusion: The allegations against Pam Bondi and, by extension, the Justice Department under her stewardship, present a stark illustration of the potential for abuse within the highest echelons of power. If these accusations hold weight, they underscore a disconcerting move towards a surveillance state—a scenario where privacy is penetrated, and power is perpetuated through the watchful eyes of a supposedly impartial Justice Department.

As this saga unfolds, one must ponder the implications of such actions on the fabric of American democracy. The chilling effect on legislative oversight, the erosion of trust in public institutions, and the broader implications for civil liberties cannot be overstated. In a democracy, transparency, accountability, and respect for the separation of powers are not optional—they are essential. The Bondi testimony, therefore, is not just a story of potential misconduct; it’s a cautionary tale of what happens when those in power forget these fundamental principles.
