'It’s about to collapse': MAGA defector forecasts end of Trump movement

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosJune 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

MAGA’s Problem Is Not Weight. It Is Rot.

Power Is the Point

The source story centers on a familiar crack in the Trump coalition: Pedro Gonzales, once a MAGA supporter, says the movement is collapsing because it cannot survive the contradiction between taboo-breaking and selective restraint. That is the smallest version of the story. The larger one is simpler: the people with actual institutional power are Trump and the officials, media figures, and movement enforcers who amplified his claims. The targets were never the ones driving events. They were the people being used.

Springfield Was Not a Mistake

Trump did not “misspeak” about Springfield. He chose a racist conspiracy theory and turned a small Ohio town into national bait. JD Vance then helped launder it, openly admitting that inventing stories can be justified if it forces attention. That is not confusion. It is strategy.

The article’s framing risks softening this by treating the episode as one boundary too far for some conservatives. That misses the point. The boundary was not crossed accidentally. It was erased on purpose, then defended with a lie about necessity. Springfield became a test case in how fast a fabricated threat can be converted into political energy.

The Blame Fell Downward

The misdirection is obvious. Immigrants were cast as predators. Springfield residents were turned into collateral damage. The people who manufactured the panic then positioned themselves as truth-tellers under siege. That is classic authoritarian theater: harm the weaker party, then claim you were only responding to public concern.

Gonzales says he left after seeing the damage in Springfield and realizing Trump and his allies had shifted from debate to enmity. That is a moral explanation, but it is also an indictment of the movement’s structure. MAGA does not merely tolerate scapegoating. It depends on it. When the lie stops working, the movement does not become principled. It becomes less useful.

Cruelty Was Not a Detour

The article says Gonzales felt vindicated after the Trump administration embraced “shockingly cruel tactics” that ended with federal agents killing two American citizens in Minneapolis during an immigration crackdown. Whatever else is inferred around that episode, the source is clear about the direction of travel: from rhetoric to state violence, with immigrants as the initial justification and American citizens as additional casualties.

That is the real pattern. The movement sells the public a story about disorder, then uses that story to excuse escalation. Once the machinery is in motion, it does not stay neatly aimed at the people the propaganda named first. It widens. That is what makes these campaigns so dangerous and so familiar.

The Dissent Is About Power Failing

The right-wing defections described here are not a conscience renaissance. They are a signal that the coalition’s political payoff is weakening. Gonzales, Tucker Carlson, Megyn Kelly, Candace Owens, and Alex Jones are not converging on decency; they are reacting to fracture. The source notes complaints about affordability, the Iran war, and the botched release of the Epstein files. That list matters because it shows what finally dents the machine: not the cruelty itself, but the realization that the cruelty is no longer producing control.

Trump’s cratering polling and erosion among young men suggest the grievance brand is losing market share. That does not make the system healthier. It means the scam is fraying.

The Pattern Is the Product

This story is not about one debunked rumor or one disillusioned commentator. It is about a political operation that converts hatred into leverage, then pretends the resulting damage is an unfortunate byproduct. The method is always the same: invent a threat, assign blame downward, reward the loudest enablers, and call the result populism.

When the lie no longer holds, the movement does not apologize. It blames the audience, changes the target, and keeps moving. That is the system.
