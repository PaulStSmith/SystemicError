‘Prolific Mini-Trump’ leaves agency insiders ‘unsettled and embarrassed'

A lesser-known member of the Trump administration has been driving the campaign to get President Donald Trump to oust Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell, The New York Times reported — and it's gotten to the point that insiders are fed up with it.

Specifically, according to the report, Bill Pulte, the head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, has championed the effort both on social media and behind the scenes, even writing the draft letter for Trump to use to fire Powell earlier this month, before he reversed course, reportedly persuaded against doing so by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

The president does not have the authority to fire the Fed chair before their term expires, legal experts have said, and even the Supreme Court has fired a warning shot over this issue.

Nonetheless, attacking Powell "has given Mr. Pulte, an heir of one of America’s largest home building families, a new prominence within the Trump administration that exceeds the usually quiet confines of the agency," said the report. "A longtime social media maven, Mr. Pulte has deployed his bombastic tone and his credibility from the construction industry to argue that the Fed’s policies are hurting the housing market and that a $2.5 billion renovation of its headquarters is an example of reckless spending by the central bank."

Pulte was present at Trump's high-profile tour of the Fed renovation at their headquarters earlier this week, where Powell schooled Trump on national television by correcting false claims about the project. Pulte has told his followers for weeks on social media, with no basis, that the project is "riddled with fraud."

As all of this has been unfolding, "Several housing policymakers and former housing regulators, who spoke on the condition of anonymity because they feared angering Mr. Pulte, said they found his use of social media unsettling and somewhat embarrassing. Others have begun to refer to Mr. Pulte as a 'mini-Trump,' given the president’s proclivity to use social media to lash out at his political critics and opponents," said the report.

All of this has also aroused the anger of Democratic lawmakers, with even Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) — who herself has criticized Powell's monetary policy decisions — condemning his behavior.

“Your job is to manage F.H.F.A., oversee our nation’s mortgage market and lower costs for American families — full stop,” Warren told Pulte in a letter earlier this week. “Your prolific activity on X and apparent decision to take time away from your duties as F.H.F.A. director to draft a letter for President Trump to fire Chair Powell are abnormal.”

Our Analysis:

The Pulte Paradox: Trump Administration's Unorthodox Crusade Against Jerome Powell

In a political landscape that seldom surprises us anymore, the recent revelations about Bill Pulte, the head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), pushing for the ouster of Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell, is a new subplot in the ongoing saga of Trump-era politicking that veers into the realm of the absurd. The New York Times report that brought this to light paints a picture of an administration where the lines between personal vendettas, policy making, and social media antics blur into an indistinguishable mess.

The Crusade Against Powell

Pulte, a figure who previously lurked in the background of the Trump administration, has apparently taken it upon himself to lead a campaign against Powell, a campaign that stretches from the corridors of power in Washington to the chaotic world of social media. It's curious to note that Pulte, "an heir of one of America’s largest home building families," has seemingly found his political voice by attacking the Federal Reserve's policies, which he claims are detrimental to the housing market. His methods, however, stray far from reasoned economic debate and veer into the territory of social media bombast and backstage politicking.

The Legal and Political Quagmire

The audacity of drafting a letter for President Trump to dismiss Powell is matched only by the ignorance of the law that suggests such a move. Legal experts and even the Supreme Court have indicated that the president lacks the authority to fire the Fed Chair before the term's completion. This move, had it been successful, would not only have been an unprecedented overreach of executive power but also a destabilizing act against the Federal Reserve's independence, a cornerstone of its ability to guide the economy without political pressure.

Social Media Antics

Pulte's engagement on social media platforms, where he has spread unfounded allegations of fraud regarding the Federal Reserve's renovation project, is emblematic of a broader trend within the Trump administration to leverage social media as a tool for policy intimidation and personal vendettas. This approach, while potentially galvanizing a base of followers, undermines the gravity and responsibility of public office. The comparison to a "mini-Trump" is apt, not only in the style of communication but also in the apparent disregard for the substantive, measured discourse expected in public governance.

The Response from Policymakers and Legislators

The reaction to Pulte's crusade has illuminated a spectrum of disapproval that crosses party lines. Housing policymakers, former regulators, and Democratic lawmakers have voiced concern and condemnation. Senator Elizabeth Warren’s rebuke underscores the departure from normative responsibilities that Pulte’s actions represent. Warren's emphasis on the core duties of the FHFA director highlights the absurdity of Pulte's preoccupation with the Fed, a preoccupation that seems to have little to do with the housing market's betterment or American families' welfare.

Conclusion: The Larger Implications

What the Pulte episode lays bare is not just an individual's misguided campaign against a Federal Reserve Chair but a broader, more disturbing trend of governance by social media provocation and personal crusade. It's a manifestation of a political culture that increasingly prizes sensationalism over substance, and personal loyalty over policy acumen. This episode should not just fade into the cacophony of Trump administration controversies but should prompt serious reflection on the norms and expectations we hold for those in public service. In the end, the real victim is not Powell or Pulte, but the integrity of our public institutions and the principles of sound, responsible governance.
