The Real Story Behind Dershowitz’s Controversial Third Term Argument

In the bizarre theater of American politics, some scripts are too audacious even for seasoned players. One such narrative is being penned by former Jeffrey Epstein lawyer and President Donald Trump ally, Alan Dershowitz, in his new book advocating for an unprecedented third term for Trump. However, Garrett Epps, Washington Monthly Legal Affairs Editor, robustly contends that Dershowitz’s arguments are nothing short of bunk.

Dershowitz’s Transformation and Questionable Advocacy

It’s crucial to juxtapose the Alan Dershowitz of the 1960s and 1970s—a respected legal mind making sound arguments—with the figure he has morphed into today. As Epps points out, this is a man who once secured a lenient deal for convicted sex-trafficker Jeffrey Epstein and is now stretching constitutional interpretations to their breaking point. This radical shift in persona and ethics in Dershowitz’s professional life cannot be overlooked when assessing the credibility of his legal arguments.

The Claims of Nonpartisanship

Dershowitz posits that his analysis is an “honest, objective, nonpartisan” look at complex legal issues, even suggesting that former President Barack Obama could benefit from this interpretation as much as Trump. This assertion quickly crumbles under scrutiny. The reality on the ground starkly contrasts with his claim: Trump, not Obama, has vocally desired a third term. Trump’s followers have shown extreme partisanship, to the extent of assaulting the Capitol. It’s Trump, currently wielding substantial political and military influence, who stands to gain from such a constitutional loophole.

Institutional Power and Responsibility

Here, Dershowitz’s institutional power as a famed legal scholar and a former Trump affiliate becomes apparent. His book is not merely an academic exercise but potentially an influential political tool. However, is he the sole author of the current situation or merely a player in a larger ensemble directed by Trump himself? While Epps challenges Dershowitz’s arguments, it’s critical to identify the broader systemic issues at play here, particularly Trump’s direct and indirect influence over public and political narratives.

The Misdirection of Responsibility

The framing of Dershowitz’s argument as a nonpartisan analysis misdirects the public from the realities of current political dynamics. It’s a classic case of using legal jargon to mask what is essentially a partisan endeavor. Epps’ critique sheds light on this by juxtaposing Dershowitz’s theoretical musings with the practical political actions and desires of Trump, revealing a clear dissonance between Dershowitz’s writings and the political landscape.

Epps’ Bold Rebuttal

Epps does not mince words in his assessment, oscillating between an outright “no” and a vehement “F---, no” to the possibility of Trump’s third term through the constitutional gymnastics proposed by Dershowitz. This bold stance, especially considering Dershowitz’s history of vindictiveness—as evidenced by his actions against critics—is both risky and commendable. It highlights a critical resistance within the legal community against what many see as a distortion of constitutional law for political ends.

Conclusion

The debate over whether Trump could or should have a third term is more than an academic discussion; it’s a litmus test for the resilience of American democratic institutions against the backdrop of political partisanship. Dershowitz’s proposal, as dissected and dismissed by Epps, underscores the ongoing struggle between factual legal standards and the manipulative tide of political narratives. In this instance, the responsibility for the potential erosion of democratic norms clearly lies with those in power pushing for an agenda that suits them, wrapped in the guise of legal legitimacy.
