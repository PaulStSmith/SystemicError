'I see slave patrols': ​Jasmine Crockett warns Supreme Court just set dangerous precedent

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

MSNBC/screen grab

Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) said she was reminded of the history of "slave patrols" after conservatives on the U.S. Supreme Court gave Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents the ability to target people based on their ethnicity.

During a Sunday interview on MSNBC, Crockett criticized President Donald Trump's crime-fighting strategy.

"So listen, if we want to figure out how we are going to be safer, we probably need people that have actual intelligence and actually will pay attention to data and facts," she explained. "But what they're doing right now, it almost feels like, you know, I've seen the memes about the purge and all these things."

"But as somebody who understands history, when I see ICE, I see slave patrols," she continued. "But if you know the history of policing in this country, then you understand that they were born out of slave patrols."

"And now with the Supreme Court saying this, it's almost like you can just go grab them up. And that is a problem. We all should have a problem with that."

Crockett pointed out that an effort to remove Black history from schools added to the problem.

"When you don't want to teach American history that includes Black history, then you lose out on the benefit of understanding that we have been down this road before," she remarked. "And it was not good. And we fixed it once. And it is a shame that we are relitigating this. And we are going to have to fix it again."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Rep. Jasmine Crockett's Condemnation of ICE and the Supreme Court's Decision

Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) has launched a scorching critique of the U.S. Supreme Court's decision that empowers Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents with the disturbing ability to target individuals based on ethnicity. Her comments, made during a Sunday interview on MSNBC, draw a harrowing parallel between ICE's modus operandi and the dark history of "slave patrols" in the United States. This comparison isn't just a metaphorical leap; it's a calculated observation grounded in the grim realities of American history and its policing practices.

The Echoes of Slave Patrols

Crockett's analogy is not merely for shock value. It's a necessary historical parallel that highlights a cyclical pattern of racial targeting and oppression. The assertion that the history of policing in the United States is intertwined with slave patrols is not just an opinion; it's a fact. The origins of some policing institutions can indeed be traced back to organizations that were established to control the movements of enslaved Africans. By invoking this comparison, Crockett underscores a systemic issue that extends beyond mere immigration enforcement to the very foundations of law enforcement in America.

The Supreme Court's Role

The decision by the conservative-majority Supreme Court, as criticized by Crockett, is not seen as just a legal ruling but as a sanctioning of racial profiling. This isn't a matter of subtle legal nuances; it's a loud, clear message about who gets to be targeted and on what basis. When Crockett says, "And now with the Supreme Court saying this, it's almost like you can just go grab them up," she's voicing a fear that many communities of color live with: the fear of being targeted solely based on ethnicity, under the guise of law and order.

The Dangers of Erasing Black History

Another critical issue Crockett touches upon is the movement to remove Black history from educational curriculums. This is not just an educational policy debate; it's a direct threat to the collective memory and understanding of America's racial injustices. The Congresswoman's point that "When you don't want to teach American history that includes Black history, then you lose out on the benefit of understanding that we have been down this road before," is a stark reminder of the consequences of ignoring or whitewashing history. It's a call to recognize patterns of oppression and address them, not erase them from memory.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

Rep. Jasmine Crockett's words serve as a clarion call to recognize the historical patterns of racial targeting and oppression that are being mirrored in today's policies and rulings. The comparison to slave patrols is not an exaggeration but a historical fact that bears relevance to the current discourse on immigration and policing. Her critique of the Supreme Court's decision and the broader implications for racial justice in America is a necessary intervention in a time when the echoes of the past are resounding all too clearly in the present.

In essence, Crockett isn't just critiquing a legal decision; she's highlighting a systemic failure to learn from history. This failure not only jeopardizes the principles of equality and justice but also threatens the very fabric of a diverse society. It's a stark reminder that the battle against racial injustice is far from over, and the path to a more just society requires vigilance, education, and a steadfast commitment to confronting and correcting systemic errors.
