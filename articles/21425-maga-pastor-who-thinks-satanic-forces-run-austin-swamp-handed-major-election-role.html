MAGA pastor who thinks Satanic forces run 'Austin swamp' handed major election role

Abbott Hands Election Policy to a Theocrat

The Appointment

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott has put Nate Schatzline, a Christian nationalist pastor and two-term state representative, into a senior advisory role over election policy. Schatzline resigned his House seat for a $275,000-a-year job that gives him “policy and strategic direction” over legislative and electoral matters, while Texas heads into a cycle shaped by redistricting fights, Abbott’s own reelection, and Ken Paxton’s Senate bid.

“Election Integrity” Is the Cover Story

The phrase “election integrity” is doing the usual camouflage work here. In the source material, Schatzline is not presented as a neutral administrator or a technocrat. He is a stop-the-steal partisan who has spent years pushing debunked claims about 2020 and advancing bills that would have made voting harder in Texas. Abbott did not stumble into this. He chose a political actor whose record points in one direction: narrowing the electorate and dressing that project up as public order.

The Actual Power on Display

The institutional power is not with Schatzline alone. Abbott holds the authority to make the appointment, and he is the one turning election administration into a loyalty test for the far right. Schatzline brings the theology; Abbott supplies the state machinery. That is the point. When a governor hands election policy to someone who thinks defeating Democrats is part of a spiritual war against “principalities,” he is not seeking integrity. He is selecting an ideology with a payroll.

Holy War as Political Method

Schatzline’s worldview matters because it erases democratic opposition as legitimate politics. If your opponents are agents of darkness, then compromise becomes betrayal and voting access becomes a battlefield tactic. The source ties him to Mercy Culture Church, the New Apostolic Reformation, and Paula White-Cain’s National Faith Advisory Board, which places him inside a network that treats politics as religious conquest. That is not some colorful background detail. It is the operating system.

Misdirection by Design

The weak framing here is the idea that this is about “election integrity” at all. The story is not confusion, and it is not a bad hire caused by careless staffing. It is deliberate alignment between a governor, a movement church ecosystem, and a candidate universe that benefits from intimidation, grievance, and procedural control. The real misdirection is the attempt to make anti-democratic power look like administrative seriousness.

The Larger Pattern

Texas is not just electing or appointing a few loud extremists. It is building a governance style in which Republican power gets protected by sacred language, manipulated election rules, and permanent emergency politics. The pattern is plain: when institutions feel threatened by democracy, they do not always abolish elections outright. They recruit believers, weaponize “integrity,” and put the state in service of the bloc most willing to confuse domination with righteousness.
