'Lies rubbed in your face’: Critics erupt over Trump’s bawdy Epstein birthday doodle

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 9, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump's repeated denials of the Wall Street Journal's reporting that he had sent a salacious doodle of a naked woman to the late financier and accused sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein in his birthday book two decades ago became much harder on Monday, when House Democrats released what they claimed to be that very image produced from a subpoena.

The image, which, in line with the Journal's description, featured a poem outlined by the naked female form, saying he and Epstein had "certain things in common" and wishing him that "every day be another wonderful secret," prompted yet another wave of mockery from commenters on social media.

"HERE IT IS: We got Trump’s birthday note to Jeffrey Epstein that the President said doesn’t exist," stated the official account for the Democrats on the House Oversight Committee. "Trump talks about a 'wonderful secret' the two of them shared. What is he hiding? Release the files!"

"President Trump filed a $10 billion dollar lawsuit against the @WSJ, its owners, publisher and two reporters, for reporting the existence of the letter, apparently produced below in compliance with subpoena, which he denied existed," wrote NBC News reporter Garrett Haake.

"MASSIVE BREAKING: Lawyers for Jeffrey Epstein’s estate just provided a copy of the birthday book Trump Signed and placed a disgusting poem in for Epstein," wrote liberal commentator Brian Krassenstein. "Congress just released the full letter and signature! I have no doubt in my mind that this is Trump's Signature. Trump must answer to the American people IMMEDIATELY and RESIGN!"

"That’s FBI Informant Donald Trump to you," wrote podcaster Jon Favreau, referencing the latest unsupported claims by House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) that Trump served as an FBI informant in the Epstein case.

"Remember when Trump said the birthday card he signed and sent to Epstein 'doesn’t exist'? Anyway, here it is," stated the account for The Lincoln Project.

"The constant denials and lying just mean you’re going to get your lies rubbed in your face and it ultimately makes any malfeasance (or even lack of malfeasance!) look worse than it might be," wrote attorney Blake Allen. "But, Trump’s browbeaten most MAGA into line on this (w/ lots of egg on their face)."

"Don't know what I expected exactly, but this is somehow worse than that," wrote Crooked Media founder Jon Lovett on X.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Doodle Drama: Trump, Epstein, and the Salacious Sketch

The Unfolding Scandal

In what appears to be a plotline ripped straight from the most lurid of political thrillers, President Donald Trump finds himself at the center of a controversy that marries the grotesque with the absurd. The Wall Street Journal's bombshell report, alleging Trump's contribution of a salacious doodle to Jeffrey Epstein's birthday book, has morphed from denials to undeniable reality. House Democrats, wielding the power of subpoena, have thrust into public view an image that Trump fervently claimed did not exist. This image, a poem enveloped by the outline of a naked female form, not only exists but serves as a testament to a bizarre camaraderie between Trump and the disgraced financier Epstein.

The Mockery and the Lawsuits

Social media, ever the arena for public spectacle, erupted in a mixture of disbelief, mockery, and outrage. The Democrats on the House Oversight Committee did not mince words, directly challenging Trump to account for the "wonderful secret" shared with Epstein. Meanwhile, Trump's response to the Wall Street Journal's reporting — a staggering $10 billion lawsuit — now seems not just an overreaction but a desperate attempt to smother a smoldering scandal.

The Claims and Counterclaims

Garrett Haake of NBC News and liberal commentator Brian Krassenstein underscored the gravity of the situation by highlighting the lawsuit and the release of the full letter and signature. Assertions of Trump's immediate need to resign were met with a peculiar and unsupported counter by House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), who branded Trump an FBI informant in the Epstein saga. This claim, seemingly plucked from the ether of political desperation, does little to address the core issue — the existence of the doodle and its implications.

The MAGA Defense and the Reality of Lies

The MAGA camp, as noted by attorney Blake Allen, has been corralled into defending the indefensible, a task made Sisyphean by Trump's incessant denials now laid bare as falsehoods. The Lincoln Project and Jon Lovett of Crooked Media captured the essence of the moment — a blend of shock and the grim realization that reality was indeed stranger and more disturbing than anticipated.

Conclusion: The Inescapable Truth

This episode is not merely about a lewd drawing or the eccentricities of two controversial figures. It's a glaring spotlight on the ease with which truth can be manipulated, denied, and then, unavoidably, confronted. Trump's vehement denials, followed by the release of the very evidence he claimed was non-existent, paint a portrait of a political landscape where accountability is often obscured by bluster and litigation.

The call for Trump to answer to the American people is more than a demand for explanations. It's a plea for a return to a baseline of integrity, where facts are acknowledged, and accountability is not an anachronism. Whether this incident will leave a lasting scar on Trump's political legacy or be dismissed as yet another storm in a teacup remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: In the age of instant information and relentless scrutiny, the truth has a way of emerging, no matter how vehemently it is denied.
