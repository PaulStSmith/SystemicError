'Awkward': Conservative analyst reveals how Trump's tariff case could blow up in his face

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

CNN screenshot

Conservative analyst Ben Shapiro argued on Wednesday night that the Supreme Court is putting President Donald Trump “in an awkward position” over his key economic policy.

The Supreme Court heard oral arguments on Wednesday in a case challenging Trump’s authority to impose sweeping tariffs under his emergency powers. Trump has argued that the tariffs are necessary to revive America’s manufacturing base. The Supreme Court seemed skeptical of those arguments on Wednesday, with Chief Justice John Roberts referring to them as a “tax” on the American people.

Shapiro argued on CNN’s “NewsNight with Abby Phillip” that the Supreme Court could reject Trump’s tariff policy, which could have a ripple effect on his presidency.

“One of the things that will be fascinating is if the court does strike it down, watch the stock market absolutely explode,” Shapiro said. “Seriously, you’ll watch the S&P 500 jump like nobody’s business, and the president is then going to be in the awkward position of having to explain why the markets are boosted by the rejection of his key policy.”

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court vs. Trump’s Tariffs: A Tense Showdown

In a recent spectacle that could only be described as a judicial rebuke waiting to happen, the Supreme Court displayed a palpable skepticism towards President Donald Trump’s use of emergency powers to impose broad tariffs, ostensibly aimed at rejuvenating America’s manufacturing sector. The scene was set for a confrontation between the highest court in the land and the executive’s economic maneuvering, with conservative analyst Ben Shapiro providing a ringside commentary that was as revealing as it was predictable.

The Supreme Court’s Skepticism: A Reality Check

At the heart of the matter is Trump’s controversial tariff policy, which he has championed as a necessary measure to restore the glory of American manufacturing. However, the Supreme Court, led by Chief Justice John Roberts, appeared unconvinced, going as far as labeling the tariffs a “tax” on the American populace. This is a significant moment, not just for its immediate economic implications, but for what it signals about the judiciary’s stance on executive overreach. When a conservative court signals its discomfort with a Republican president’s policy, one must pause and consider the gravity of the executive’s actions.

Ben Shapiro’s Analysis: A Glimpse into Conservative Rationalization

Enter Ben Shapiro, the conservative analyst known for his rapid-fire delivery and staunch defense of conservative ideals. Shapiro’s take on the matter, as aired on CNN’s “NewsNight with Abby Phillip,” provides an interesting lens through which to view the conservative rationalization of Trump’s economic policies. Shapiro posits that the Supreme Court striking down Trump’s tariff policy could lead to a stock market surge, a scenario he suggests would put Trump “in an awkward position.”

This assertion is layered in irony. First, it tacitly acknowledges that the market might actually favor the removal of what many see as protectionist tariffs, which have been criticized for sparking trade wars and raising prices for American consumers. Second, Shapiro’s prediction exposes the fragility of Trump’s economic narrative—that his policies are unilaterally beneficial for the economy and, by extension, the stock market.

The Market’s Reaction: A Telling Indicator

Shapiro’s speculation about the market’s response is particularly telling. It underscores a broader recognition that Trump’s economic policies, particularly his tariff regime, are not universally acclaimed panaceas for America’s economic challenges. The notion that the S&P 500 could “jump like nobody’s business” in the event of the tariffs being struck down speaks volumes about the perceived economic drag these policies have imposed.

Conclusion: A Policy in Peril

As we stand on the precipice of a potentially landmark Supreme Court decision, the critique embedded in Shapiro’s analysis—whether intentional or not—shines a spotlight on the precarious nature of Trump’s tariff policy. It’s a policy that, far from being an unmitigated success, has divided economists, strained international relationships, and now, faces the ultimate litmus test in the Supreme Court.

The awkward position Trump may find himself in, as Shapiro suggests, is not just about explaining a buoyant stock market in the face of a judicial rebuke. It’s about reconciling the reality of his policies’ tangible impact on the American economy and populace. As we await the court’s decision, one thing is clear: the ramifications of this legal battle will reverberate far beyond the halls of the Supreme Court, challenging the very foundation of Trump’s economic agenda.
