'This man is not well!' 'Dripping in sweat' Trump alarms in latest interview

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as he speaks to members of the media on board Air Force One en route to Joint Base Andrews, U.S., November 2, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

As much as President Donald Trump demonizes the mainstream media, he loves the spotlight. And on Sunday night, Trump’s interview with Norah O’Donnell for CBS News’ “60 Minutes” was aired.

The interview is generating a variety of reactions on X.

Trump’s MAGA loyalists are reflexively praising Trump’s interview performance.

Eric Daugherty, chief content creator for the right-wing Florida’s Voice News, tweeted, “BREAKING: The White House just released the FULL uncut version of President Trump’s 60 Minutes interview - which CBS SPLICED into just 27 minutes. The original is 73 minutes long. 47 held up VERY well through the entire thing. The fake news doesn’t stand a chance!”

But other X users were highly critical of Trump’s responses to O’Donnell’s questions.

The Bulwark’s Tim Miller — a Never Trump conservative, former GOP strategist and frequent guest on MSNBC — tweeted, “60 Minutes did not air the part where Trump discusses his success in extorting the network and calls them Fake News. This edit is harmful to me and I’m considering suing.”

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-New York) sarcastically wrote, “Maybe I should file a complaint with the FCC against the Trump White House for editing his unhinged 60 Minutes interview. It will use the exact same language Trump lodged against Vice President Harris.”

Max Flugrath, communications director for Fair Fight Action, posted, “So, Trump knows who Bari Weiss is, but not Changpeng Zhao, whose company helped the Trump family make $550 million and he just pardoned less than 2 weeks ago?”

Liberal Gen-Z influencer Harry Sisson commented, “This Trump 60 Minutes interview has been a total disaster. He can’t answer questions on his blatant corruption, he sounds clueless on foreign policy, and he continues to defend his tariffs which are hurting Americans. Trump is a complete failure.”

Sisson, in a separate tweet, remarked, “This is Trump on 60 Minutes right now. He looks absolutely awful. He’s incoherent, rambling, and looks like he’s dripping in sweat. This man is not well!”

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth Behind Trump’s “60 Minutes” Spectacle

In a world where Donald Trump’s relationship with the media oscillates between fervent love and outright disdain, his latest rendezvous with CBS’s “60 Minutes” has once again thrown the spotlight onto this tumultuous affair. The reactions, predictably, vary with the political wind, but let’s dissect the facts and the fallacies with the precision of a scalpel on this media spectacle.

The MAGA Echo Chamber

Eric Daugherty of Florida’s Voice News, a right-wing trumpet, heralds the release of the “FULL uncut version” of Trump’s interview as if unveiling a holy relic to his followers. This assertion, cloaked in the language of transparency, conveniently glosses over the essential function of journalism – to edit for clarity, brevity, and relevance. It’s not the length of the interview that matters, but the substance within it. To claim victory over “fake news” by virtue of endurance in an interview is akin to claiming mastery of the sea for simply not drowning.

The Critical Voices

Then, there are voices like Tim Miller and Max Flugrath who point out the glaring omissions and the baffling ignorance displayed by Trump in the interview. Miller’s comments on the editing choices raise a valid point about the responsibility of news media to present a comprehensive view of their subjects. However, the crux of the matter lies in Trump’s apparent lack of awareness or outright denial of significant elements of his presidency and personal dealings, such as the transaction with Changpeng Zhao’s company. Ignoring such aspects doesn’t erase them; it only magnifies the questions.

The Liberal Lens

Liberal critiques, like those from Harry Sisson, paint a picture of Trump as not just politically inept but physically unwell. While personal attacks on appearance are low-hanging fruit in political discourse, the observations on policy and governance stand on firmer ground. Trump’s handling of tariffs and foreign policy, as highlighted in the interview, underscores a broader critique of his administration’s approach: often more focused on spectacle than substance.

The FCC Complaint Farce

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer’s sarcastic suggestion of filing a complaint with the FCC against the Trump White House for editing the interview is a masterstroke in highlighting the absurdity of Trump’s own frequent threats and complaints against his critics. This maneuver underscores a fundamental truth: in the realm of politics, especially in Trump’s America, the line between serious governance and petty grievances has become perilously thin.

Conclusion

What we have, then, is not just a “disaster” of an interview, as some would claim, but a microcosm of the Trump presidency itself. It’s a blend of bravado and bluster, with a hefty dose of misinformation and evasion. The reactions on X (formerly Twitter) are a testament to the polarized lens through which America views its leaders. Yet, amid the cacophony of digital voices, the truth, as always, lies not in the length of one’s screentime but in the weight of their words and actions.

In this instance, Trump’s “60 Minutes” appearance is less about illuminating the man behind the office and more about reinforcing the narratives we’ve come to associate with his brand of politics: divisive, deflective, and ever-dramatic. The real question isn’t about how well Trump held up under scrutiny but rather, how well our political discourse can recover from the erosion of truth and civility that spectacles like these exacerbate.
