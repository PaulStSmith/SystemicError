'Needs to be immediately fired': DHS lawyer's wild online behavior stuns legal expert

When DHS Turns Its Accounts Against Judges

The Target Is the Court

The source story is simple enough: legal commentator Michael Popok says DHS general counsel James Percival has been using official, taxpayer-funded social media accounts to attack federal judges by name, including Judge Paula Xinis after her ruling in the Kilmar Abrego Garcia matter. That is the surface. The real story is what it reveals about how power is being used.

This Is Not Noise

Percival is not some random online agitator with a keyboard and a grievance. He is a top lawyer at the Department of Homeland Security, operating under the authority of the federal government. When an official in that position labels judges “radical” or “criminal” from official accounts, the message is not expressive venting. It is institutional intimidation dressed up as commentary.

The Chain Runs Upward

Popok is right to point higher. He says Percival is taking cues from Todd Blanche, the acting attorney general, who was reportedly heard talking about “a war against the federal judiciary.” That framing matters. If the executive branch is casting the courts as enemies, then attacks like Percival’s are not accidents or personal excess. They are what happens when the people at the top normalize hostility toward judicial independence and hand lower-level loyalists permission to act on it.

Safety Is the Point

Popok also notes that judges are increasingly worried about their personal safety, and that Justices Amy Coney Barrett and Elena Kagan have both testified to those fears. That is not a side note. It is the consequence. Once officials start naming judges as enemies from government platforms, the purpose is not argument. It is pressure. The judiciary cannot do its job if judges have reason to fear that rulings will trigger targeted harassment from the executive branch.

The Real Misdirection

The weak framing here would be to treat this as a professionalism problem or a social-media lapse. It is neither. The damage is structural: official power is being used to delegitimize the branch that is supposed to check it. And the personnel story matters too. Popok says Percival is a right-wing radical who rose to this post after working for former Florida Attorney General Ashley Moody. That is not an isolated résumé detail. It is the pipeline: partisan enforcers, elevated into federal legal authority, then turned loose on the courts.

The Pattern

This is how democratic institutions get softened up before they are openly defied: first the judges are smeared, then the smears are laundered through official channels, then the public is told the real problem is the courts themselves. The weapon is not confusion. It is administrative power used to create a climate where judicial independence looks optional and executive resentment looks normal. That is the pattern.
