Belgium fires back at Trump after defeating the US in the FIFA World Cup

Based on an article by María Teresita Armstrong-Matta

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20262 min read

Trump Didn’t Save the Team. He Hijacked the Match.

The Real Power Was in the Compliance

The source story is simple enough: Donald Trump pressured FIFA officials to overturn Folarin Balogun’s one-game suspension, FIFA did it, Balogun played, and the U.S. still lost 4-1 to Belgium and was eliminated. That is the whole machine in miniature. Trump did not need to win the match to claim power. He only needed the institution to bend.

FIFA Made the Decision

The important actor here is not the player, and not even Trump alone. FIFA held the authority to keep the suspension in place or refuse the pressure. It chose to yield. That is the institutional fact worth sitting with. When a global sports body reverses itself after presidential pressure, the scandal is not just political meddling. It is the body’s willingness to treat political interference as a condition of business.

The Point Was Never Sport

Trump’s intervention did not produce a better result for the United States. It produced a spectacle. The team still got blown out, and the president’s fingerprints now sit on the embarrassment. The Belgian Red Devils understood the assignment immediately when they posted “Overturn this.” They turned the whole episode back onto Trump because the story was never really about Balogun’s suspension. It was about a politician trying to convert a sports dispute into a display of authority.

Misdirection as a Governing Style

This is how damage gets laundered. A presidential intervention becomes “help.” A controversial suspension becomes a national rescue mission. A humiliating loss then gets narrated as if the problem were the team, or the player, or some random sports controversy, instead of the decision to inject presidential ego into an institution that was functioning without him. That is misdirection, plain and simple: deliberate interference recast as concern.

The Laughingstock Was Engineered

The source notes that the World Cup had been a U.S. success story until Trump got involved. That matters. The humiliation did not come from some unavoidable accident of sport. It came from a president who could not resist sticking his nose into a functioning event and a governing body that let him. The result was predictable: the match became about him, and the world got a fresh reminder that American institutions are often strongest only until power decides to cosplay as usefulness.

The Larger Pattern

This is the broader political lesson: authoritarian politics does not always announce itself with laws and uniforms. Sometimes it arrives as meddling, vanity, and institutional surrender. A powerful man inserts himself, a weak institution adjusts, and the public is left to absorb the embarrassment while the people with actual authority pretend they were just responding to circumstances. That is not confusion. It is governance by intrusion.
