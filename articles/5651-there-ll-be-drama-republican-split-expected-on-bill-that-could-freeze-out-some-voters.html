'There'll be drama': Republican split expected on bill that could freeze out some voters

A bill set to be presented to the House of Representatives could freeze out military members stationed overseas from voting in elections.

Exceptions in the bill do address these concerns, according to co-author Chip Roy (R-Texas), but proponents are calling for some tighter definitions when it comes to defining who can and cannot vote.

Roy believes this will not be an issue and that “true absentee ballots” would be allowed, and said he is keen to get the bill in front of Donald Trump to have it signed into law.

He told Politico, “They get on the Senate floor, they can call the question, if there are people willing to speak … there’ll be drama, and then we’ll see what happens. We’ll see who wins, but that’s what we’re supposed to do.”

It could be a bill the president approves of quickly, with Trump airing his dislike for mail-in ballots in the past. He once called mail-in ballots “corrupt” and in recent days has called on an effort for the Republican Party to “nationalize” voting in 15 unnamed states.

He said, “The Republicans should say, ‘We want to take over. We should take over the voting, the voting in at least many — 15 places. The Republicans ought to nationalize the voting.”

Trump’s call for a political party to control the election cycle comes not long after administration officials made moves to exert more control on the process.

He added, “If Republicans don’t get them out, you will never win another election as a Republican. It’s crazy how you can get these people to vote. If we don’t get them out, look, Republicans will never win another election.”

Voter ID has also been a target of Trump’s rhetoric, with the president saying he “ought to pass” the SAVE America Act.

He said, “Our elections are crooked as hell, and you can win — not only win elections over that and not only win future elections, but you’ll win every debate because the public is really angry about it.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Chill on Military Votes: A Dive into the Proposed Bill and Trump’s Election Maneuvers

In the latest episode of political theater, a bill teetering on the edge of controversy is set to be showcased to the House of Representatives, potentially freezing out military members stationed overseas from casting their votes in elections. The co-author of this contentious piece of legislation, Chip Roy (R-Texas), asserts that exceptions within the bill adequately address concerns regarding disenfranchisement. However, critics argue for a need for “tighter definitions” to delineate clearly who is eligible to vote, spotlighting the ambiguity and potential for exclusion inherent in the bill’s current language.

Roy’s cavalier attitude towards the bill’s trajectory and its reception in both houses—envisioning “drama” on the Senate floor and reducing the democratic process to a spectacle of victory and defeat—is both alarming and telling. It’s a stark reminder of the current political climate where the integrity of the electoral process is often gambled away in power plays.

Enter Donald Trump, the former President, whose disdain for mail-in ballots is well-documented and unabashed. His endorsement of the bill comes as no surprise, given his history of casting doubt on the legitimacy of mail-in voting, labeling it “corrupt” without substantiation. Trump’s recent rallying cry for the Republican Party to “nationalize” voting in 15 unnamed states is nothing short of an authoritarian overture. This call to action is not about securing the electoral process but about seizing control, underlining a dangerous pivot towards undermining democratic norms.

Trump’s fixation on voter ID laws and the propagation of the narrative that elections are “crooked” serves to further erode trust in American democracy. His support of the SAVE America Act, under the guise of rectifying so-called electoral corruption, is a thinly veiled attempt to disenfranchise voters likely to oppose him. The rhetoric employed by Trump is not only divisive but also strategically designed to rally his base around the false narrative of a stolen electoral process.

The Implications: Beyond the Bill

The ramifications of this bill, coupled with Trump’s broader electoral strategy, extend far beyond the immediate legislative outcomes. By targeting military members’ ability to vote—individuals who sacrifice immensely for the nation—the bill sends a chilling message about who is considered a valuable participant in our democracy. The broader agenda to nationalize voting, under Republican oversight, poses a grave threat to the principles of free and fair elections, laying the groundwork for unprecedented levels of partisanship in the management of electoral processes.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The discourse surrounding this bill and Trump’s electoral maneuvers is emblematic of a larger battle for the soul of American democracy. It underscores the necessity for vigilance and advocacy for electoral integrity, transparency, and inclusivity. As the narrative unfolds, it is incumbent upon us, the electorate, to critically evaluate the motivations behind such legislative efforts and their potential impact on the democratic process. The fight for a truly representative democracy is far from over, and it is a fight that requires the engagement and resistance of all who value the principles upon which the United States was founded.
