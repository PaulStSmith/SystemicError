Trump 'clearly flailing' as Epstein crisis refuses to go away: MSNBC

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Based upon a press availability on Tuesday where Donald Trump expressed dismay at more questions about his administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files, The Atlantic contributor Jonathan Lemire suggested the president has a legitimate crisis on his hands.

From his perch as a co-host on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," the longtime Capitol Hill reporter raised an eyebrow at Trump's inability to control members of his own party who want the files related to the accused pedophile released.

Noting a memo from the Department of Justice that claimed there was nothing in the files worth releasing to the public that created the MAGA firestorm that continues to grow, columnist David French told the panel, "This is a wild moment. I knew it would be a problem when I saw that memo. I knew it would be a problem for him. I did not see how much of a problem it would be, and how hard it would be for him to tamp it down."

"Very fascinating, frankly, to watch Donald Trump, the president of the United States grapple with this, a guy who was an acquaintance of Jeffrey Epstein, who once called him a terrific guy, who acknowledged, jokingly, his affinity for very young women," offered co-host Willie Geist. "Trump talking about Jeffrey Epstein frankly just looks uncomfortable when we see him there yesterday talking about this."

"And clearly flailing," Lemire interjected. "I mean, yesterday on the tarmac there at Joint Base Andrews, talking about how boring the story is, he wouldn't understand why anyone would talk about it. Mind you, it was just two days prior that, as David mentioned, Donald Trump's defense was that it was a conspiracy created by Barack Obama."

"I don't take that at face value," he added. "That's not boring if you're saying the former president of United States created this, and it just shows that they have not landed on a good defense, as you noted."

Our Analysis:

The Tangled Webs of Denial: Trump, Epstein, and the Unraveling MAGA Saga

In an era where the absurd has become the norm, the latest chapter in the Donald Trump saga unfolds with a predictably chaotic narrative. The former president, no stranger to controversy, has found himself ensnared in a fresh debacle concerning his administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files. According to Jonathan Lemire of The Atlantic, Trump's dismay over continued questioning on this topic signals a legitimate crisis. This is not just another fleeting news cycle; this is indicative of a deeper, systemic rot that threatens to undermine what's left of the MAGA movement's credibility.

The Uncomfortable Spotlight

Trump, a figure who once thrived under the glare of media scrutiny, now appears visibly discomforted by the resurgence of the Epstein saga. His description of Epstein as a "terrific guy" and his joking acknowledgment of Epstein's preference for very young women are not just troubling remarks but are emblematic of a disturbing disregard for the gravity of Epstein's crimes. The fact that Trump is now floundering in his attempts to dismiss the relevance of the Epstein files is telling. His defense, absurdly pointing fingers at Barack Obama, is not just a feeble attempt to deflect blame but a glaring testament to his inability to confront the issue with any semblance of accountability or introspection.

A Crisis of Their Own Making

The Department of Justice's memo, which claimed the Epstein files contained nothing of public interest, has only added fuel to the fire. Rather than quelling the controversy, it has sparked a MAGA firestorm, revealing deep fractures within the party Trump once claimed to dominate unequivocally. The mounting demand from members of his own party for the release of the Epstein files is not just a rebellion; it's a clear sign of the eroding trust and influence Trump has within the ranks.

David French's commentary on the situation underscores the severity of the crisis. The realization that the memo would be problematic did not fully capture the extent to which it would exacerbate Trump's predicament. It's not merely a problem; it's a quagmire of Trump's own making, a wild moment that underscores the chaotic and untenable nature of his leadership.

The Lack of a Coherent Defense

The absence of a coherent defense strategy is perhaps the most damning aspect of this entire fiasco. Trump's attempt to label the story as "boring" and unworthy of discussion is a transparent effort to diminish its significance. Yet, this tactic falls flat in the face of serious allegations and unanswered questions. It's not boring; it's deeply concerning. The attempt to blame Obama is not just a diversion but a laughable example of Trump's penchant for conspiracy theories as a means of evasion.

The Verdict

As we peel back the layers of this sordid affair, what becomes increasingly clear is the inherent weakness of Trump's position. His inability to control the narrative, to offer a believable defense, or even to maintain unity within his own party signals a broader crisis. This is not just about Epstein; it's about a pattern of behavior, a culture of impunity, and a leadership style that thrives on chaos and confusion.

In a just world, the Epstein saga would be the final nail in the coffin of Trump's political ambitions. However, given the Teflon nature of his career so far, one must wonder what, if anything, will truly hold him accountable. What remains clear, however, is that Trump's handling of the Epstein files is not just a personal crisis but a symptom of a larger, systemic failure. A failure that speaks volumes about the moral bankruptcy at the heart of the MAGA movement.
