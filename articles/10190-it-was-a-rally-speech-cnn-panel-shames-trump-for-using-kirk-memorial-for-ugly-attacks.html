'It was a rally speech': CNN panel shames Trump for using Kirk memorial for ugly attacks

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Exploitation of Tragedy: Trump's Memorial Rant

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a memorial service for slain conservative commentator Charlie Kirk at State Farm Stadium in Glendale, Arizona, U.S., September 21, 2025. REUTERS/Caitlin O’Hara

President Donald Trump’s speech Sunday at the memorial service for Charlie Kirk quickly devolved into familiar remarks about targeting his political opponents — remarks that were promptly ridiculed Monday by a panel on CNN.

As the last speaker at the event, which was reportedly attended by around 200,000 people, Trump paid tribute to Kirk as the “greatest evangelist for American liberty,” but also used his speech as an opportunity to announce that his Justice Department was investigating “networks of radical left maniacs,” and declared that he hated his political opponents.

CNN’s Audie Cornish said she was “a little surprised” to hear such comments during what was a memorial service and celebration of life, to which senior CNN reporter Edward-Isaac Dovere said he wasn’t.

“I mean, no, because Donald Trump gives the same speech no matter what venue he's in, even at what was supposed to be a funeral or celebration of Charlie Kirk's life. It was a rally speech,” Dovere said.

“When we see the things that are being done in the name of Charlie Kirk's killing and responding to it, many of them are things that the president and top aides around him have been talking about doing for a long time. Trump, here, is using Kirk's assassination as a pretext for pursuing more political investigations.”

Kirk was shot and killed earlier this month while speaking at a Turning Point USA event at Utah Valley University, an attack that drew bi-partisan condemnation. Critics, however,saythe Trump administration has seized on the assassination to target political adversaries, with Trump immediately blaming the “radical left” — despite evidence to the contrary — and vowing investigations into organizations he claims helped create the conditions for the killing.

Our Analysis:

The Exploitation of Tragedy: Trump's Memorial Rant

In what can only be described as a crass exploitation of a solemn occasion, President Donald Trump transformed a memorial service into a political soapbox. The service, intended to honor Charlie Kirk, deviated into a familiar tirade against Trump's political adversaries, sparking a mixture of ridicule and dismay. The event, attended by an estimated 200,000 individuals, should have been a moment of reflection and unity. Instead, it became yet another platform for Trump’s relentless pursuit of personal vendettas.

Kirk, hailed by Trump as the “greatest evangelist for American liberty,” met a tragic end, assassinated while speaking at a Turning Point USA event. This moment of national grief, however, was hijacked by Trump, who saw it not as an opportunity to unify or heal but as a chance to further his own political narrative. His declaration of investigations into “networks of radical left maniacs” and his outright expression of hatred towards his political opponents was not only shockingly inappropriate for a memorial service but underscored his persistent use of divisive rhetoric at times when leadership demands unity.

CNN’s response, particularly from Audie Cornish expressing surprise, captures the sentiment of many. Yet, as senior reporter Edward-Isaac Dovere pointed out, this behavior is par for the course for Trump. It's a sad testament to how predictable Trump's diversion of any event into a self-serving rally has become. This isn't about the tragic loss of a life or the mourning of a community; it's about Trump, yet again.

Critics argue that Trump's immediate leap to blaming the “radical left” for Kirk's assassination, without evidence, and his vow to launch investigations into organizations he claims are responsible, is not only irresponsible but dangerous. It's a clear demonstration of how Trump continues to manipulate events, even those as tragic as Kirk's assassination, to stoke division and rally his base against an often unspecified and nebulous enemy. This isn’t leadership; it’s opportunism at its most cynical.

The real tragedy, beyond the appalling loss of Charlie Kirk, is the continued erosion of political discourse in the United States. When a president uses a memorial service as a platform for political attacks, it reflects a profound disrespect not only to the deceased and their family but to the nation as a whole. It’s a stark reminder of how far the standards of public conduct have fallen.

In essence, Trump’s speech at the memorial service was not about honoring Charlie Kirk or addressing the collective grief of a community. It was a calculated move to exploit a tragedy for political gain. This act of turning a memorial into a political rally not only disrespects Kirk’s memory but also the office of the Presidency itself. It’s a somber reflection of the current state of American politics, where even the most sacred moments are not spared from the clutches of opportunistic exploitation.

The question that looms large is: where do we draw the line? If the sanctity of a memorial service can be so brazenly disregarded, what remains off-limits? It’s a chilling precedent, one that further divides rather than unites, and it’s a stark reminder of the systemic error at the heart of our current political discourse.
