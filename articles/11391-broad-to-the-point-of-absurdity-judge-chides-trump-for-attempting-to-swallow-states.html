'Broad to the point of absurdity': Judge chides Trump for attempting to 'swallow' states

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Federal Overreach: A Broad Brush with Legal Absurdity

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy (not pictured) and European leaders amid negotiations to end the Russian war in Ukraine, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 18, 2025. REUTERS/Al Drago

A federal judge slammed the Trump administration on Tuesday for attempting to apply its immigration powers too broadly.

Judge Sharon Johnson Coleman, in the Northern District of Illinois, was asked to determine whether federal immigration laws supersede Illinois' state labor laws. The Trump administration argued that federal law ought to supersede the Illinois Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act, which would allow it to continue conducting immigration raids at workplaces in the state.

Coleman noted that the Trump administration's lawyers relied on a reading of the federal Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA) that she found was too broad.

"Because the provisions of the Privacy Act challenged by the federal government are not expressly preempted by IRCA and do not intrude upon the federal government’s constitutional powers in the space of immigration and foreign affairs, and because the federal government’s broad interpretation of its power to regulate matters of immigration wouldswallow the historic powers of the states over employment-related issues, the Court grants the State’smotion to dismiss and denies the federal government’s motion for a preliminary injunction," Coleman wrote in her opinion.

Coleman also accused Trump administration lawyers of asking the court to ignore the context of cases the government cited as precedent for their argument.

"This interpretation of IRCA’s preemption clause is broad to the point of absurdity," Coleman's opinion reads in part.

Our Analysis:

Federal Overreach: A Broad Brush with Legal Absurdity

In a striking rebuff to the Trump administration's attempts at broadening its immigration enforcement reach, Judge Sharon Johnson Coleman of the Northern District of Illinois delivered a legal smackdown that resonates far beyond the courtroom’s walls. The case at hand centered on whether federal immigration laws trump Illinois' state labor laws, specifically the Illinois Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act. The Trump administration, in its typical heavy-handed fashion, argued for federal supremacy to facilitate its aggressive immigration raids. However, Judge Coleman's decision not only halted these ambitions in their tracks but also served as a pointed critique of the administration's overzealous interpretation of federal power.

The Meat of the Matter: Federal vs. State

At the heart of this legal showdown was the Trump administration's assertion that the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA) should override Illinois' protections against workplace raids. Such an argument, as per Judge Coleman's analysis, stretched the bounds of IRCA to a breaking point. It's a classic example of federal overreach, where the administration sought to dilute state sovereignty in favor of an expansive, and arguably unconstitutional, exertion of immigration control.

Coleman's Counter: A Lesson in Judicial Restraint

Judge Coleman's ruling is a masterclass in judicial restraint and constitutional respect. She pointed out that the Trump administration's lawyers were not just overreaching but attempting to rewrite the rulebook entirely. Their interpretation of IRCA was, to use Coleman's own words, "broad to the point of absurdity." This wasn't just a legal disagreement; it was a fundamental misreading of federal powers.

The Real Loser: State Sovereignty

What's particularly galling about the administration's stance is its blatant disregard for the historic powers of states over employment-related issues. Coleman's decision underscores a critical facet of American federalism: states are not mere administrative districts of the federal government but sovereign entities with their own rights, especially when it comes to regulating employment within their borders. The administration's attempt to bulldoze Illinois' workplace privacy protections was not only misguided but a direct threat to the principle of state sovereignty.

A Wider Implication: The Shadow of Overreach

This case is more than a mere legal spat; it's emblematic of the Trump administration's broader strategy of using immigration policy as a sledgehammer. By attempting to usurp state powers through a warped interpretation of federal law, the administration showed a troubling willingness to flout constitutional norms for political ends. Judge Coleman's rebuke, therefore, is not just a victory for Illinois but a beacon for states nationwide defending their rights against federal encroachment.

Conclusion: A Victory for Reason

In the end, Judge Coleman's decision is a powerful affirmation of legal sanity in an era increasingly characterized by executive overreach. The ruling not only protects Illinois' right to safeguard its workers from invasive immigration raids but also sends a clear message to the federal government: there are limits to your power, and they begin where state sovereignty and common sense prevail. In a political landscape rife with "broad to the point of absurdity" interpretations of law, Coleman's verdict stands out as a reminder that, sometimes, the law is indeed on the side of reason.
