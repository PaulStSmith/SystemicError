Golfers take swing at Trump over ‘unlawful assault’ on public golf course

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 15, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Golf Course Gambit: Privilege Over Public Space

U.S. President Donald Trump holds scissors to cut the ribbon during the opening ceremony for Trump International Golf Links near Aberdeen, Scotland, Britain July 29, 2025. Alastair Grant/Pool via REUTERS

Two Washington-area golfers have launched a legal challenge against the Trump administration over its efforts to transform a public golf course into what one described as “yet another private playground for the privileged and powerful,” The Washington Post reported Saturday.

In December, the Trump administration terminated the lease of a nonprofit organization that managed Washington, D.C.’s public golf courses, including the well-known East Potomac Golf Links. Since then, President Donald Trump has suggested that plans are underway to transform the course into a “beautiful, world-class, U.S. Open-caliber course,” the Post reported.

However, the two aforementioned Washington-area golfers, Dave Roberts and Alex Dickson, have since filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration to halt those efforts, and were joined by several organizations in their legal challenge, including the DC Preservation League, Democracy Defenders Fund, and the Democracy Forward Foundation.

“East Potomac Golf Links is a testament to what’s possible with public land and why public spaces matter,” Roberts said, according to the Post. “It deserves better than becoming a dumping ground for waste and yet another private playground for the privileged and powerful.”

The Post reached out to the Trump administration for response, and was told that Trump was, in fact, hoping to “redevelop” Washington, D.C.’s public golf courses – which White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers called “decrepit” – in order to restore “glamour and prestige.”

Skye Perryman, president and chief executive of Democracy Forward, accused the Trump administration of waging an “unlawful assault” on Washington, D.C.’s public spaces, and vowed to fight alongside the two golfers in their legal challenge against Trump.

“We are acting to save this priceless part of our national park system from being another casualty of a reckless administration,” Perryman told the Post. “We are honored for the partnership of our plaintiffs in fighting back against this unlawful assault on our cherished public space.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Golf Course Gambit: Privilege Over Public Space

The recent legal challenge mounted by two Washington-area golfers against the Trump administration’s plans to overhaul a public golf course into a “world-class” private venue encapsulates a broader narrative of privileging the elite at the expense of public goods. According to The Washington Post, the administration’s move to terminate the lease of a nonprofit organization managing Washington, D.C.’s public golf courses, including the notable East Potomac Golf Links, marks a stark pivot towards commodifying communal spaces for the benefit of the few.

The Privileged Playground

The transformation of East Potomac Golf Links into a “beautiful, world-class, U.S. Open-caliber course,” as President Donald Trump envisions, is emblematic of a troubling trend: the encroachment of private interests on public lands. This project, as Dave Roberts, one of the plaintiffs in the lawsuit against the Trump administration, poignantly puts it, threatens to turn a public asset into “yet another private playground for the privileged and powerful.” This sentiment is not just a lament for a lost golf course; it is a rallying cry against the systemic dismantling of public spaces.

Decrepit or Demolished?

The Trump administration defends its actions by labeling the current state of Washington, D.C.’s public golf courses as “decrepit,” a term that conveniently obscures the value these spaces offer to the broader community. White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers’ assertion that the redevelopment would restore “glamour and prestige” blatantly ignores the intrinsic value of accessible recreational spaces. It’s an argument steeped in elitism, suggesting that only through privatization and exclusivity can true value be realized.

Legal Battle as a Last Resort

The lawsuit filed by Roberts, Alex Dickson, and supported by organizations like the DC Preservation League, Democracy Defenders Fund, and the Democracy Forward Foundation, represents a critical stand against the Trump administration’s “unlawful assault” on public land. Skye Perryman, president and chief executive of Democracy Forward, frames the legal challenge as a defense of the national park system against a “reckless administration” determined to prioritize profit over people.

This legal confrontation is not merely about preserving a golf course; it’s a battle for the soul of public spaces across America. As the Trump administration seeks to remake a public good into a private luxury, the lawsuit serves as a reminder of the ongoing struggle against the commodification of communal assets.

Conclusion: A Battle Beyond the Links

The fight to save East Potomac Golf Links is emblematic of a larger war over the values that will shape American society. Will we stand by as public assets are stripped for parts and sold to the highest bidder, or will we assert the importance of preserving spaces that serve all, not just the well-heeled few?

The Trump administration’s plans for the golf course are not just about sports or recreation; they are a microcosm of a broader ideological battle over access, equity, and the very definition of public space in America. As this legal challenge unfolds, it serves as a poignant reminder of what is at stake when the public good is viewed through the lens of private gain.
