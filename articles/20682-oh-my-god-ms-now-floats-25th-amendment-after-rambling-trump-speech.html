'Oh my God': MS NOW floats 25th Amendment after 'rambling' Trump speech

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

The Point Is Not the Gaffe. It Is the Office.

A Presidency Protected by Its Surroundings

The source story is built from a handful of Trump clips at the NATO summit in Ankara: name fumbles, country confusion, meandering tangents, and a cable-news chorus wondering aloud about the 25th Amendment. That is the surface. The real story is that Trump still occupies the most powerful office in the world while the people around him, who are actually empowered to intervene, remain spectators.

The 25th Amendment Is Not a Talk-Show Prop

Joe Scarborough’s suggestion that the Cabinet should remove Trump may be legally serious, but in this context it also exposes a brutal institutional fact: if a president is truly unfit, the safeguard depends on the very appointees and loyalists he selected. That is not a neutral design flaw. It is a political veto embedded inside the executive branch, where obedience often outranks constitutional duty.

So the question is not whether a pundit can say “25th Amendment” on television. The question is why the people with formal authority to use it are never the ones being forced to answer for their inaction.

Symmetry Is the Scam

The segment leans on a familiar cable-news move: if Trump is unraveling, then Biden’s verbal miscues must be dragged in as balancing material. Brzezinski and Geist both reach for that comparison, as if the existence of one aging Democrat somehow sterilizes the danger of an erratic Republican president.

That framing is lazy and politically useful to the wrong people. Biden’s public slips were used to pressure him out of a reelection run. Trump’s apparent confusion, by contrast, is treated as content, an object for panel debate rather than a governing crisis. One party’s weakness became internal discipline. The other’s becomes a spectacle that his allies and the media can metabolize without consequence.

The Media Loves the Clip More Than the Consequence

The source article is less interested in power than in performance. It turns presidential instability into a montage and then into a comparison game. That is how the press often launders danger: by converting institutional failure into an even-handed personality puzzle.

But a stumble from a sitting president is not just a meme. In a system already shaped by loyalty tests, propaganda, and cowardice, the more important fact is not that Trump mangled Zelenskyy or Japan and Iran in the same breath. It is that the apparatus around him is built to absorb embarrassment without forcing accountability.

What This Story Actually Reveals

The deeper pattern is not senility, and it is not cable-news hypocrisy alone. It is the normalization of executive decay inside a political order that keeps rewarding the people who let it happen. The Cabinet’s silence, the media’s symmetry trap, and the public’s forced consumption of every incoherent remark all serve the same function: they keep attention on the symptom while leaving the structure intact.

If Trump is unfit, the failure is not his alone. It belongs to the officials who enable him, the commentators who flatten the stakes, and the institutions that keep treating democratic dysfunction as just another panel segment.
