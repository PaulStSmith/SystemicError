'That might be your way to try to twist my words': Trump EPA head snaps at CNN host

Kasie Hunt, Lee Zeldin (CNN screenshot)

Donald Trump's head of the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) got snippy with CNN's Kasie Hunt on Sunday morning when she tried to get a clarification from him.

During his appearance on "State of the Union," Lee Zeldin, a former GOP lawmaker who failed in his bid to become governor of New York, was pressed by Hunt who asked him about the policy changes he is making since taking over.

"The Supreme Court ruled in overturning the Chevron doctrine in West Virginia versus EPA, Michigan versus EPA, that agencies like the EPA can't just use vague language in statute and try to make it be whatever we want it to be," he told the host. "The major policy doctrine also says that when you're going to reach something like an endangerment finding and then have trillions of dollars of regulation, that's something that should be decided by our elected members of Congress and passing statute."

Saying previous officials made "a lot of mental leaps," he added, "They say carbon dioxide when mixed with a whole bunch of other, well, mixed gases, in some cases not even admitted for mobile sources, they say that that contributes to global climate change, Doesn't say causes, contributes how much? They don't say, but it's north of zero –– not much more than zero. "

"So you're sounding pretty skeptical of this overall scientific consensus that these greenhouse gas emissions are the overwhelming man-made climate change driver," Hunt asked.

"That might be your way to try to twist my words," the Republican fired back. "But what I'm saying is that we get our power at EPA from what the law states and the Supreme Court in recent cases have been very clear."

"So what I was just describing, with all the mental leaps used in the 2009 endangerment finding, Section 202 of the Clean Air Act, doesn't allow all of these different mental leaps as the Supreme Court made clear in recent years," Zeldin insisted. "So I'm not going to get creative with the law. We're going to read the plain language and if Section 202 of the Clean Air Act gets amended by Congress, then we'll follow that new law."

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Obfuscation: Lee Zeldin's EPA Dodge

In a recent tête-à-tête on CNN's "State of the Union," Lee Zeldin, the new head of the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under Donald Trump, performed what can only be described as a verbal ballet, dodging direct questions with the grace of a politician well-versed in the art of obfuscation. Kasie Hunt, in her pursuit of clarity, found herself up against a wall of legalese and evasion.

The Art of Dodging

Zeldin's strategy was clear from the start: cloak his responses in the dense fog of Supreme Court rulings and statutory interpretation. He cites the overturning of the Chevron doctrine and decisions in West Virginia versus EPA and Michigan versus EPA as the backbone of his argument that the EPA's hands are tied by the literal interpretation of the law. This is a convenient shield, allowing him to deflect any pressure to acknowledge the overwhelming scientific consensus on climate change and its primary drivers.

Skepticism Masked as Legalism

When pressed on the issue of greenhouse gas emissions and their link to climate change, Zeldin's response is a masterclass in skepticism dressed as legal fidelity. He challenges the previous administration's "mental leaps" in linking carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases to climate change, trivializing their impact as "north of zero –– not much more than zero." This is a bold claim, flying in the face of established scientific consensus that these emissions are a significant driver of climate change.

The Twisting of Words

Zeldin's accusation that Hunt is trying to "twist my words" is a classic deflection tactic. By framing his stance as a strict adherence to the law, as interpreted by the Supreme Court, he sidesteps the fundamental issue at hand: the role of the EPA in combating climate change. His insistence on waiting for Congress to amend the Clean Air Act is a call to inaction, a bureaucratic shrug in the face of a global crisis.

Conclusion: A Performance Worthy of Scrutiny

Lee Zeldin's appearance on "State of the Union" was less about clarifying the EPA's stance on climate change and more about defending a do-nothing approach under the guise of legalism. His performance was a calculated attempt to sidestep the urgent need for environmental action, wrapped in the veneer of statutory obedience. It's a troubling signal of the direction the EPA is taking under his leadership, one that prioritizes legal technicalities over the looming threat of climate change.

This evasion, this deliberate choice to ignore the weight of scientific evidence, is not just a failure of policy; it's a failure of moral and global responsibility. As the head of the EPA, Zeldin has a duty not just to the letter of the law but to the planet and its inhabitants. His responses on "State of the Union" reveal a preference for legal gymnastics over environmental stewardship, a stance that we must challenge and scrutinize at every turn.
