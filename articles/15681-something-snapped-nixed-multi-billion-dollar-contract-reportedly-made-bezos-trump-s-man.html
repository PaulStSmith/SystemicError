'Something snapped': Nixed multi-billion dollar contract reportedly made Bezos Trump's man

Jeff Bezos, Amazon founder and owner of The Washington Post, has perplexed D.C. insiders with his dramatic transformation from civic-minded entrepreneur to MAGA faithful, according to a new piece in Politico.

Bezos was famously pictured alongside fellow tech billionaires Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk at Trump's second-term inauguration, cementing what Politico columnist Michael Schaffer wrote was an image that looked "more self-serving than selfless."

"The man who once praised democracy’s guardrails has begun to look like he’s just another rich guy trying to curry favor with a transactional president," Schaffer wrote.

He quoted one Washington observer who said of Bezos, “People say it’s Invasion of the Body Snatchers. Something snapped with him.”

Schaffer recounted how Bezos once refused to get involved with editorial decisions at The Post, before changing his tune completely.

"Even after Joe Biden became president and Bezos felt moved to criticize him about taxes, he did so on his personal social media, leaving the pages of the newspaper to professional journalists," Schaffer wrote, quoting a 2018 interview where Bezos maintained, “I would be humiliated to interfere. I would be so embarrassed. I would turn bright red. It would feel icky; it would feel gross.”

But all that changed when Bezos "overruled his editors to kill a long-planned endorsement of Kamala Harris and ditch the Post’s tradition of bipartisan opinions."

Schaffer traced Bezos's metamorphosis to a multibillion-dollar Pentagon contract Amazon lost to Microsoft, ostensibly because "Trump didn’t like the Post’s coverage."

"The whiplash has caused chaos at the Post, which has seen an exodus of top talent and come under sharp criticism from onetime loyalists like former editor Martin Baron, whose memoir depicted Bezos as standing steadfast in the face of very real financial pressure from the first Trump administration," Schaffer wrote, adding that Baron has since accused Bezos "of being driven by cowardice in the face of White House intimidation."

According to Schaffer, Bezos did not offer a comment for the piece.

Our Analysis:

The Bezos Betrayal: From Civic Hero to MAGA Devotee

In a bewildering turn of events that reads more like a political thriller than a reputable businessman's biography, Jeff Bezos, the Amazon founder and owner of The Washington Post, has ostensibly morphed into a MAGA enthusiast, according to Politico. This transformation is not just surprising; it's downright alarming.

A Picture Worth a Thousand Words: The sight of Bezos rubbing elbows with tech moguls Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk at Trump's second-term inauguration is not just "more self-serving than selfless," as Politico's Michael Schaffer aptly puts it—it's a stark betrayal of the democratic ideals he once vocally supported. The image reeks of opportunism, signaling a disturbing alignment with a faction known for its transactional politics and disdain for democratic norms.

Invasion of the Body Snatchers: The comparison to Invasion of the Body Snatchers couldn't be more apt. Bezos, once a paragon of civic-minded entrepreneurship, appears to have undergone a radical transformation. His previous refusal to interfere with The Washington Post's editorial decisions has given way to a blatant manipulation of its content, including the shocking overruling of his editors to kill an endorsement of Kamala Harris. This isn't just a change of heart; it's a full-blown identity crisis that undermines the journalistic integrity of one of America's most storied newspapers.

The Root of All Evil?: Schaffer traces Bezos's shift back to a multibillion-dollar Pentagon contract Amazon lost to Microsoft, allegedly because of Trump's disdain for The Washington Post's coverage. If true, this suggests that Bezos's political rebranding is nothing more than a cynical attempt to curry favor with the powers that be, sacrificing principles for profit. The implication here is clear: when faced with financial pressure and White House intimidation, Bezos chose cowardice over courage.

The Fallout: The repercussions of Bezos's actions are profound. The Post has reportedly seen an exodus of top talent and a barrage of criticism from former loyalists, including ex-editor Martin Baron. This isn't just about one man's political flip-flopping; it's about the erosion of trust in a venerable institution, the destabilization of a newsroom, and the potential chilling effect on journalistic freedom.

Conclusion: Jeff Bezos's journey from a respected entrepreneur to a MAGA adherent is more than perplexing—it's a cautionary tale of what happens when wealth and power are wielded without regard for democratic values or journalistic integrity. His transformation serves as a grim reminder that no amount of money can buy back a reputation once it's lost in the murky waters of political opportunism. The real losers here, as always, are the readers and the dedicated journalists who believed in the mission of The Washington Post. Shame on Bezos for proving that sometimes, the villains in our stories are those we least expect.
