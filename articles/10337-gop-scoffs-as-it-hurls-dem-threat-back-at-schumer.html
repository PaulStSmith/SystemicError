GOP scoffs as it hurls Dem threat back at Schumer

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20255 min read

Republicans seemed unbothered on Wednesday that Democrats have threatened to force a government shutdown, believing the GOP holds all the cards heading into the fall.

Democrats have said they oppose the current Republican stopgap funding plan, arguing that it does not include key provisions such as extending Obamacare subsidies, reversing steep Medicaid cuts, and providing inadequate negotiation over health and social program funding.

But Republicans who spoke to Raw Story didn't appear concerned.

Rep. Richard Hudson (R-NC), chairman of the National Republican Congressional Committee, told Raw Story he feels confident the GOP has more leverage.

"How are you feeling about the shutdown politics? Do you guys feel like you have a stronger hand?" Raw Story asked.

"Oh, yeah. We're not even playing games," Hudson replied. "We're just doing it clean. You really gonna shut it down?"

Hudson said it'll take a few weeks, and a funding fight could ensue in November, as Democrats will have a "leverage point" once the continuing resolution ends.

"I can't speak for what [House Speaker Mike Johnson] is going to do. I don't know," he acknowledged.

Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) told Raw Story Democrats are taking a "big risk" with trying to angle for a shutdown.

"I think it comes down to who shows the best faith in terms of negotiations," he said.

He warned Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) might be making a mistake by "waiting for an invitation" from Republicans to negotiate.

"If I'm the minority leader, I'm coming up with what I think is a good-faith offer. And put it out there," he said.

Tillis said Democrats' base should "own" a shutdown if Schumer is pressured to follow through on threats to shutter the government.

"I think this is the first time in modern political history that a shutdown — full title to the shutdown — could be conveyed to the Democrats for letting their base shut us down," he added.

Meanwhile, Rep. Ralph Norman (R-SC) gave a more blunt assessment of the possibility of a shutdown.

"That's not a debate. I'm not worried about that at all," he said.

Rep. Pat Ryan (D-NY) told Raw Story his constituents are less concerned about a shutdown, and more vocal about whether Congressional Democrats will let President Donald Trump "keep overreaching and not driving down costs."

"None of us should be talking about that term," he said.

Ryan said he and his colleagues have a choice before them.

"Do we want more of Trump's budget? More overreach on a bunch of bad errors? Or do we want to get back to affordability and lowering costs?" he said.

"My constituents don't care about the means so much as they care about the ends," Ryan added.

He said Americans are with Democrats in that they want lower costs — not military troops deployed against U.S. citizens.

"Public will matters," he said.

Our Analysis:

The Brinkmanship of Budget Battles: A GOP Confidence Game

In the latest episode of Capitol Hill's budgetary brinkmanship, Republicans are projecting a smug confidence over the threat of a government shutdown. Their demeanor, as reported by Raw Story, reveals not just a political strategy but a dangerous game of chicken with the welfare of millions of Americans hanging in the balance.

Republicans: Unfazed and Unyielding

At the heart of this impasse is the GOP's stopgap funding plan, conspicuously missing critical provisions that Democrats argue are non-negotiable: extending Obamacare subsidies, reversing Medicaid cuts, and ensuring adequate negotiation over health and social program funding. Despite these glaring omissions, Republican representatives, such as Rep. Richard Hudson (R-NC) and Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), seem to wear their indifference as a badge of honor. Hudson's cavalier attitude, asserting that the GOP is "not even playing games," encapsulates the party's dismissive stance towards genuine concerns affecting millions.

Tillis's comments, suggesting that Democrats are taking a "big risk" and should proactively make a "good-faith offer," smack of a disingenuous attempt to shift blame and responsibility. It's a classic move in the GOP playbook: create a crisis, then demand the opposition solve it under threat of national harm.

Democrats: Pressured and Positioned

The Democratic response, as articulated by Rep. Pat Ryan (D-NY), attempts to redirect the conversation towards what Americans genuinely care about: affordability and lowering costs. Ryan's assertion that his constituents are more concerned with outcomes—such as preventing President Trump's overreach and focusing on economic relief—rather than the political machinations leading to those outcomes, is telling. It underscores a fundamental difference in priorities between the two parties.

However, Ryan's stance, while noble, may underestimate the impact of a government shutdown on those very constituents. The term "shutdown" may not be on their lips, but the consequences of such an event certainly affect their lives. Democrats are in a precarious position, caught between capitulating to a flawed GOP plan and being blamed for a shutdown that could hurt the people they aim to protect.

The GOP's Shutdown Gamble: A Risky Bet

What the GOP perceives as leverage could more accurately be described as recklessness. The willingness to risk a government shutdown over a high-stakes game of political chicken reveals a troubling disregard for the well-being of the American people. The assertion by Sen. Tillis that Democrats could "own" the shutdown is a manipulation of the narrative, attempting to absolve the GOP of responsibility for a crisis they are instrumental in creating.

Moreover, the Republican strategy reflects a broader trend of governance by brinkmanship, where vital services and the livelihoods of millions are used as bargaining chips in political negotiations. This approach not only undermines the stability and functionality of government but erodes public trust in its institutions.

Conclusion: A Call for Reason and Responsibility

As this budgetary battle unfolds, it's crucial to remember the real stakes: not the political fortunes of either party, but the health, safety, and economic well-being of the American populace. The GOP's current strategy, marked by a troubling blend of confidence and indifference, threatens to deepen divisions and exacerbate hardships for countless individuals.

It's time for Republicans to abandon their high-stakes gamble and approach negotiations with the seriousness and good faith the American people deserve. Only through genuine collaboration and compromise can the looming threat of a government shutdown be averted, ensuring the government remains functional and responsive to the needs of its citizens. The GOP holds cards, indeed, but the game they're playing risks far more than they seem to realize.
