'Trump has betrayed us': Candace Owens attacks Trump as 'deep state' president

Right-wing podcaster Candace Owens said that President Donald Trump "betrayed" his MAGA supporters by becoming part of the "deep state" after refusing to release files on convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

During a Wednesday interview with conspiracy theorist Alex Jones, Owens declared that "Bibi Netanyahu is president of the United States."

"Now, with everything happening in Gaza, and Trump turning a blind eye to it, pretending that it's totally fine, it's become very clear that Bibi Netanyahu has power over Trump," she explained. "And I think the real mask-down moment was, of course, him trying to gaslight us about the Epstein files. That's ridiculous."

"So the fact that he intentionally read the room wrong and then said, 'If you're still interested in the Epstein files, I don't want you as my supporter anymore.' He tweeted that effectively. Ridiculous. Absolutely ridiculous. No one should accept that."

Owens admitted that she had initially believed that Trump "was going to be a solution to the deep state," but changed her mind because "now it seems that he's become it."

Owens also slammed the decision to transfer Epstein's accomplice, Ghislaine Maxwell, to a low-security prison.

"It's probably nicer than your house and my house, if we're being honest," Owens told Jones. "And it saddens me to say this, truly, someone that was really invested in [Trump]. I'm wondering whether or why he betrayed us, you know, what they have on him. We know that Israel has been running the most effective worldwide blackmailing."

The podcast host offered the theory that "Trump got funded somehow via Epstein for the world, you know, Miss America, whatever it was that he was involved in."

"Yeah, Trump, I don't think your supporters have left you," she remarked. "I think you've abandoned your supporters."

"We have to just acknowledge the fact that Trump has betrayed us on the matter of speech," Owens added.

Our Analysis:

The Candace Owens Conundrum: A Dive into the Deep End of Conspiracy

Right-wing podcaster Candace Owens has taken a dramatic leap from staunch Trump supporter to proclaiming a "betrayal" by the former president, a move that is as bewildering as it is revealing. In a recent interview with conspiracy theorist Alex Jones, Owens made a series of startling statements that deserve a closer, more critical examination.

Trump, Netanyahu, and the Alleged Deep State Dynamics

Owens' claim that "Bibi Netanyahu is president of the United States" is a bold assertion, bordering on the absurd. The statement, meant to underscore Trump's alleged subservience to foreign influence, particularly in the context of the situation in Gaza, is a simplistic and misleading portrayal of international relations and political influence.

Her criticism of Trump's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files is perhaps more grounded in a tangible grievance among many of his supporters, who had hoped for a full unveiling of the Epstein scandal's depths. However, her leap to suggest this as evidence of Trump's integration into the "deep state" is a massive oversimplification of the term and its implications.

The Epstein Files: A Litmus Test for Loyalty?

Owens expresses outrage over Trump's apparent dismissal of those fixated on the Epstein case, interpreting it as a betrayal of his base. This moment, however, highlights a broader issue within political movements - the challenge of maintaining a cohesive narrative when the realities of governance or legal constraints come into play. Owens's reaction is emblematic of a purity test that no political figure could realistically pass, given the complexities of legal and national security considerations.

Ghislaine Maxwell’s Prison Transfer: A Red Herring?

The focus on Ghislaine Maxwell's prison conditions, while not irrelevant, diverts attention from more substantial issues at hand. Owens's comparison of Maxwell's prison to "nicer than your house and my house" is a sensationalist jab that lacks substantive critique. It's a missed opportunity to discuss the justice system's inconsistencies and the treatment of high-profile inmates more critically.

The Blackmail Theory: A Dangerous Game

Owens's suggestion that Trump is somehow compromised by international blackmail is a dangerous and unfounded theory that feeds into the larger, often anti-Semitic conspiracy theories about global control. Her casual linkage of Israel to a "worldwide blackmailing" operation is not only baseless but also contributes to harmful stereotypes and misinformation.

Abandonment or Evolution?

Owens's parting shot that Trump has "abandoned his supporters" rather than supporters leaving him over legitimate grievances or disillusionments reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of political allegiance. It's a two-way street, conditioned on actions and policies, not unwavering loyalty to an individual, regardless of their decisions.

Conclusion: The Owens Paradox

Candace Owens's transition from Trump advocate to critic on the basis of deep state conspiracy theories and misinterpretations of political complexity is a paradox. It reveals less about Trump's supposed betrayals and more about the precariousness of political ideologies built on personality cults and conspiracy theories.

In the realm of political discourse, Owens's commentary is a cautionary tale of what happens when loyalty to an individual trumps (pun intended) reasoned critique and when complex geopolitical and legal issues are reduced to sensationalist soundbites. As observers and participants in this discourse, it's critical to approach such claims with skepticism and demand a higher standard of evidence and reasoning.
