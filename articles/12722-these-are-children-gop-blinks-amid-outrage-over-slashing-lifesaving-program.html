'These are children': GOP blinks amid outrage over slashing 'lifesaving' program

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analysis of GOP's Budget Bill Reversal on PEPFAR Funding

FILE PHOTO: Russell Vought, U.S. President Trump’s nominee to be director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), testifies before a Senate Budget Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 22, 2025. REUTERS/Kaylee Greenlee Beal/File Photo

Republicans have reissued cuts made in the 2026 budget bill that would have ended funding for the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief, or PEPFAR. However, on Tuesday, Republicans announced that they would pass another bill stopping the cuts. It would reinstate the full $9 billion in funding in the budget.

The Huffington Post's Igor Bobic quoted Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) telling reporters, “I don’t see it getting back in," meaning the cuts wouldn't return.

Semafor congressional bureau chief Burgess Everett confirmed, "OMB Director Russ Vought says that the PEPFAR cuts will not be included in the final recissions package."

There was a huge uprising from those who had fought for funding to help fight HIV/AIDS transmission. U2 lead singer Bono, who has been a big part of the PEPFAR program, responded to the cuts by asking, "Who would want that on their conscience? I mean, cutting jobs or whatever DOGE does. These are not job losses. These are life losses. These are children.”

"We’re all trying to do calculations. Bill Gates says a million children will die,” Bono said in an interview in Cannes.

Gates added his voice to the ire, saying in a post on X, "The devastating effects of these cuts are entirely preventable—and it’s not too late to reverse them."

The week before, Gates said that the funding cuts had already killed people.

"A lot of cuts are being made in foreign aid programs,” he said during an address in Ethiopia in June. “Some of those cuts are being made so abruptly that there are complete interruptions in trials, or medicines are still sitting in warehouses and are not available. And these cuts are something that I think are a huge mistake.”

Similarly, the George W. Bush Presidential Center executive director told the Senate Appropriations Committee that the program, founded in 2003, has saved the lives of "26 million people and allowed nearly 8 million babies to be born HIV-free."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of GOP's Budget Bill Reversal on PEPFAR Funding

In a stunning display of political backpedaling, the Republicans have made an about-face on the previously proposed cuts to the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) in the 2026 budget bill. The restoration of the full $9 billion in funding comes after a significant outcry from both domestic and international advocates, showcasing the influence of public and high-profile pressure on political decisions. This move, while commendable, raises serious questions about the initial rationale behind the funding cuts and the GOP's commitment to global health initiatives.

The Flip-Flop

Senator Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) and OMB Director Russ Vought's statements confirm the reversal, indicating a clear retreat from the previously hawkish stance on budget cuts. This raises eyebrows - not for the change per se, but for the lack of foresight and understanding of the implications such cuts would have had on global health, had they gone through. It's a relief to see reason prevail, but the oscillation points to a concerning volatility in policy decision-making, especially on matters of life and death.

The Outcry

The uproar from advocates, celebrities, and philanthropists like Bono and Bill Gates illustrates the broad spectrum of support for PEPFAR. Bono's condemnation of the cuts as not just job losses but "life losses" underscores the human cost at stake. Meanwhile, Gates' grim projection of a million children dying as a result of the cuts, and his assertion that the cuts had already resulted in deaths, add a sobering, data-backed perspective to the debate. It's a stark reminder of the tangible impacts of political decisions on global health crises.

The Consequences of Cuts

The George W. Bush Presidential Center's testimony before the Senate Appropriations Committee highlights the monumental achievements of PEPFAR since its inception in 2003. The program's role in saving 26 million lives and preventing nearly 8 million HIV transmissions to newborns illustrates the critical importance of sustained funding. The initial decision to slash PEPFAR's budget, therefore, appears not just ill-conceived but dangerously negligent, disregarding two decades of progress in combating HIV/AIDS globally.

Conclusion: A Lesson Learned?

The GOP's reversal on PEPFAR funding cuts, while a victory for global health advocates, is a chilling reminder of the fragility of funding for life-saving programs. The episode should serve as a wake-up call for the Republican Party and all policymakers: decisions with global health implications must be made with careful consideration, grounded in data and humanity, not political expediency. The world cannot afford to gamble with lives in the balance.
