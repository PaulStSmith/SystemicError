'Chaos at the Pentagon': Memo authorizing defense missile 'went unsigned for 3 weeks'

President Donald Trump at Mar-a-Lago on February 18, 2025 (Official White House Photo by Daniel Torok/Flickr)

Between reports of discussing a U.S. military operation in Yemen in two separate chats on the messaging app Signal and clashes with Pentagon officials, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is facing numerous calls for him to resign or be fired. But President Donald Trump, so far, is sticking by Hegseth, and White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt is declaring that he remains in good standing with Trump's administration.

According to CNN reporters Kaitlan Collins, Natasha Bertrand, Jake Tapper and Kevin Liptak, Trump is unlikely to fire Hegseth. Yet some Trump Administration officials are worried about "chaos" at the Pentagon.

In an article published by CNN on April 23, the journalists explain, "Trump has…. been soliciting feedback from people around him about Hegseth's performance during conversations, according to two people familiar with the comments. So far, most, if not all, has been positive about the Pentagon chief. Trump is extremely hesitant to fire any Cabinet official at this point in his term, much less Hegseth, given how hard his team fought to get him confirmed in the first place. Yet the state of affairs in his inner circle has troubled some senior officials who want to see changes made to how the secretary's team in the front office of the Pentagon operates."

Collins, Bertrand, Tapper and Liptak add, "The chaos prevailing at the Pentagon has not been lost on the White House, where officials have watched with concern as Hegseth struggles to contain the dysfunction and as his inner circle implodes."

One example of that "chaos" at the Pentagon, according to the journalists, involved Joe Kasper, Hegseth's former chief of staff at the Pentagon.

Kasper, they report, "clashed, in recent months, with" three Pentagon staffers Hegseth fired.

"Several West Wing staffers had grown frustrated about what they described to others as Kasper's lack of responsiveness, often complaining about the difficulty in getting Hegseth's chief of staff on the phone or to respond to their requests," Collins, Bertrand, Tapper and Liptak explain. "At one point toward the beginning of Hegseth's tenure, the White House was trying to reach Kasper to get Hegseth to sign a memo kickstarting the process of developing Trump's Golden Dome missile shield, but the memo went unsigned for three weeks, according to a person familiar with the matter."

The CNN reporters continue, "In another example of the disorderly nature of the front office, at one point in March, Hegseth directly asked the director of (the U.S. Department of Defense's) special access programs — which are among the most highly classified programs within the DoD — to read Elon Musk into more than two dozen programs having to do with China, according to multiple people familiar with the episode. But Hegseth did not first run the idea through a policy process that included lawyers, the people said, and ultimately, DoD ethics lawyers in the Standards of Conduct office said it would not be appropriate. The idea died."

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Pentagon Decorum: The Hegseth Saga

In a narrative that reads like a political thriller yet sadly unfolds in the corridors of power, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth finds himself enmeshed in controversy, a testament to the tumultuous tenure of the Trump administration. The fact that President Donald Trump is standing firm behind Hegseth, amidst calls for resignation or dismissal, is hardly surprising. Yet, it underscores a disturbing trend of loyalty trumping competence. The administration's unwavering support for Hegseth, despite the "chaos" at the Pentagon, is a stark reminder of how political allegiance is often valued over national security interests.

The Teflon Defense Secretary?

CNN's comprehensive report unveils a Pentagon under siege from within. Hegseth's seemingly cavalier approach to discussing U.S. military operations on Signal chats and his reported friction with Pentagon officials paints a picture of a department in disarray. Yet, the most alarming revelation is the apparent indifference of the Trump administration to the ongoing pandemonium. Karoline Leavitt, the White House Press Secretary, asserts Hegseth's solid standing within the administration, a stance that flies in the face of accountability and transparency.

Trump's reluctance to dismiss any Cabinet official, especially Hegseth, is not just a matter of loyalty. It's a reflection of the administration's broader issue with acknowledging and correcting missteps. The report suggests that Trump values the battles fought to confirm his picks over the consequential wars waged within his administration's ranks. This stubbornness to cling to controversial figures is not just a bad look for the administration; it's a security risk.

A Pentagon in Peril

The so-called "chaos" at the Pentagon, as detailed by CNN, is not merely administrative turbulence; it's a symptom of a deeper malaise affecting the heart of America's defense apparatus. Joe Kasper, Hegseth's former chief of staff, emerges as a central figure in the dysfunction narrative. His alleged unresponsiveness and the internal conflicts leading to the dismissal of Pentagon staffers are indicative of a leadership crisis.

Moreover, Hegseth's attempt to involve Elon Musk in highly classified programs without due process is not just an oversight; it's a flagrant disregard for protocol and ethics. This cavalier attitude towards sensitive national security matters is not just troubling; it is downright dangerous.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The Hegseth saga is more than a story of one man's flawed tenure at the Pentagon. It's a reflection of a broader issue of governance under the Trump administration. The unwavering support for Hegseth in the face of glaring mismanagement and ethical breaches speaks volumes about the administration's priorities.

In a functioning democracy, accountability is not optional. It is essential. The Trump administration's reluctance to address the "chaos" at the Pentagon is a dereliction of duty. Loyalty to individuals should never outweigh loyalty to the principles of governance and national security.

The defense of the United States and its interests should never be compromised by political allegiances or personal loyalties. The Hegseth debacle is a stark reminder of the need for a return to principled leadership and accountability at all levels of government. It's high time for the Trump administration to recognize this fundamental truth.
