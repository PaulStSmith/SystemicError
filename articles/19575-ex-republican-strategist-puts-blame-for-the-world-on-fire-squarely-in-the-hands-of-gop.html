Ex-Republican strategist puts blame for 'the world on fire' squarely in the hands of GOP

President Donald Trump has set “the world on fire,” according to a former Republican strategist who worked for President George W. Bush — and it’s entirely because of Trump voters.

In a Wednesday Substack essay titled “Why the world is on fire,” political consultant Steve Schmidt argued that America is sending the planet toward World War III but is not “branding” it or using a “marketing campaign.”

“There has been no debate, no plan, and no thought given by Donald, his stooges and politicized generals about the second-and third-order effects of their decisions,” Schmidt said. “This is escalating.”

With 15 nations involved in combat, and Russia still at war with Ukraine, Schmidt concluded that we now “have war, chaos, economic crisis, corruption and insanity served up non-stop, extolling the wrong against the right, while abusing American citizens with violence, including murder.”

As he summed it up earlier in the editorial, “We had peace, and we chose Trump.”

Last month, Schmidt and former CNN anchor Jim Acosta blasted the Supreme Court for even allowing Trump to run for president and gain as much power as he has upon taking office.

“Seems to me a part of this is … that the Supreme Court, under John Roberts, released this Frankenstein, and now they don’t know how to rein him in. They’ve tried to cut him off at the pass [on some things] but John Roberts is responsible for this mess,” Acosta said.

“The Roberts court has destabilized our American society through partisan rulings,” Schmidt replied. “We have a corrupt Supreme Court with [Judge Samuel] Alito’s misconduct, Clarence Thomas’s misconduct, flying around with all these billionaire extremist doners from here to there and everywhere.”

He added, “The court has lost its reputation for a reason, and now we have Trump’s visage looming down at the American people from the Department of Justice, which is a corrupt institution that can’t be trusted, filled with corrupt prosecutor who abuse their oath, abuse the Constitution, and abuse the American people in the name of power.”

Also last month, Schmidt pointed out in a Substack post that the world’s billionaires like Jeff Bezos, Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk and Tim Cook have decided to bend the figurative knee to Trump, but that doesn’t mean the rest of humanity must join them.

“We recognize that the world’s richest people of the most powerful corporations, the big media companies, the big tech companies, the big banks have made a decision for the privilege of resting their head on a Mar-a-Lago pillow,” Schmidt said in his Substack post. “They are satisfied to live on their knees, but we are not.”

In September, Schmidt’s disgust with Trump made headlines when he threatened to rent a billboard attacking Disney CEO Bob Iger for capitulating to Trump’s demand that talk show host Jimmy Kimmel be suspended for criticizing the Trump movement.

“We will put billboards up in L.A. with Bob Iger’s picture, a yellow stripe under it and the word ‘COWARD,’” Schmidt said. “It’s a billboard town and everyone can reflect on it as they drive by ... the cowardice is appalling.”

Our Analysis:

The Firestarter: Trump’s World According to Steve Schmidt

In a recent outburst of verbal pyrotechnics, Steve Schmidt, a former Republican strategist turned political commentator, lays the blame for the world’s current turmoil squarely at the feet of President Donald Trump and, by extension, his voters. In his Substack essay, Schmidt paints a dystopian picture of a world teetering on the brink of World War III, a scenario he attributes to what he perceives as Trump’s reckless foreign policy and domestic governance—or lack thereof.

The Accusation: Igniting Global Chaos

Schmidt’s core argument is that Trump, “his stooges, and politicized generals” have plunged the world into chaos, with 15 nations involved in combat and the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war serving as prime examples. He criticizes the Trump administration for its alleged lack of planning, debate, or consideration of the consequences of its actions, which he believes are propelling the planet towards an unprecedented catastrophe.

The Supreme Court’s Role: Enablers of Chaos?

Adding a layer to the critique, Schmidt, alongside former CNN anchor Jim Acosta, points fingers at the Supreme Court under Chief Justice John Roberts. They argue that the Court’s partisan rulings have destabilized American society, indirectly facilitating Trump’s rise to power and his subsequent influence on global affairs. The misconduct of justices such as Samuel Alito and Clarence Thomas, as well as their interactions with billionaire donors, are cited as evidence of a broader corruption that has undermined the integrity of the Court and, by extension, American democracy itself.

Billionaire Complicity: Kneeling Before the Throne

Schmidt doesn’t stop at political figures; he extends his criticism to the world’s billionaires—naming Jeff Bezos, Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk, and Tim Cook—accusing them of acquiescing to Trump’s demands for the sake of their own comfort and privilege. This, according to Schmidt, is emblematic of a broader societal surrender to Trump’s whims, sacrificing principles and integrity for access and influence.

The Disney Debacle: A Case Study in Cowardice

In what can only be described as a dramatic flourish, Schmidt’s feud with Disney CEO Bob Iger over the suspension of talk show host Jimmy Kimmel—a suspension demanded by Trump—serves as a microcosm of the larger issue. Schmidt’s proposed billboard campaign branding Iger a “coward” is not just about Disney or Kimmel; it’s a symbolic stand against what Schmidt views as widespread capitulation to Trump’s authoritarian tendencies.

Analysis: A Fiery Rhetoric Reflecting Deep Divisions

Schmidt’s commentary, while scathing, underscores a profound concern among certain segments of the American political landscape regarding Trump’s influence on both domestic politics and international relations. His assertions, though dramatic, speak to the fears of those who view Trump’s presidency as a symptom of deeper systemic failures—failures of governance, justice, and moral leadership.

However, Schmidt’s arguments, rich in fiery rhetoric, could benefit from a more nuanced acknowledgment of the complexities of global politics and the multifaceted reasons behind the support for Trump. While his critique of billionaire complicity in Trump’s agenda raises valid questions about the intersection of wealth, power, and politics, the portrayal of a world on fire solely due to Trump and his supporters oversimplifies a vastly complicated global landscape.

In essence, while Schmidt’s analysis captures the angst and frustration of a significant portion of the American populace, it perhaps leans too heavily on vilification and less on the exploration of solutions or the root causes of the societal divisions Trump so effectively exploits. The real challenge, then, is not just to extinguish the fires set by Trump’s administration but to address the systemic errors that allowed them to ignite in the first place.
