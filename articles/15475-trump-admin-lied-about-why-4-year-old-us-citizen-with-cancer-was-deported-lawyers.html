Trump admin lied about why 4-year-old US citizen with cancer was deported: lawyers

Attorneys for families being ripped apart by President Donald Trump's deportations are claiming the removals of three U.S. citizen children are illegal, reported NBC News on Monday evening — and even include a child with stage 4 cancer.

Furthermore, the lawyers assert, the administration is lying about the circumstances under which they were deported.

"On Monday, Trump administration border 'czar' Tom Homan said the three U.S. citizen children, all under 10 years old, were placed on deportation flights at their mothers’ request. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said the children weren’t deported but 'went with their mothers,' adding that as citizens they could come back if there's someone in the United States who 'wants to assume them,'" reported Suzanne Gamboa, Carmen Sesin, and Julia Ainsley.

But according to the lawyers, their families weren't given a choice, and Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers stalled their efforts to make arrangements for the children, ensuring they would be sent away with their mothers.

Per the report, "One mother who was about to be deported was allowed less than two minutes on the phone with her husband to figure out what would become of her 2-year-old U.S. citizen son," while one of the other mothers "wasn’t allowed to speak with attorneys or family members before she was deported, accompanied by her U.S.-born children," despite ICE agents knowing the child had stage 4 cancer.

Trump's mass deportation efforts, which are often allegedly designed to circumvent due process or judicial review, have become a national flashpoint of tensions as voters sour on Trump's agenda.

One of the most prominent is the case of Maryland family man Kilmar Abrego Garcia, who was deported to an infamous Salvadoran megaprison despite a protective order barring his removal to that country. Multiple courts have ordered Trump to "facilitate" his return, but the administration asserts they cannot order a foreign leader to hand over a prisoner from their soil.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Cruelty: Trump's Deportation Machine Targets Children

The Trump administration's immigration enforcement policies, as reported by NBC News, have descended into a realm where not even children, U.S. citizens with every legal right to remain in their country, are spared. These arenities are not just egregious; they are a glaring testament to a systemic failure that prioritizes ruthless deportation over human compassion and legal rights.

The Deception of Choice

Trump administration officials, including the so-called border 'czar' Tom Homan and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, have attempted to paint a picture of compliance and voluntary departure, claiming the children were deported "at their mothers' request" or "went with their mothers." This narrative is not just misleading; it's insultingly simplistic. Lawyers for the families have countered these claims, stating that these families were given no real choice, and efforts to arrange for the children's care within the U.S. were actively obstructed by ICE officers. This isn't about choice; it's about coercion.

A Harrowing Lack of Compassion

The details shared about the treatment of these families are harrowing. One mother was given less than two minutes to decide her 2-year-old U.S. citizen son's fate over the phone. Another wasn't allowed to speak with attorneys or family members before being deported with her U.S.-born children, one of whom is battling stage 4 cancer. This level of disregard for basic human dignity and the rights of U.S. citizens is appalling. It's a stark reminder that the administration's deportation frenzy is not about legality or safety but about enforcing a ruthless agenda at any cost.

The Bigger Picture: Systemic Cruelty

These cases are symptomatic of a broader, more systemic issue within the Trump administration's approach to immigration. The deportation of Maryland family man Kilmar Abrego Garcia to a notorious Salvadoran prison, despite court orders barring such an action, underscores a chilling willingness to bypass due process and judicial oversight. This is not just about enforcing immigration laws; it's about a deliberate strategy to instill fear and suppress any form of dissent or resistance.

Conclusion: A Moral and Legal Bankruptcy

The Trump administration's mass deportation efforts, especially those targeting vulnerable children and flouting legal protections, reveal a moral and legal bankruptcy at the heart of its immigration policies. The attempt to justify these actions under the guise of legality and voluntary departure is a farce. It's a clear indication that the administration's priorities are skewed towards punishment and exclusion, rather than justice or the wellbeing of individuals and families.

This is not just a failure of policy; it's a failure of humanity. As voters and as a nation, we must question the cost of such policies on the fabric of our society and on the principles we claim to uphold. The administration's actions are a stark reminder of the need for vigilance, advocacy, and resistance against policies that strip away basic rights and dignity under the guise of national security or immigration control.
