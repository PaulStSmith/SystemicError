President Donald Trump has repeatedly declared that he has a mandate from the American people to stem immigration, but a new Gallup Poll reveals attitudes have shifted since he took office.

Gallup posted Friday, "When asked if immigration is generally a good thing or bad thing for the country, a record-high 79% of U.S. adults call it a good thing; a record-low 17% see it as a bad thing."

Other highlights of the poll showed "30% of Americans want immigration decreased, down from 55% a year ago." It also showed waning support for Trump's border wall and mass deportation policy.

Gallup based the findings on a June 2-26 sample of 1,402 U.S. adults, "including oversamples of Hispanic and Black Americans, weighted to match national demographics."

CNN's Aaron Blake called the numbers "stunning" when summing up the poll: "About 7 in 10 independents disapprove of Trump's handling of immigration -- despite a historic drop in border crossings. And a 21st Century-high 79% of Americans said immigration is a 'good thing' -- up from 64% last year."

Blake offered a caveat, however: "Worth noting that other pollsters haven't shown independents souring quite so much on Trump's immigration policies. A Marist poll from last month showed indies disapproved 59-36. But that's still lopsided."

Prem Thacker with Zateo News exclaimed, "Wow....It’s almost like Americans were never fundamentally, inevitably 'anti-immigration' and Democrats never had to just put their hands up and meekly chase Republicans (and lose), and affirm all their anti-immigrant positions that helped get us here at all!"

Global Refuge's Tim Young declared, "the pendulum has swung drastically since last year" on the issue of immigration, while The Cato Institute's David J. Bier called it an "absolute bloodbath for Trump."

Sahil Kapur with NBC News called the poll the result of "backlash politics taking effect," and Journalist Doug Henwood declared, "Support for immigration hits a record; Trump deep underwater on the issue."

Our Analysis:

The Great American Shift: A Deep Dive into the Changing Tide of Immigration Sentiment

In a political climate where President Donald Trump's rhetoric has been anything but subtle, the latest Gallup Poll serves as a stark reminder that the American populace is not a monolith to be swayed by the whims of its leaders. The poll, a beacon of shifting attitudes, reveals a seismic shift in how Americans perceive immigration - a topic that has been at the fiery heart of political debates and policies. Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned critic.

The Numbers Speak: A Surge in Pro-Immigration Sentiment

According to Gallup, a record-high 79% of U.S. adults now see immigration as a good thing for the country, with a record-low 17% viewing it in a negative light. These numbers are not just statistics; they are a loud repudiation of the fear-mongering and divisive rhetoric that has dominated the airwaves. It's a slap in the face to the narrative that America wants to close its doors.

The Wall Crumbles: Declining Support for Restrictive Policies

It's particularly telling that this shift in perspective coincides with waning support for Trump's hallmark policies: the border wall and mass deportations. The idea that a physical barrier could solve complex social and economic issues was always a simplistic solution to a nuanced problem. The Gallup findings underscore the growing realization among Americans that isolationism and xenophobia are not the path forward.

Methodology Matters

Gallup's methodology, including oversamples of Hispanic and Black Americans to match national demographics, paints a more inclusive and representative picture of American attitudes. This approach acknowledges the diverse fabric of the U.S. populace, a factor often glossed over by those peddling a one-size-fits-all narrative on immigration.

Independents Break Rank

The significant disapproval of Trump's immigration handling by independents - about 7 in 10 according to CNN's Aaron Blake - is a testament to the issue's transcendence beyond partisan lines. It's a myth-busting moment, revealing that concerns about immigration and policy effectiveness cut deeper than mere political allegiance.

A Reality Check for Both Parties

Prem Thacker's commentary hits a nerve. The Democratic Party, often seen as playing second fiddle to the GOP's hardline stance on immigration, might find in these numbers a call to action. It's a moment to reassess and potentially recalibrate, understanding that the American public's appetite for inclusive, humane, and sensible immigration policies is far from satiated.

A Backlash Against Backlash Politics

The term "backlash politics" has often been thrown around, but what we're witnessing here is perhaps its purest form. The surge in pro-immigration sentiment is a direct response to policies and rhetoric perceived as hostile or inhumane. It's a collective pushback against the notion that to be patriotic is to be anti-immigrant.

Conclusion: The Tides of Change

As the dust settles, the message from the Gallup Poll is clear: America's heart is not as closed as some would have you believe. The pendulum's swing towards pro-immigration sentiment is a loud, resounding declaration that fear and division will not dictate the nation's course.

In the face of these findings, one must ponder whether Trump and his acolytes will heed the call of the masses or double down on their divisive strategies. History will be the judge, but for now, the American people seem to be charting a different path—one that embraces diversity and the rich tapestry of cultures that immigrants bring to the United States.

The question remains: Will our political leaders listen, or will they continue to play a dangerous game of Russian roulette with the core values of the American experiment? Only time will tell, but the Gallup Poll has undoubtedly added a fascinating chapter to the ongoing narrative of America's identity and values.
