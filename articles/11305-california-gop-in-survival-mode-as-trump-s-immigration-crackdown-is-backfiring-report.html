California GOP in 'survival mode' as Trump's immigration crackdown is 'backfiring': report

President Donald Trump’s immigration crackdown is now “backfiring” on California Republicans, according to new polling data that one political science expert said has put California GOP members in "survival mode.”

Conducted and reported on by Politico, the new polling shows that around 80% of California voters – including a majority of Republicans – indicated that they believed undocumented immigrants already in the United States should have a pathway to remain in the country.

With the White House having a daily migrant arrest quota of 3,000, California has been the hotbed for immigration raids, raids that have left some immigration officers demoralized for having to “arrest gardeners.” And now, elected Republicans in the state are starting to feel political pressure from their constituents.

“Republicans in the state are in a kind of survival mode,” said Jack Citrin, a political science professor at UC Berkeley, speaking with Politico Friday. “They’re caught between their loyalty to Trump, and there may be local pressures on them.”

Trump sailed to victory in 2024 largely in part due to his stance on immigration, but recent polling has suggested the president is losing support amid his administration’s mass deportations, which have been chaotic and sometimes violent. A CNN poll from late July found that 55% of respondents said that Trump had gone “too far” with his immigration crackdown, up ten points since February.

“I don’t dismiss for one second that the fear in our communities is real," said California Sen. Suzette Martinez Valladares, a Republican state senator, speaking with Politico in late July. "It is. I’ve had constituents on both sides articulate that it’s heartbreaking, heart-wrenching, what’s going on.”

Citrin, who also helped conduct the poll alongside Politico, said the chaotic nature of the Trump administration’s deportation policy had contributed to Republican voters’ shrinking support for the efforts.

“(There’s) an impulse of empathy and humanity, and realism about the difficulty and the unfairness of all of a sudden, out of the blue, coming from people who might have been here for 10 years, have families here, et cetera,” Citrin said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Immigration Crackdown: A California GOP Quagmire

In an ironic twist of political fate, President Donald Trump’s hardline immigration stance is not just backfiring—it’s setting fire to the California Republican Party. According to a Politico report, the relentless immigration raids and the administration's daily migrant arrest quota of 3,000 have left both immigration officers and the GOP in a state of dismay, revealing a profound disconnect between Trump’s policies and the will of California voters.

Voters’ Discontent: A Clear Message

The poll, as reported, uncovers a staggering reality: around 80% of California voters, including a majority from the Republican base, support a pathway for undocumented immigrants to remain in the U.S. This isn’t just a statistic; it’s a loud and clear repudiation of Trump’s immigration crackdown. The notion that even Republican voters are advocating for empathy and realism starkly contrasts with the administration's draconian measures, which have not only been chaotic but, at times, outright violent.

The GOP’s Survival Mode

The article highlights the precarious position of the California GOP, caught in the crossfire between allegiance to Trump and the palpable local pressures from their constituencies. Jack Citrin, a political science professor at UC Berkeley, aptly describes this as a “kind of survival mode.” This isn’t just a political conundrum; it’s an existential crisis for the state's Republicans, who are now forced to navigate the treacherous waters between party loyalty and the clear will of their constituents.

A Failing Strategy

Trump’s 2024 victory, largely attributed to his immigration stance, now appears to be a Pyrrhic one, at least in California. The administration's policies, described by Politico as sometimes "chaotic and sometimes violent," have not only demoralized immigration officers but have also begun to erode the president’s support base. A CNN poll underscores this shift, noting a significant increase in the number of respondents who believe Trump has gone “too far” with his immigration crackdown.

Empathy vs. Enforcement

Senator Suzette Martinez Valladares’ remarks to Politico underscore a critical aspect of the immigration debate: the real and palpable fear gripping communities across California. Her acknowledgment of the “heartbreaking, heart-wrenching” realities faced by her constituents points to a profound disconnect between the Trump administration’s policies and the everyday lives of the people they affect.

Conclusion: A GOP at Crossroads

Citrin’s commentary on the “impulse of empathy and humanity” among Republican voters offers a glimmer of hope. It suggests that beneath the political rhetoric and hardline policies, there's a recognition of the unfairness and human cost of abrupt deportations. The GOP in California, and perhaps across the nation, stands at a critical crossroads. Will they continue to align with Trump’s divisive policies, or will they listen to the growing chorus of their constituents calling for compassion and realism in immigration reform?

The Politico article serves not just as a report on polling data but as a barometer for the shifting political winds in California. The state’s Republicans must decide whether to sail with these winds or be swept away by them.
