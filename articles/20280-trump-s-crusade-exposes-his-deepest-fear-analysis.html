Trump's crusade exposes his deepest fear: analysis

Impeachment as Memory Against a Regime of Erasure

The Stakes Are Not Procedural

The source argues that impeaching Donald Trump would matter even without removal, because the point is to force his corruption, abuse of power, and defiance of the courts into public view. It cites polling showing voters name corruption, self-enrichment, abuse of power, and contempt for the courts as the main grounds, while Trump and his allies now try to erase the impeachment record altogether.

Power Sits With Trump, Not the Theater

The real institutional power in this story belongs to Trump and the Republican officials covering for him. The House can impeach with a simple majority, but the Senate’s two-thirds threshold makes conviction nearly impossible when party loyalty is functioning as a shield. That is not a neutral design flaw. It is a structure that lets a president accumulate scandal, then rely on partisan discipline to convert accountability into performance art.

Trump understands this. That is why he is pushing to expunge his impeachments. The goal is not legal clarity. It is reputational laundering.

The Cover Story Is “Hyperpartisanship”

Mike Johnson’s language about a “hyperpartisan attack job” is the familiar laundering mechanism: recast documented misconduct as partisan theater, and the accused becomes the victim of procedure rather than the author of the offense. That framing does not rebut the facts described in the source. It tries to bury them under procedural disgust.

This is how institutions excuse themselves. They do not deny the wrongdoing. They declare the record itself illegitimate.

What Trump Was Actually Impeached For

The source is explicit about the charges: extorting Ukraine for dirt on Joe Biden and trying to overturn the government after Jan. 6. Those are not ambiguous misunderstandings or clerical errors. They are deliberate abuses of office, and the article’s core point is that impeachment exists for exactly this kind of conduct.

Yet the political conversation keeps sliding away from the substance and toward the spectacle of failure: no conviction, no removal, therefore supposedly no consequence. That is a convenient standard for the people enabling the misconduct. It treats a captured Senate as a verdict on innocence.

Expungement Is a Lie About History

The push to expunge the impeachments is not about law. It is about narrative control. If the record can be scrubbed, then the offense can be recoded as a misunderstanding, the accountability as overreach, and the man as persecuted rather than constrained.

That is the larger tactic here: convert political culpability into memetic victimhood, then demand that institutions participate in the rewrite. Even the source’s legal note makes the point bluntly: expungement would not erase the impeachment itself. It would only reveal how far Trump’s allies will go to pretend the past never happened.

The Pattern

This is what institutional decay looks like in practice. A president abuses power, allies normalize it, party leadership shields it, and then everyone involved asks the public to accept the cleanup as seriousness. The corruption is not only in the acts themselves. It is in the machinery built to dilute, delay, and finally erase them.

Impeachment, in that context, is less a pathway to removal than a fight over whether reality survives partisan management.
