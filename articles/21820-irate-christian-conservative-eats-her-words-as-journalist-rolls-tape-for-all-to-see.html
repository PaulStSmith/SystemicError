Irate Christian conservative eats her words as journalist rolls tape for all to see

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Common Sense, Blood, and a Lie About Power

The Source of the Story

Colorado activist Erin Lee went after journalist Kyle Clark after he quoted her own language about children being “covered in the blood of Jesus.” Clark responded with receipts: video, transcript, and the context Lee was trying to erase. The immediate fight is over one segment and one post. The actual story is about a political movement trying to launder sectarian extremism as “non-partisan” public concern.

Who Has the Power

Lee is not just a commenter with an opinion. She founded Protect Kids Colorado, which has pushed ballot initiatives aimed at restricting trans kids’ participation in school sports and access to gender-affirming surgeries. That is institutional power: ballot access, campaign framing, coalition building, and pressure on state politics. Clark has a microphone. Lee has an organized campaign. Those are not the same thing.

The Complaint Is Not About Accuracy

Lee’s grievance is that Clark quoted her. That is not a journalism problem. It is a propaganda problem. She said the fight over trans children was a “spiritual battle,” and she talked about covering kids in the blood of Jesus. Clark did not invent that language. He exposed it. The demand here is not for fairness; it is for obedience to a cleaned-up self-image that the record does not support.

“Non-Partisan” as Camouflage

Lee’s claim that she is building a “broad, non-exclusive coalition for the greater good” is the standard move of movements that know their real project sounds ugly out loud. The source already gives away the frame: Protect Kids Colorado was founded “under the guidance” of self-described Christian nationalist Lance Wallnau, and Lee herself called “gender-ideology” a “state-mandated religion.” That is not civic moderation. It is religious authoritarianism dressed up as parental concern.

Misdirection Is the Point

The weak framing here would be to treat this as a messy clash between an activist and a reporter, or as a misunderstanding over tone. It is neither. It is deliberate misdirection: attack the messenger, deny the quotation, recast explicit ideology as harmless concern, and pretend the campaign is “common sense” while targeting trans children with state-level exclusion. The political mechanism is familiar because it works. Make the cruelty sound like protection. Make the ideology sound like neutrality. Make the reporting sound like the offense.

The Larger Pattern

This is what modern anti-democratic politics often looks like at the local level: not a single dramatic seizure of power, but a steady effort to capture institutions, redefine abuse as virtue, and punish anyone who describes the machinery honestly. The reporter’s job is to quote. The activist’s job is to hide behind the quote and claim persecution. The real threat is not that the language was exposed. It is that the movement behind it expects the public to accept the disguise.
