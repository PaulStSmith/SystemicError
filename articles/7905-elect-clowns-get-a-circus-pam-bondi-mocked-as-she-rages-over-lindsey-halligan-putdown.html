'Elect clowns, get a circus': Pam Bondi mocked as she rages over Lindsey Halligan putdown

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

The Department of Justice issued a joint statement in support of Lindsey Halligan after federal judges questioned her role as a prosecutor.

Two magistrate judges and a district court judge in Virginia told prosecutors in open court they didn’t believe Halligan’s name should be listed on criminal case filings after another judge found last week that she was not lawfully serving as U.S. attorney.

Attorney General Pam Bondi and her top deputy Todd Blanche expressed support for President Donald Trump’s appointee.

“Certain district court and magistrate judges in the Eastern District of Virginia are engaging in an unconscionable campaign of bias and hostility against U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan and her line AUSAs,” wrote Bondi and Blanche. “Lindsey and our attorneys are simply doing their jobs: advocating for the Department of Justice’s positions while following guidance from the Office of Legal Counsel.”

“They do not deserve to have their reputations questioned in court for ethically advocating on behalf of their client,” the officials added. “This Department of Justice has no tolerance for undemocratic judicial activism. We will continue fighting for public safety in courtrooms across the country, and we will not be deterred by rogue judges who fail to live up to their obligations of impartiality because of their own political views. The American people, and the people of the Eastern District of Virginia, deserve nothing less.”

The statement set off a furious reaction on social media, where many users noted that Halligan had been ruled ineligible because she hadn’t been Senate confirmed after 120 days of a vacancy in the post and had not been sworn in by the judges of the court.

“Fact check: Lindsey Halligan is not and never has been US Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia,” wrote Adam Klasfeld, editor in chief of All Rise News.

“There is no US Attorney named Lindsey Halligan,” agreed X user Lily Cooper. “There’s an insurance lawyer by that name illegally impersonating a US Attorney. Thank you for your attention to this matter.”

“Hello @FBI, I’d like to report a person falsely assuming or pretending to be an officer or employee acting under the authority of the United States in violation of 18 U.S.C. Sec. 912,” posted attorney Darren Kaplan.

“What reputation?” wondered X user Bellacissa. “She’d have to have a reputation as attorney before someone could question it. And that’s the problem. She doesn’t.”

“Or… put in competent people that actually are pros with the skills to do the job (assuming any of them want to work for you, which is highly doubtful),” said political activist Brian Mistrot. “Elect clowns. Get a circus.”

“Please notice that there is not a single allegation of any specific wrongdoing; just empty smears,” added Chris Kelly, a former Missouri Democratic state legislator.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Curious Case of Lindsey Halligan and the Department of Justice’s Defiance

In a recent turn of events that could easily be mistaken for a subplot in a legal drama, the Department of Justice (DOJ), under the guidance of Attorney General Pam Bondi and her top deputy Todd Blanche, has thrown its weight behind Lindsey Halligan. Halligan, a figure who has sparked controversy and confusion, found herself at the center of a judicial whirlwind. Federal judges in Virginia have raised eyebrows—and valid questions—about her position, or lack thereof, as a U.S. attorney.

The Heart of the Matter

The crux of the issue lies in the legitimacy of Halligan’s role. Despite being named by President Donald Trump, her appointment has been scrutinized due to a lack of Senate confirmation and the formal swearing-in typically required for such positions. This oversight, or deliberate omission, has led to a clash not just in the courtroom but in the court of public opinion, as evidenced by the social media uproar following the DOJ’s statement.

DOJ’s Stance: A Defiant Declaration

Bondi and Blanche’s statement is a masterclass in defensive rhetoric, painting Halligan and her associates as victims of a “campaign of bias and hostility” by certain judges. This narrative, however, seems to wilfully ignore the legal prerequisites for Halligan’s supposed role. The assertion that these legal professionals are being unjustly targeted for “ethically advocating” on behalf of their client—the DOJ—sidesteps the foundational issue of Halligan’s eligibility to do so.

Social Media: The Public Jury

The reaction on social media platforms underscores a broader public skepticism towards the DOJ’s defense of Halligan. Users, including legal experts and political activists, have pointed out the glaring contradiction at the heart of this controversy: one cannot defend the reputation of a position that was never legally held in the first place. The reference to Halligan as an “insurance lawyer...illegally impersonating a US Attorney” by social media user Lily Cooper adds a layer of irony to the DOJ’s indignation over questioned reputations.

The Bigger Picture

What this debacle reveals is a troubling disregard for the legal protocols and checks that underpin the U.S. justice system. The DOJ’s narrative, accusing judges of “undemocratic judicial activism,” is a strategic redirection from the core issue—the legitimacy of Halligan’s appointment. This raises serious questions about the department’s commitment to the rule of law when it suits political or personal agendas.

Conclusion: Elect Clowns, Get a Circus

The situation can be aptly summarized by political activist Brian Mistrot’s comment: “Elect clowns. Get a circus.” This episode is less about the individuals involved and more about what it signifies—a systemic error where political appointments and defenses thereof can flout legal norms with alarming audacity. The DOJ’s stance, rather than clarifying the situation, only adds to the growing circus, undermining public trust in the institutions designed to uphold justice and democracy.

In essence, the controversy surrounding Lindsey Halligan is not just a question of legal technicalities but a glaring spotlight on the current administration’s approach to governance and justice. As the DOJ doubles down on its defense, the American public is left to wonder about the integrity of their justice system and the lengths to which its leaders will go to protect one of their own, legality be damned.
