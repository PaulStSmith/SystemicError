'He sucks': White House insiders lining up against Mullin as patience runs out

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

The White House Is Angry About Tone While ICE Keeps the Knife

Not a Personnel Drama

The source wants to sell this as a palace feud: Markwayne Mullin annoys MAGA, flatters Democrats, and gets dragged by the White House for being too agreeable. That is the decorative layer. The real story is that a DHS official is operating inside a deportation and policing apparatus serious enough to suspend most vehicle stops, and the political class is fighting over loyalty optics instead of the violence of the system itself.

Who Holds Power

The institutional power sits with DHS and ICE, backed by the White House. They decide whether officers stop vehicles, how immigration enforcement is carried out, and whose lives get made into collateral damage after “deadly shootings.” That is the decision-making center here. Governors can praise or complain. Democrats can signal approval. None of that changes who controls the machinery.

The Friction Is About Loyalty, Not Harm

The White House officials in the piece are not upset because the policy was too harsh or too lenient. They are upset because Mullin talked to Democrats and accepted their praise. That is the level of political seriousness on display: not whether ICE is coercive, but whether a Cabinet official sounds sufficiently tribal while overseeing coercion.

The article’s framing lets that distortion stand. It treats friendliness with Democrats as the scandal and the enforcement order as background noise. That is backward. If an agency is suspending vehicle stops after deaths, the central question is not whether its director plays well with the base. It is why this agency has the power to terrorize communities in the first place.

Democrats Praising a Pause Is Not a Strategy

Josh Shapiro and Wes Moore praising Mullin may reflect a temporary relief from a specific ICE practice, but praise is a weak response to a system that should not exist in its current form. Celebrating a narrower enforcement move inside ICE does not confront the underlying institution. It normalizes the agency by treating a partial retreat as statesmanship.

That is the democratic coalition’s recurring mistake: applauding small mercy inside a violent structure while leaving the structure intact. If ICE is the problem, the answer is not better optics, better management, or nicer rapport with a DHS director. The answer is abolition.

The Real Pattern

This story exposes how authoritarian politics survives through personnel drama and ritualized outrage. The base gets told to rage at disloyal tone. Democrats get invited to reward the least brutal version of the same machinery. Meanwhile, the state keeps the power to stop vehicles, police movement, and enforce fear.

That is the pattern: cruelty protected by bureaucracy, and misdirection protected by commentary about personality. The system does not need consensus to keep working. It only needs enough officials willing to manage it, enough opponents willing to praise its softer edges, and enough coverage willing to mistake that for politics.
