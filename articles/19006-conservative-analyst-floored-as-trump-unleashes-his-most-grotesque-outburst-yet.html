Conservative analyst Bill Kristol was floored on Saturday after President Donald Trump unleashed his “most grotesque” outburst yet.

Trump posted on Truth Social that he is “glad” former FBI Director and Special Counsel Robert Mueller had passed away over the weekend. Mueller, who had been battling Parkinson’s disease, led the 2016 investigation into ties between Trump’s presidential campaign and the Russian government, and issued a report that concluded the Russians favored Trump to win, even though it stopped short of calling the efforts “collusion.”

“Robert Mueller just died. Good, I’m glad he’s dead. He can no longer hurt innocent people! President DONALD J. TRUMP,” Trump wrote.

Kristol, editor-at-large for The Bulwark, discussed Trump’s comments on a new episode of the “Bulwark Takes” podcast with host Tim Miller.

“Nothing’s unimaginable, obviously, in the era of Trump and in the character of Trump, but it is on the edge of the worst things he’s said and the most grotesque,” Kristol said.

He noted that it seems unlikely Trump will face any pushback from the Republican caucus.

“Will there be anyone who attempts to preserve any sense of dignity and respect for people who serve their country in public life?” Kristol asked.

Our Analysis:

The Grotesque Dance of Power and Decorum: Trump, Mueller, and the Republican Response

In the latest episode of political theater that seems to defy even the most elastic boundaries of decorum, former President Donald Trump’s comments on the passing of Robert Mueller, the former FBI Director and Special Counsel, have ignited a firestorm of criticism and reflection. As dissected in the article detailing conservative analyst Bill Kristol’s reaction, the situation is layered, complex, and demands a nuanced analysis beyond the surface-level shock and awe.

Who Holds the Institutional Power?

At first glance, the power dynamics might seem straightforward: Trump, a former president and a towering figure in the Republican Party, wields significant influence both within the party and among the public. Yet, his current position is one of an outsider looking in, having lost the institutional power of the presidency. Conversely, the Republican caucus, as part of the legislative branch, holds tangible legislative power but appears reticent or powerless to counter Trump’s influence. This apparent power imbalance is at the heart of the issue.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

The decision in question is Trump’s unfiltered expression of gladness at Mueller’s death, an action solely attributable to Trump. The outcome is multifaceted: it has exacerbated the polarization within American politics, further eroded the norms of public discourse, and spotlighted the Republican Party’s internal conflict regarding how to manage Trump’s provocations.

Misdirected Responsibility?

The article’s framing primarily casts Trump as the provocateur and Kristol as the critic, questioning whether the Republican caucus will act against Trump’s incendiary rhetoric. This framing, while accurate in its depiction of events, perhaps inadvertently shifts the focus away from a more systemic issue: the broader political and media ecosystem that amplifies and rewards such outbursts.

The responsibility for Trump’s comments lies solely with him. However, the larger question of why such comments are made and publicized with little to no consequence speaks to a systemic failure that extends beyond an individual or party. It implicates the media, the political elite, and, by extension, the electorate itself.

Analyzing the Republican Response

Kristol’s skepticism about the Republican caucus’s likelihood to push back against Trump is telling. It reflects a party deeply entangled with Trump’s brand of politics, suggesting that the institutional power within the Republican Party is not leveraged to uphold decorum or accountability but is instead held hostage by the fear of political reprisal.

The inaction or silence of the Republican caucus is not a sign of powerlessness but a deliberate choice, one that prioritizes political expediency over principle. The caucus, theoretically, has the means to censure or distance itself from Trump’s remarks but chooses not to, indicating a tacit complicity or at least a calculated acquiescence.

Conclusion: The Real Culprits

The grotesqueness of Trump’s outburst is undeniable. However, focusing solely on Trump or expecting the Republican caucus to act against their perceived self-interest misses the larger, systemic issue. The real story here is not just about one man’s lack of decorum or another’s expectation of decency; it’s about a political and media landscape that fosters, amplifies, and rewards such behavior.

In this light, misplaced criticism or expectations serve only to obfuscate the root causes. The responsibility rests not only on the shoulders of those who speak or remain silent but also on the very structure that grants them the stage and the microphone. To address the problem, one must look beyond individuals and consider the systemic reforms necessary to change the incentives and consequences within American politics.
