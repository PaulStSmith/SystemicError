Trump admin dealt major blow as migrant's trial canceled and 'vindictiveness' hearing set

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Salvadoran migrant who lived in the U.S. legally with a work permit and was erroneously deported to El Salvador, is seen wearing a Chicago Bulls hat, in this handout image obtained by Reuters on April 9, 2025. Abrego Garcia Family/Handout via REUTERS

President Donald Trump’s legal war on a Salvadoran immigrant was dealt a bitter blow on Friday, as a federal judge in Tennessee canceled the current date for his trial on human smuggling charges — and will instead hold a hearing on the legality of the prosecution.

The order, by U.S. District Judge Waverly Crenshaw, was first reported by NBC Washington’s Paul Wagner. It comes after Kilmar Abrego Garcia’s legal team presented evidence that he was being targeted for selective and vindictive prosecution.

“Once a defendant establishes a prima facie showing of vindictiveness, ‘a presumption arises in defendant’s favor,’” wrote Crenshaw, an appointee of former President Barack Obama. “The Court has already found that Abrego has made such a showing, entitling him to discovery and an evidentiary hearing on why the government is prosecuting him ... Given this, the burden has shifted to the government.”

Crenshaw added that if the one-day evidentiary hearing on vindictive prosecution, now set for Jan. 28, goes against the government, the charges against Abrego could be dismissed outright.

It’s the latest setback for the Trump administration in the Abrego case, after a court ruling earlier this month that he must be released from federal lockup.

Abrego, who has lived for years with his family in Maryland, was mistakenly deported to the infamous CECOT megaprison in El Salvador earlier this year, despite a standing court order prohibiting his removal to that country. The Trump administration has claimed he is involved with the criminal gang MS-13, based on a hearsay allegation from a now-suspended police detective. Abrego has denied any gang involvement.

After months of the government claiming it lacked jurisdiction to repatriate Abrego to the United States, officials finally bowed to public outcry and brought him back, but immediately charged him with gang-related trafficking offenses. Officials also vowed to deport him to another country; Abrego has stated he would accept deportation to neighboring Costa Rica, but the administration has resisted this and instead, repeatedly and fruitlessly, tried to get various African countries to take him in.

To fight claims of vindictive prosecution, the Trump administration has asserted that no one in a politically appointed office was involved with the decision to press charges against Abrego, but recent filings suggest this is not true.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration’s Legal Quagmire: A Case of Selective Prosecution?

In a revealing twist to President Donald Trump’s aggressive immigration policy, a federal judge in Tennessee has thrown a wrench into the machinery of the administration’s legal battle against Salvadoran immigrant Kilmar Abrego Garcia. The recent decision by U.S. District Judge Waverly Crenshaw to cancel the trial date for human smuggling charges against Abrego and instead hold a hearing on the legality of the prosecution is a stark embodiment of the judicial pushback against what appears to be a selective and vindictive prosecution.

Selective Prosecution: A Dangerous Precedent

The heart of this matter lies in the claim, supported by Crenshaw, that Abrego was targeted for selective and vindictive prosecution. This isn’t merely a procedural hiccup; it’s a fundamental challenge to the integrity of the legal system. When a defendant can show evidence of being singled out by the prosecutorial powers that be, it raises alarming questions about the fairness and impartiality of the justice system. The fact that Crenshaw, an appointee of former President Barack Obama, has found sufficient prima facie evidence to entertain such a hearing shifts the burden of proof onto the government. This isn’t just a setback for the Trump administration; it’s a litmus test for the rule of law.

The Troubling Case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia

Abrego’s saga is a disheartening narrative of alleged governmental overreach. Mistakenly deported to the notorious CECOT megaprison in El Salvador—a move in direct violation of a standing court order—his ordeal puts a human face on the collateral damage of aggressive immigration policies. The Trump administration’s insistence on painting Abrego as an MS-13 affiliate without solid evidence and its subsequent scramble to find a country—any country—to deport him to after being forced to repatriate him underscores a disturbing readiness to circumvent due process in the name of immigration control.

Questionable Claims of Non-Political Prosecution

The administration’s defense that no politically appointed officials were involved in the decision to prosecute Abrego rings hollow in the face of recent filings suggesting otherwise. This assertion, now seemingly contradicted by emerging evidence, paints a troubling picture of possible political interference in prosecutorial decisions. If the judiciary finds that the prosecution of Abrego was indeed vindictive and politically motivated, it would be a damning indictment of the Trump administration’s approach to immigration enforcement.

Conclusion: A Call for Judicial Vigilance

The upcoming evidentiary hearing on January 28 could potentially lead to the dismissal of charges against Abrego. Beyond the immediate implications for Abrego and his family, this case serves as a critical reminder of the judiciary’s role in checking potential abuses of prosecutorial power. As this case unfolds, it will undoubtedly continue to serve as a barometer for the health of the American legal system under the strain of political pressures. The Abrego case isn’t just a legal battle; it’s a test of America’s commitment to justice, fairness, and the rule of law.
