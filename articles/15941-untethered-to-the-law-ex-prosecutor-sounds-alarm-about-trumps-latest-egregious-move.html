'Untethered to the law': Ex-prosecutor sounds alarm about Trump’s latest 'egregious' move

Attorney and author Shan Wu is writing in the Daily Beast that Trump is demanding the power to prosecute individuals and whole groups of people alike without either the benefit of court or legal language.

Trump is using executive orders at “a record-breaking” pace to “further personal grievances” that are untethered to the law,” says Wu. This includes his presidential memoranda accusing his own former officials, Miles Taylor and Chris Krebs, of crimes including, in the case of Taylor, treason — which carries a potential death sentence.

The president, who is himself a convicted felon, accused Taylor of treason in an order entitled “Addressing Risks Associated with an Egregious Leaker and Disseminator of Falsehoods.” According to Trump, Taylor “illegally published classified conversations,” and claims it is forbidden for “a government employee to improperly “discloses sensitive information for the purposes of personal enrichment and undermining our foreign policy, national security, and Government effectiveness" to sow chaos and distrust in Government.

Trump adds this sort of conduct “could properly be characterized as treasonous and as possibly violating the Espionage Act,” although Wu claims Trump’s use of the word “treason” has “about as much legal precision as someone flipping off another driver in a fit of road rage.”

The definition of treason “shall consist only in levying War against [the United States], or in adhering to their Enemies, giving them Aid and Comfort,” which Wu says is nowhere near the way Trump wants to use it. But when government authorities refuse to address the abuse of the Presidential office to press personal grievances, they’re saying the president “is free to indict, adjudicate and impose punishments for criminal accusations against a group of people—or a single person like Taylor or Krebs—through a mere decree.”

To be specific, Taylor wrote an opinion piece for a newspaper, like many New York Times and local newspaper guest writers. Wu says this does not count as “levying War,” however badly Trump may want it to.

“Trump’s accusations against (Taylor and Krebs) appear nonsensical because they don’t fit the law. But maybe that’s the very point, namely that the Trump administration wants to change the law by any means possible,” Wu warns.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Quest for Unfettered Prosecutorial Power: A Dangerous Precedent

In an op-ed that reads like a thriller—only far more terrifying because it's real—Shan Wu, writing for the Daily Beast, unveils a chilling narrative about former President Donald Trump's alleged attempts to wield prosecutorial power to an unprecedented and arguably unconstitutional extent. Trump, according to Wu, is not just bending the rules; he's attempting to rewrite the very fabric of U.S. legal principles to settle personal scores—all through the misuse of executive orders.

Trump's Misuse of Executive Orders: Wu claims Trump is using executive orders at a "record-breaking" pace as a vehicle to "further personal grievances." This isn't governance; it's vendetta by fiat. The specific accusations against former officials Miles Taylor and Chris Krebs, whom Trump accuses of crimes including treason, are not just alarming because of their severity but because of the sheer absurdity and legal inaccuracy of the claims.

The Mischaracterization of Treason: The charge of treason, as Wu rightly points out, has a very specific definition under U.S. law—one that in no way encompasses the actions of Taylor or Krebs. To equate publishing an opinion piece with "levying War against [the United States]" is not just a stretch; it's an outright contortion of legal language. Trump's application of the term is likened to "someone flipping off another driver in a fit of road rage"—a comparison as damning as it is apt.

The Dangerous Precedent: What Wu's analysis brings to the forefront is not just the absurdity of Trump's accusations but the terrifying precedent they set. If a president can, through sheer will and disregard for legal standards, indict and adjudicate through "a mere decree," where does that leave the separation of powers? Where does it leave the rule of law? This isn't a slippery slope; it's a freefall into autocracy.

The Silence of Government Authorities: Perhaps the most alarming aspect of this saga, as highlighted by Wu, is the inaction of government authorities in the face of such blatant abuse of presidential power. This silence is complicity. It sends a message that the president, or at least this president, is above the law—a notion antithetical to everything the United States purports to stand for.

Conclusion: Wu's warning is clear: Trump's actions represent not just a personal vendetta but a fundamental threat to the legal and constitutional framework of the United States. It's a narrative that demands not just attention but action. The question now is not just about the legal limits of executive orders but about the very soul of American democracy. What Wu lays bare is a scenario where the law is mutable, justice is subjective, and power, unchecked, can be wielded as a weapon against perceived enemies. This isn't just a systemic error; it's a systemic failure.
