Donald Trump Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins found herself desperately searching her memory on CNN for one trade deal the president has pulled off when asked about his boast in a Time magazine interview.

Appearing on CNN's "State of the Union," with host Dana Bash, the very talkative Rollins responded to a question about Trump's much derided "200 deals" boast that has since fallen apart by promising something might be coming soon.

"The president told Time magazine, quote, 'I have made 200 deals," Bash pointed out to her guest. "He didn't give us any details about what those could be, what countries he's talking about. Has he actually inked any trade deals and with whom?"

Rollins first pivoted to an earlier discussion about the price of eggs –– a hot topic in the 2024 election –– which led host Bash to interrupt with, "Let's focus on the trade and on the deals."

"Yes, for sure," Rollins replied. "On the deals, we have 100 countries that are knocking on the door, I believe. I'm not in the room, I'm not negotiating the trade deals, but my understanding is we should have several this week that are coming forward that are very, very close."

" China is a very important one," he quickly added. "Every day we are in conversation with China, along with those other 99 countries that have come to the table. Many countries have already flown to Washington and are meeting with [Commerce] Secretary Lutnick, meeting with [Treasury] Secretary Bessent, meeting with our USTR Jamison Greer. And there is a tremendous amount of progress on all fronts that we'll see again more this week."

Pressed by Bash, "You said that there are talks with China every day. Who are the talks with and at what level because the Chinese are saying that's not true," Rollins demurred.

"Well, according to our team in Washington, the conversations are ongoing regarding multiples of trade," she attempted. "But here –– multiples of the trade goods that are coming out and going in –– but the bottom line with China is this they need us more than we need them. They need our money, they need our markets, they need our consumers and all of the above. So the president has been, I believe, exactly correct. I mean, he is the ultimate dealmaker."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Spin: Brooke Rollins and the Phantom Trade Deals

In a recent CNN appearance that can only be described as a masterclass in obfuscation, Brooke Rollins, the Agriculture Secretary under Donald Trump, embarked on a verbal journey to nowhere when pressed about Trump's dubious claim of having orchestrated "200 deals." This claim, which appeared in a Time magazine interview, has since crumbled under the slightest scrutiny, much like a house of cards in a stiff breeze.

The Art of the Pivot

Rollins' initial response to a direct question about these alleged trade deals was nothing short of a dance—a pivot to the price of eggs, of all things. This move, while creative, was transparently a stalling tactic. It's the political equivalent of pointing at nothing and yelling "Look over there!" before running in the opposite direction.

The Phantom Deals

When cornered by CNN's Dana Bash to stay on topic, Rollins embarked on a vague journey, mentioning "100 countries that are knocking on the door" and alluding to imminent deals "very, very close" to fruition. The mention of China as a "very important one" was a fleeting attempt to add weight to her claims. Yet, when pressed for specifics, especially regarding the daily talks with China—a claim the Chinese have outright denied—Rollins' response was nebulous at best.

The Ultimate Dealmaker?

Rollins’ assertion that Trump is "the ultimate dealmaker" is, under these circumstances, laughably hyperbolic. The true hallmark of a dealmaker is not in the boasting of nonexistent victories, but in the tangible, verifiable successes that can be presented when asked. By this measure, the administration’s achievements in this arena seem as substantial as a mirage.

The Reality

The reality here is stark and unmistakable. Rollins' appearance on CNN was less about providing clarity on Trump's claims and more about deflecting and disseminating uncertainty. This strategy, while not new in the political playbook, is dishearteningly effective at muddying the waters. It relies on the hope that the audience will be too overwhelmed by the barrage of unsubstantiated claims to question their veracity.

Conclusion

In the absence of concrete details or evidence to support the claim of "200 deals," the public is left with more questions than answers. This episode is a reminder of the importance of holding public figures accountable for their statements, demanding clarity, and not settling for the smoke and mirrors of political rhetoric. Rollins' performance on CNN was not only a disservice to the truth but a glaring example of how far some will go to avoid admitting that their emperor has no clothes—or in this case, no deals.
