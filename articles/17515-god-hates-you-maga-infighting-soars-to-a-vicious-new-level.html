'God hates you': MAGA infighting soars to a 'vicious' new level

By Paulo Santos

The Theater of Cruelty: How MAGA Infighting Masks Broader Destructive Agendas

The Theater of Cruelty: How MAGA Infighting Masks Broader Destructive Agendas

Power Play Disguised as Personal Feuds

In the realm of far-right politics, figures like Candace Owens and her MAGA counterparts do not merely engage in disputes; they wage wars of personal destruction that serve as smoke screens for deeper, more systemic issues. The recent escalation of tensions between Owens and other MAGA influencers, as described in a Bulwark article by Will Sommer, is emblematic of a movement that thrives on internal conflict to distract from its lack of coherent policy or constructive political agenda. Owens’s baseless accusation against Erika Kirk over the death of Charlie Kirk is not just a personal attack but a strategic move within a chaotic power structure.

The Role of Media Amplification

The media’s role in covering these internal conflicts often amplifies the spectacle rather than scrutinizing the implications. By focusing on the “vicious” attacks and personal dramas, coverage can inadvertently elevate these figures’ profiles and normalize their extreme tactics. Reports that highlight phrases like “only crazy can match crazy” and recount specific insults traded on social platforms contribute to a circus-like atmosphere, diverting attention from the ideological and practical impacts of the movement’s broader agenda.

Misdirection and Scapegoating

This internal strife within the MAGA movement, particularly the attacks on Owens, serves as a convenient scapegoat mechanism. By vilifying Owens, other influencers can position themselves as slightly less extreme, which subtly shifts the Overton window in their favor. The real danger here is not just in the personal destruction of public figures but in how these feuds misdirect public attention from policies and actions that can have real and harmful impacts on society.

Consequences of Character Assassination as Strategy

The strategy of character assassination within the MAGA ranks reveals a crucial aspect of its operational tactics: it is built on and sustained by creating enemies, both within and outside its ranks. This approach is not only about consolidating power but also about ensuring continuous engagement from its base. The emotional intensity of these conflicts keeps supporters activated not by loyalty to substantive policy but by a never-ending series of vendettas and grievances.

Broader Political Pattern and Systemic Insight

The perpetual infighting among MAGA influencers like Owens, Kraus, and Loomer is a microcosm of a larger, more disturbing trend in American politics where conflict is not just a byproduct, but a tool of governance. This strategy of division and distraction allows for the advancement of agendas that often go unchecked because the spectacle takes center stage. The real systemic error here is not just the personal moral failings of these figures, but a political culture that rewards sensationalism over substantive dialogue and critical analysis. The end result is a populace more attuned to personal insults than to policies that shape their lives, a dangerous shift in democratic engagement.
