Progressive Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) unleashed a furious attack on MAGA lawmaker Rep. Byron Donalds (R-FL), who she said "made a fool of himself on national television."

Donalds was asked on “Meet the Press Now" whether he thinks everyone — citizens and non-citizens — are entitled to due process.

“When it comes to due process, that is a privilege reserved for American citizens,” Donalds replied. "When people are in our country illegally, there is a semblance of due process, but not nearly at the level of a United States citizen, of an American. There are two different standards."

Donalds insisted that people who come to the country are guests. And that privilege "can be revoked," he said.

However, Congress' website clarifies that the Fifth Amendment "provides that no person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law."

Ocasio-Cortez slammed her colleague over his remarks in a fundraising email Monday evening.

"Representative Byron Donalds made a fool of himself on national television, saying that constitutional rights are only for US citizens. That is incorrect," she said.

Ocasio-Cortez noted Constitutional rights are "about the soil" of the United States.

"This is the land of the free. It's not that there are some people that have rights and other people that don't have rights. We are a nation of due process. The government is required to conduct itself along the standards of due process," she noted. "It is the government that is required to uphold standards of respecting free speech. It is the government that is required to honor and respect the requirements around illegal search and seizure. Don't let them think that it's on you. It's on them."

Ocasio-Cortez accused Donalds of "going on television trying to rip the Constitution apart, trying to tell people that America is not America."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Clash Between Ideologies: Rep. Ocasio-Cortez vs. Rep. Donalds

In an era where political polarization seems to deepen with each passing moment, the recent exchange between Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and Rep. Byron Donalds (R-FL) encapsulates a fundamental disagreement on the interpretation of the U.S. Constitution and the rights it guarantees. This confrontation, far from being a mere squabble between political rivals, underscores a profound and troubling misunderstanding of constitutional law by some of our elected officials.

Rep. Byron Donalds' Misguided Commentary

Donalds' assertion on “Meet the Press Now” that due process is a "privilege reserved for American citizens" is not just incorrect; it's dangerously misleading. Suggesting that there are "two different standards" of due process for citizens and non-citizens fundamentally misrepresents the clear language of the Fifth Amendment. This misguided statement implies a tiered approach to human rights that is both unjust and un-American.

The Constitution is unequivocal in its wording: "no person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law." Notice the deliberate choice of the word “person” over “citizen.” This is not an oversight or an accident. It is a clear indication that the rights encapsulated within are not exclusive to American citizens but extend to anyone within the borders of the United States.

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez's Defense of Constitutional Rights

Ocasio-Cortez's rebuttal to Donalds is not just a defense of her colleague's flawed interpretation but a rallying cry for the preservation of American values. Her emphasis on constitutional rights being "about the soil" of the United States rather than the citizenship status of an individual is a poignant reminder of what truly makes America the land of the free. Her assertion that these rights are non-negotiable and universal within U.S. borders is a testament to the foundation upon which the United States was built—a nation of laws and due process.

By accusing Donalds of attempting to "rip the Constitution apart," Ocasio-Cortez is highlighting a disturbing trend among some lawmakers to reinterpret or outright ignore the foundational principles of American governance when it suits their political agenda. This is not merely a matter of differing opinions but a dangerous maneuver that threatens the very core of American democracy.

The Real Issue at Hand

This confrontation goes beyond two politicians at odds. It's a microcosm of a larger battle being waged in the halls of Congress, on national television, and in the streets of America. It's about whether the United States will adhere to its principles or allow them to be eroded by misinformation and political expediency.

Donalds' comments and the ideology they represent pose a real threat to the understanding and application of constitutional rights. If elected officials can blatantly misinterpret fundamental rights, what stops them from attempting to restrict or revoke those rights from certain groups?

Conclusion

The exchange between Ocasio-Cortez and Donalds is a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance in the face of attempts to undermine constitutional rights. It's imperative that we, as a society, remain informed and push back against any narrative that seeks to diminish the protections guaranteed by the Constitution. Let us not be swayed by the rhetoric of division and misinformation. The principles of due process and equal protection under the law must stand inviolable, now and forever.
