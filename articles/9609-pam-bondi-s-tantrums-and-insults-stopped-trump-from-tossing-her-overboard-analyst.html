Pam Bondi's 'tantrums and insults' stopped Trump from tossing her 'overboard': analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 14, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The “Mean Girl” Strategy: Trump’s Legal Enforcer

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s surly performance before a Senate Oversight Committee last week provided a clue as to why Donald Trump has kept her around despite pressure from a very vocal faction of the disgruntled MAGA base.

That is the opinion of Salon’s Amanda Marcotte who, on Monday, compared Trump’s pick as the nation’s top lawyer to Regina George, the queen bee of the popular film “Mean Girls” — and added, “the resemblance was too strong to be a coincidence.”

“Bondi’s resemblance to the blonde brat from millennial movie lore wasn’t just cringeworthy in a woman pushing 60,” she wrote. “It also solves a mystery that’s persisted for months,” adding that Bondi’s “mean girl” act is just the type of attitude the president admires over substance.

As she said, Trump loyalists have been baying for Bondi’s head because they don’t feel she has been going after Trump’s perceived enemies fast enough and they are frustrated by her performance since being confirmed.

“They’re mad she won’t release the Epstein files, the voluminous documentation collected by the FBI into the criminal goings-on of deceased sex criminal Jeffrey Epstein,” she wrote. “They can’t seem to grasp her reason, even though it’s obvious to everyone outside of the Trump cult: Her boss is reportedly in the files, and what’s documented probably doesn’t look good.”

She added that, “MAGA commentators got mad at Bondi when she expressed an openness to prosecuting ‘hate speech‘, even though she was clearly talking about criticism of Trump and not the traditional bigotries over which the right is so defensive.”

According to the columnist, Trump is definitely “feeling the heat” over Bondi, shown by his now infamous Truth Social post in which he urged her to speed up the prosecution of his critics, which is why her Senate appearance was critical to her survival.

Writing that Bondi’s efforts to “derail the proceedings with her spoiled princess routine” was a success in Trump’s eyes, she added, “Bondi likely knows harassing innocent people isn’t quite enough to please her mercurial boss, thus her ridiculous performance in front of the Senate last Tuesday. Showing off her mean girl skills to Trump involved two adolescent tactics: Tantrums and insults.”

“MAGA influencers have a lot of power over the White House. One reason Trump’s approval ratings on every major issue keep falling is that he is far more interested in pleasing conspiracy theorists and bigots than he is about the opinions of average Americans. So it’s noteworthy that he seems to be loyal to Bondi, even though a cheap way to excite his base would be to throw her overboard,” she suggested.

“He wants a mean girl to be his enforcer, someone who can bring sorority president energy to the task of lying and helping him break the law. When it comes to shamelessness, for Bondi, there’s no limit. And that’s what Trump loves about her.”

Our Analysis:

The “Mean Girl” Strategy: Trump’s Legal Enforcer

In what can only be described as a theatrical display of defiance, Attorney General Pam Bondi’s recent Senate Oversight Committee appearance has sparked a flurry of analysis and speculation. The comparison drawn by Salon’s Amanda Marcotte between Bondi and Regina George from “Mean Girls” is not just a clever quip but a scathing indictment of the current state of political discourse and strategy within the Trump administration.

The Age of the Mean Girl Act

The assertion that Donald Trump values attitude over substance in his legal enforcer is both alarming and unsurprising. Bondi, pushing 60, embracing a “mean girl” persona is emblematic of a broader, more disturbing trend: the prioritization of loyalty and spectacle over legal and ethical standards. The critique of Bondi’s “mean girl” tactics as a means to curry favor with Trump highlights a fundamental misalignment in the administration’s values. The notion that a serious legal professional would resort to “tantrums and insults” to navigate Senate proceedings is not only cringeworthy but a dire commentary on the state of governance.

The MAGA Base’s Discontent

The dissatisfaction among the MAGA base regarding Bondi’s performance—and their clamoring for the release of the Epstein files—underscores a perverse irony. Their frustration stems not from a desire for justice or transparency but from a vendetta-fueled witch hunt against Trump’s adversaries. Marcotte’s observation that Trump’s allegiance to Bondi, despite mounting pressure to disavow her, speaks volumes about his priorities. It’s clear: Trump’s preference for a “mean girl” enforcer over a principled attorney general serves his interests, not those of justice or the American people.

The Cult of Personality and Its Discontents

Trump’s manipulation of MAGA influencers and his base’s loyalty to him, even at the expense of competent governance, reveals a dangerous cult of personality. The fact that Trump’s approval ratings continue to plummet across major issues, yet he remains fixated on appeasing the fringes of his base, is a stark reminder of his administration’s detachment from reality and responsibility. Bondi’s Senate performance, rather than being an outlier, is a calculated move within this context—a demonstration of loyalty to Trump’s brand of politics over the nation’s legal and ethical standards.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

The loyalty Trump demands—and receives—from figures like Bondi is indicative of a broader systemic failure. This isn’t merely about one attorney general’s questionable tactics; it’s about an administration’s wholesale embrace of spectacle, loyalty, and vindictiveness over substantive governance. The “mean girl” strategy, as employed by Bondi, is not just a personal failing but a symptom of a much larger malaise—a political culture that rewards obfuscation, division, and loyalty to individuals over principles.

In the final analysis, Bondi’s Senate appearance and the dynamics it reveals between her and Trump are a microcosm of a disturbing trend in American politics: the erosion of democratic norms and the rise of a governance style that is more concerned with personal vendettas and loyalty tests than with serving the American public. This is not just a systemic error but a dangerous trajectory that requires urgent correction.
