Trump crammed a 'devil’s deal' down Republicans’ throats: conservative

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Trump’s Kleptocracy Is Not a Bug. It Is the Operating System.

Power Sits With the Thief

The source is not really about Bret Stephens’s discomfort. It is about Donald Trump holding institutional power so brazenly that even some Republicans now have to decide whether they want to live inside the theft or admit what they are backing. The reported facts are plain enough: Trump’s family reportedly enriched itself by billions, Trump’s slush-fund settlement collapsed, and his former personal attorney Todd Blanche, now acting as interim attorney general, allegedly schemed to give the Trump family immunity from audits.

That is not drift. That is the state being bent around a family business.

Corruption With a Flag on It

What Stephens and the panel are circling is not ordinary Washington sleaze. It is naked self-dealing at the center of executive power, defended by the people who are supposed to police it. Calling it “corruption” is accurate, but even that understates the political fact pattern. When the president’s inner circle uses government office to shield private enrichment, the question is no longer whether rules were bent. The question is who still pretends the rules exist.

The answer, inconveniently, is many of the people who know better.

The Devil’s Bargain Is the Rot

Stephens tries to salvage the logic of Republican accommodation by treating it as tragic realism: maybe the party had to swallow Trump to get judges, sanctions, NATO, whatever scraps might still be extracted from the wreckage. That is not a defense. It is a confession that the party has converted principle into a trading card.

Lindsey Graham is offered as the model: stay close, stay useful, maybe cash out one useful result before the whole thing collapses. Liz Cheney is offered as the foil: tell the truth, lose your seat, become politically irrelevant. That contrast is the whole disease. In Trump’s GOP, moral clarity is punished and compliance is rewarded. The party’s elite lesson is not loyalty to the country. It is loyalty to access.

The Hunter Biden Smokescreen Worked Exactly As Designed

The source notes that Trump’s corruption “puts into the shade” the years Republicans spent obsessing over Hunter Biden’s dealings. That matters. The Hunter Biden fixation was never just hypocrisy; it was a deliberate method of moral laundering. Inflate a weaker scandal, repeat it until it feels existential, and use that manufactured outrage to dull attention to the actual power theft happening in front of everyone.

This is how reactionary politics works now: smaller accusations are used as camouflage for larger crimes. The goal is not consistency. It is distraction.

Principle Is Not Power, and That Is the Point

Stephens says Cheney sacrificed herself “on an altar of truth” and is “not an influential person anymore.” That line is supposed to sound tragic. It should sound damning. In a healthy political order, truth should not be career suicide and corruption should not be the path to leverage. But Trumpism has inverted those incentives, and too many Republican notables have adapted to the inversion instead of breaking it.

So the real story is not that some Republicans are uneasy. It is that the party has accepted a structure where access to power is more valuable than constitutional restraint, and where the cost of telling the truth is political exile.

The Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like before it fully hardens: a leader turns public office into private leverage, enlists institutional gatekeepers to normalize the theft, and leaves the party elite arguing over whether they can do a little damage control and still call themselves serious. They cannot.

A party that treats kleptocracy as a negotiation strategy has already surrendered the premise of democratic government.
