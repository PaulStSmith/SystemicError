'I don't even think this Supreme Court can turn a blind eye to this': Trump put on notice

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough criticized President Donald Trump's deployment of National Guard troops in Democratic cities as counterfactual and plainly unconstitutional.

The president has ordered troops into Los Angeles and Washington, D.C., ostensibly to protect against violent crime, and has threatened to send more to Baltimore and Chicago, but the "Morning Joe" host said those Democratic cities are far safer than areas governed by Republicans in the South and elsewhere.

"The chance of of having violent acts committed upon you in [House Speaker] Mike Johnson's Louisiana, in red state Louisiana, red state that Donald Trump carried and every Republican has carried since Bill Clinton," Scarborough said. "The chances of of being murdered in Louisiana 400 times higher than in California. Let me say that again, let me underline that again: You have a 400 percent higher chance of being murdered in red state Louisiana, Mike Johnson's home state, than you do on the left coast in Gavin Newsom's California."

"There is no emergency, there is no logic to Chicago, to San Francisco, if you're looking at the numbers, if you're looking at data, I don't even think this Supreme Court can turn a blind eye to this," he added. "They just can't, because data is data. Numbers are numbers, and the numbers are clear, and the numbers don't justify – [there is] no emergency. Send those troops to Shreveport, Louisiana, send them to Mike Johnson's district. Send them to Little Rock, Arkansas, send them to Memphis and Nashville, Tennessee. Send them to red states where they need them."

All of the cities Trump has targeted have Black mayors, as well, and each of them have seen their crime rates decline in recent years.

"All of them have lower crime rates per capita than the red state cities I mentioned," Scarborough said.

"Get them away from the Apple store in Georgetown, right, get them to southeast," Scarborough added. "Get them to places where families want to be safe, but in a partnership with with the mayor, with the D.C. city council, with the very people that that that run that city day in and day out."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Scarborough's Critique of Trump's National Guard Deployment

Joe Scarborough's critique of President Donald Trump's deployment of National Guard troops to Democratic cities slices through the administration's justifications with the precision of a scalpel, exposing a blend of political maneuvering and constitutional overreach. His argument, grounded in stark statistical contrasts and constitutional principles, deserves a meticulous unpacking.

Counterfactual Justifications

Scarborough points out the elephant in the room: the deployment of troops is ostensibly for protecting against violent crime, yet the statistics tell a different story. The invocation of violent crime rates as the basis for this action falls apart under scrutiny. When Scarborough highlights the disparity in crime rates—specifically, murder rates between Democratic and Republican cities—he isn't just splitting hairs. He's shining a light on the misuse of federal power for what appears to be partisan gain.

The claim that cities like Los Angeles and Washington, D.C., are under some sort of siege by violent crime is not just misleading; it's a strategic falsehood. The assertion that these cities are more dangerous than their Republican counterparts is not just wrong; it's demonstrably false. Scarborough's use of data to debunk this myth is a masterclass in fact-based argumentation.

Unconstitutional Overreach

Scarborough doesn't mince words when he labels the deployment "plainly unconstitutional." The federal government's deployment of troops within the United States has always been a matter of hot debate, but doing so without clear justification or emergency—and seemingly targeting cities based on political affiliation—crosses a line. This isn't just a matter of political disagreement; it's a fundamental challenge to the constitutional balance of power and the principle of federalism.

The Political Undercurrent

The targeting of Democratic cities, all of which have Black mayors, cannot be ignored. This action sends a chilling message about the use of federal power to intimidate and control cities based on their political leadership rather than any objective criteria of need or emergency. Scarborough's call to instead deploy resources to cities genuinely struggling with violent crime—regardless of their political leadership—highlights the partisan selection of targets for this deployment.

Conclusion: A Call for Rationality and Constitutionality

Scarborough ends on a note that is both a plea and a challenge: to redirect resources to where they are genuinely needed, in partnership with local authorities, and grounded in factual assessments of crime. His commentary is not just a critique but a call to action—for a return to rationality, constitutionality, and genuine concern for public safety over political gamesmanship.

In essence, Scarborough's analysis is a scathing indictment of the Trump administration's actions, grounded in a deep respect for facts, constitutional principles, and the genuine safety needs of American communities. It's a narrative that demands not just attention but action, in defense of the foundational principles of American governance.
