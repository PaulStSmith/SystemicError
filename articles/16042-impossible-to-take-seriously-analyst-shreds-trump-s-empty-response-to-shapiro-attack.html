'Impossible to take seriously': Analyst shreds Trump's 'empty' response to Shapiro attack

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 17, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks as he attends a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 10, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump's condemnation of an arson attack on Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro's residence on Monday earned no applause from an analyst at The Atlantic, who called his response "empty."

When asked about the attack, Trump referred to the suspect as "probably just a whack job" and stressed that "such incidents cannot be tolerated."

"The attacker was not a fan of Trump. I understand, just from what I read and from what I’ve been told, the attacker basically wasn’t a fan of anybody," Trump said. "Certainly a thing like that cannot be allowed to happen."

Vice President JD Vance called the act "really disgusting violence" while Attorney General Pam Bondi expressed relief that Shapiro and his family were not harmed.

But Trump didn't initially comment on the attack over the weekend, only addressing it directly when questioned on Monday.

Ali Breland, staff writer at The Atlantic, wrote Monday night that the president may have been "quick to condemn political violence" — but that "it’s hard to take him at his word."

"These kinds of condemnations of political violence are good. They’re also meaningless—especially when taken in the broader context of Trump’s governing style," wrote Breland.

She questioned whether it was any coincidence that political violence has increased since Trump first ran for office. He praised violence when it was "useful," she noted, and "made leveraging the threat of it endemic to his style of politics." That includes the Jan. 6 riot at the Capitol, where Trump "appeared okay with violence if it helped him maintain the presidency," she wrote.

"All of this is to say that when Trump condemns acts of political violence, it’s impossible to take him seriously. In this specific case, the attack on Shapiro served no clear benefit to Trump, which is why he was able to so quickly speak out against it," wrote Breland. "Compare that with how he’s talked about the [Paul] Pelosi hammer attack, which he has used as fodder to mock the Pelosis. Trump’s relationship to political violence is the same to anything and anyone else in his orbit: If something benefits him, it’s welcome. If not, he might dismiss it."

Sparky's Analysis:

Trump's Condemnation: A Hollow Gesture Amid a History of Incitement

In the wake of an arson attack on Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro's residence, President Donald Trump's response has been dissected and deemed "empty" by critics, reflecting a broader pattern of inconsistency and opportunism in his stance toward political violence. Trump's attempt to distance himself from the attack and its perpetrator by labeling the suspect as "probably just a whack job" and asserting that "such incidents cannot be tolerated" fails to mask the underlying hypocrisy embedded in his history of political engagement.

Trump's Selective Outrage: A Tactical Play?

Trump's belated condemnation, coming only when directly questioned and after a conspicuous silence over the weekend, rings hollow, especially against the backdrop of his turbulent relationship with political violence. His administration, marked by a brazen embrace of violence when politically expedient, has seen a troubling rise in such incidents. The Atlantic's Ali Breland points out this inconsistency, noting Trump's quick shift in tone when addressing acts of violence that offer no clear political benefit to him, as opposed to those he can exploit for his advantage.

A Pattern of Political Opportunism

This pattern is starkly evident in Trump's varied responses to different incidents of political violence. The attack on Shapiro, lacking utility for Trump, was met with a swift denunciation. In contrast, the January 6 Capitol riot and the attack on Paul Pelosi have been treated differently, with Trump appearing to condone or even mock these incidents when it suited his narrative or political objectives. Such selective outrage underscores a cynical manipulation of political violence—condemned when convenient, leveraged when beneficial.

The Broader Implications of Trump's Rhetoric

Trump's cavalier attitude towards political violence not only undermines the gravity of these incidents but also contributes to a toxic political climate where violence is normalized as a tool for political discourse. His rhetoric, laced with insinuations of violence, has arguably emboldened individuals and groups predisposed to such actions, casting a long shadow over the political landscape and raising concerns about the enduring impact of his words.

Conclusion: The Empty Echoes of Trump's Condemnation

In sum, Trump's response to the arson attack on Governor Shapiro's residence is emblematic of a broader, more disturbing trend: a wilful exploitation of political violence when it serves his interests, and a tepid disavowal when it does not. This duality not only reveals a lack of genuine commitment to democratic principles but also poses a significant threat to the fabric of American political life. As such, Trump's condemnations of political violence cannot be taken at face value but rather seen as part of a larger strategy of political opportunism and manipulation.
