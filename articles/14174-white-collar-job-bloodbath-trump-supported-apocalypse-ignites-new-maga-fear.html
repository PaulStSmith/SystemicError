'White-collar job bloodbath': Trump-supported 'apocalypse' ignites new MAGA fear

During President Donald Trump's second term, some of the tech leaders who were critical of him in the past — including Amazon's Jeff Bezos and Facebook's Mark Zuckerberg — have tried to keep the peace with him. And Tesla/SpaceX/X.com leader Elon Musk is a major Trump ally.

Yet tensions exist within the MAGA movement. "War Room" host Steve Bannon isn't shy about attacking Musk, fearing that tech bros could undermine MAGA's populist goals. And now, Bannon is among the MAGA Republicans who is voicing his concerns about Trump's recent support of artificial intelligence (AI).

Axios' Tal Axelrod, in an article published on Thursday, May 29, reports, "The Trump Administration's embrace of AI acceleration is testing MAGA's populist roots, as fears mount of a jobs apocalypse that could destabilize the movement's economic foundation. Why it matters: MAGA leaders are engaged in a high-stakes juggling act — championing American dominance in AI while sounding the alarm over its potential to wipe out millions of jobs for young and working-class Americans. The debate is more nuanced than the Trump Administration's public posture, which has prioritized deregulation and aggressive investment as necessary to outpace China in the global AI race. "

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei recently told Axios that AI could eliminate half of all entry-level white-collar jobs — a warning that, according to Axelrod, "rippled through the MAGA media ecosystem on Wednesday."

On his "War Room" vodcast, Bannon told viewers, "We have to get ahead of this. Or we're going to have mass unemployment, particularly among entry-level people under 30."

Similarly, Turning Point USA's Charlie Kirk, on his radio show, warned, "What you are going to see is one of the most dramatic job displacements, and it's going to be a top issue in the 2028 campaign."

In a May 28 post on X, formerly Twitter, Kirk wrote, "For decades, elites were smug about their economic security compared to blue collar people. 'Learn to code' was the line, while globalism and offshoring hollowed out the industrial base. But now, because of AI, the jobs of elites — and their children — are in grave danger. Dario Amodei, CEO of AI firm Anthropic, predicts an AI bloodbath."

Axios' Jim VandeHei tweeted on the subject as well.

On May 29, VandeHei posted, "MAGA leaders @charliekirk11 @Bannons_WarRoom and others join Antrophic CEO in warning of possible white-collar job bloodbath and its impact on '28 elections."

VandeHei also discussed AI during a May 29 appearance on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," stressing that within the MAGA movement, there is "deep unease" about AI — which, VandeHei told hosts Joe Scarborough and Mika Brzezinski, "could be really problematic for" MAGA in 2028.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the MAGA Movement's AI Conundrum

The latest twist in the ever-evolving narrative of the MAGA movement comes in the form of a technological specter that even its most ardent supporters cannot ignore: Artificial Intelligence (AI). As reported by Tal Axelrod for Axios, a rift has emerged within the ranks of MAGA, centered around the Trump Administration’s enthusiastic embrace of AI acceleration. This development, while showcasing an attempt to assert American dominance in the global tech race, paradoxically threatens the very fabric of the populist economic foundation that MAGA purports to champion.

The Irony of Tech Titans and Populist Rhetoric

First, let's address the elephant in the room: the coziness between certain tech leaders and President Trump. Figures like Jeff Bezos, Mark Zuckerberg, and notably, Elon Musk, have seemingly warmed up to Trump, despite past criticisms. This alliance between tech moguls and a president who rode to power on waves of populist sentiment is, at best, a marriage of convenience, and at worst, a glaring contradiction of the MAGA movement's "anti-elite" posture.

Elon Musk, in particular, stands out as a “major Trump ally,” despite his ventures directly contributing to the tech advancements that now stir anxiety within the movement. This juxtaposition of Musk as both ally and emblem of the feared tech elite encapsulates the MAGA movement's internal contradictions.

The Bannon-Musk Dichotomy

Steve Bannon, a figure synonymous with the populist underpinnings of MAGA, finds himself at odds with Musk and the broader AI agenda. Bannon's concerns are not unfounded. The fear that "tech bros" could undermine MAGA's populist goals speaks to a broader anxiety about the displacement of the very demographic MAGA claims to represent: young and working-class Americans.

The AI Apocalypse: A Populist Nightmare

The crux of the Axios article highlights a looming crisis that transcends political affiliations: the potential for AI to obliterate millions of jobs. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's prediction that AI could eliminate half of all entry-level white-collar jobs is a harrowing prospect. This isn't merely an economic issue; it's a societal time bomb.

Bannon and Charlie Kirk's warnings about mass unemployment and job displacement are not just rhetoric; they are a reflection of a genuine concern that has the potential to redefine the political landscape. Kirk's observation that AI threatens the jobs of "elites — and their children" marks a significant shift in the discourse, suggesting that the AI-induced job crisis will spare no class, thereby challenging the traditional populist vs. elite narrative.

The MAGA Movement's AI Dilemma

The MAGA movement's engagement with AI presents a fascinating paradox. On one hand, there's the desire to maintain American technological supremacy, especially against rivals like China. On the other, there's the palpable fear of the socioeconomic upheaval that such advancements could bring about.

This duality reflects a broader dilemma within conservative populism: the struggle to reconcile the allure of technological and economic progress with the imperative to protect the livelihoods of the "common man." As the 2028 elections approach, the MAGA movement finds itself at a crossroads, navigating the precarious balance between championing AI innovation and mitigating its potentially devastating impact on the workforce.

Conclusion

The Axios article sheds light on the MAGA movement's precarious dance with AI, revealing deep-seated anxieties that could redefine its future trajectory. The irony of tech leaders aligning with a populist president, the division within the movement's ranks, and the existential threat posed by AI to the workforce collectively paint a picture of a movement grappling with the very forces of modernity it seeks to master. As the debate unfolds, it becomes increasingly clear that the MAGA movement's stance on AI is not just a policy position, but a litmus test for its populist credentials in an age of unrelenting technological advancement.
