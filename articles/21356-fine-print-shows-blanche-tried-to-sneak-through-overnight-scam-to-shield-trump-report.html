Fine print shows Blanche tried to ‘sneak through’ overnight ‘scam’ to shield Trump: report

The Fine Print Was the Crime Scene

Misdirection in a Suit and Tie

The source story is simple: Todd Blanche announced that the Justice Department had “rescinded” the anti-weaponization fund tied to payouts for Capitol rioters, but the notice allegedly left Trump’s IRS immunity deal standing. That is the point. The headline action was cleanup; the real work was preservation.

The language is the tell. “Anti-weaponization fund” is political laundering dressed up as bureaucratic terminology. It takes a corrupt bargain and gives it the perfume of principle. The actual subject here is not weaponization. It is whether the state will be bent around Trump’s personal exposure while his allies are handed protection and cash.

Power Sat With Trump and DOJ

The institutional power in this story sits with Trump and the Justice Department that served him. Trump agreed to drop a $10 billion lawsuit against the IRS in exchange for a $1.7 billion taxpayer-funded settlement, and the reported deal also granted him, his family, and his businesses immunity from IRS audits. That is not confusion. That is the exercise of leverage by a man who treats public institutions as instruments of private self-defense.

Blanche’s notice matters because it shows how executive power tries to rewrite its own record after the fact. If the memo really reaffirmed Trump’s IRS immunity while purporting to rescind only part of the arrangement, then the administration did not reverse course. It performed a narrowing gesture for public consumption while preserving the core benefit.

The Sacrifice Was Always Selective

MeidasTouch’s read is brutal and plausible: Trump was willing to throw the Jan. 6 defendants under the bus while protecting his own IRS immunity deal. That is consistent with the logic of this arrangement. The people who stormed the Capitol are useful as symbols, not as clients. Once they stop serving the larger political story, they become disposable.

That is not a clean-up failure. It is how patronage works when it is organized around grievance and loyalty instead of law. The rioters get used as cannon fodder for a movement; Trump keeps the legal shield. The state, meanwhile, is asked to swallow the contradiction and call it procedure.

Republicans Played Catch-Up With No Moral Center

The Senate Republican pressure in the story is revealing for what it is not. Two Republicans reportedly threatened Blanche’s confirmation unless he terminated both the fund and the immunity agreement. That is not a defense of democratic norms. It is a late-stage attempt to sterilize a deal after the stink became impossible to hide.

If Republican oversight only appears when a confirmation vote is at risk, then it is not oversight. It is hostage management. And even then, the goal was reportedly not to confront the underlying corruption of the settlement, but to make the paperwork less embarrassing.

Bureaucratic Language as Cover

This is how authoritarian politics often survives inside legal institutions: not by announcing the abuse, but by burying it in interpretive memos, rescissions, and “official” notices that do less than they claim. The source notes that MeidasTouch argued the document may not even be valid because all parties had not signed off, as the original agreement required. If that is true, then the administration was not just misleading the public. It was relying on the appearance of process to simulate legitimacy.

That tactic is the broader pattern. Name the thing in a way that obscures what it does. Issue a notice that pretends to resolve a scandal while preserving the benefit. Use the language of rule-following to shield rule-breaking. That is not accidental weakness. It is institutional cowardice in service of a political patron.

The Real Lesson

The story is not about one bad memo. It is about a governing style that treats public law as a series of adjustable barriers protecting Trump’s interests first and everyone else’s afterward. The anti-weaponization fund, the IRS immunity, the midnight notice, the missing signatures, the confirmation pressure: all of it points to the same structure. Power acts, allies clean up, and the damage is repackaged as administrative correction.

That is the system. Not chaos, not misunderstanding, not “controversy.” A deliberate effort to use government machinery to absorb Trump’s liabilities and distribute the costs downward.
