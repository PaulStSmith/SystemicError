'Broke his brain': Former aide says Trump never recovered from 2020 trouncing

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Delusion Is Not the Story. His Power Is.

The Useful Diagnosis

Sarah Matthews, Trump’s former press secretary, told MS NOW that he remains fixated on the lie that he won in 2020, that advisers told him he lost, and that he surrounded himself with people who told him what he wanted to hear. She framed his obsession as a broken ego, a man unable to accept defeat, now dragging Republicans back into the wreckage of January 6 and the election lie as the 2026 midterms approach.

That explanation is emotionally satisfying because it makes the problem sound psychological. It is also too small. Trump’s instability matters only because he still commands a political machine that turns personal grievance into institutional damage.

Who Has Power

The relevant actor is not a wounded narcissist in the abstract. It is a former president with a captive party, a loyalty-based media ecosystem, and a Republican coalition still willing to orbit his claims. That is the power structure on display here. Matthews says he was advised he lost, refused reality, and then “surrounded himself with enablers.” That is the whole system in miniature: one man at the center, a network of adults in the room who chose not to be adults, and a party that normalized submission.

The lie about 2020 is not sustained by confusion. It is sustained by hierarchy. Trump does not merely believe nonsense; he benefits from a political culture that rewards spectacle, punishes dissent, and treats democratic legitimacy as optional when it is inconvenient.

The Misdirection

Matthews tries to pull the story toward Trump’s damaged ego, and that is only half the issue. The more important question is why so much of the political class still discusses his election denial as if it were a personal quirk instead of an ongoing assault on democratic reality.

That framing launders responsibility. It turns deliberate disinformation into pathology. It recasts a strategic lie as a mental glitch. But Trump’s fixation on 2020 is not some private loop of grievance. It is a public tool for disciplining Republicans, energizing followers, and keeping the party organized around fraud fantasies instead of governing.

The Enablers Matter More Than the Meltdown

Matthews says advisers told him the truth and he rejected it. That detail matters because it identifies the failure point: not ignorance, but accommodation. The people around Trump saw the collapse coming and stayed close enough to polish it. That is how authoritarian politics metastasize in plain sight. Not through one dramatic seizure of power, but through a long chain of refusals to say no.

The weak point in the story is not Trump’s wounded pride. It is the Republican structure that keeps rewarding the lie after everyone with eyes can see the damage. Every time Republican officials, media figures, and consultants act as though this is merely Trump being Trump, they help convert a stolen-public-trust machine into normal party business.

What Voters Actually Need

Matthews is right about one thing: most people are not waking up thinking about January 6. They are thinking about housing, health care, gas, and groceries. That is precisely why the election lie persists inside Republican politics. It is a diversion from material failure. When a party cannot offer people lower costs or a functioning state, it falls back on grievance, revenge, and mythology.

Trump’s fixation on 2020 is not a distraction from Republican politics. It is the method. The party uses cultural resentment and election fraud fiction to keep attention away from the fact that it has little to offer beyond cruelty, spectacle, and loyalty tests.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple: democratic breakdown rarely announces itself as a coup. It arrives as repetition, excuses, and elite cowardice. A defeated politician refuses reality. His allies soften the edges. Commentators pathologize the lie instead of naming the power behind it. And a major party keeps moving as though normalcy can survive inside a fraud machine.

The issue is not that Trump cannot admit he lost. The issue is that too many powerful people have built a political order where he never has to.
