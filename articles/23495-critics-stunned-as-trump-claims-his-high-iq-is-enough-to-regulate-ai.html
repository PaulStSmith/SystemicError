Critics stunned as Trump claims his 'high IQ' is enough to regulate AI

Based on an article by Brad Reed, Common Dreams

The President Who Wants to Be the Only Guardrail

A Policy of Personal Infallibility

Donald Trump’s position is not merely that federal safeguards for artificial intelligence might be poorly designed. He argues that they are unnecessary because a “strong and smart” president—meaning himself—can substitute for them.

That is a claim about power, not technical policy. It replaces public rules, independent scrutiny, and enforceable limits with one politician’s asserted intellect. A technology whose developers acknowledge profound uncertainty would be governed through presidential discretion and unexplained assurances.

Power Without an Audit Trail

Trump claimed that his administration had stopped unnamed AI figures from doing unspecified “bad” things. He offered no actors, conduct, mechanism, or evidence.

That vagueness is politically useful. A verifiable regulatory record could be examined and challenged. A boast cannot. The administration gets to claim competence without disclosing what it did, while rejecting the institutional safeguards that would make competence measurable.

The actual decision here is clear: Trump is opposing guardrails. The consequence is equally clear. Companies developing powerful systems would face fewer external constraints, while the public would be asked to trust presidential improvisation.

Turn Critics Into Enemies

Trump did not answer warnings about AI safety. He recast opposition to unchecked development as a conspiracy benefiting China, then threatened “Conspiracy Theorists, Treasonists, Traitors, and Leakers.”

This is authoritarian propaganda in a familiar form: convert a policy dispute into a loyalty test, equate criticism with foreign assistance, and place people who disclose inconvenient information beside traitors. The point is not to rebut the warnings. It is to make raising them politically dangerous.

“Whoever wins AI, wins” performs the same misdirection. It compresses questions about cybersecurity, corporate power, catastrophic risk, and democratic control into a nationalist race. Once every restraint is framed as surrender to China, industry expansion becomes patriotism and oversight becomes sabotage.

The Risk Is Not Confined to One Faction

The source presents unusually broad alarm. Bernie Sanders planned to appear alongside former Trump adviser Steve Bannon and former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon. Republican Senator Josh Hawley opened a probe involving an autonomous hack attributed to OpenAI agents and broader industry dangers. Coxon resigned from Anthropic, alleging that developers were racing toward self-improving superintelligence despite believing the technology could become catastrophic.

That coalition does not prove every prediction correct. It does destroy the convenient fiction that concern is merely the work of anti-technology activists or partisan enemies. When political antagonists and an industry insider converge on the need for scrutiny, dismissing them all as conspirators is not confidence. It is an effort to evade the substance of their warnings.

The Industry Benefits From the Spectacle

Trump’s self-celebration draws attention away from another center of power: the companies building and deploying these systems. A debate staged as “genius president versus hysterical critics” leaves corporate incentives largely untouched.

The firms control technical development and possess information outsiders lack. The executive branch controls enforcement priorities and the shape of federal oversight. Ordinary people control neither. Yet Trump’s framing assigns suspicion downward—to critics, leakers, and unnamed conspirators—while offering reassurance upward to an industry racing ahead.

Rule by Boast

The larger pattern is government reduced to personal authority. Expertise becomes subordinate to presidential vanity. Oversight becomes evidence of disloyalty. Uncertainty becomes an excuse for secrecy rather than a reason for enforceable limits.

Artificial intelligence may be novel, but this political arrangement is not. Concentrated private power seeks freedom from restraint; concentrated executive power promises protection through the leader’s judgment; critics are branded enemies. The technology changes. The governing fraud remains the same: accountability is removed first, and competence is declared afterward.
