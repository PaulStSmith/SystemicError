The sick scam to rob your Social Security and give it to Wall Street

Based on an article by Dean Baker

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20263 min read

The Rich Call It Moderation When They Want Your Benefits

The People With Power

This story is not about a neutral budget problem. It is about who gets to define the problem in the first place. The people with actual leverage here are the rich tax-cut beneficiaries, the politicians who handed them those breaks, and the media owners who turn that arrangement into a fake national consensus.

The source is blunt about the core fact: the deficit panic is being staged to protect wealth, not to discipline it. Republicans created the hole with tax cuts for their donors and then refused to touch the spending that would make their allies angry. Now the same political class wants ordinary workers to pay for the shortfall.

What The Source Is Exposing

The article is reacting to a Washington Post editorial that urges debt reduction by targeting the “retirement state,” with Social Security and Medicare first in line. Its proof is built on a sleight of hand: it uses average earners to make Social Security look overly generous, then quietly pivots to arguing for cuts that would land on everyone else.

That is not analysis. It is laundering. If the claim is that high-income retirees get too much, then the relevant comparison is high-income taxes versus high-income benefits. The source says the paper had that data available and chose not to use it. That choice tells you exactly what kind of argument this was meant to be.

The Misdirection

The central trick is to present class-specific austerity as fiscal common sense. Average workers are used as the face of a problem caused by the wealthy. Retirees are framed as overpaid because the owners of the system want to shift blame away from the people who broke the revenue base.

Social Security is supposed to be progressive. That is the point. Moderate-income workers get stronger paybacks relative to what they paid in; high-income workers do not. Pretending those design features prove the system is too generous is not moderation. It is intellectual fraud in the service of benefit cuts.

The Real Budget Story

The source is also clear that the real imbalance is not a mysterious shortage of discipline in programs like Social Security. It is the tax side, where the rich have taken a growing share of national income and refused to pay accordingly. That is the actual structural fact buried under the deficit sermon.

The same pattern appears in Medicare. The paper points to the gap between taxes and benefits, but the source notes the deeper reality: the United States spends far more per person on health care than other wealthy countries, without better outcomes. The excess goes to hospitals, insurers, drug firms, equipment makers, and doctors. So the system is not “too generous” to retirees. It is too generous to the health care industry.

What This Really Serves

The push for private 401(k)s is the tell. It replaces a public guarantee with a more expensive, more fragile system that routes money through financial firms. That is not efficiency. It is transfer. It moves risk onto workers and fees onto everyone while enriching the people already positioned to harvest the waste.

This is what establishment “balance” looks like when wealth controls the frame. The same class that benefited from tax cuts wants to cut earned benefits, and the media infrastructure owned by rich people packages that demand as responsible governance. The result is a political scam dressed up as prudence.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple: when elites create a fiscal problem, they demand that workers absorb the fix. When tax cuts blow a hole in the budget, they do not call it failure. They call for “shared sacrifice,” which in practice means extracting sacrifice from people who had no hand in the theft.

That is the real meaning of the article’s target. The issue is not a deficit in the abstract. It is an ownership structure that lets the wealthy write the rules, then uses media institutions to disguise class aggression as moderation.
