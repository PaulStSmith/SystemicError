“Emergency Authorization”: The Fossil Fuel Industry’s Favorite Magic Word

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Let’s not mince words: they’re coming for the land.

Not just any land, but the land you and I supposedly own—the national parks, the wildlife preserves, the tribal grounds, the vast public commons protected over generations with blood, sweat, lawsuits, and signatures. Places where generations have camped, hiked, prayed, hunted, and healed. The lungs and heart of a nation now being auctioned off by the pound. And now? The Department of the Interior is preparing to carve them up for oil, gas, coal, and mineral extraction, under the Orwellian justification of an “energy emergency.”

You know, the kind of emergency where the U.S. is the largest exporter of natural gas in the world. The kind where we’re producing more oil than Saudi Arabia, while our fossil fuel companies rake in historic profits and pay historic dividends. Where we generate more energy than we consume. A real page-turner of a crisis, if the script were written by ExxonMobil.

What’s actually happening is this: the Interior Department is slashing environmental review times from two years to just 14–28 days. That’s not oversight. That’s rubber-stamping on steroids. They’re invoking emergency powers to override bedrock laws like:

  • The Endangered Species Act

  • The National Environmental Policy Act

  • The National Historic Preservation Act

Laws that weren’t passed for convenience. Laws that were written to slow down the runaway train of extraction and ask: “What might this destroy?” Laws that forced industry to, for once, answer for its appetite. Now, those guardrails are being ripped up and paved over with drilling rigs.

But who needs due process when there’s profit to be made? Why bother asking if a project will decimate a fragile ecosystem when you can just declare the paperwork an enemy of the state and fire up the drills by next Tuesday?

The Real Emergency

This is the same strategy we’ve seen play out before—declare an “emergency,” and suddenly you can do whatever the hell you want: suspend laws, detain people, skip court orders, toss civil liberties in the shredder. Remember the so-called border emergencies? The immigration panics? Same playbook. Now, that emergency toolkit is being repurposed to drill, mine, and gut public land with impunity. No hearings. No comment period. No tribal consultation. No impact studies. Just bulldozers and nondisclosure agreements.

And let’s not pretend this is some rogue agency acting in isolation. The video transcript makes it abundantly clear: every single Republican senator is on board. Every. Single. One. This is not political nuance. This is coordinated demolition of democratic oversight, greenwashed in the language of national interest.

And if you think electing someone different next cycle will magically reverse it, let me remind you: you can’t un-extinct a species. You can’t un-detonate a sacred site. You can’t un-leak a pipeline or reassemble a demolished cultural artifact. Once land is drilled, stripped, and poisoned, it’s gone. Period. And it doesn’t matter how many regrets politicians tweet afterward.

Silence is Complicity

The most insidious part? This move is designed not only to bypass protections but to silence resistance. Emergency declarations allow for rapid action—and if citizens push back, the state has legal cover to label dissent as “interference with emergency operations.” They can declare a protest unlawful. They can detain you for obstruction. They can call environmentalists a threat to national security. And they will.

They can move fast, and hit hard, while the rest of us are still figuring out what just happened. They will count on our fatigue, our information overload, our fractured timelines and doomscrolling thumbs. By the time outrage coalesces, the ink is dry and the landscape is ash.

This isn’t just bad governance. It’s smash-and-grab capitalism with the full weight of federal authority behind it. It’s fossil-fueled authoritarianism dressed in red, white, and blue.

And it's happening now.

What Can You Do?

You raise hell. You don’t ask nicely. You call your senators—especially if they’re Republicans. You flood congressional offices with phone calls. You show up, speak out, get loud, get annoying, get inconvenient. You make their staff know you by name and dread your number. Because the people doing this are counting on your exhaustion. Your despair. Your distraction. Your scrolling.

They want you placated. They want you quiet. They want you cynical and checked out.

Don’t give them that.

If we don’t stop this—if we let them wrap their greed in the flag of emergency and bulldoze the very soul of this country—then we deserve the scorched earth they leave behind.

But I don’t believe that’s how this story ends. I believe we fight.

I believe we make them regret underestimating us.

And I believe we defend what is ours—not with guns or hate, but with an unstoppable, inconvenient, unrelenting roar.
