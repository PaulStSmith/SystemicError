'League of its own': Nicolle Wallace pinpoints Trump comment showing he's way out of touch

"There are twists and turns today in the melodrama that is Donald Trump's handling of the U.S. economy," she said.

Trump has imposed a tariff on China at 145%, but on Friday, he suggested around 80% "seems right," as Reuters reported.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and chief trade negotiator Jamieson Greer will meet with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng in Geneva for the negotiations on Saturday, Wallace noted.

"All of that — layoffs, bankruptcy, the prospect of empty shelves — that is what people here in the real world are worried about," Wallace continued, citing economists who spoke to the New York Times.

"While it differs from company to company, some executives have said that tariffs above 50 percent are generally enough to freeze exports to the United States," the report said. "Companies that are not able to find an alternative source of supply for their products outside China are facing the prospect of bankruptcy and layoffs as the summer grinds on and even 25 percent tariffs can be crippling."

Wallace mentioned that larger retailers might be able to absorb the costs, while small businesses may not be able to. However, NPR financial correspondent Maria Aspan chimed in, saying it isn't only small businesses anymore.

"We are also seeing some of the biggest businesses in the country pulling or cutting their financial guidance. Including, for instance, Barbie maker Mattel saying, 'We are going to pass on — or we will have to pass on these costs to consumers,'" Aspan said.

"And about that, Donald Trump says no one needs 30 dolls," Wallace quipped. "You can do with two. No one buys 30 dolls."

She said that the ordeal feels like politics has been "turned upside down," because in a normal world it would be seen as a huge gaffe.

"This is sort of the political tone deafness that, you know, George H.W. Bush was damaged irreparably for not, you know, recognizing, sort of, the scanner at the grocery store," Wallace recalled, harkening back to the 1992 political gaffe in which Bush was described as "amazed" by a barcode scanner. As the Associated Press reported, the reality is that he was impressed that the scanner could also now weigh fruit and vegetables.

Still, the supermarket scanner myth grew to represent a politician out of touch with everyday American life. Wallace said that Trump's "they don't need to have 30 dolls" comment is in that same vein.

"I mean, there are in the course of the history of presidential politics and the coverage of presidential politics, this is along the lines of tone deafness and a league of its own.

Rev. Al Sharpton said that it isn't only being tone deaf.

"The implicit statement that you get from him saying no one needs 30 dolls is he's, again, implying working-class people just splurge and spend money irresponsibly," Sharpton said.

Our Analysis:

Tariff Tumult: Trump's Economic Rollercoaster

Nicolle Wallace of MSNBC's "Deadline White House" recently dissected President Donald Trump's erratic approach to managing U.S.-China trade relations. In a display of what can only be described as a policy whiplash, Trump's stance on tariffs has swung from imposing a staggering 145% to suggesting that 80% "seems right." This flip-flop, as reported by Reuters, is a microcosm of the broader melodrama that has characterized Trump's economic policies.

The Real World Consequences

Wallace didn't shy away from highlighting the dire consequences of such unpredictable policy shifts. Layoffs, bankruptcies, and the looming specter of empty shelves are not mere speculations but tangible fears for many Americans. The New York Times lends weight to this argument with economists pointing out that tariffs above 50 percent could effectively paralyze exports to the United States. The situation grows increasingly grim, especially for companies unable to diversify their supply chains away from China. Even tariffs as "low" as 25 percent are described as crippling.

The Disproportionate Impact on Small vs. Large Businesses

It's a tale of two economies where larger retailers might weather the storm by absorbing the costs, while small businesses could face existential threats. However, NPR financial correspondent Maria Aspan warns that the damage isn't confined to small businesses alone. Giants like Mattel are also feeling the pinch, forced to consider passing on costs to consumers. Trump's cavalier attitude towards this, suggesting that "no one needs 30 dolls," exemplifies a shocking disconnect from the economic realities faced by both businesses and consumers alike.

Political Tone Deafness: A Historical Perspective

Wallace adeptly draws parallels between Trump's comments and past political gaffes, such as George H.W. Bush's infamous supermarket scanner moment. This comparison is not just about highlighting a lack of touch with the average American's daily struggles but underscores a broader issue of political tone deafness. Trump's remark on doll ownership trivializes the broader economic anxieties plaguing many American households.

Rev. Al Sharpton expands on this by interpreting Trump's comments as not just tone-deaf but as an insinuation that working-class people are prone to financial irresponsibility. This narrative not only distorts the reality of economic hardship but also alienates a significant segment of the American populace.

In Conclusion: A Melodrama with Real Stakes

The ongoing saga of Trump's tariff policy is more than a mere political melodrama; it's a clear and present danger to the U.S. economy's stability. The erratic nature of these policies, coupled with a blatant disregard for their impact, reveals a troubling disconnect between the administration and the very fabric of American society. As negotiations with China continue, one can only hope for a resolution that prioritizes economic stability over political caprice. But if past behavior is any indicator, America should brace itself for more twists and turns on this rollercoaster ride.
