House members beg Mike Johnson to cancel lengthy recess and confront a 'catastrophic risk'

Congress Has Time for Recess, Not for Governing

The Power Sits With the Speaker

Three House Democrats are asking Speaker Mike Johnson to cancel a looming six-week recess and keep the chamber in Washington until it considers safeguards for advanced artificial intelligence. Their letter cites stalled bipartisan proposals, including a requirement that AI systems have a human-controlled “kill switch.” Johnson has scheduled an energy-cost vote concerning data centers, but the reporting gives no indication that he will allow broader AI regulation to reach the floor before Election Day.

The institutional hierarchy is not complicated. Reps. Sam Liccardo, George Whitesides, and Lori Trahan can circulate a letter. Johnson controls the House schedule. Their urgency is rhetorical; his authority is operational.

If the chamber leaves without debating the legislation, “Congress” will not have drifted innocently into inaction. House leadership will have chosen recess over consideration of bills already waiting for attention.

A Schedule Is a Political Decision

Coverage of legislative paralysis often treats the congressional calendar like weather: a six-week absence is “looming,” the window is “narrow,” and bills somehow become “stalled.” That language conceals agency.

The House is scheduled to reconvene for one week before departing until November 9. That timetable was made by people with power, and it can be changed by them. Calling the window narrow describes the constraint without naming its author.

Johnson’s planned data-center bill also shows that the House is capable of acting selectively. Leadership is not confronting an empty docket or an unknowable issue. It is choosing which consequences deserve floor time and which can be deferred past an election.

The Apocalyptic Pitch Is Also a Distraction

The lawmakers weaken their own case by leaning on predictions that advanced AI could destroy humanity within a decade and by imagining an agentic AI writing a post-apocalyptic history titled “Why Congress Slept.” Those warnings may convey sincere alarm, but they also invite Congress to perform concern about an immense hypothetical while avoiding scrutiny of its immediate decisions.

The more concrete fact is enough: researchers have raised severe concerns, bipartisan safeguards exist, and House leaders have not scheduled them for debate. Regulation does not require certainty about extinction. It requires institutions willing to set limits before private technological power outruns public oversight.

By centering catastrophe, the letter risks turning governance into science-fiction theater. That framing gives opponents an easy escape: dispute the most dramatic forecast and ignore every narrower reason for regulation.

Bipartisanship Without a Vote Is Decoration

The bills are described as bipartisan, a label Washington routinely treats as proof of seriousness. But bipartisan sponsorship means little when leadership can prevent the House from voting.

This is how institutional cowardice works in practice. Legislators endorse concern, draft safeguards, sign letters, and then surrender the decisive question to gatekeepers. The existence of agreement becomes a public-relations asset rather than a route to law.

Liccardo and his co-signers correctly identify the recess as a test. But it is not primarily a test of whether “lawmakers” care in some collective, foggy sense. It is a test of whether Johnson will use his authority to permit action—and whether other members will impose any political cost if he refuses.

Delay Is the Policy

Congressional inaction is routinely portrayed as the absence of policy. It is often policy by another means.

Leaving advanced AI largely unregulated preserves the existing distribution of power: technology companies continue developing systems while elected officials postpone enforceable constraints. Whatever Johnson’s motives, that is the practical consequence of denying safeguards floor time.

The larger pattern extends far beyond AI. Leaders manufacture scarcity in the legislative calendar, use that scarcity to bury inconvenient questions, and then describe the resulting paralysis as an institutional failure no individual controlled. The system did not sleep. The people running it set the alarm, heard it, and chose recess.
