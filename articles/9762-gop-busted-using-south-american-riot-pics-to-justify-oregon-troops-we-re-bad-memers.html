GOP busted using South American riot pics to justify Oregon troops: 'We're bad memers'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 8, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Law enforcement officers deploy smoke grenades to disperse protesters gathered outside the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) headquarters in south Portland, Oregon, U.S., October 5, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

The Oregon Republican Party is facing backlash after it shared a social media post about a planned military deployment in Portland using a fabricated image composed of photographs from South America.

The party’s official accounts on Facebook, Instagram, and X claimed, “President Trump on Sunday deployed 300 California National Guard troops to Portland, Oregon after a judge ruled that the Oregon National Guard could not be deployed to keep federal facilities and personnel in Portland safe.”

The accompanying image appeared designed to support Trump’s claim that Portland’s protests were out of control, showing a line of riot police and a crowd with flares creating red smoke. However, the image was a digital composite of two unrelated photographs from different South American countries, The Guardian reported Tuesday.

The police officers in the image originated from a 2008 Getty Images photograph of “South American riot police,” with a clear indication of its non-U.S. origin: an officer’s shield marked “Policia” in Spanish or Portuguese. The specific location appears to be Ecuador, based on additional context in similar archival images, according to The Guardian.

The fiery protest scene was a separate stock photograph taken by a Brazilian photographer in 2017, sourced from the free image site Pexels.

When a journalist pointed out the image’s inauthenticity, the Oregon Republican Party responded on social media with a cavalier admission: “We’re not reporters, just bad memers.”

Ultimately, a federal judge blocked Trump’s attempt to deploy California’s National Guard to Portland.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Fabrication: Oregon Republican Party’s Misleading Tactics

In a recent, dubious maneuver, the Oregon Republican Party decided to wade into the deep, murky waters of misinformation, utilizing a concocted image to dramatize events in Portland. This isn’t merely a case of political spin; it’s a deliberate fabrication, designed to stoke fear and manipulate public perception. Let’s dissect the layers of this deception.

1. The Misuse of Images: The central piece of this farce is an image, a digital Frankenstein’s monster stitched together from photographs taken not in the tumultuous streets of Portland, or anywhere in the United States for that matter, but from South America. One half of the image hails from a 2008 photograph of South American riot police, with the word “Policia” boldly betraying its non-U.S. origin. The other half? A fiery protest scene captured by a Brazilian photographer in 2017. This isn’t just misleading; it’s a flagrant attempt to deceive.

2. The Intent to Deceive: The choice to use such an image was no accident. It was a calculated attempt to support Trump’s narrative that Portland’s protests were spiraling into chaos, necessitating military intervention. This narrative, bolstered by a fabricated image, was aimed at justifying extreme measures against civic unrest. It’s a classic case of fearmongering, using falsehoods to validate an aggressive stance.

3. The Casual Dismissal: When called out on their blatant use of a fake image, the Oregon Republican Party’s response was dismissively cavalier: “We’re not reporters, just bad memers.” This flippant acknowledgment of misinformation is troubling. It implies a lack of accountability and a disregard for the truth, qualities that are toxic in any political landscape. It’s an admission of guilt delivered with a smirk, undermining the very fabric of informed debate and discussion.

4. The Blocked Deployment: Ultimately, this tale of deceit ends with a federal judge blocking the Trump administration’s attempt to deploy California’s National Guard to Portland. This judicial intervention serves as a reminder that, despite attempts to manipulate public perception and policy with falsehoods, there are still checks in place. However, the damage inflicted by such misinformation campaigns can be enduring, sowing division and distrust.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The Oregon Republican Party’s actions are a stark reminder of the power of images and narratives in shaping public opinion and policy. This incident underlines the necessity for vigilance and scrutiny in our consumption of political messages. It’s imperative that we demand accountability from our political parties and representatives, insisting on truth and transparency. Misinformation, especially when wielded by those in power or seeking to influence power, is not just “bad memeing.” It’s a dangerous tool that can have real-world consequences, eroding trust in institutions and fomenting unnecessary conflict.

In a world where truth seems increasingly malleable, let’s not forget the importance of holding those who seek to manipulate it to account. Fabrications, no matter how trivial they may seem, must be challenged and exposed. Democracy thrives on informed debate, not manufactured chaos.
