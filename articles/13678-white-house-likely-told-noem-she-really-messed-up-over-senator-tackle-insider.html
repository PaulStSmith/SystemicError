White House likely told Noem she 'really messed up' over senator tackle: insider

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 14, 20254 min read

U.S. Senator Alex Padilla (D-CA) is detained and removed from the venue after interrupting the press conference held by U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem and others, in Los Angeles, California, U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Aude Guerrucci

Reacting to Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) being manhandled by Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem's security team when he tried to ask her a question on Thursday, DC insider Maria Teresa Kumar suggested Noem's comments afterward show she was called on the carpet by the White House.

During an appearance on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," the head of Voto Latino pointed out that the normally defiant Noem later tried to put out the firestorm resulting from video of the U.S. senator being forced to the floor and handcuffed when she appeared on Fox News after.

Speaking with Fox, Noem stated incorrectly that Padilla never identified himself before reporting that she met with him afterward for ten to fifteen minutes to clear the air and listen to his concerns.

Speaking to the MSNBC hosts, Kumar expressed outrage at Padilla's treatment, and then added, "You have Secretary Noem, who is charged with 250,000 agents around the country, and she cannot de-escalate a basic press conference. Instead, it has to go to the maximum where they have to handcuff a sitting senator. That actually goes to a broader issue: is she the right person for this job? Does she understand that right now? "

"I believe that there is someone from the White House that called her and said, 'You know what? You really messed up right now. You have to go now sit down and talk to the senator.'" she suggested.

"I have to tell you that I have known Alex Padilla for a very long time. In his character, he is someone who is staid. This is perhaps the most excited I've ever seen him," she added before pointing out, "He is just doing his job."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Manhandling Incident Involving Sen. Alex Padilla and Secretary Kristi Noem's Security Team

In a recent display of what can only be described as a gross mismanagement of power and a blatant disregard for democratic norms, Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) was manhandled by Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem's security team. This incident, occurring when Padilla merely attempted to ask a question, throws a glaring spotlight on the escalating tensions and the erosion of civil discourse within the halls of power.

The Incident and Its Aftermath

According to reports, the situation escalated to the point where Padilla was forced to the floor and handcuffed, a treatment that is not only humiliating but also profoundly alarming for a sitting U.S. senator. The actions of Noem's security team are not just an overreaction; they are a dangerous precedent in the treatment of elected officials. This incident begs the question: since when did attempting to engage in dialogue become a crime worthy of physical restraint?

Following the incident, Secretary Noem, rather than standing firm on her team's actions, attempted to quell the uproar it caused. On Fox News, she incorrectly stated that Padilla never identified himself, a claim that seems both unlikely and irrelevant given the senator's public recognition. Noem's attempt to smooth over the incident with a subsequent meeting to listen to Padilla's concerns does little to address the underlying issue of her team's aggressive response to a simple question.

The Broader Implications

Maria Teresa Kumar's comments on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" cut to the heart of the matter. She rightly calls out Noem for her inability to de-escalate a basic press conference, leading to an unnecessary and extreme reaction. Kumar's questioning of Noem's suitability for her position touches on a critical point: leadership requires judgment. Noem's handling of this incident, and her subsequent comments, showcase a disturbing lack of judgment that should concern us all.

Moreover, Kumar's insight into the White House's likely reaction suggests that Noem's attempt to mitigate the situation was not entirely voluntary but a response to higher-up pressure. This acknowledgment by the White House, while implied, points to an understanding of the inappropriateness of Noem's team's actions.

Final Thoughts

The incident involving Sen. Padilla and Secretary Noem's security team is not just about a failed attempt at communication or a misunderstanding. It's a symptom of a larger disease eroding the foundations of our democratic institutions. When elected officials can be treated with such disdain and aggression for merely performing their duties, it signals a troubling shift towards authoritarianism.

Sen. Padilla's ordeal is a stark reminder of the importance of holding our leaders and their teams accountable for their actions. Democracy thrives on dialogue, transparency, and respect—values that seem to be in short supply within Noem's team. As citizens, we must demand better from our public servants, insisting on a return to civility and a respect for the democratic process. The incident may have passed, but its implications will resonate far into the future, serving as a cautionary tale of what happens when power forgets its purpose.
