Vicious attack ad drops as Texas Republicans sling AI slop at each other in ugly primary

By Paulo Santos

The Texas Republican primary runoff for U.S. Senate is getting uglier, with interests backing incumbent Sen. John Cornyn and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton trading increasingly ugly barbs — and attack ads filled with AI slop.

On Wednesday, the pro-Cornyn super PAC Texans for a Conservative Majority dropped an ad full of AI-generated video clips. The ad featured a deepfaked picture of Paxton swiping on a dating app to illustrate accusations of marital infidelity. It also included AI-generated clips of people who supposedly received state grants under Paxton’s leadership. These clips depicted transgender activists receiving taxpayer-funded transition surgery recovery, face-tattooed Latino “illegals” seeking to learn “how to avoid deportation,” liberal protesters with blue and purple hair, and even a Soviet soldier dressed for a Red Army military parade.

“Ken Paxton is distracted. When the Attorney General is focused on himself, it’s bad for Texans,” said the ad’s robotic voiceover. “If this is how he acts in Texas, we can’t afford Ken Paxton in Washington.”

“This ad was made with generative A.I. But Ken Paxton’s behavior is 100% real,” stated a small disclaimer at the end of the clip.

Paxton and Cornyn have been at each other’s throats for months, with polls suggesting Donald Trump supporters are leaning in Paxton’s favor. The president has so far avoided making an endorsement in the race.

This is not the first time AI has factored into political attacks in the Texas GOP primary.

Earlier this year, the Paxton campaign dropped an ad attacking Cornyn for his bipartisan work on drug abuse with Democratic Rep. Jasmine Crockett, showing the two of them slow-dancing at a rodeo. Crockett ran for the Democratic nomination in this year’s election, but lost to state Rep. James Talarico.

Our Analysis:

The Misuse of AI in Political Warfare: The Texas GOP Primary Runoff

In the latest escalation of intra-party conflict, the Texas Republican primary runoff for the U.S. Senate between incumbent Sen. John Cornyn and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has taken a dark turn into the realm of AI-generated misinformation and character assassination. The use of AI to fabricate imagery and narratives in political advertisements is not just a new low for campaign ethics; it’s a disturbing glimpse into the future of political discourse.

Institutional Power and Decision Makers

First, let’s dissect the power dynamics and decision-making processes at play. The immediate actors are the pro-Cornyn super PAC Texans for a Conservative Majority and the Paxton campaign. These entities are leveraging AI technology to create and disseminate false or misleading representations of their opponents. It’s crucial to recognize that while the candidates themselves may not be directly creating these ads, they are undeniably the beneficiaries and, through their association with these PACs, indirectly responsible for the content.

Super PACs, due to their ability to raise unlimited sums of money from corporations, unions, associations, and individuals, wield significant power in the political landscape. They are, however, prohibited from coordinating directly with campaigns. This technical separation offers candidates a degree of plausible deniability about the content of super PAC ads. Yet, this does not absolve them of moral responsibility, especially when such ads serve to bolster their electoral prospects.

Misdirection and Misplaced Blame

The article’s framing suggests a tit-for-tat dynamic between the Cornyn and Paxton campaigns, with AI-generated content as the latest battlefield. However, this narrative risks trivializing the grave implications of using AI for political misinformation. By focusing on the spectacle, there’s a danger of missing the broader issue: the erosion of democratic discourse and the potential for AI to be weaponized against the public interest.

It’s essential to question not just the legality of these actions, but their impact on democratic norms and values. The use of AI to generate misleading content blurs the line between fact and fiction, undermining informed public debate and trust in the political process.

False Equivalence and Lazy Narratives

One could argue that both sides are equally culpable in this arms race of misinformation. However, such a stance would be a false equivalence. Each instance of misinformation must be evaluated on its own merits, including the content, intent, and potential impact. The specifics of the AI-generated ads mentioned—depicting exaggerated and stigmatizing portrayals of various groups—reveal a targeted attempt to appeal to prejudice and fear, tactics that have long-term consequences for social cohesion and trust.

Moreover, the narrative of ‘politics as usual’ does a disservice to the public and to those political actors who strive to maintain integrity in their campaigns. It’s not just about one bad actor; it’s about a system that increasingly incentivizes sensationalism and division over substantive debate.

Conclusion

In the Texas GOP primary runoff, the real story isn’t just the ugly turn of campaign tactics; it’s about the emerging threat of AI in political manipulation and the urgent need for ethical standards and regulations. While Cornyn and Paxton continue their battle for the Senate seat, the integrity of political discourse hangs in the balance. It’s a stark reminder that in the age of AI, the line between reality and manipulation is increasingly blurred, with profound implications for democracy itself.
