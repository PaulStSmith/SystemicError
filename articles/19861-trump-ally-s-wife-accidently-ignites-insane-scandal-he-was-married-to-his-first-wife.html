Trump ally's wife accidently ignites 'insane' scandal: 'He was married to his first wife'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 16, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Valentine’s Day Faux Pas or a Window into Character?

Photo credit: Gage Skidmore

The wife of close Donald Trump ally Byron Donalds posted on social media to honor her husband, but the dates she used raised red flags with observers, and former cable TV host Mehdi Hasan declared that Donalds “was married to his first wife at the time, just married in fact.”

Erika Donalds took to social media to celebrate Valentine’s Day, posting a photo of them together in the morning and writing, “Remembering the spot where Byron asked for my number in the Oglesby Student Union at Florida State in the fall of 1999.”

She further added, “Our first ‘date’ came a few months later - on Valentine’s Day 2000. Thanks, babe, for making all 27 of our Valentine’s Days together so special.”

Hasan, however, posted a screenshot from Wikipedia revealing that Donalds married his first wife in 1999 and didn’t get divorced from her until 2002. He married Erika Donalds the following year, according to public records.

“Insane that she just tweeted out these dates given it is a matter of public record that he was married to his first wife at the time, just married in fact,” Hasan wrote.

PR pro Nate March also chimed in, writing, “Something about this timeline isn’t quite timing right to me.”

Ex-Obama delegate Cory Provost added, “It doesn’t surprise me that a leading candidate for the Republican party cheated on his wife...seems to be the qualification.”

He then added, “We aren’t even going to discuss the fraud, or should we...” before trailing off.

Even self-identified MAGA supporters joined in the comments to ask questions, with one Trump-supporting user writing, “Wasn’t he married at that time??”

Our Analysis:

A Valentine’s Day Faux Pas or a Window into Character?

In a recent social media spectacle, Erika Donalds, the wife of Byron Donalds—a staunch ally of Donald Trump—attempted a heartfelt homage to her husband that quickly spiraled into controversy. The post, intended to celebrate their enduring love, inadvertently highlighted a timeline that suggests a romantic entanglement during Byron Donalds’ first marriage. Mehdi Hasan, a former cable TV host, didn’t miss a beat, pointing out the discrepancy with the precision of a prosecutor presenting a case. Let’s peel back the layers of this incident, shall we?

Erika Donalds’ post on Valentine’s Day was meant to be a testament to a love story spanning over two decades. However, her mention of their first “date” in Valentine’s Day 2000, set against the backdrop of Byron Donalds’ marriage timeline—married in 1999, divorced in 2002, and remarried to Erika the following year—raises more than just eyebrows; it raises questions about integrity, honesty, and the personal conduct of those in or adjacent to political power.

Mehdi Hasan’s intervention was not just a jab but a spotlight on the discrepancy. By simply referencing publicly available records, Hasan transformed what could have been an overlooked post into a conversation about character and accountability. It’s a classic case of the past refusing to stay buried, and it’s particularly piquant given the political climate and the personalities involved.

The reactions to this revelation are telling. Nate March, a PR professional, and Cory Provost, an ex-Obama delegate, both expressed their skepticism and unsurprise, respectively. Provost’s comments cut deeper, suggesting a pattern of behavior within the Republican party that aligns with unethical conduct. This isn’t just gossip; it’s a reflection on the standards to which we hold our public officials and their circles.

What’s perhaps most intriguing is the reaction from self-identified MAGA supporters. Even within Donalds’ ideological camp, there are those willing to question the narrative, indicating that issues of personal integrity might transcend political allegiance. This is a crack in the facade that could widen into a chasm if not addressed.

In a broader sense, this incident is a microcosm of the current political discourse. It’s not just about a potentially adulterous affair; it’s about the transparency, truthfulness, and moral fiber of those in the political sphere. If we can’t trust them to be honest about their personal lives, how can we trust them with governance and policy?

As for Erika Donalds’ Valentine’s Day post, it’s a stark reminder that in the digital age, the past is never really past, and attempts to paint a rosier picture can backfire spectacularly. It’s a lesson in the importance of integrity, both personal and political, and a call to the public to remain vigilant and questioning.

To the Donalds and their defenders, this might seem like a tempest in a teapot. But for those concerned with the ethical conduct of public figures, it’s yet another red flag in a field already too scarlet. In the end, the true measure of a person’s character is not in how they craft their public image, but in the actions and decisions they make when they think no one is watching. Byron Donalds’ and Erika Donalds’ Valentine’s Day post inadvertently served as a reminder of that timeless truth.
