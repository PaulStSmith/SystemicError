'No going back' for next president as Trump makes US reversal 'impossible': analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 29, 20263 min read

The Misplaced Blame for Irreversible Diplomacy

In a recent piece by Salon’s Mike Lofgren, a narrative unfolds asserting that former President Donald Trump has irrevocably damaged U.S. international relations, with actions and policies that his successors will supposedly find impossible to reverse. Lofgren’s depiction of Trump’s foreign policies as a catastrophic and unalterable roadblock is not only overly simplistic but also skews the realities of presidential power and diplomatic flexibility.

Deciphering Institutional Power

First and foremost, it is critical to understand the institutional powers at play. The President of the United States, while influential, does not operate in a vacuum. Decisions, especially those pertaining to foreign policy and geopolitical strategies, involve an array of actors including but not limited to the State Department, the Defense Department, Congress, and various international allies and adversaries. To project the notion that Trump single-handedly and permanently altered the trajectory of U.S. foreign relations is to misunderstand the complexities of governance.

Analyzing the Decisions

Lofgren points to specific actions such as the stance against Venezuela and Iran, and the bizarre interest in acquiring Greenland, as examples of Trump’s detrimental legacy. However, each of these decisions, while influenced by the president, were also shaped by broader strategic calculations and inputs from various governmental bodies. Moreover, the ability to undo these decisions lies not just with the sitting president but with the potential shifts in global politics, international response, and future U.S. legislative and executive actions.

The Role of Media Framing

The framing of Trump’s impact as irreversible is not just an oversight but a misdirection. It absolves current and future leaders from taking responsibility or action to amend or alter the course set by a previous administration. By declaring the situation a “point of no return,” Lofgren inadvertently strips subsequent administrations of their agency, implying that efforts to mend fences or change directions are futile. This is a dangerous precedent to set, as it discourages accountability and proactive governance.

Beyond the Hyperbole

While Trump’s presidency was undoubtedly controversial and marked by a series of aggressive foreign policy decisions, the hyperbolic assertion that these actions have permanently cornered U.S. diplomacy is unfounded. History is replete with instances where new leaders have significantly shifted their countries’ foreign policies. To claim that Trump’s actions are beyond rectification is to deny the dynamic nature of international relations.

Moreover, Lofgren’s portrayal of Trump—focusing heavily on his personal flaws and alleged intellectual deficiencies—while perhaps factually accurate, does little to contribute to a substantive discussion on policy and governance. Such personal attacks may resonate with certain readerships but they detract from the more pressing analysis of systemic and strategic political decisions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, while it is indisputable that Trump has left a mark on U.S. foreign policy, the narrative that this impact is irreversible not only misrepresents the power dynamics within the U.S. government but also underestimates the capacity for future administrations to effect change. The resilience of U.S. institutions and the inherent checks and balances designed to mitigate the overreach of any single administration suggest that while the path may be difficult, it is certainly not impassable. As observers and analysts, it is our duty to avoid conflating sensationalism with analysis, and to maintain a clear-eyed view of the potential for political and diplomatic evolution.
