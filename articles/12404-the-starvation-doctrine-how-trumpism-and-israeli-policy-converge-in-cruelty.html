The Starvation Doctrine: How Trumpism and Israeli Policy Converge in Cruelty

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 25, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Starvation as Policy in GazaTrumpism and the Death of AidThe Shared Ethos: Authoritarian DeprivationAmerica’s ComplicityThis Is a PreviewThe Response Must Be Radical

There is a throughline of violence that does not always wear a uniform or carry a gun. Sometimes, it comes disguised in bureaucratic jargon, wrapped in legalese, or justified as "national security." Sometimes, it looks like a child with a distended belly in Rafah. Sometimes, it’s a shuttered USAID office in South Sudan. And in both cases, the ideology is the same: withhold help, watch them suffer, call it strategy.

Starvation as Policy in Gaza

The humanitarian crisis in Gaza has reached a catastrophic stage. According to reports from Time Magazine, WHO, and the United Nations, over 113 people—most of them children—have already died from starvation and acute malnutrition. Thousands more are critically at risk. Aid convoys have been stalled, bombed, or shot at. Hospitals are operating without basic medical supplies. And those who dare to seek food risk death by sniper or drone.

This is not a natural disaster. This is policy.

Israel has used starvation as a siege weapon. The closure of crossings, the bombardment of supply depots, and the deliberate obstruction of humanitarian aid are not collateral consequences; they are part of a systemic plan to degrade Palestinian life. Gaza is not just being destroyed—it is being starved into submission.

The Israeli government's justification rests on a cynical calculus: blame Hamas, punish everyone. Starve the baby to hurt the militant. It's a grotesque logic, one that echoes war crimes statutes. And yet it continues, with American-made bombs and American-funded silence.

Trumpism and the Death of Aid

If you think this cruelty is limited to the Middle East, look closer to home.

Donald Trump didn’t just mishandle foreign aid; he deliberately dismantled it. USAID—once a flawed but functional tool of American diplomacy and humanitarian assistance—was gutted during his tenure.

  • Budgets were slashed.

  • Entire offices dealing with global health, food security, and climate resilience were closed or defunded.

  • Career experts were replaced with political appointees who saw aid not as a necessity but as a bargaining chip.

Under Trump, foreign aid became a weapon of retribution. Countries that didn’t fall in line with his ideology were cut off. Programs supporting maternal health, education, and food distribution were axed under the guise of “America First.” The global gag rule prevented NGOs from even mentioning abortion, under penalty of losing all U.S. support.

Sound familiar? It should. Because it's the same doctrine being used in Gaza: starve them into obedience.

The Shared Ethos: Authoritarian Deprivation

What binds Israeli policy in Gaza and Trumpism's destruction of aid programs is a shared ethos of authoritarian deprivation. In both frameworks:

  • Empathy is seen as weakness.

  • Aid is viewed as an undeserved gift, rather than a shared obligation.

  • Suffering is not an emergency—it's leverage.

This is the politics of punishment by hunger. It is not about borders. It is not about budgets. It is about power over life and death.

Whether it’s ICE separating children from their parents and locking them in cages, or IDF soldiers shooting at starving civilians trying to reach a UN food truck, the logic is the same: control through cruelty.

America’s Complicity

The United States, for all its performative concern, remains complicit. American bombs fall on Palestinian homes. American dollars fund the Israeli blockade. American vetoes block ceasefires. And the American public, dulled by propaganda and poisoned by indifference, turns away.

Joe Biden may not be Donald Trump, but his administration’s feckless response to Gaza suggests a continuity of cowardice. The machinery that Trump built—ICE, border walls, defunded global aid, militarized humanitarian policy—is still running, even if the driver changed.

The longer we tolerate this, the deeper we entrench the starvation doctrine into the fabric of governance.

This Is a Preview

Gaza is not an aberration. It is a test case. If the world accepts children dying of hunger because the government in power deems them politically inconvenient, it won’t stop there.

Trumpism already brought that ideology home:

  • Migrants denied water in the desert.

  • Asylum seekers made to wait in cartel-controlled zones.

  • Refugees blocked from entry during wars America helped start.

And if Trump returns in 2025, with a cabinet of Heritage Foundation loyalists and Project 2025 blueprints in hand, you can expect a full-fledged embrace of siege politics: at the border, in the courts, in foreign policy.

Gaza is the future authoritarianism wants.

The Response Must Be Radical

We do not defeat the starvation doctrine with tepid policy tweaks. We do not reform cruelty. We abolish it.

That means:

  • Abolish ICE, because its entire function is racialized, state-sponsored punishment.

  • Restore and expand global aid programs, not as charity, but as moral responsibility.

  • Sanction states that use starvation as a weapon—even if they are "allies."

  • And most of all, break the political consensus that says some lives are negotiable.

Because when you make hunger a weapon, you are declaring war on the human spirit.

And we cannot be neutral in that war.
