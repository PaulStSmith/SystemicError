'They'll turn against us': MAGA billionaire's huge warning resurrected after election rout

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20255 min read

A warning by tech billionaire Peter Thiel five years ago about young voters and socialism was resurfaced after this week’s election wins by New York Mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani and other Democrats.

The PayPal co-founder sent a private message in 2020 offering a theory to explain why millennials were more open to socialist ideas than other generations, and Thiel argued that student debt and the housing costs were to blame for the shift, reported Business Insider.

“When 70% of Millennials say they are pro-socialist, we need to do better than simply dismiss them by saying that they are stupid or entitled or brainwashed; we should try and understand why,” Thiel wrote in a January 2020 email to top Facebook officials, including CEO Mark Zuckerberg, President of Global Affairs Nick Clegg and venture capitalist Marc Andreessen.

Billionaire investor Chamath Palihapitiya posted a screenshot of the email on X after Mamdani, a self-described Democratic Socialist, defeated former New York governor Andrew Cuomo, who ran as an independent, and Republican Curtis Sliwa to become the next mayor of the nation’s largest city.

“Too much student debt and lack of affordable housing keeps young people with negative capital for too long,” Palihapitiya wrote on X. “And without a stake in the capitalist system, they will turn against it.”

Thiel, often described as a libertarian, spoke in support of President Donald Trump at the 2016 Republican National Convention and has been a mentor to Vice President JD Vance.

“From the perspective of a broken generational compact, there seems to be a pretty straightforward answer to me, namely, that when one has too much student debt or if housing is too unaffordable, then one will have negative capital for a long time and/or find it very hard to start accumulating capital in the form of real estate; and if one has no stake in the capitalist system, then one may well turn against it,” Thiel wrote in his email to Facebook leaders.

Our Analysis:

The Thiel Theory: A Closer Look at Generational Discontent

In an article that resurfaces Peter Thiel’s prophetic warning about the appeal of socialism to younger voters, we find ourselves confronted with the stark reality of our current political and economic landscape. Thiel, a tech billionaire and noted libertarian, made headlines five years ago with his insightful, albeit private, musings on the reasons behind millennials’ gravitation towards socialist ideas. With the recent electoral victories of Democrats like New York Mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani, a self-described Democratic Socialist, Thiel’s words have never seemed more prescient.

Thiel’s Theory: Student Debt and Housing Costs

The crux of Thiel’s argument, as reported by Business Insider, centers on the twin burdens of student debt and unaffordable housing. “Too much student debt and lack of affordable housing keeps young people with negative capital for too long,” Thiel posits, a sentiment echoed by billionaire investor Chamath Palihapitiya. This “negative capital” scenario, as Thiel elaborates, leaves many young individuals disenfranchised from the capitalist system—a system that seems increasingly rigged against their favor.

To dismiss the millennial and subsequent generational cohorts’ socialist leanings as mere folly or the result of indoctrination is, as Thiel rightly points out, to miss the forest for the trees. The heart of the issue lies in the economic disenfranchisement faced by a significant portion of the populace. When 70% of Millennials express pro-socialist sentiments, it’s not a trend to be scoffed at; it’s a glaring indication of systemic failure.

A Broken Generational Compact

Thiel’s commentary is a scathing indictment of the broken promises to younger generations. The implicit generational compact—that education and hard work would lead to prosperity and homeownership—has been shattered. In its place, we find a landscape of insurmountable student debt and a housing market that’s increasingly out of reach for the average American. Thiel’s analysis, while coming from a place of privilege and power, nonetheless hits the nail on the head: when people feel they have no stake in the capitalist system, their allegiance to it wanes.

The Role of Powerbrokers

It’s worth noting the audience to whom Thiel addressed his concerns: the upper echelons of Facebook’s leadership, including CEO Mark Zuckerberg and venture capitalist Marc Andreessen. This choice of audience is telling. It underscores a recognition that the issues at hand are not merely economic or political but deeply entwined with the technological and social platforms that shape our discourse and understanding.

However, one must question whether these powerbrokers are the right allies in addressing the root causes of disenfranchisement. While Thiel’s analysis may be accurate, his and his audience’s vested interests in the status quo complicate the potential for genuine reform. The tech elite, for all their innovation and disruption, remain deeply entrenched in a capitalist system that has benefited them immensely.

Conclusion: Beyond Diagnosis to Action

Peter Thiel’s warning about socialism’s appeal is a clarion call to address the systemic issues plaguing our economic and political systems. Yet, for all the insight offered, the real challenge lies in moving beyond diagnosis to action. It’s not enough to understand why millennials and subsequent generations feel disenfranchised; what’s required is a concerted effort to redesign our economic structures to be more inclusive, equitable, and just.

As we move forward, the question remains: will those in positions of power heed Thiel’s warning, or will they continue to prop up a system that increasingly serves the few at the expense of the many? For meaningful change to occur, it will take more than acknowledging the problem—it will require a radical reimagining of what our economic and political systems look like.
