Senate GOP unanimously abandons Trump after he opens door to Ghislaine Maxwell pardon

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

Republicans Oppose the Pardon They Are Powerless to Stop

The Useful Gesture

Senate Republicans lined up against a possible pardon for Ghislaine Maxwell, but the move was a performance, not a constraint. The actual power sits in the Oval Office, and Donald Trump has refused to rule out clemency for a convicted sex trafficker with whom he moved in elite social circles for years. A symbolic Senate vote is not accountability. It is cover.

Power Still Runs Through Trump

This story is not about whether Republicans found their moral center. It is about whether they are willing to confront the man who holds the pardon power. They are not. When Trump says he is “open to the question,” he is not musing in the abstract. He is broadcasting that even after conviction, even after the abuse tied to Epstein’s network, the door remains open if loyalty or leverage demands it.

That is the real structure here: Trump retains unilateral authority, and the party that could impeach, investigate, or oppose him prefers procedural theater.

Blanche Is the Real Alarm Bell

The deeper corruption is not just Trump’s evasiveness. It is the installation of Todd Blanche, Trump’s former personal attorney, as acting attorney general while he handles the Epstein files. Blanche met Maxwell for nine hours, then she was moved to a lower-security prison. Survivors were not given anything like that access.

Dani Bensky’s testimony makes the hierarchy brutally clear: nine hours for Maxwell, not nine minutes for survivors. That is not an accident of scheduling. It is an institutional choice about whose claims matter and whose pain can be managed as background noise.

The Senate’s Moral Posture

Jacky Rosen forced the vote through unanimous consent, and no Republican objected. That tells you almost everything. They were willing to go on record against clemency only when the cost was zero. They did not use the moment to confront Trump’s power, Blanche’s proximity, or the machinery that keeps Epstein-era elites insulated from consequences.

This is how elite institutions manufacture innocence. They denounce the outcome they cannot stop and avoid the mechanisms that produce it.

Justice as Theater

The language of the resolution is strong about “sexual exploitation of children,” but the political behavior around it is weak. Republicans can oppose a pardon and still preserve the structure that makes a pardon plausible. They can stage outrage and keep the person who might execute the pardon in power. They can criticize Maxwell while refusing to challenge the network of protection around her.

That is not a break with Trumpism. It is Trumpism with a facial expression.

The Pattern

The pattern is familiar: concentrated power, selective indignation, and institutions that prefer symbolic distance to material confrontation. The Senate gets to look resolute. Trump gets to keep the leverage. Blanche gets to operate inside the state while carrying the president’s personal history with him. Survivors are left to rely on public embarrassment as a substitute for justice.

This is how authoritarian-adjacent politics works in plain sight: not by hiding the abuse, but by turning accountability into a ceremony no one with real power intends to honor.
