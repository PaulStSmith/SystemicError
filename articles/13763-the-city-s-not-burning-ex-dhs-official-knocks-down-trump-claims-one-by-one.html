'The city's not burning': Ex-DHS official knocks down Trump claims one by one

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Dissecting Trump's Misleading Claims on Los Angeles ProtestsThe Exaggeration of UnrestThe Misuse of Military ForceThe Bigger Picture: Power, Not Immigration

A former homeland security official walked through a number of president Donald Trump's claims about the Los Angeles protests and found that almost none of them were accurate, and some were unlawful.

The president has called out thousands of National Guard troops and hundreds of U.S. Marines to the city, where protesters have clashed with local police over the Trump administration's recent immigration crackdown, and former Department of Homeland Security undersecretary Juliette Kayyem fact-checked the president's justification for escalating the conflict.

"I think what you're seeing is the by the administration is a standard of description of what's going on in Los Angeles that sort of, you know, is, I would say, exaggerated," Kayyem said, "in terms of [saying that immigrants are] taking over the country, California wouldn't exist, insurrection, incitement, war – all of this language to describe what is essentially, right, it is unruly, violent rioters, at times, who are who are part of a larger, peaceful protest against part of these ICE raids."

"So the question isn't, are there bad things going on in Los Angeles?" Kayyem added. "Yes, I mean, yes, you see it – there are burning cars, there's unruliness, there is looting. But is that supposed to be dealt with with a federalized military or in a civil society with police officers and firefighters and state police?"

Gov. Gavin Newsom has sued the administration for calling up the state National Guard, over which he is commander-in-chief, without his authorization, and he warned in an address Tuesday night that the president was seeking "blanket authority" over other states' guards as part of a “brazen abuse of power” that “inflamed a combustible situation.”

"That is the debate that the White House wants it to be a debate about immigration," Kayyem said. "Gov. Newsom, as we heard last night in his speech, says this is a debate about power and federalism and this and the military in American society, and I have come to believe that that Newsom is probably right in that regard. When you look at the streets in Los Angeles – I'm from Los Angeles. Yes, it's bad. It's a big city, the city is not falling apart. It is having a public safety problem."

" CNN This Morning" guest host Erica Hill agreed, saying that Trump's claims about the situation in Los Angeles were easily debunked.

"Yeah, the city is not burning, despite what the president may claim," Hill said.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump's Misleading Claims on Los Angeles Protests

In a world where political rhetoric often flirts dangerously close to the edge of fiction, former President Donald Trump's claims about the Los Angeles protests serve as a masterclass in misinformation. A recent analysis by Juliette Kayyem, a former Department of Homeland Security undersecretary, exposes the chasm between Trump's narrative and the reality on the ground. Let's dive into the specifics and unravel the web of exaggeration and deceit spun by the former president.

The Exaggeration of Unrest

Trump's depiction of the Los Angeles protests as a battleground, necessitating the deployment of thousands of National Guard troops and hundreds of U.S. Marines, is nothing short of apocalyptic. Yet, as Kayyem points out, this narrative grossly overstates the situation. The protests, largely against the Trump administration's immigration crackdown, indeed saw moments of violence and chaos. However, to describe these events as akin to an insurrection or a war is not only inaccurate but also irresponsibly inflammatory.

The Reality: A Peaceful Majority

Kayyem's analysis underscores a critical point often lost in Trump's portrayal: the majority of protestors are peaceful. Yes, there have been instances of unruliness and looting, but these do not define the entirety of the protest movement. The former president's narrative conveniently omits this fact, opting instead for a blanket characterization that serves only to demonize and delegitimize the protestors' grievances.

The Misuse of Military Force

The decision to call in the military to quell the protests is a contentious one, raising profound questions about the role of the military in civil society. Kayyem rightly questions the necessity and appropriateness of this response, suggesting that such measures are disproportionate to the scale and nature of the unrest. This is not a war zone; it is a city grappling with public safety issues amid a backdrop of political and social unrest.

Governor Newsom's Lawsuit: A Clash Over Power and Federalism

The response from California's Governor Gavin Newsom further illuminates the deeper issues at play. Newsom's lawsuit against the Trump administration for deploying the National Guard without state authorization is a stark reminder of the tension between state and federal powers. His warning of a "brazen abuse of power" by the president not only highlights the immediate overreach but also signals the potential for a dangerous precedent in the use of military force within the United States.

The Bigger Picture: Power, Not Immigration

Kayyem and Newsom both recognize that, at its core, this debacle is not merely about immigration. It's about power, federalism, and the role of the military in American society. Trump's focus on immigration serves as a smokescreen for a much more alarming agenda: the consolidation of power through the militarization of domestic issues.

Conclusion: Debunking Trump's Claims

In closing, the analysis of Trump's claims about the Los Angeles protests reveals a troubling pattern of misinformation and exaggeration. The narrative put forth by the former president is not only misleading but dangerously escalates tensions by portraying a distorted view of reality. It is imperative that we critically examine these claims, understand the broader implications, and recognize the peaceful intentions of the majority of protestors. As Kayyem and Newsom have shown, the truth is often far more complex than the simplistic and sensationalist narrative offered by Trump.
