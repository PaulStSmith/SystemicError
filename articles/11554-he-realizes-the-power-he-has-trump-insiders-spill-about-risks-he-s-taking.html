'He realizes the power he has': Trump insiders spill about 'risks' he's taking

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Permanent Federal Takeover Ambitions: Trump's Second Term Escapades

Donald Trump (ALLISON ROBBERT/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo)

A White House insider said that President Donald Trump’s federal takeover of Washington, D.C. could very well “end up permanent,” according to reporting Saturday from CNN.

“I could see where federalizing the police ends up permanent,” said a person described as someone “close to the White House,” speaking with CNN. “There’s plenty more than can be done.”

The nation’s capitol is currently swarming with more than 1,600 federal officers and several dozen members of the National Guard as part of Trump’s efforts to crack down on crime in the city, though the operation thus far has produced mixed results, with just over 100 arrests made during the first three days.

Trump has solid legal footing to take control over Washington, D.C., but for only 30 days. Extending the takeover any longer would require approval in the Senate, which remains highly unlikely given the GOP’s slim Senate majority.

Nevertheless, Trump, according to those close with the president and the White House, has felt emboldened during his second term, and far more likely to act beyond what his presidential authority permits.

“He is far more confident this term than when he first came to Washington,” said someone described by CNN as a “person close to Trump.” “He is self-assured in his decisions, and he is far more willing to take risks.”

A White House official, also speaking with CNN on the condition of anonymity, said that Trump felt “liberated” after taking back control of the White House last year, and after surviving countless legal battles that he viewed as a political prosecution.

“After 2021, everyone counted him for dead,” the White House official told CNN “A lot of what is driving his agenda stems from the fact he believes he was given a mandate in ‘24. Now he is enacting.”

Another factor insiders said was contributing to the president’s more aggressive administration the second go around was his current cabinet. Trump’s cabinet during his first presidency was fraught with leaks, dissent and high-profile departures.

Trump’s cabinet during his second term, however, was a different story.

“He’s got loyal people around him now,” said a Trump advisor, speaking with CNN on the condition of anonymity. “And he realizes the power that he has and the things that he can do.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Permanent Federal Takeover Ambitions: Trump's Second Term Escapades

The recent CNN report unveils a troubling prospect: a potential permanent federal takeover of Washington, D.C., under President Donald Trump's administration. This narrative, sourced from insiders "close to the White House," paints a vivid picture of a presidency emboldened to the point of recklessness. Let's dissect the layers of implications and the audacious moves that could redefine the boundaries of presidential authority.

The Temptation of Permanence:

The idea that the federalization of the police in D.C. could become a permanent fixture is not only alarming but fundamentally anti-democratic. The presence of more than 1,600 federal officers and National Guard members ostensibly to combat crime, while only making "just over 100 arrests" in its initial days, raises questions about efficiency versus control. The notion, as reported, that "there's plenty more than can be done" hints at an appetite for expanded federal intervention, a slippery slope toward authoritarianism.

Legal Grounds and Senate Hurdles:

Trump's legal authority to take control over Washington, D.C., albeit temporary for 30 days, introduces a precarious balancing act between law and overreach. The necessity of Senate approval to extend this takeover highlights a critical check on presidential power. However, the report's suggestion that Trump feels "far more confident" in his second term to push the boundaries of his authority is a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic norms when faced with a determined executive.

A Presidency Emboldened and "Liberated":

Descriptions of Trump feeling "liberated" after his electoral victory and surviving "countless legal battles" depict a president unshackled by the constraints of his first term. This perceived mandate in ‘24, as cited by a White House official, fuels a dangerous bravado to enact an agenda without restraint. The advisory circle's transition from a cabinet "fraught with leaks, dissent, and high-profile departures" to one with "loyal people" signals a shift towards an echo chamber, amplifying the risks of unchecked power.

The Dangers of Loyalty Over Checks:

The emphasis on loyalty within Trump's second-term cabinet is a red flag for governance. Loyalty, while valuable, becomes perilous when it supersedes the duty to challenge and advise based on principle and the public good. A cabinet reshaped in the president's image, devoid of dissent, paves the way for a presidency less accountable and more prone to overreach.

The Bottom Line:

The CNN report sheds light on a momentous shift in the Trump presidency, marked by an emboldened approach to governance, an appetite for expanded federal control, and a surrounding echo chamber of loyalty. The potential for a permanent federal takeover of D.C., under the guise of combating crime, represents a significant leap towards authoritarianism, challenging the very fabric of American democracy.

The implications of such a move, both legal and societal, demand rigorous scrutiny and opposition. As this narrative unfolds, it becomes imperative for the media, public, and legislative bodies to remain vigilant, ensuring that the checks and balances enshrined in the Constitution are more than mere words on parchment but active defenses against the overreach of power.
