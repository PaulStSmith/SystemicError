GOP leader tries to laugh off major Mar-a-Lago bruising — with claim that shuns evidence

A shock victory in a Florida election is being seen as a massive blow to the Republican Party ahead of the midterms — but a leading Republican is shrugging it off.

In fact, Majority Leader of the House of Representatives Steve Scalise bucked widely held beliefs — and claimed his party will be flipping seats in December.

Donald Trump’s administration was dealt an embarrassing blow as the Democratic Party flipped the Florida state House seat that contains Mar-a-Lago Tuesday. Winning candidate Emily Gregory took the Florida District 87 state House seat with 51 percent to 49 percent, a margin of just over 750 votes.

Speaking to Raw Story about the reason for the swing to Democratic Party, Scalise suggested it was merely a case of low voter turnout. He said, “Now, special elections are always unique because they’re very low turnout.

“You know, surely you look at those and see there are things we can learn and improve upon when the big election comes.”

Scalise went on to suggest there had been less focus on the special election in Trump’s backyard as the GOP had a larger stake in success at the midterm elections. He said, “And obviously, November is the election that we are focused on. We’ve still got primaries to go through in a lot of states. There are runoffs in Texas. There’s always going to be elections, but the big election is going to be the November midterm.

“And we’re laser-focused on that and not only holding the House, but the opportunity to grow our majority. We’ve got some real opportunities in a number of districts to actually flip more seats from Democrat to Republican, and that’s our focus.”

The Mar-a-Lago seat flip in the historically red seat shocked pundits, and CNN expert Harry Enten said it was likely a sign of things to come for the GOP.

“What is happening right now in Mar-a-Lago is unlikely to stay a Mar-a-Lago. It is likely to expand nationwide and to expand in the midterm elections as well,” he said.

The Mar-a-Lago loss could precede the largest GOP midterm loss in 100 years, according to analysis from The Daily Beast. Martha McHardy wrote, “Historically, the largest House losses include Democrats losing 116 seats in 1894, Republicans losing 77 in 1922, and Democrats losing 72 in 1938, with other notable losses in 1974 and 2010.

“By comparison, the Democrats’ 41‑seat gain in 2018—the largest in 44 years—was still well below 70.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Florida Election Upset

In the wake of an unexpected Democratic victory in Florida, a state House seat that even encompasses Mar-a-Lago, the political commentary sphere is ablaze with prognostications and hot takes. The Republican response, particularly from House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, seems to downplay the significance of this event. But is the analysis and finger-pointing missing the larger picture? Let’s dissect the narrative, identify the true holders of power, and assess the accountability of decisions made.

Institutional Power and Decision Making

At first glance, the loss of a historically Republican seat would appear as a significant setback for the GOP, especially coming from an area as symbolically tied to the party as Mar-a-Lago. However, Steve Scalise’s comments suggest a strategic minimization of the event’s importance, attributing the loss to low voter turnout and the unique nature of special elections.

Who Holds the Power? The Republican Party, with its institutional control over its campaign strategies and narratives, certainly has the power here. The decision to prioritize certain elections over others, and how to respond to losses, lies squarely within the party’s strategic planning committees and leadership, including figures like Scalise.

Who Made the Decision? The framing of the loss as minor and not indicative of a larger trend is a decision made by the party’s leadership, aimed at controlling the narrative and maintaining morale ahead of more consequential elections.

Misdirection and Accountability

The article’s framing, and Scalise’s comments within it, seem to misdirect responsibility away from any substantive analysis of why the seat was lost. By attributing the loss to low voter turnout and the nature of special elections, there’s an implicit argument that the outcome was more a fluke than a symptom of deeper issues within the party or its policies. This overlooks the potential accountability of the GOP’s strategies, messaging, and candidate selection in not only this election but in broader electoral trends.

Rejecting False Equivalence and Lazy Narratives

The suggestion that this loss is merely a bump in the road overlooks a critical opportunity for reflection and adjustment within the GOP. While it’s true that special elections can produce atypical results, dismissing out of hand the possibility that this upset signals broader dissatisfaction or shifts in the electorate is dangerously complacent. Moreover, comparing this loss to historical losses or gains without context serves to obfuscate rather than elucidate.

The narrative that the GOP is focused on the “big election” in November and views the loss of the Mar-a-Lago seat as inconsequential is a convenient scapegoat. It minimizes the potential for learning and adaptation, which are essential in politics. Scalise’s assertion that the party is aiming to flip more seats from Democrat to Republican without addressing the reasons behind the recent loss seems like wishful thinking rather than strategic planning.

Conclusion

The real story behind the Florida election upset isn’t just about a surprise Democratic win or a Republican loss; it’s about the interpretation of these events and the strategic responses to them. The GOP’s leadership, particularly figures like Steve Scalise, are using this moment to reinforce their focus on future victories, potentially at the expense of understanding the current political landscape.

It’s essential to critically examine these narratives and the decisions that underpin them. While it’s natural for parties to want to project confidence and minimize losses, there’s a fine line between strategic optimism and delusional thinking. The GOP’s dismissive stance could either be a calculated attempt to maintain morale or a misreading of the political tea leaves. Only the forthcoming elections will reveal the true efficacy of this approach.
