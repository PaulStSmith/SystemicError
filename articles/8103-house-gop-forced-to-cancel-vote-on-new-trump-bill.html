House Republicans were forced on Wednesday to cancel an upcoming vote on the SCORE Act, a controversial piece of legislation backed by President Donald Trump that would give a sweetheart deal to college sports leagues at the expense of players.

According to Punchbowl News’ Jake Sherman, Republicans lacked the votes on the legislation, as Democrats were unifying against it, and a handful of conservative hardliners were also skeptical, with Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX) already confirmed as against it.

Republicans may reschedule a vote as soon as tomorrow.

The bill would give antitrust protections to the National Collegiate Athletics Association (NCAA), pre-empting state regulations on the issue, and clarify nationwide that student athletes are not employees, precluding many labor rights and limiting their ability to profit off their name, image, and likeness.

A number of Democrats co-sponsored the bill, but opposition from the Democratic side is growing.

On Wednesday, the Congressional Black Caucus issued a statement in opposition to the bill.

“We can all agree that college athletes need stronger protections. Unfortunately, the SCORE Act doesn’t provide them,” said the group. “It would permanently strip college athletes of labor and employment rights, including the right to unionize; prevent them from challenging harmful or anticompetitive conduct; and grant the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) and conferences sweeping immunity when their actions jeopardize athletes’ education, health, safety, or financial well-being.”

A number of hard-right lawmakers have gripes with the bill, too.

“This is not supposed to be a market, not in that classic sense,” Roy said of his opposition. “This is not supposed to be NFL-lite, but that is how we’re treating it.”

Our Analysis:

House Republicans Hit a Roadblock with the SCORE Act

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of political miscalculation, House Republicans were compelled to retract their plans to vote on the controversial SCORE Act. This piece of legislation, fervently supported by President Donald Trump, reeks of preferential treatment for college sports leagues at the glaring expense of the players themselves.

Punchbowl News’ Jake Sherman sheds light on the Republicans’ embarrassing shortfall in votes, a predicament brought about by a rare coalition of Democrats and a sprinkle of conservative hardliners. Leading the charge against the bill is Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX), marking a notable fracture within the Republican ranks.

The SCORE Act, in its essence, is a thinly veiled attempt at bolstering the National Collegiate Athletics Association (NCAA)’s grip over college sports. By granting antitrust protections and overriding state regulations, the bill seeks to cement a dystopian reality where student athletes are denied the basic rights of employees. This includes restricting their ability to profit from their name, image, and likeness – a direct assault on their financial independence and personal agency.

Despite the initial backing from certain Democrats, the tide is swiftly turning. The Congressional Black Caucus has stepped into the arena with a damning critique of the bill. They argue that rather than fortifying protections for college athletes, the SCORE Act would effectively strip them of crucial labor and employment rights, including the right to unionize. Moreover, it would hand the NCAA and its conferences an unchecked license to jeopardize athletes’ education, health, safety, and financial stability.

This bill is not just a misstep; it’s a glaring testament to the skewed priorities of its proponents. Rep. Chip Roy’s comments highlight the ideological rift within the opposition, painting the bill as an attempt to professionalize college sports in a manner akin to the NFL, albeit without the corresponding rights and protections for the players.

The Bottom Line

The SCORE Act is a striking example of legislative overreach, designed to benefit the powerful at the expense of the vulnerable. It’s a bill that seeks to entrench inequality in college sports, prioritizing profits and institutional control over the well-being and rights of student athletes. The growing opposition to the bill, both from within and outside the Republican Party, underscores a broader discontent with policies that serve the few at the cost of the many.

As the House Republicans scramble to salvage the vote, the debacle serves as a poignant reminder of the inherent flaws within our current political and collegiate sports systems. The SCORE Act is not the solution; it’s part of the problem. And as this saga unfolds, one can only hope that it sparks a broader conversation about the rights of student athletes and the need for a more equitable approach to collegiate sports.
