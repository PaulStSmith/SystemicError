President Donald Trump is taking his supporters for a ride on the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case, former federal prosecutor Andrew Weissmann told MS NOW’s Nicolle Wallace on Friday’s edition of “Deadline: White House“ — and the new dump of documents only proves that further.

“Andrew, we’ve been talking theoretically. You’ve been reading,” said Wallace. “Anything strike you in terms of what the Department of Justice has made public?”

“I want to turn to something that you said at the outset, which is that we’re going to look for what’s in there, but we’re going to also look for what’s not in there,” said Weissmann. “And I just want to talk for a moment about what [Deputy Attorney General] Todd Blanche said today, because it is outrageous. It is. To me, this is just a huge joke that is basically treating the MAGA base as a bunch of fools.”

“So Todd Blanche said today that Donald Trump for years has wanted to make all of this public,” said Weissmann. “Well, we know that’s not true, because if he wanted to do it, he could have done it on day one of his presidency and he hasn’t. Two, he said, well, as you’ve reported, that he’s going to have produced hundreds of thousands, but few hundred thousand documents today and a few hundred thousand he expects later. Well, the law, as you’ve said, required it to be produced today. So what he is saying right now is they’re in violation of the law. That’s point two.”

“And then I did a little math because in September of this year, the Department of Justice put out an official statement that said there’s nothing to see here. And they’ve gone through the 300GB of information. Well, a few hundred thousand and a few hundred thousand does not equal 300GB. That is just not possible. It’s not plausible. And so the question is where are the what is not being produced? I mean, to me this is just going to scream coverup because you have them not complying with the law. Lying, in my view, to the American people about the president’s wishes ... the math doesn’t equal the number.”

“To me, that is a huge part of this story, is what is not being produced,” he added. “A responsible DOJ would actually have had a press conference and they would have talked through the measures they took. They would say, this is what we’ve done, here’s the procedures, here’s what we did instead. You know that there’s a problem with what they’re doing when they do this through a sort of short-form announcement on Fox News that doesn’t answer any of that. To me, that’s the story right now.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Theatre of the Absurd: Trump, Epstein, and the DOJ’s Latest Charade

In a recent episode of Deadline: White House on MS NOW, former federal prosecutor Andrew Weissmann delivered a scathing critique of the unfolding drama surrounding President Donald Trump and the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case. The conversation, peppered with revelations and accusations, unfurled a narrative so convoluted it could only find its place in the annals of contemporary political absurdity.

Weissmann points out the glaring inconsistencies and outright fabrications in the narrative peddled by Todd Blanche, the Deputy Attorney General, regarding Trump’s intentions and actions related to the Epstein case documents. Let’s dissect these claims with the surgical precision they deserve.

The Great Pretender: Trump’s Illusion of Transparency

Blanche’s assertion that Trump has long desired to make these documents public is laughable at best and insidiously misleading at worst. If Trump’s intentions were so pure, why wait until the eleventh hour of his presidency to act on them? This isn’t a hallmark of transparency; it’s political theatre, designed to mislead and manipulate.

Legal Boundaries: Skirting Around the Law

The Deputy AG’s statement about the document release not only raises eyebrows but also flags potential legal violations. The fact that the documents were not all released as mandated screams non-compliance. It’s not just a minor oversight; it’s a flagrant disregard for legal requirements, suggesting that the Trump administration feels emboldened to operate above the law.

A Mathematical Impossibility: The Missing Bytes

Weissmann’s astute observation regarding the discrepancy in the volume of documents – or the lack thereof – exposes yet another layer of deception. The claimed document release volume doesn’t come close to matching the 300GB of information purportedly reviewed by the Department of Justice. This isn’t just creative accounting; it’s a deliberate attempt to obfuscate and withhold information from the public, hinting at a deeper, more insidious cover-up.

A Story of Omission: What’s Not Being Said

The crux of Weissmann’s argument, and arguably the most damning, is what’s left unsaid. The absence of a comprehensive DOJ briefing on the process and decisions surrounding these documents speaks volumes. In lieu of transparency, we’re fed snippets through a media outlet known for its partisan slant, leaving more questions than answers. This isn’t just poor communication; it’s a strategic silence.

Conclusion: The Mockery of Democracy

What we witness here is a brazen mockery of the principles of transparency and accountability that underpin a functioning democracy. The Trump administration, with its allies, has crafted a narrative so riddled with holes, contradictions, and legal ambiguities that it would be laughable if it weren’t so profoundly disturbing.

It’s not just about the Epstein case or the documents; it’s about the enduring impact of lies, deception, and manipulation on the fabric of American democracy. The MAGA base, along with the American public at large, deserves better than this circus of untruths. But as long as these theatrics continue unchallenged, the joke, tragically, is on us.
