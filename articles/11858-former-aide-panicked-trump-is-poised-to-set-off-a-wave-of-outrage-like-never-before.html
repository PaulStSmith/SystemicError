Former aide panicked Trump is poised to set off a wave of 'outrage like never before'

President Donald Trump’s former White House press secretary Sean Spicer issued his former boss a dire warning Friday in an appearance on the podcast The Morning Meeting, cautioning the president of an unprecedented fallout should he issue a pardon to among America’s most notorious sex traffickers.

Rumors continue to circulate that Trump is weighing a pardon for Ghislaine Maxwell, who is currently serving a 20-year sentence for sex trafficking in association with the late Jeffrey Epstein, who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, and is alleged to have maintained a ‘client list’ of powerful figures for blackmail purposes.

Were Trump to ultimately issue a pardon for Maxwell, the consequences would likely be “outrage like never before,” Spicer argued.

“There will be outrage like never before if she gets a deal, this will transcend partisan lines,” Spicer said.

“I'm telling you that if that happens, the number of people that care about this issue, care about those young girls, care about justice for so many of these people that have gotten off, I hope to God nobody in the White House has led him to believe that that's the case, that that would be a smart move.”

Speculation as to a pardon has only intensified after Maxwell was quietly moved to a minimum-security prison last week, a move that required the Trump administration to waive federal prisoner rules to facilitate. Trump’s Justice Department has also met with Maxwell in an effort to, according to Trump’s attorney and DOJ deputy attorney general Todd Blanche, identify potential co-conspirators of Epstein.

Critics, however, have labeled Trump’s new fixation on Maxwell as a ploy to exonerate himself, having long maintained a close friendship with the disgraced financier across decades. One security expert, Marcy Wheeler, said she suspected Trump might dangle a plea deal in front of Maxwell in exchange for incriminating information on his political enemies such as former President Bill Clinton, also a longtime friend of Epstein’s.

Spicer, however, urged Trump to immediately rule out issuing a pardon for Maxwell, and shifted blame for any potential pardon talks to the president’s staff.

“I would say he would be under the impression that he was going to be able to say that they 'got something' from her, further information,” Spicer said.

“I think anyone who sells him on this is lying to him. I think this is very akin to (former FBI Director James) Comey; when he fired Comey, he was told 'if you fire Comey, everyone's going to cheer you on,' and I think people misled him on how that was going to go down. I think this is similar.”

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Dance of Pardons: Trump, Maxwell, and the Specter of Outrage

In what reads like a script discarded for being too implausible even for the most sensational political thriller, we find ourselves staring down the barrel of a scenario that could further contort the already grotesque visage of American politics. Sean Spicer, President Donald Trump's former White House press secretary, has taken to the airwaves with a prophetic warning of catastrophic fallout should Trump decide to pardon Ghislaine Maxwell, a name synonymous with some of the most heinous crimes of our age.

The Pardon That Could Ignite Unprecedented Fury

Maxwell, currently serving a 20-year sentence for her role in a sex trafficking ring that's the stuff of nightmares, has become the center of a rumor mill that's as disturbing as the crimes she's implicated in. The notion that Trump would even consider a pardon for Maxwell is, on its face, an affront to justice and a mockery of the suffering of her victims. Spicer, in his appearance on The Morning Meeting, doesn't mince words: “There will be outrage like never before if she gets a deal, this will transcend partisan lines,” he asserts. And Spicer is spot on. This isn't a matter of left vs. right; it's a matter of right vs. wrong.

A Strategic Misstep of Colossal Proportions

The article paints a picture of a president potentially flirting with a decision that could very well be the final nail in the coffin of his political legacy. To pardon Maxwell would not only be a slap in the face to her victims but also a signal to the world that power and connections can indeed trump justice (pun intended). That Spicer feels compelled to publicly issue such a warning speaks volumes about the gravity of the situation and the potential for irreversible damage to Trump's standing, not just politically but historically.

Blaming the Messengers

Spicer's attempt to shift blame to the president's advisors is a classic move, reminiscent of the old "bad advice" defense. It's akin to saying, "It's not the emperor’s fault he’s naked; it's those who told him he was wearing clothes." While there may be truth to Spicer’s claim that Trump is being misled, it ultimately does little to absolve Trump of responsibility. As president, the buck stops with him, and he alone bears the weight of his decisions.

A Ploy for Political Leverage?

The article also hints at a darker motive behind Trump's interest in Maxwell: the potential extraction of incriminating information on political adversaries. If true, this would be a disturbing exploitation of the justice system for personal political gain. It suggests a willingness to use the pardon power not as a tool for mercy, but as a weapon against political enemies—a development that should concern us all, regardless of political affiliation.

In Conclusion

The scenario laid out in this article is a stark reminder of the dangerous waters we navigate when the lines between justice and political expediency become blurred. Spicer's warning, whether heeded or not, underscores a truth we must not forget: that the actions of those in power today will be judged by the annals of history tomorrow. Let us hope that this judgment reflects a commitment to justice and decency, rather than a capitulation to the darkest impulses of political strategy.
