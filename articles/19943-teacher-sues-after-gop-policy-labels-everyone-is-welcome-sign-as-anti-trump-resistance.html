Teacher sues after GOP policy labels 'everyone is welcome' sign as anti-Trump resistance

By Paulo Santos

Raul Labrador in Cleveland, Ohio in August 2015 (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

In deep red Idaho — a state that President Donald Trump won by 36.5 percent in 2024 — public schoolteacher Sarah Inama hung up, in her classroom, a poster that read, “Everyone is welcome here.” But Inama was ordered by school officials to take it down, and now, she is filing a lawsuit in response.

According to her lawsuit, the poster’s opponents viewed it as “political resistance” to the “rise of President Donald Trump” — and West Ada School District administrators told her that “not everyone agrees that ‘everyone is welcome,’ so it is a political opinion.”

One of the poster’s critics was Monty Hyde, principal at Lewis & Clark Middle School (where Inama was teaching social studies when she put up the poster).

School officials, according to Law & Crime reporter Chris Pérez, allege that the poster was a violation of Idaho House Bill 41 — which Idaho Gov. Brad Little, a Republican, signed into law in March 2025. The law forbids certain types of flags and banners on public school property and is being condemned as “censorship” by the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) in Idaho.

Pérez reports, “School officials deemed Inama’s poster as violating HB 41 after Idaho Attorney General Raul Labrador defined ‘poster’ as falling within the statutory definition of a ‘banner,’ according to her complaint. He made the determination and then reiterated it in a June 2025 opinion filed with the state’s Department of Education. Labrador concluded that the ‘Welcome Poster’ and others like it in Inama’s classroom, including ones with rainbow colors, ‘cannot be displayed in Idaho schools’ because the posters ‘are part of an ideological/social movement which started in Twin Cities, Minnesota following the 2016 election of Donald Trump,’ according to Inama’s complaint.”

Labrador, a Republican, vigorously defended HB 41 in an op-ed published by right-wing Fox News on July 14, 2025.

The Idaho attorney general wrote, “The rainbow colors and progressive symbols accompanying these messages make their political purpose unmistakable…. These seemingly neutral terms mask a comprehensive worldview that undermines parental authority over children’s moral development.”

Our Analysis:

The Politics of Inclusion: Examining Idaho’s Controversial Decision

In the heart of Idaho, a state that bleeds as red as a sunset, a public schoolteacher named Sarah Inama found herself at the center of a storm for hanging a simple, yet apparently subversive poster in her classroom. The message? “Everyone is welcome here.” A sentiment so basic, so fundamental to the principles of education and inclusivity, one might think it would be universally embraced. Yet, in the context of Idaho’s deep political red, this message was seen not as a beacon of welcome but as a declaration of political warfare.

The saga unfolds with Inama being ordered by school officials to remove the poster, an act that catapulted her into filing a lawsuit. At the heart of the controversy is the claim that the poster represents “political resistance” to Donald Trump’s presidency. The West Ada School District administrators, backed by a questionable interpretation of Idaho House Bill 41, have positioned a statement of inclusivity as a political stance, suggesting that not everyone agrees that ‘everyone is welcome.’ This line of reasoning is not just disturbing; it’s a dangerous slide towards sanctioned exclusion.

The Legal Quagmire

Idaho House Bill 41, signed into law by Republican Governor Brad Little in March 2025, is the legal instrument being used to justify the poster’s removal. This law, which restricts certain types of flags and banners on public school property, has been thrust into the spotlight as a tool of censorship. The ACLU of Idaho has rightly condemned it as such. But it’s the interpretation of the law by Idaho Attorney General Raul Labrador that truly reveals the ideological battleground at play. Labrador’s determination that a poster falls within the definition of a ‘banner’ is a stretch, to say the least. But it’s his justification that the rainbow colors and progressive symbols on Inama’s poster represent a “comprehensive worldview that undermines parental authority over children’s moral development” that exposes the true colors of HB 41.

A Worldview Under Siege

Labrador’s defense of HB 41, as outlined in his op-ed for Fox News, is a masterclass in political obfuscation. The argument that inclusivity and acceptance undermine parental authority is not just flawed; it’s fundamentally misleading. The notion that welcoming everyone, regardless of background, belief, or identity, is a political act rather than a human one speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse.

The irony, of course, is that those who champion the bill under the guise of protecting children’s moral development are the same voices advocating for a world in which not everyone is welcome. This isn’t about parental rights or moral development; it’s about controlling the narrative, about ensuring that the only worldview presented is one that aligns with a specific political ideology.

The Ideological/Social Movement Bogeyman

The claim that the posters are part of an “ideological/social movement” originating from the Twin Cities post-2016 election is a red herring. It’s a tactic designed to frame basic human decency and acceptance as part of a broader political agenda. The attempt to politicize inclusivity, to frame it as a weapon wielded by the left against the right, is a transparent effort to justify exclusion and discrimination.

Conclusion: The Battle for the Soul of Education

What’s at stake here is more than just a poster. It’s about the soul of education in America. It’s about whether schools are places where all students are welcomed and valued, or whether they become arenas for political indoctrination. Sarah Inama’s lawsuit is not just a fight for her rights as a teacher; it’s a battle for the heart of what it means to educate. In a world where ‘everyone is welcome’ is seen as a threat, we must question what values we’re truly upholding.

The case of Sarah Inama vs. the West Ada School District is a microcosm of a larger struggle, one that transcends Idaho and speaks to the very essence of what kind of nation America wants to be. As this case unfolds, it will undoubtedly continue to spark debate, but one thing is clear: the fight for inclusivity, for the simple right to say “everyone is welcome,” is a fight worth having.
