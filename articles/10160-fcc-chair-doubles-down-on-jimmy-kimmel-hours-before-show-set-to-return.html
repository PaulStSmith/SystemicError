FCC chair doubles down on Jimmy Kimmel hours before show set to return

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

People display signs as they protest at the El Capitan Entertainment Centre, where “Jimmy Kimmel Live!” was recorded for broadcast, following his suspension for remarks he made regarding Charlie Kirk’s assassination, on Hollywood Boulevard in Los Angeles on Sept. 18, 2025. REUTERS/David Swanson

Federal Communications Commission Chair Brendan Carr is doubling down on his attempt to punish Jimmy Kimmel — and now blames Democrats for the wave of backlash after Disney and ABC pulled the show last week.

“Democrats just keep digging themselves a deeper & deeper hole on Kimmel,” Carr wrote on X. “They simply can’t stand that local TV stations—for the first time in years—stood up to a national programmer & chose to exercise their lawful right to preempt programming. We need to keep empowering local TV stations to serve their communities of license.”

TV station groups Nexstar Media and Sinclair Broadcasting have said they plan to boycott Kimmel’s show after ABC and its parent company Disney announced it will return Tuesday — backtracking on its earlier decision to pull the show after comments made about Charlie Kirk.

Nexstar and Sinclair plan to replace it with news programming for about a quarter of the stations they control, which is about 20% of ABC local affiliates, the New York Times reported.

Carr last week threatened Jimmy Kimmel’s suspension on a conservative podcast, saying Disney should “take action,” just hours before the network pulled the show and suspended the late-night host “indefinitely” over his remarks about Trump and slain MAGA influencer Kirk.

Disney on Monday announced that it had “thoughtful conversations with Jimmy, and after those conversations, we reached the decision to return the show on Tuesday.”

The suspension of “Jimmy Kimmel Live!” had been condemned by lawmakers, free speech advocates and notable Hollywood stars. Protests broke out in Los Angeles outside the studio where the show is taped.

The company’s stock dropped in the days following as calls grew to boycott ABC/Disney and Hulu amid the ongoing pressures from conservative affiliate owners Sinclair and Nexstar.

Nexstar is trying to close a $6.2 billion merger with Tegna, another TV station owner, and needs the FCC’s and the Trump administration’s approval to secure the deal, The Times reported. The show could ultimately move to another TV station owner.

Former FCC Chair Tom Wheeler has accused Carr of using his leverage to approve corporate mergers to “bludgeon” regulated companies like Nexstar and Sinclair, both of which have corporate mergers awaiting his approval. He said the chairman’s justification for getting involved was improper.

“The FCC does not have a roving mandate to police speech in the name of the public interest,” Wheeler said.

Our Analysis:

The FCC’s Kimmel Conundrum: A Tangled Web of Politics, Power, and Media

In an era where the lines between politics, media regulation, and corporate interests are increasingly blurred, the saga involving Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Chair Brendan Carr, ABC’s “Jimmy Kimmel Live!”, and media giants like Nexstar Media and Sinclair Broadcasting unfolds like a modern-day Shakespearean drama. Yet, unlike a work of fiction, the implications of this spectacle are profoundly real and deeply concerning.

The Root of the Controversy

At the heart of the matter is Carr’s vehement reaction to Jimmy Kimmel’s comments about Charlie Kirk, which led to a temporary suspension of Kimmel’s show by Disney and ABC. Carr’s stance, as vocalized on the social media platform X, criticizes Democrats for supposedly undermining the rights of local TV stations. However, this narrative conveniently overlooks the broader context: the chilling effect that political pressure can exert on free speech and the autonomy of media outlets.

The Power Play

Carr’s assertion that local TV stations are merely exercising their “lawful right to preempt programming” to “serve their communities of license” is a thinly veiled euphemism for a more sinister reality. This isn’t about empowering local stations; it’s about leveraging federal regulatory power to suppress dissenting voices and manipulate media coverage under the guise of localism.

Moreover, the involvement of Nexstar and Sinclair, both of which plan to boycott Kimmel’s show, adds another layer of complexity. These aren’t disinterested parties nobly standing up for community values; they are colossal media conglomerates with vested interests and a history of pushing a conservative agenda. The fact that Nexstar is in the midst of seeking FCC approval for a $6.2 billion merger with Tegna only adds to the stench of quid pro quo that permeates this entire ordeal.

The Misuse of FCC Authority

Former FCC Chair Tom Wheeler’s criticism of Carr is particularly telling. Wheeler accuses Carr of using his position to “bludgeon” regulated companies, a stark departure from the FCC’s historic stance of non-interference in content-related matters. The FCC’s mandate has never been to police speech, yet Carr’s actions suggest a dangerous willingness to do just that, wielding the power of regulatory approval as a weapon against perceived political adversaries.

The Broader Implications

What we are witnessing is not just a dispute over a late-night comedy show; it’s a microcosm of a much larger battle over the control of information, freedom of expression, and the role of government in regulating the airwaves. It raises fundamental questions about the integrity of our media system, the independence of regulatory bodies like the FCC, and the health of our democracy itself.

In their rush to exert control and impose a partisan agenda, figures like Carr and the conglomerates backing him not only undermine the principles of free speech but also erode public trust in the institutions meant to serve as the guardians of that very freedom. It’s a dangerous game with potentially far-reaching consequences for the fabric of American society.

Conclusion

As this saga continues to unfold, it serves as a stark reminder of the fragility of our democratic institutions and the need for vigilance in protecting them from the corrosive influence of power and politics. The case of Jimmy Kimmel, Brendan Carr, and the FCC is not merely a footnote in the annals of media history; it is a cautionary tale about the perils of politicizing regulatory power and the enduring importance of safeguarding free expression from those who would seek to curtail it for their own ends.
