'Claptrap-laden diatribes': JD Vance leveled by legal scholar over his outbursts on X

By Paulo Santos

Republican U.S. vice presidential nominee Senator JD Vance speaks at Tucson Speedway in Tucson, Arizona, U.S. October 9, 2024. REUTERS/Go Nakamura

Vice President J.D. Vance was taken to task by the conservative National Review's Andrew McCarthy for his constant running to X to post disingenuous attacks on opponents of the Donald Trump administration's immigration policies.

In a column posted Saturday morning, the legal analyst claimed the VP, a Yale Law School graduate, knows fully well the administration is denying immigrants their right to due process, and that Vance is making his claims more as a performance for MAGA devotees.

Labeling Donald Trump's second-in-command as "infuriating," he added the VP is "too smart not to know that the nonsense he spouts is nonsense. Well-framed nonsense, to be sure."

ALSO READ: 'Retribution or bust’: 'Secretary of Retribution' joins J6 leaders to demand mass arrests

He then added, "Vice President Vance issued one of his claptrap-laden diatribes on social media Wednesday, slamming 'the media and the far left' who are 'weeping over the lack of due process' in the Trump administration’s illegal deportations of people it alleges — probably correctly in most instances — are members of criminal gangs."

Using that as a springboard, he noted that Trump's goal is to "illustrate that he has amassed uncheckable power," and for Vance that's "the point of the vice president’s post: Due process is for whiners, and if you’re 'weeping' over its sudden death, you’re the problem."

"Adopting the habit of Trump officials of making up their own facts as well as their own law, Vance argues that all the administration is doing is returning an illegal alien to his home country," he accused. "This is so mendacious it’s hard to decide where to begin — even accepting for argument’s sake Vance’s blithe claim that Abrego Garcia is an MS-13 member, which, again, Trump officials chant as a mantra but no one has actually proved."

"He says we who want the law enforced don’t have a plan for deporting 20 million people — and that’s true, for there is no such four-year plan; there’s just 'do the best you can to materially reduce the illegal population, get on a trajectory for bigger reductions over time, and then manage illegal immigration like we manage other ordinary crime.'" he wrote before pointing out, "But to flip it around, Vice President Vance does not tell us what his plan is for rapidly deporting 20 million people. That’s because such a plan cannot include faithfully executing the law. And he knows it."

Our Analysis:

The Charade of Due Process Under the Trump Administration: Unveiling J.D. Vance's Disingenuous Rhetoric

In a striking revelation by the National Review's Andrew McCarthy, Vice President J.D. Vance finds himself at the epicenter of a scathing critique, not for his lack of intellect, but rather for his deliberate dissemination of what can only be described as political theater. Vance, a Yale Law School graduate, is accused of knowingly distorting the truth about the Trump administration's immigration policies, specifically their blatant disregard for due process. This isn't just a matter of political disagreement; it's a calculated performance aimed at appeasing the MAGA crowd, at the expense of fundamental legal principles.

The Art of Disguising Tyranny as Patriotism

Vance's antics, as illuminated by McCarthy, aren't merely misguided or ill-informed—they are a conscious choice to prioritize political expediency over legal and ethical integrity. To label immigrants, probably correctly identified as gang members in most instances, as unworthy of due process is to strip them of their basic human rights, based on association rather than individual guilt or innocence. This stance is not just legally indefensible; it is morally reprehensible.

The Mendacity of Vance's Claims

The vice president's argument—that the administration is simply "returning an illegal alien to his home country"—is a gross oversimplification that ignores the complex realities of immigration, asylum, and international human rights law. Even if we were to accept Vance's assertion that all deported individuals are gang members, which is a highly dubious claim, the lack of due process remains a glaring violation of the principles upon which the United States was founded.

The Illusion of a Plan

Vance's critique of those advocating for the enforcement of immigration laws without a comprehensive plan to deport 20 million people is a classic example of a straw man argument. It's an attempt to shift focus from the administration's failures to a non-existent demand from their critics. However, the vice president's failure to provide a viable alternative only highlights the administration's lack of commitment to lawful and humane immigration policies.

Conclusion: The Tyranny of Nonsense

What we're witnessing is not just a failure of policy but a failure of principle. The willingness of J.D. Vance to peddle falsehoods and engage in legal sophistry is indicative of an administration more concerned with consolidating power than upholding the rule of law. The real issue at hand is not whether Vance understands the legal and ethical implications of his statements—he undoubtedly does. The issue is that he, and by extension the administration he represents, chooses to ignore them in favor of political gain.

The critique by McCarthy is not just a condemnation of Vance but a call to action for all who value justice, due process, and the integrity of our legal system. It's a reminder that the battle for the soul of our nation is waged not only in the halls of Congress or the courts but in the integrity with which our leaders speak and act. In this regard, Vance and the Trump administration have been found woefully lacking.
