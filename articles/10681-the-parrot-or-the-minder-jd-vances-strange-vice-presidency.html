The Parrot or the Minder? JD Vance’s Strange Vice Presidency

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 9, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There’s something peculiar about the images streaming out of Trump’s new White House. No matter the occasion—press briefings, photo ops, staged outbursts—you’ll often spot JD Vance hovering directly behind Trump, never more than a few steps away. He isn’t simply nearby in the West Wing or present at meetings. He is physically positioned as Trump’s shadow, an omnipresent prop in the theater of power.

At first glance, it looks like a loyalty display—a visual mantra: Trump in front, Vance behind. The pirate and his parrot. But look closer, and a more unsettling interpretation emerges: Vance isn’t just there to flatter Trump’s ego. He’s there to manage him.

The Tradition of Invisible VPs

Vice presidents traditionally live in the margins. Kamala Harris conducted foreign diplomacy and advocacy tours without trailing Biden like a hall monitor. Al Gore ran technology and environmental initiatives without needing to hover over Clinton’s every breath. Even Dick Cheney—the archetype of vice presidential overreach—worked his dark arts behind the curtain, not literally glued to George W. Bush’s flank.

The VP’s job is proximity to power, yes, but not physical surveillance. They’re supposed to be a heartbeat away, not a footstep away.

Trump’s Decline on Display

Why, then, does Vance linger? Because Trump isn’t the showman he once was. His public lapses are no longer rare gaffes but recurring events: wandering off stages, staring blankly when he loses his place, mixing up names, dates, and facts in ways even his most fervent defenders can’t spin as “strategic.” He is often visibly absent in his own mind.

The optics of decline are dangerous for an administration whose entire mythology rests on projecting strength. Enter Vance. By staying within arm’s reach, he plays the dual role of minder and safety net:

  • Stage Management: If Trump veers, Vance can redirect. A hand on the elbow, a whispered word, a gesture—all invisible except to those who know what they’re seeing.

  • Damage Control: Should Trump lash out or stumble, Vance is already in frame to intercept, clarify, or provide cover.

  • Succession Branding: By appearing constantly beside Trump, Vance is normalized as the inevitable heir. The message: if Trump falters, the movement continues seamlessly through Vance.

Fealty Meets Caretaking

Make no mistake, Vance is still enacting fealty. Trumpism thrives on public submission, and his constant presence reinforces the idea that he owes his position to Trump’s patronage. But this isn’t mere sycophancy. It’s practical crisis management. Trump is brittle. The court knows it. So they’ve stationed the crown prince as both ornament and handler.

It’s a grotesque twist on the optics of monarchy: the sovereign out front, the designated regent hovering just behind, ready to assume authority when the king collapses. The image doesn’t project strength—it telegraphs fragility.

The American Monarchy Illusion

The American presidency has always flirted with monarchic theater, but this iteration is pure farce. Biden, Reagan, Obama—all had VPs who operated as deputies, not decorations. Even in moments of presidential weakness (Reagan post-assassination, Biden’s stumbles), no one felt the need to glue a VP to the president’s shoulder for appearances.

What we’re witnessing now is the institutionalization of decline. Trump cannot be left unsupervised, and the administration knows it. Vance isn’t simply there. He is required.

The Dangerous Precedent

This should terrify anyone with even a basic respect for democracy. If the sitting president requires a minder to function, then the public is being governed by proxy, whether or not that proxy has been elected to do so. Vance’s omnipresence is an admission: Trump is no longer fully capable of managing his own office, yet the charade must continue to preserve power.

And what happens when the minder slips? When Trump, unguarded for even a moment, lashes out, wanders into incoherence, or reveals weakness that can’t be staged away? The entire edifice cracks.

Vance as Both Symbol and Strategy

JD Vance’s vice presidency is not about policy, not about governance, and certainly not about representing the American people. It is about optics of control and continuity:

  • To Trump’s base, he is the ever-loyal son, proving his worth by standing sentinel.

  • To the GOP establishment, he is the designated understudy, training the audience for the inevitable succession.

  • To those paying attention, he is the minder of a declining autocrat, the human seatbelt strapped across a presidency careening out of control.

Conclusion: The Puppet and the Handler

When future historians examine the Trump-Vance years, they may describe Vance as the most visible vice president in U.S. history—not because he was powerful, but because he was necessary. Never before has the role of vice president been reduced to bodyguard duty, equal parts courtier and caretaker.

JD Vance is not just a parrot perched on Trump’s shoulder. He is a minder, a chaperone for a president whose decline is so plain that it cannot be hidden without staging. The White House has become a theater of managed frailty, and the country is governed by the illusion of strength propped up by the ever-present silhouette of its vice president.

That isn’t leadership. It’s hospice care dressed up as authoritarian pageantry.
