'Kids are watching': Trump scolded by GOP senator for firing off death threats

President Donald Trump’s demand for certain Democratic members of Congress to be executed earned a stern rebuke on Thursday from Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), reported the Huffington Post’s Igor Bobic.

“Words carry a lot of weight,” said Tillis. “Kids are watching and the president of the United States should always be thinking less about the adults you’re reacting to, to probably what was objectionable behavior by the Democrats, and the kids that are watching, too.”

The controversy stems from a video put out this week by a handful of Democratic lawmakers who served in the military, including Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) and Rep. Jason Crow (D-CO), reminding servicemembers that they have a legal and constitutional obligation to refuse to carry out orders that are unlawful, like the shooting of unarmed civilians.

But Trump was enraged by this, seeing it as an attempt to undermine the chain of command.

Taking to Truth Social in multiple posts, Trump called for these Democrats to be arrested, called the video “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH!” and reposted a commenter saying that George Washington would have hanged them all.

Tillis, who is retiring from the Senate after next year, is not alone among right-wing figures in pushing back on Trump’s attacks on Democratic veterans.

“There is no insurrection or sedition without the use of force,” posted Fox News commentator and former federal prosecutor Andrew McCarthy. “Disobeying a lawful order is insubordination, not insurrection or sedition. Disobeying an unlawful order is required. That is all.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Call for Execution: A Dangerous Precedent

In a move that should shock no one but still manages to horrify, President Donald Trump’s latest tirade has crossed yet another line in the ever-blurring realm of political discourse. His demand for the execution of certain Democratic members of Congress has, astonishingly, only earned a “stern rebuke” from Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), as reported by the Huffington Post’s Igor Bobic. This response is emblematic of the tepid pushback from the GOP, which has become all too common in the face of Trump’s escalating rhetoric.

“Words carry a lot of weight,” Tillis notes, an observation that would be commendable if it weren’t so painfully obvious. His concern for the impressionable minds of America’s youth watching this unfold is valid, yet it’s overshadowed by the glaring issue at hand: a former President of the United States is calling for the execution of political adversaries, based on their exercise of free speech and duty to the Constitution.

The Roots of Controversy

The controversy stems from a video by Democratic lawmakers, including military veterans Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) and Rep. Jason Crow (D-CO), reminding service members of their obligation to refuse unlawful orders—such as the shooting of unarmed civilians. Trump’s interpretation of this as an attempt to undermine the chain of command is not just a misreading of the situation; it’s a dangerous misrepresentation of legal and constitutional duties.

Trump’s reaction on Truth Social, calling the video “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH!“ and endorsing a commenter’s view that George Washington would have hanged them all, is nothing short of alarming. It’s a testament to the former President’s willingness to distort history and law to serve his narrative, equating lawful dissent with sedition and insurrection.

A Lone Voice of Reason?

Tillis, soon retiring, represents a dwindling faction within the GOP willing to openly criticize Trump. His voice, however, lacks the urgency and condemnation such statements from Trump warrant. Comparatively, Fox News commentator and former federal prosecutor Andrew McCarthy provides a more pointed critique, stating, “There is no insurrection or sedition without the use of force,” and clarifying that “disobeying an unlawful order is required.”

The Bigger Picture

The real issue here transcends partisan politics; it’s about the integrity of our democratic institutions and the principles upon which the United States was founded. When a former President calls for the execution of political opponents, it’s not just a breach of decorum; it’s a threat to the very foundation of democracy.

The lukewarm responses from Tillis and others in the GOP only serve to underscore the party’s complicity in Trump’s dangerous rhetoric. It’s not enough to rebuke the words; there must be a denunciation of the intent and a recognition of the harm such statements cause to the fabric of our society.

In a time when the lines between political hyperbole and incitement to violence are increasingly blurred, the need for clear, strong, and principled leadership has never been more critical. Yet, as Trump’s latest outburst shows, and the GOP’s response confirms, such leadership appears to be in short supply.
