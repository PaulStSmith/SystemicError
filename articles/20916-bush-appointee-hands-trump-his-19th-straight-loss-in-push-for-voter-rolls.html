Bush appointee hands Trump his 19th straight loss in push for voter rolls

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20262 min read

Trump Is Not “Seeking Voter Integrity.” He Is Testing How Much Election Power He Can Seize.

The Real Story

The reported fact is simple: Donald Trump’s Justice Department lost again, this time in Colorado, bringing the administration’s streak to 19 straight court defeats in its push to obtain state voter rolls. Judge Philip Brimmer said the federal government had no legal authority to override the states and try to purge voters from the rolls.

That is the story. The rest is camouflage.

Where Power Actually Sits

The institutional power in this case belongs to Trump’s administration, acting through the Justice Department under Todd Blanche. The target is state election machinery, because that is where voter access can be constrained, delayed, and chilled. The states are not the aggressors here; they are the jurisdictions being leaned on.

This matters because the conflict is not between two equal interpretations of election administration. It is between a federal executive trying to expand its reach and state systems, intervenors, and courts that are still saying no.

A Bureaucratic Costume for a Political Project

Calling this “voter reform” is a lie of presentation. The pattern described here is not neutral administration. It is an attempt to collect sensitive voter data, impose federal control where Congress did not grant it, and build an apparatus that can be used to make voting harder.

Judge Brimmer’s ruling cuts through the costume. The United States, he wrote, had not shown the legal basis required for its claim. In plain English: the administration tried to force a power it does not have.

Misdirection Is the Point

The usual trick is to make deliberate anti-voter behavior look like confusion, compliance work, or administrative housekeeping. That is how authoritarian projects often travel in daylight: as paperwork, not police raids.

The source is clear that Trump’s side keeps losing in court, yet the effort continues. That persistence is the point. Repeated defeats do not mean the campaign is misguided. They mean the campaign is willing to spend political capital, time, and legal resources to keep pressuring the system until something gives.

Blanche, Courts, and the Empty Claim of Competence

Todd Blanche’s apparent Senate Judiciary Committee clearance does not sanitize the record. The article notes the irony plainly: on the same day he advanced toward confirmation, reports emerged of his 19th consecutive loss in these cases.

That is not a trivial footnote. It shows a government apparatus that is not merely overreaching, but failing repeatedly while still demanding more access. A normal administration would take repeated losses as evidence of bad law or bad faith. This one treats court defeats as a speed bump.

The Larger Pattern

This is part of a broader authoritarian pattern: lose the legal battle, keep the pressure on the institutions, and recast the attack as election administration. The goal is not just to win a single case. It is to normalize federal intrusion into voting systems, build capacity for suppression, and make democratic participation more burdensome for the people most likely to resist reactionary power.

The courts are still blocking this one. That is the immediate fact. The deeper fact is that Trump’s project keeps trying to turn the machinery of state into a weapon against the electorate itself.
