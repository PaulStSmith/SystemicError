‘Oh for the love of God’: Overlooked Trump post hints at ‘justification’ for major attack

President Donald Trump launched a major military operation against Iran early Saturday, citing Tehran’s refusal to renounce pursuing nuclear weapons, but critics say a social media post he made shortly after the strikes began may reveal the true “justification” for the unprecedented attack.

Dubbed Operation Epic Fury, the U.S. strikes on Iran were launched just after midnight early Saturday morning. A few hours later, Trump took to social media and made several posts, one of which, critics claim, may reveal what the president’s true motivations were for the attack.

“Donald Trump had just posted on Truth Social what appears to be a justification for war with Iran, claiming Tehran tried to interfere in the 2020 and 2024 U.S. elections,” reads a social media post from the news outlet Drop Site News. “Earlier, in a speech, he said Iran posed an ‘imminent threat’ to Americans which necessitated toppling the regime.”

The social media post in question included a link to a news article from Just the News, a right-leaning online news site. In the article, the outlet reported on supposed efforts by Iran to interfere in the U.S. 2020 election on behalf of former President Joe Biden, an election that Trump has repeatedly – and falsely – claimed was “rigged” against him and “stolen.”

“Oh for the love of God,” wrote Anthony Zenkus, a Columbia University professor, in a social media post on X.

“First Venezuela, now Iran,” wrote Eva Golinger, a journalist, author and lawyer who specializes in international law, in a social media post on X. “Next they will be saying it was Cuba.”

And Fernando Antonio, a purported Florida-based attorney, argued that Trump’s potential belief that Iran may have aided Biden in the 2020 election would be used as a pretense to enact federal control over future U.S. elections.

“Here is the real reason,” Antonio wrote in asocial media poston X. “Drum up imminent threat to launch a war on Iran. Use the war on Iran to create an ‘emergency.’ Lob bs claims that Iran ‘interfered’ with election to push emergency order to try to take over elections.

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Dawn of Operation Epic Fury: Unraveling Trump’s True Motives

In the shadowy hours of early Saturday, President Donald Trump set the global stage ablaze with the launch of Operation Epic Fury against Iran. The ostensible reason? Tehran’s stubborn pursuit of nuclear weaponry. Yet, a dive beneath the surface of this military maneuver reveals a murky pool of motivations, particularly highlighted by Trump’s post-strike social media activities. Critics argue, and with good reason, that these actions shine a glaring light on the actual impetus behind this sudden act of aggression.

Social Media: The Window to the Soul of Justification

Only hours into the operation, Trump took to Truth Social, laying down a series of posts that seemed less about national security and more about settling personal scores. One post, cited by Drop Site News, suggested that Iran’s alleged attempts to interfere in the 2020 and 2024 U.S. elections were at the heart of Trump’s decision to strike. This claim, linked to an article from the right-leaning Just the News, echoes Trump’s incessant and baseless allegations that the 2020 election was “rigged” against him.

Let’s be clear: The leap from alleged election interference to launching missiles is a broad and dangerous one, especially when the premise itself is as shaky as Trump’s claims of election fraud. It’s a leap that reeks of using national security apparatus for personal vendettas, a move that’s as reckless as it is reprehensible.

Critics Chime In: Echoes of Disbelief and Alarm

The chorus of critics has been swift and scathing. Anthony Zenkus, a Columbia University professor, expressed exasperation at the situation, a sentiment likely echoed across much of the academic and political spectrum. Eva Golinger and Fernando Antonio raise points that cannot be ignored. Golinger’s comparison to Venezuela and the slippery slope to implicating countries like Cuba in election interference narratives is a stark reminder of how quickly justification for conflict can spiral out of control based on flimsy evidence or outright falsehoods.

Antonio’s perspective cuts even deeper, suggesting an ulterior motive of using the war as a smokescreen to impose federal control over U.S. elections. If true, this would represent a chilling abuse of power, turning an international conflict into a tool for domestic political manipulation.

The Real Threat: Misusing Military Might for Political Gain

What stands out most glaringly from this entire episode is the dangerous precedent it sets. The use of military action based on dubious justifications not only undermines the integrity of international relations but also poses a direct threat to the principles of democracy. When wars can be started on the whim of personal vendettas or unfounded claims, the global order is at risk of descending into chaos.

Operation Epic Fury, with its bombastic name and questionable underpinnings, could well be remembered not for any strategic or moral victory but as a stark example of the misuse of presidential power. It serves as a cautionary tale of what happens when the lines between national security and personal grievances are not just blurred but obliterated.

In sum, this military operation, shrouded in the pretext of preventing nuclear proliferation, may in fact reveal a more sinister objective: leveraging the immense power of the U.S. military to settle personal scores and manipulate domestic political landscapes. As details continue to emerge, one thing becomes painfully clear—Operation Epic Fury may well be an epic folly, with consequences far beyond what its architects anticipated.
