'It's not fair!' CNN's Jennings goes off on treatment of Trump by 60 Minutes

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Audacity of False Equivalence: Trump, CBS, and the Conservative Distortion Field

Scott Jennings (CNN screenshot)

CNN conservative Scott Jennings went on a tirade on Saturday morning after "Table for Five" host Abby Phillip pointed out that Donald Trump is trying to use his presidential bully pulpit to enrich himself in his battle with CBS.

Near the conclusion of the Saturday morning show, the viral Wake Forrest University commencement speech given by "60 Minutes" host Scott Pelley took center stage which led to the two butting heads.

Reflecting on Pelley telling the graduates, "You are the educated. You are the compassionate. You are the fierce defenders of democracy, the seekers of truth, the vanguard against ignorance. You are millions strong across our land. I might be sorry that you were picked by history for this role. But maybe that was the plan. Hard times are going to make you better and going to make you stronger," Trump's lawsuit was hotly debated.

'"Trump as president is saying that the government is going to on his private behest, stop a company from doing a business transaction because he wants it," Phillip stated. "That's different from settling legal disputes before you go into a merger and acquisition. That's Trump using the government to go after a company so that he can get dollars out of it, millions, millions and millions of dollars."

"Look, I know he sued them because he believes he was treated unfairly or they treated the Kamala Harris interview unfairly and tried to put their thumb on the scale of a presidential election," the conservative replied. "They're having conversations about settling it. We'll see how it shakes out."

He then continued, as he voice rose, "Actually, I think he has a point about it. And if you've watched "60 Minutes" for five minutes over the last several years, you would know it's not fair!"

'I mean, I mean, this is this is this is one of the shows, this is one of the outlets that conservatives would point to most often to say, you know, this is the state of American journalism," he complained. "It's only here to try to hurt Republicans, hurt conservatives, hurt Donald Trump."

Our Analysis:

The Audacity of False Equivalence: Trump, CBS, and the Conservative Distortion Field

In an era where the absurd often masquerades as logic, CNN conservative Scott Jennings' recent tirade stands as a textbook example of the dangerous blurring of ethical lines. As reported, Jennings butted heads with "Table for Five" host Abby Phillip over Donald Trump's ongoing legal skirmish with CBS, a scenario that disturbingly illustrates Trump's attempts to leverage presidential power for personal gain.

The Context: At the heart of this dispute lies Trump's lawsuit against CBS, a maneuver Phillip astutely identifies as an attempt to use governmental power to extract millions from a company. This isn't about justice; it's about greed cloaked in the guise of fairness. Trump's actions, as Phillip notes, transcend the normal boundaries of legal disputes and veer into the territory of using government influence as a weapon for personal enrichment.

Jennings' Justification: In a stunning display of cognitive dissonance, Jennings leaps to Trump's defense, arguing that the former president's lawsuit has merit based on perceived unfair treatment by "60 Minutes". His reasoning? A belief that the program has been unfair to conservatives and Trump for years. This is a dangerous conflation of editorial judgment with supposed systemic bias, a leap that ignores the fundamental principles of journalistic integrity and the complexity of media critique.

The Bigger Issue: Jennings' tirade isn’t just about a disagreement on a news show; it's emblematic of a broader, more troubling trend. The insistence on viewing every critical report through a lens of partisan bias undermines the very foundation of democratic discourse. It's an attempt to delegitimize the press, a move straight out of the authoritarian playbook.

What's at Stake: At the core of this debate is the principle of freedom of the press, a cornerstone of democracy. When individuals like Jennings attack the media for perceived biases, especially in defense of a figure like Trump who is attempting to use his political power for personal gain, they aren't just engaging in a rhetorical skirmish. They're participating in a campaign that threatens the integrity of democratic institutions.

The Real Bias: Jennings' critique of "60 Minutes" and, by extension, the broader media landscape, as being unfairly biased against conservatives and Trump, misses a crucial point. Journalism's role is not to play cheerleader for any party or individual but to hold power to account. Trump's lawsuit and Jennings' defense of it reveal a profound misunderstanding of this principle, or worse, a willful disregard for it.

In Conclusion: Jennings’ comments and the situation they respond to serve as a stark reminder of the ongoing assault on objective truth. When figures like Trump can manipulate the levers of power to silence criticism and when commentators like Jennings defend these actions, they aren't just attacking a television program or a network. They're undermining the very principles of free speech and accountability that are essential to a functioning democracy. The real "state of American journalism" isn't about bias against conservatives; it's about the relentless pursuit of truth in the face of power. That's not a flaw; it's a feature. And it's a feature we must defend against those who would rather turn it off.
