Trump is stark raving mad — and this proves it

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s $5 Billion Lawsuit Is Not About Defamation. It Is About Silencing Reality.

The Power At Work

The central fact is simple: a sitting president is using the machinery of personal legal threat to punish a liberal think tank for publishing a report he does not like. The Center for American Progress said Trump’s National Guard deployments have done little to reduce violent crime. Trump answered with a $5 billion defamation threat, routed through his personal lawyer, Alejandro Brito.

That is not a dispute over evidence. It is an attempt to turn presidential grievance into intimidation.

The Target Is The Message

The report is the trigger, but not the issue. The issue is that an outside organization named a failure and Trump wants the punishment to be so grotesque that others think twice before doing the same. That is how authoritarian pressure works in practice: not by proving the facts wrong, but by making the cost of stating them feel unbearable.

If the studies supporting CAP’s conclusion are accurate, then the threat is even more revealing. Trump is not merely contesting analysis. He is trying to erase the political damage that comes from being measured against outcomes and found wanting.

Who Enabled This

Trump made the decision. Brito carried it. That distinction matters, but not enough to blur responsibility. The president is the one using his office and his personal legal apparatus to menace critics. The lawyer is not a neutral technician here; he is the instrument making a frivolous threat legible as official action.

The article mentions that Brito has already been associated with failed Trump suits and a Florida judge recently referred him for possible discipline over another case involving the IRS. That history matters because it shows this is not competent legal advocacy. It is persistent loyalty to a client who confuses litigation with domination.

The Framing Is Rotten

The most dishonest move in coverage like this is to treat the lawsuit as eccentric, emotional, or merely reckless. That softens the real offense. A president threatening a think tank with a $5 billion defamation action because its report embarrassed him is not confusion. It is retaliation.

The article is right to call the suit absurd, but the deeper point is institutional. Trump is testing whether the presidency can be converted into a private intimidation machine. The question is not whether his legal theory survives a federal court. The question is how much damage he can inflict before a court ever gets there.

What This Pattern Reveals

This is the same political logic behind every strongman tantrum dressed up as process. Blow up the scale. Personalize the grievance. Weaponize lawyers. Flood the zone with threats so that critics spend time defending their right to speak instead of forcing accountability for what was said.

The National Guard claim matters because it exposes the underlying fraud: public force deployed for spectacle, then defended with legal menace when the results do not match the branding. That is the pattern. Stage power, deny failure, punish the people who measured it.

The Systemic Meaning

This is not one more Trump outrage sitting in isolation. It is a window into how authoritarian politics tries to survive contact with evidence. When facts become inconvenient, the response is not correction. It is coercion.

A democratic politics has to be able to say this plainly: the problem is not that Trump is “going too far.” The problem is that he is using the authority of the presidency to normalize punishment for speech, analysis, and dissent. That is the illness. The lawsuit is just the symptom.
