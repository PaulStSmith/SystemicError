Steve Bannon, who served in Trump's first administration, said on a new episode of his podcast, "The War Room," that Trump is focusing too much of his time on the Ukraine war. Instead, Bannon argued that he should continue expanding his power over local police forces.

Last week, Trump federalized the police force in Washington, D.C., to address local crime. He also threatened to expand the actions to Democratic-run cities like Baltimore and Los Angeles. Legal experts have argued that Trump doesn't have the legal authority to federalize police forces outside of D.C.

"The war we got to win is right here in this country, and it's in Washington D.C.," Bannon said. "It's going to be in New York City. It's in Chicago, and most importantly, right now, with Gavin Newsom and all his big talk, it's in Los Angeles, California. That's the war that we got to be focused on."

"I want to give a security guarantee to the citizens of the United States on these invaders right now. That's the security guarantee I want," he added.

Bannon also criticized Trump for accepting an audience with European leaders at the White House on Monday, even though they were not officially invited. He claimed they attended to take Trump's time away from more important issues like immigration.

"This entire war is so that Ukraine could be a Western country," Bannon said. "Well, I don't give a tinker's d---."

Our Analysis:

Bannon's Bold Break with Trump: A Closer Look

Steve Bannon, once a stalwart ally of President Donald Trump and a key figure in his first administration, has taken a sharp turn away from his former boss. On a recent episode of his podcast, "The War Room," Bannon delivered a blistering critique of Trump's current focus on the Ukraine war, advocating instead for an expansion of Trump's power over local police forces. This pivot highlights a rift in the Trump camp, revealing divergent priorities that could have profound implications for American democracy and its legal foundations.

Trump's Federalization Frenzy

Trump's recent move to federalize the police force in Washington, D.C., ostensibly to tackle local crime, alongside threats to extend these actions to Democratic-run cities like Baltimore and Los Angeles, signals a troubling push towards centralization of power. Bannon, not content with these measures, is pushing for even more aggressive federal intervention in local law enforcement. This initiative raises alarm bells for several reasons:

  • Legal Limbo: Legal experts have cast serious doubts on the legality of Trump's ambitions to federalize police forces outside of D.C. This skepticism suggests that the Trump administration could be veering into legally dubious territory, testing the limits of federal authority in ways that could further strain the already tenuous balance of power between federal and local governments.

  • Democratic Degradation: The push to expand federal control over local police forces, particularly in Democratic-run cities, smacks of a politically motivated power grab. By targeting cities that are political strongholds for Democrats, Trump and his allies like Bannon appear to be weaponizing federal authority to undermine local governance in areas where they lack political control.

Bannon's War at Home

Bannon's rhetoric shifts the battlefield from international conflicts to domestic arenas, framing American cities as the "real" war zones. His language conjures images of a nation under siege, not from external threats, but from its own internal divisions. Bannon's call to focus on cities like Washington, D.C., New York City, Chicago, and Los Angeles, specifically calling out California Governor Gavin Newsom, positions these urban centers as battlegrounds in a hyperbolic struggle for control of the country's future direction.

"The war we got to win is right here in this country," Bannon declares, diverging sharply from traditional conservative rhetoric that emphasizes external threats over internal governance issues. This inward turn represents a significant escalation in the rhetoric of political conflict, suggesting a willingness to cast aside democratic norms and legal constraints in favor of a more authoritarian approach to governance.

Bannon's Disregard for the Ukraine War

Bannon's dismissal of the Ukraine conflict as irrelevant to American interests is both shortsighted and dangerously isolationist. His statement, "This entire war is so that Ukraine could be a Western country... Well, I don't give a tinker's d---," reflects a profound misunderstanding of the geopolitical stakes at play, including the preservation of democratic sovereignty in the face of authoritarian aggression. Such a stance not only undermines international alliances but also signals a worrying indifference to the principles of self-determination and democratic solidarity.

Conclusion: Bannon's Vision and Its Implications

Steve Bannon's critique of Trump's focus on Ukraine and his call for an intensified assertion of federal power over local police forces reveal a vision of America that is at odds with democratic principles and the rule of law. Bannon's rhetoric, marked by a disdain for international engagement and a preference for authoritarian measures at home, poses a stark challenge to traditional American values of democracy, federalism, and global leadership.

As Bannon's comments ripple through the political landscape, they serve as a reminder of the ongoing internal battles within the Trump camp and the broader conservative movement. These divisions, characterized by a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism, underscore the critical need for vigilance and resistance to protect the foundations of American democracy.
