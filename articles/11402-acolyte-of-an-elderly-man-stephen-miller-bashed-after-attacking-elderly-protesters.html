'Acolyte of an elderly man': Stephen Miller bashed after attacking 'elderly' protesters

White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller speaks, while joined by U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, during a visit to greet members of the National Guard, at Union Station in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 20, 2025. REUTERS/Al Drago/Pool

President Donald Trump's deputy chief of staff, Stephen Miller, was on hand with Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth on Wednesday when the group visited a Washington, D.C. Shake Shack.

A crowd of protesters and travelers shouted up at the second floor of the Union Station balcony as National Guard soldiers surrounded them.

"All these demonstrators that you've seen out here in recent days, all these elderly white hippies, they're not part of the city and never have been," Miller, who is from California, told the press. "We're gonna ignore these stupid white hippies that all need to go home and take a nap because they're all over 90 years old."

Photos and videos of the protesters appeared to show they were significantly younger, though Miller's comment sparked mockery, given that he works for one of the oldest presidents in history.

The 79-year-old Trump remained at the White House during the outing.

"Narrator: Miller is the acolyte of an elderly man suffering from dementia," quipped lawyer David Lurie.

"He sounds so dumb. Why does the demographic of the protesters matter? He’s mad because it’s not enough 'Blacks' out there. White people live in DC. How is he so damn stupid!?" said Philadelphia lawyer and analyst Zaquelle LaBelle.

"Stephen Miller is upset because 'elderly white hippies' are showing too much energy opposing his fascism. Uncool, man," mocked former Chicago Tribune metro editor Mark Jacob wrote on BlueSky.

"This just in: Stephen Miller thinks he's a hepcat," joked former MSNBC host Keith Olbermann.

"On top of everything else, is Stephen Miller wearing A TAN SUIT?" noted investigative reporter Sarah Posner. It harkens back to a fake scandal around former President Barack Obama, who wore a tan suit on Aug. 28, 2014, resulting in a massive uproar by Fox News hosts and conservative media.

"Stephen Miller was a loser in college, and now we all must pay for it. --sincerely, someone who remembers him from school," wrote law professor Anthony Michael Kreis, who cited the comments from a fellow Miller grad.

New Republic staff writer Greg Sargent cited recent polls showing that 79% of Washington, D.C. residents oppose the Trump takeover. That includes 80% of Black residents and 67% of Hispanic Washingtonians.

"I take comfort in the idea that, for the rest of his miserable life, he'll wonder how much phlegm and/or feces has been added to his restaurant meals," cracked host Bob Cesca.

"Pee Wee German hates the elderly," mocked The Lincoln Project.

"I'm confused, elderly white people need to go home and take a nap... but a 79-year-old white man should have an illegal third term," one BlueSky user replied to a video of the comments.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Ignorance: Stephen Miller's Latest Theatrical Farce

In a recent spectacle that could only emerge from the theater of the absurd that is the current political landscape, Stephen Miller, President Donald Trump's deputy chief of staff, took center stage. The venue? A Shake Shack in Washington, D.C., surrounded by National Guard soldiers, a crowd of protesters, and a bewildering array of statements that would make Orwell facepalm.

Elderly White Hippies: A Distraction or a Delusion?

Miller's assertion that the demonstrators, whom he disparagingly labeled as "elderly white hippies," are "not part of the city and never have been" is a masterclass in delusional thinking. His comments not only trivialize the legitimate concerns of the protesters but also highlight a profound disconnect with reality.

The Irony of Ageism: The irony of Miller's remarks cannot be overstated. At 79, Donald Trump, the man Miller serves, stands as one of the oldest presidents in history. This makes Miller's ageist jabs at protesters not just hypocritical but comically absurd. The mockery was swift, with observers quick to point out the inconsistency of criticizing the age of demonstrators while serving under an elderly leader himself.

The Tan Suit Scandal: Fashion Faux Pas or Farcical Distraction?

In what can only be described as a bizarre twist, Stephen Miller's choice of attire, a tan suit, became a focal point of discussion. This seemingly innocuous fashion decision harkens back to a "scandal" involving former President Barack Obama, illustrating the penchant for conservative media to clutch at straws in their quest for outrage. The fixation on such trivial matters as a suit color, amidst a sea of pressing national concerns, underscores the superficiality and sensationalism that often permeate political discourse.

The Demographic Dilemma: Missing the Forest for the Trees

Miller's fixation on the demographic makeup of the protesters is not just misguided; it's irrelevant. The racial or age composition of a group of demonstrators has no bearing on the validity of their cause. His comments reflect a broader trend in political rhetoric that seeks to delegitimize dissent by attacking the messenger rather than addressing the message. This tactic, while not new, remains as ineffective and divisive as ever.

Conclusion: The Theater of the Absurd Continues

Stephen Miller's comments at the Shake Shack incident are emblematic of a larger issue within political discourse: the tendency to focus on the inconsequential at the expense of the substantive. In an era where style often trumps substance, where distractions and diversions are par for the course, Miller's performance is a reminder of the farcical nature of much of our current political dialogue.

As we navigate through the cacophony of the 24-hour news cycle, it's crucial to remember the importance of focusing on the issues that matter, rather than getting lost in the noise. In the end, Miller's remarks, much like the tan suit scandal, will be remembered not for their significance, but for their absurdity.
