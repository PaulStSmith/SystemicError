Trump could be sued by 1M people over ‘mind-boggling’ corruption: ex-official

The Court Built the Shield, Trump Filled It With Cash

The Real Power Structure

The core fact here is not Trump’s “mind-boggling” greed. It is that he occupies the presidency while exploiting the presidency, and the Supreme Court has already given him a legal perimeter for official acts. That is the power arrangement: one institution laundering impunity, another turning office into a revenue stream, and a political class still acting surprised that the arrangement produces theft at scale.

Brief Context

Richard Stengel’s appearance on MS NOW was pointed at the obvious: Trump’s financial disclosures show a massive increase in personal wealth, while his meme coin has left nearly a million buyers with roughly $3.81 billion in losses. Stengel also pointed to conflicts of interest, including the UAE’s reported $400 million purchase of Trump’s meme coin and the Court’s 2024 immunity ruling.

Corruption Is Not the Whole Story

Calling this “corruption” is accurate but too soft if it stays there. Corruption suggests a deviation from the system. This is the system working exactly as designed for a politician who understands that public office can be used as private capital. Trump is not accidentally blurring the line between state and self. He is making the line irrelevant in public, at scale, and daring everyone to treat that as normal.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The article’s framing risks hiding the main villain behind a fog of astonishment. The scandal is not that Trump claims the stock market lifted all boats while his own fortune jumps by $2.2 billion. The scandal is that institutional restraint has become so weak that he can profit inside the presidency and then explain it away as generic prosperity. That is not confusion. It is cover.

Who Enabled This

Trump made the choices, but he did not create the conditions alone. The Supreme Court widened the protective zone around presidential conduct. The media ecosystem then turns the resulting corruption into a spectacle of personality, which is useful to Trump because spectacle diffuses accountability. And the political system keeps responding as though the problem is excessive brazenness rather than the legal and institutional architecture that permits it.

The Larger Pattern

This is what democratic decay looks like when it is monetized: immunity on one side, extraction on the other, and public outrage converted into content. A president who can enrich himself through office, sell tokens to the gullible, and shield official conduct behind court-made doctrine is not just a corrupt individual. He is the proof that institutional cowardice eventually becomes a business model.
