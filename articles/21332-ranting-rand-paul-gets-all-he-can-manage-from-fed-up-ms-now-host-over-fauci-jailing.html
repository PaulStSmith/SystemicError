Ranting Rand Paul gets all he can manage from fed-up MS NOW host over Fauci jailing

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Rand Paul’s Fauci Ritual Is a Cover for Trump

The Real Center of Power

The source story is simple enough: Rand Paul went on TV to attack Anthony Fauci, got confronted with his own old calls to jail him, and then tried to pivot into the familiar conservative fog machine. But the real question is not Fauci. It is who had the authority to shape public reality in early 2020, and who is still trying to launder that power failure into a scientist’s personal scandal.

That answer is Donald Trump. Fauci was a health official. Trump was president. Paul knows the difference. He is pretending not to.

The Scapegoat Has a Purpose

Paul’s routine is not an argument. It is a substitution. He shifts blame from the White House to a public health expert because Fauci is the easier target: technical, bureaucratic, and politically disposable. Trump, by contrast, held the bully pulpit, controlled the executive branch, and spoke directly to the country while the virus spread.

The hosts in the report did what too much political coverage still fails to do: they forced the comparison Paul wanted to avoid. Trump publicly minimized the virus while privately telling Bob Woodward it was “far more serious.” That is not confusion. That is deliberate split-brain politics, a president saying one thing to the country and another to insiders while the consequences fell on everyone else.

Trump Was Not Misled

Paul’s dodge only works if Trump is treated as a bystander to his own presidency. He was not. The source makes that plain. Trump was telling Americans the virus would “blow over” at the same time he had already admitted otherwise in private. That is not a minor inconsistency. It is the governing method of an administration that treats truth as a tactical asset, not a public obligation.

Paul’s refusal to even say Trump’s name while answering the question says more than his words do. It is the posture of a partisan shield operator. The point is not to establish responsibility. The point is to keep the accountability pointed downward, toward the scientist, the agency, the expert, the credentialed messenger who can be made to carry blame on behalf of the powerful.

Fauci as Political Decoy

The attack on Fauci works because it offers the right wing a cleaner fantasy than the truth. If the pandemic response can be narrated as one doctor’s bad judgment, then the broader collapse of executive honesty, federal coordination, and Republican discipline can be erased. If Fauci is the villain, then Trump becomes merely a man surrounded by bad advice, not the man who set the tone.

Paul’s claim that Fauci’s “main culpability” was supporting lab funding is doing the same work. It reframes a national emergency into an abstract grievance about expertise itself. That is a useful trick for the anti-democratic right: turn institutions into conspirators, turn professionals into culprits, and turn the people with actual executive authority into spectators.

What This Framing Hides

The story also exposes the weakness of political media when it finally decides to challenge a Republican on live television. The confrontation is useful, but it is still downstream of the larger failure: too many outlets let Republicans weaponize scandal language against the least powerful actors in the room while escaping the obvious question of who had command responsibility.

Paul was not merely “criticizing” Fauci. He was participating in a broader conservative project to replace governance with blame theater. The function of that theater is to protect executive impunity. If the public is trained to blame scientists, inspectors, bureaucrats, and civil servants, then presidents and senators can keep treating catastrophe as someone else’s memo problem.

The Pattern Is the Point

This is the governing style of the modern right: manufacture a villain below you, shield the authority above you, and call the result accountability. Paul’s performance is not an outlier. It is a template. When Republicans control power, they deny responsibility. When they do not, they weaponize retrospection to punish the institutions that were asked to manage the damage.

The lesson is not that Fauci was perfect. It is that perfection is the wrong standard when the real actors are lying from the top. The relevant question is who had power, who used it, and who is still trying to bury that answer under a pile of performative rage. In this story, the answer still points back to Trump, and Paul’s entire act exists to keep it from staying there.
