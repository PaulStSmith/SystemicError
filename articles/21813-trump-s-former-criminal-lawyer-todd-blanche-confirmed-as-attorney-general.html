Trump's former criminal lawyer Todd Blanche confirmed as attorney general

The Justice Department Has Been Handed to Trump’s Defense Team

A Loyalist Gets the Gavel

Todd Blanche, the lawyer who defended Donald Trump in Trump’s New York criminal case, has now been confirmed as attorney general by a 50-49 Senate vote. That is the story in one sentence: the Department of Justice has been placed under the control of a man whose professional identity is tied to protecting the president who installed him.

The confirmation was not a mystery and it was not a neutral personnel choice. It was a transfer of power into the hands of a political loyalist, dressed up as routine governance.

Who Actually Decided

The real decision-maker here is Trump. Blanche himself said he was honored by the trust and confidence Trump placed in him. That is not subtle, and it should not be treated as if it were.

The Senate then provided the necessary cover. Lisa Murkowski, Susan Collins, and Democrats voted no. Bill Cassidy briefly staged concern, then remained inside the Republican line. The institution did exactly what it has learned to do under Trump: manufacture a narrow procedural drama around a result that was always structurally available to him.

The Frame Is the Problem

The source notes Murkowski’s objections: the handling of the Epstein files, sweeping immunity protections for Trump, his family, and their businesses, statements to anti-abortion groups, and the targeting of former administration staff and sitting senators. Those are not random misgivings. They are a description of how power is being used.

What this confirmation story risks obscuring is that the issue is not Blanche’s temperament or qualifications in some abstract sense. The issue is that the Justice Department is being staffed to protect one man, his circle, and his political operation. Calling that “leadership” is propaganda by vocabulary.

The Senate Knows What It Is Doing

Murkowski’s statement is unusually direct for the Senate, which is usually happiest when it can pretend that constitutional decay is just a matter of personal discomfort. She said the country needs an attorney general who will check the worst impulses of this administration. That is a damning sentence because it assumes the obvious: the administration has impulses that need checking, and the Senate is no longer reliably doing that job.

But the larger failure belongs to the Republican caucus that keeps converting concern into confirmation. They are not confused. They are enabling the capture of the law-enforcement machinery by a president who already treats institutions as instruments of revenge and insulation.

Justice as Shield, Not Constraint

An attorney general is supposed to serve as a constraint on executive abuse. This confirmation points in the opposite direction. Blanche is arriving not as an independent legal steward but as a defender elevated into state authority. That matters because the DOJ is one of the few institutions with the power to decide whether misconduct is investigated, buried, delayed, or sanctified.

The most revealing detail in the source is not the vote count. It is the combination of Blanche’s Trump defense work, the cited concerns over immunity protections, and the reported targeting of political enemies. That is how the system is being rewritten: not through a single dramatic seizure, but through the steady conversion of law into loyal service.

The Pattern Is Openly Authoritarian

This is the recurring pattern. Trump rewards personal loyalty. Republican senators absorb the damage and vote anyway. Democratic opposition, plus a few Republican defections, is used to stage the illusion of scrutiny. The institutions remain standing, which lets everyone pretend they are still operating normally.

They are not. When the Justice Department is handed to the president’s defender, the message is plain: accountability is subordinate to protection, and state power exists to serve the ruler first. That is not institutional health with rough edges. It is the administrative language of authoritarian rule.
