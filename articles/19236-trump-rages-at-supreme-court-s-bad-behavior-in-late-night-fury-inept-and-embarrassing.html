Trump rages at Supreme Court's 'bad behavior' in late-night fury: 'Inept and embarrassing'

President Donald Trump unfurled a 492-word attack on the U.S. Supreme Court over its decision to strike down his tariffs.

The 79-year-old president aired his grievances in a lengthy Truth Social post Sunday night at 10:45 p.m., saying the justices – three of whom he appointed during his first term – should have given him what he wanted on the topic.

“The decision that mattered most to me was TARIFFS!” Trump posted. “The Court knew where I stood, how badly I wanted this Victory for our Country, and instead decided to, potentially, give away Trillions of Dollars to Countries and Companies who have been taking advantage of the United States for decades. Our Supreme Court has made these Countries very happy but, as the Court pointed out, I have the absolute right to charge TARIFFS in another form, and have already started to do so.”

Trump believes that Justice Brett Kavanaugh’s 63-page dissent, joined by Justices Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito, cracked open the door for him to impose tariffs using other legal means, which he has been scrambling to do since the court loss last month.

“I want to thank Justices Alito, Thomas, and Kavanaugh for their Wisdom and Courage pertaining to the TARIFF case, and for understanding, in addition to the Law, that our ‘Unfriendly Competitors’ should not be reimbursed and rewarded for the decades of Damage they have caused THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA,” Trump posted. “I will fight hard to make sure that this does not happen!”

Trump accused the court’s liberals of voting as a bloc and complained that the conservative majority do not.

“The Democrats on the Court always ‘stick together,’ no matter how strong a case is put before them — There is rarely even a minor ‘waver,’” Trump posted, misspelling “waiver.” “But Republicans do not do this. They openly disrespect the Presidents who nominate them to the highest position in the Land, a Justice of the United States Supreme Court, and go out of their way, with bad and wrongful rulings and intentions, to prove how ‘honest,’ ‘independent,’ and ‘legitimate’ they are. The Democrat Justices just vote Democrat — They always stick together!”

He then returned to his long-standing and baseless complaints about his 2020 election loss to Joe Biden.

“Our Country was unnecessarily RANSACKED by the United States Supreme Court, which has become little more than a weaponized and unjust Political Organization,” Trump posted. “The sad thing is, they will only get worse! They wouldn’t even call out The Rigged Presidential Election of 2020, because they said that I, as President of the United States, did not have ‘standing’ to challenge it, and now, with time, it has been conclusively proven to be stolen — And look what happened to our wonderful Nation by allowing a grossly incompetent man, Sleepy Joe Biden, to be our ‘President.’”

“This completely inept and embarrassing Court was not what the Supreme Court of the United States was set up by our wonderful Founders to be,” the president added. “They are hurting our Country, and will continue to do so. All I can do, as President, is call them out for their bad behavior! This statement about the United States Supreme Court will cause me nothing but problems in the future, but I feel it is my obligation to speak the TRUTH. Thank you for your attention to this matter. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN! President DONALD J. TRUMP.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Trump’s Supreme Court Tirade

Institutional Power at Play

Before dissecting the fiery rhetoric of former President Donald Trump’s 492-word broadside against the U.S. Supreme Court, it’s crucial to understand the institutional framework within which this drama unfolds. The Supreme Court, as the judicial arm of the U.S. government, holds the constitutional authority to interpret laws, including those related to tariffs. The President, meanwhile, wields executive power, which includes the implementation of these laws within the constraints set by Congress and interpreted by the Court.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

The crux of Trump’s grievance lies in the Supreme Court’s decision to strike down his tariffs. This decision was made by a majority of justices, based on their interpretation of U.S. law and the Constitution, reflecting the Court’s institutional role as arbiter of legal disputes. Trump’s appointees—Justices Brett Kavanaugh, Clarence Thomas, and Samuel Alito—dissented, but the majority opinion reflects the institutional consensus.

Misdirection and Responsibility

Trump’s tirade against the Supreme Court for not siding with his views on tariffs misdirects responsibility and demonstrates a misunderstanding—or deliberate misrepresentation—of the Court’s role. The Supreme Court is not there to “give” the President what he wants but to interpret the law. Trump’s critique that the Court has become a “weaponized and unjust Political Organization” sidesteps the essential independence of the judiciary from the executive branch, a cornerstone of American democracy meant to prevent the concentration of power.

The framing of Trump’s criticism falsely suggests that the justices, particularly those he appointed, owe him loyalty in their rulings. This is a dangerous conflation of personal allegiance with institutional duty. The justices’ “disrespect” that Trump perceives is, in fact, a demonstration of the system’s designed checks and balances at work.

The Real Issue

Trump’s assertion that the Court’s decision could potentially “give away Trillions of Dollars to Countries and Companies” is a dramatic oversimplification that ignores the complex nature of international trade and tariffs. Tariffs are a tool within a broader economic policy framework, not a panacea for trade imbalances. The decision to strike down Trump’s tariffs likely rested on legal and constitutional grounds, not on a simplistic dichotomy of win/lose against other countries.

Moreover, Trump’s segue into unrelated grievances about the 2020 election and personal attacks on President Joe Biden further muddies the waters, drawing attention away from the substantive legal and constitutional issues at hand. This tactic of shifting blame and creating diversions is characteristic of Trump’s style but does little to address the real questions of law and governance that the tariff decision raises.

Conclusion

Trump’s attack on the Supreme Court over its tariff decision is a misdirected attempt to undermine the judiciary’s independence and to obscure the limits of presidential power. The real story here is not about loyalty, betrayal, or international economic conspiracy. It’s about the fundamental principles of American governance: separation of powers, checks and balances, and the rule of law. These principles ensure that no single branch of government, including the presidency, can override the constitutional order. Trump’s failure to recognize or respect these limits is the true “bad behavior” that deserves scrutiny.
