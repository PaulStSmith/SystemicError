Gasps as foreign leader brags on live TV about 'how he manipulated Trump': ‘The gall’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosSeptember 2, 20263 min read

Netanyahu Didn’t “Influence” America. He Exposed It.

The Boast

Benjamin Netanyahu reportedly told Israeli viewers on Channel 14 that he spent years using his “ability to influence” American leaders and that it “took a long time to bring the United States to its senses.” That is the useful part of the story: not the shock value, but the admission. A foreign prime minister is not denying the pressure campaign. He is campaigning on it.

The immediate context matters. Netanyahu was speaking while he was underwater in the polls ahead of Israel’s October elections, and his pitch to voters was built on a war that much of the public does not want. He was not describing strategy in the abstract. He was advertising power.

Who Actually Held Power

The article’s cleanest fact is also its ugliest implication: Donald Trump made the decision to launch the war against Iran. A White House insider told Bloomberg in March that the decision was “partly motivated by pressure from outside allies,” with Netanyahu chief among them. That is not the language of inevitability. It is the language of capture.

Netanyahu did not command the U.S. military. Trump did. That is the chain of responsibility. Trump is the actor who signed onto escalation. Netanyahu is the politician who boasted about moving him. The rest is excuse-making.

The Misdirection Machine

The real scandal is not that one Israeli leader tried to influence U.S. policy. Allies lobby. That is routine. The scandal is how openly the system tolerates the process when it serves war.

Netanyahu described a pipeline through American television, through “consultants and members of Congress” who are closer to cable news than to actual constituents. That is the model: elite media as a pressure chamber, lawmakers as spectators, and public opinion as something to be worked around rather than answered to. The source is blunt about the compliance of the U.S. media ecosystem, and it should be. The problem is not misunderstanding. The problem is a political class that treats access as expertise and airtime as legitimacy.

The War Was Not an Accident

This was not a sudden drift into conflict by confused leaders. Netanyahu has pushed for a U.S. attack on Iran since at least the 1990s, and the source notes that he also confirmed support last year for “Greater Israel.” That is a long political project, not a one-off intervention.

So when Netanyahu says he helped move the United States into war, the statement should be read in sequence: years of agitation, years of media access, years of elite normalization, then escalation. The outcome was not forged by misunderstanding. It was produced by persistence, leverage, and a U.S. political system willing to let a foreign leader pull on its levers.

The Point of the Performance

Netanyahu’s Hebrew-language boast to Israeli voters and his English-language praise of U.S. leaders are not contradictions. They are the same performance aimed at two audiences. In English, he flatters the powerful. In Hebrew, he brags that he manipulated them. That is not diplomacy. It is political theater built on asymmetry.

The article’s framing is weak only if it treats this as an oddity. It is not odd. It is how imperial arrangements survive: one side supplies the force, the other supplies the permission structure, and both pretend the outcome emerged from necessity.

Systemic Error

The broader pattern is simple. U.S. political institutions do not merely fail to resist foreign influence; they often create the conditions for it by rewarding war hawks, laundering elite consensus through cable news, and insulating decision-makers from the people who will live with the consequences.

Netanyahu’s boast is embarrassing because it is honest. It strips away the ceremonial language that usually hides the relationship. The republic was not tricked by mystery. It was worked over by power, and then it went to war.
