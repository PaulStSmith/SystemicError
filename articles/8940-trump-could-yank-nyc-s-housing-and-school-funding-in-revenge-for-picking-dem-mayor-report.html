Trump could yank NYC's housing and school funding in revenge for picking Dem mayor: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Vendetta Against NYC’s Progressive Turn: A Deep Dive

Donald Trump (Reuters)

President Donald Trump is enraged at New York City’s election of left-wing progressive Zohran Mamdani in the mayoral race — and is already considering revenge plots that could defund the federal component of critical city services, Axios reported on Thursday.

The president has spent months attacking Mamdani, who favors policies like free bus service, rent freezes, and publicly-owned grocery stores in lower-income areas, as a “Communist,” and threatening various acts of retribution.

According to the report, there are a number of federal contributions Trump could try to revoke or reduce.

“New York City is supposed to receive $7.4 billion in federal funds in fiscal 2026, according to an analysis by the New York State Comptroller’s Office. That accounts for 6.4% of the city’s budget,” said the report. In particular, the city’s education department gets over $2 billion in federal money, and the Department of Social Services gets $1.5 billion.

In terms of the percentage of federal money going to city departments, the report continued, “The Administration for Children’s Services ... gets 39% of its money from Washington,” while “The Department of Housing Preservation & Development — the agency tasked with creating and preserving affordable housing in a city where hundreds of thousands struggle with housing costs — gets 50% of its budget from the feds.”

That could particularly stymie Mamdani’s efforts to build new housing, a massive part of his platform, although it also comes at a moment when New Yorkers overwhelmingly voted to streamline the permitting process for private housing development.

Trump has already made his willingness to punish jurisdictions that oppose him clearly known, as he has deployed federal forces, including a surge of immigration agents, to cities like Los Angeles and Chicago, leading to a wave of protests.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Vendetta Against NYC’s Progressive Turn: A Deep Dive

The Art of Political Retribution: Trump’s Fury at NYC’s Leftward Shift

In a move that reeks of political vengeance, President Donald Trump is reportedly seething over New York City’s election of Zohran Mamdani, a beacon of progressive politics, as its mayor. Trump’s ire is not just a matter of personal animosity; it heralds a potential financial siege on the city, with threats to withdraw federal funding that underpins critical services. This scenario, as outlined in an Axios report, showcases a disturbing blend of political retribution and policy manipulation.

Mamdani’s Sin: Advocating for the Underprivileged

Mamdani’s platform, vilified by Trump as “Communist,” is fundamentally about extending a helping hand to those at the city’s margins. Free bus service, rent freezes, and publicly-owned grocery stores in lower-income areas are not radical ideas concocted to undermine America. Rather, they are thoughtful policies aimed at addressing the systemic inequalities plaguing one of the world’s most affluent cities. Trump’s characterization of such policies not only misrepresents their intent but also demonizes efforts to foster a more equitable society.

The Financial Guillotine Over NYC

Trump’s threats to cut federal funding are particularly alarming, given the substantial role this money plays in the city’s budget. According to the New York State Comptroller’s Office, New York City is poised to receive $7.4 billion in federal funds in fiscal 2026, accounting for 6.4% of its budget. The education department and the Department of Social Services are among the key beneficiaries, with billions at stake. The Administration for Children’s Services and the Department of Housing Preservation & Development, crucial for affordable housing, rely heavily on federal dollars.

A Calculated Attack on Progress

Trump’s potential financial retribution is not just an attack on Mamdani; it’s an assault on the very constituents who elected him. By targeting federal funding, Trump is undermining efforts to improve housing affordability, enhance social services, and elevate the quality of life for New York’s most vulnerable populations. This strategy is not governance; it’s political warfare, aimed at penalizing political dissent and stifling progressive policies.

The Irony of “Punishing” Democracy

Trump’s readiness to deploy federal forces against cities that oppose his worldview, as seen in Los Angeles and Chicago, juxtaposes starkly with the democratic process that saw Mamdani elected. It’s ironic—and deeply troubling—that Trump’s response to a fair election is to consider punitive measures that would primarily harm the electorate. This approach not only undermines the principles of democracy but also signals a dangerous willingness to leverage federal power against political adversaries.

In Conclusion: A Test of Resilience for NYC

As New York City braces for potential financial retribution from the Trump administration, the situation underscores a broader challenge facing American democracy. When elected officials use their power to punish opposition rather than engage with it constructively, the very fabric of democratic governance is strained. New Yorkers’ overwhelming support for progressive policies, as demonstrated in recent elections, is a mandate for change—one that deserves to be met with respect, not retribution. Trump’s vendetta against NYC is not just a political skirmish; it’s a litmus test for the resilience of democratic institutions in the face of authoritarian impulses.
