Right-wing activist orders fake ballots to prove fraud — and ends up convicted

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 26, 20265 min read

Conservative activist Harry Wait said he ordered ballots in the names of prominent local politicians four years ago to expose the risks of voter fraud. Prosecutors determined he was right that voter fraud was an issue — and he was the culprit.

On Tuesday night, a jury in Wisconsin convicted Wait of one felony count of identity theft and two misdemeanor counts of election fraud. The jury acquitted him on a second identity theft charge. He faces a maximum penalty of seven years in prison, though he is unlikely to receive such a severe punishment.

The verdict came as President Donald Trump and his allies focus on the dangers of voter fraud and pressure Senate Republicans to pass legislation requiring proof of citizenship to register and photo identification to vote.

Voter fraud is extremely rare, and Wait’s case demonstrates that those attempting to expose it can face serious legal consequences if they exceed legal boundaries.

Wait, a 71-year-old retired business consultant and president emeritus of HOT Government — a group advocating for “honest, open and transparent leadership” — has long criticized Wisconsin’s online ballot ordering system. In 2022, he used that system to request ballots in the names of State Assembly Speaker Robin Vos and Racine Mayor Cory Mason.

Wait requested the ballots be sent to his home and then publicly announced his actions, stating he was highlighting a flaw that “bad actors could exploit to cast ballots for other people.” The state Justice Department launched an investigation and charged him.

One municipal clerk sent Wait a ballot, while the other did not. Election officials stated that such actions are “extremely rare” and would have been “quickly caught on to” regardless of disclosure. The state tracks ballot destinations and investigates voter inquiries about unauthorized ballot requests.

Despite the charges, Wait has remained steadfast about his actions. “I’d do it again in a heartbeat, because to save the republic, soldiers have to draw blood and blood be drawn,” Wait told The Washington Post in 2022.

His supporters have championed him as a hero, wearing “Free Harry” T-shirts and military-style dog tags at rallies. During his trial, backers filled the Racine courtroom.

Wait became involved in election practices scrutiny after Trump’s 2020 loss. Multiple reviews have confirmed the election results were correctly tallied, but Wait maintains the election was rigged. Trump has similarly continued claiming the election was stolen, and his administration recently seized ballots in Georgia and election data in Arizona.

Wait is not alone in facing legal consequences for election-related activities. Milwaukee’s deputy elections director Kimberly Zapata created three false identities to request military ballots, claiming she sought to identify a system flaw. Fired immediately, she was convicted in 2024 of absentee-ballot fraud and misconduct in office, receiving one year probation.

In Colorado, former Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters is serving a nine-year prison sentence for conspiracy to commit criminal impersonation related to voting machine hard drive copying. In Michigan, two attorneys and a former state lawmaker face charges related to voting machine access efforts.

Our Analysis:

The Misdirection of Blame in the Voter Fraud Narrative

Identifying the Power Players

The recent conviction of conservative activist Harry Wait for voter fraud has been a flashpoint in ongoing national debates about election integrity. Wait’s actions, framed by some as an attempt to expose vulnerabilities in the election system, instead led to his being charged and found guilty of one felony count of identity theft and two misdemeanor counts of election fraud. This case unfolds against a backdrop where President Donald Trump and his allies amplify concerns over voter fraud, pressuring legislative actions that tighten voting requirements.

Decision-Makers and Their Impact

At the heart of this narrative are several key actors: - Harry Wait, who made the deliberate decision to order ballots in the names of prominent local politicians. - Wisconsin’s judicial system, which adjudicated Wait’s actions as illegal, leading to his conviction. - Election officials and the state Justice Department, responsible for maintaining the integrity of the election process and pursuing investigations into fraudulent activities.

It’s essential to differentiate between the institutional power held by the state’s judicial and electoral systems and the individual action taken by Wait. The article correctly identifies Wait as the decision-maker behind the fraudulent ballot requests, yet it’s crucial to scrutinize the broader narrative that frames such incidents within the context of widespread voter fraud—a claim extensively disputed by numerous studies and reports indicating that voter fraud is exceedingly rare in the United States.

Framing and Misdirection

The article subtly intertwines Wait’s conviction with ongoing political rhetoric, suggesting a larger, more pervasive problem of voter fraud that necessitates stricter voting laws. This narrative, heavily promoted by Trump and his allies, seeks to justify legislative measures that could disenfranchise voters, particularly those from marginalized communities. By focusing on isolated incidents like Wait’s, there’s a risk of misdirecting responsibility toward individuals attempting to “expose” flaws in the system, rather than addressing the systemic issue of misinformation and its impact on public trust in electoral processes.

Furthermore, the juxtaposition of Wait’s conviction with legislative efforts to require proof of citizenship and photo identification for voting implies a cause-effect relationship not supported by the evidence of voter fraud’s rarity. This misdirection serves to bolster claims for stricter voting regulations that could have the unintended (or perhaps intended) consequence of suppressing voter turnout.

Conclusion: The Real Culprit - Misinformation

The narrative surrounding Harry Wait’s conviction is emblematic of a larger, more concerning trend: the weaponization of isolated incidents of voter fraud to propagate a narrative that undermines faith in the electoral system. The real story here isn’t just about Wait or the specifics of his conviction; it’s about how such narratives are used to justify restrictive voting laws that could harm democratic participation.

Critically, the article fails to emphasize the systemic issue of misinformation—both in the perpetuation of the voter fraud myth and in the strategic framing used by political figures to advance restrictive voting agendas. In viewing the situation through this lens, it becomes clear that misplaced blame and scapegoating serve only to distract from the genuine threats to electoral integrity: misinformation and the erosion of public trust.

In dissecting this narrative, one must look beyond the individuals to the institutions and ideologies that empower and propagate these misleading narratives. The fight against voter disenfranchisement requires a clear-eyed recognition of where the true power lies and the real consequences of misdirected blame.
