Trump's 'aura of inevitability' cracking as ex-GOP insider flags proof of his 'collapse'

The Artifice of Invincibility: Unpacking the Real Threat Beyond Trump’s Waning Control

The Illusion of Power and Its Unraveling

Steve Schmidt, a notable GOP strategist and vocal critic of Donald Trump, has recently articulated a crucial shift in the American political landscape: Trump’s grip on power is weakening. Schmidt’s analysis, detailed in his Substack newsletter, points to a series of defeats and resistances—from blocked funds to public humiliations of his allies—as clear indicators that Trump’s once-feared stronghold is crumbling. This narrative isn’t just about a faltering leader; it’s about the dangerous vacuum his potential downfall creates.

Who Really Holds Power?

In his newsletter, Schmidt paints Trump as a strongman whose power was always more perception than reality, rooted in fear rather than genuine allegiance or ideological commitment. However, it’s essential to recognize that Trump’s seeming decline does not necessarily equate to a loss of institutional power for the forces he represents. The key players in this saga are still the GOP leaders and the judiciary, who have either upheld or challenged Trump’s initiatives. Their decisions shape the political landscape far more than Trump’s personal influence.

Misdirected Blame and Simplified Narratives

Schmidt’s focus on Trump’s personality—his fear and aggression—may misdirect from broader, more systemic issues. By personalizing the problem to Trump, there’s a risk of overlooking the institutional and ideological continuities that will outlast his leadership. Trump’s decline is portrayed almost as a self-contained drama, one that underestimates the resilience of the political structures and agendas he has championed.

The Dangers of a Post-Trump Vacuum

The real concern, as Schmidt suggests, isn’t just what happens if Trump falls, but what follows him. History shows us that the collapse of one strongman often leads to the rise of another, potentially more ruthless leader. The GOP’s recalibration away from Trump doesn’t necessarily signal a shift toward moderation but could be a strategic repositioning to maintain its base and power. The danger lies not in Trump’s personal decline but in who or what is positioned to replace him.

Broadening the Lens: Institutional Continuity Over Personal Change

The fixation on Trump’s personal decline obscures the resilience of the authoritarian, populist, and nationalist currents within the GOP that existed before Trump and will likely persist after him. The structural issues, such as the weaponization of fear, the manipulation of media, and judicial partisanship, are not tethered to one man’s fate. These are embedded within the party and the political system more broadly.

Conclusion: The Deep Roots of Political Rot

Steve Schmidt’s insights into Trump’s faltering facade of invincibility offer a window into the ongoing shifts within the Republican Party. However, the focus must not remain narrowly on Trump as an individual. As we witness potential shifts in power dynamics, the broader implications for democratic health and the systemic roots of authoritarian tendencies must remain at the forefront of political discourse. The true measure of political transformation will be in how deep the roots of change can penetrate into the institutional bedrock, beyond the superficial shifts in perceived power.
