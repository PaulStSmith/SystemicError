'He looks like hell': Trump bashes legendary comedian after stinging rebuke

U.S. President Donald Trump wears a ‘Trump Was Right About Everything!’ hat, as he visits The People’s House: A White House Experience museum, in Washington D.C., U.S., August 22, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

President Donald Trump uncorked an attack on David Letterman a day after the legendary late-night comedian delivered blistering criticism of Jimmy Kimmel's abrupt suspension from ABC.

When asked Thursday at The Atlantic Festival 2025 his thoughts on Kimmel being yanked off the air, Letterman replied, “This is misery."

“I feel bad about this,” he said. “We see where this is all going, correct? It’s managed media. And it’s no good. It’s silly. It’s ridiculous. And you can’t go around firing somebody because you’re fearful or trying to suck up to an authoritarian criminal administration in the Oval Office. That’s just not how this works."

Letterman warned comedians won't be the only ones targeted by the Trump administration.

“In the world of somebody who is an authoritarian, maybe a dictatorship, sooner or later, everyone is going to be touched,” he said.

He then scolded Trump some more, and called it a "premeditated" ouster.

“The institution of the president of the United States ought to be bigger than a guy doing a talk show.”

Kimmel’s show was suspended indefinitely over his remarks after the killing of conservative activist Charlie Kirk. Kimmel’s monologue accused MAGA supporters of exploiting Kirk’s death for political gain and mocked how Trump publicly grieved the loss.

In classic Trump fashion, the president took to his Truth Social platform to attack Letterman.

"Whatever happened to the very highly overrated David Letterman, whose ratings were never very good, either. He looks like hell, but at least he knew when to quit. LOSER!!!"

Our Analysis:

The Letterman-Trump-Kimmel Triangle: A Spotlight on Media Manipulation and Authoritarian Tendencies

In what feels like a throwback to the days of celebrity feuds dominating the headlines, we find ourselves amidst a rather peculiar spat involving David Letterman, Donald Trump, and, by extension, Jimmy Kimmel. Only, this isn't just tabloid fodder; it's a symptom of a far more sinister disease plaguing our political and media landscapes.

Letterman's Lament

David Letterman, a figure whose wit has often been sharpened against the grindstone of political absurdity, voiced his concerns over Jimmy Kimmel's suspension from ABC in a manner that was both blunt and alarming. Describing the suspension as "misery" and "ridiculous," Letterman didn't just mourn the loss of comedic freedom; he sounded the alarm on what he perceives as a descent into "managed media." His remarks at The Atlantic Festival 2025 weren't just a defense of a fellow comedian but a dire warning about the erosion of free speech under the guise of appeasing what he calls an "authoritarian criminal administration."

Trump's Retort

In a response that was as predictable as it was devoid of originality, Donald Trump took to Truth Social to unleash a barrage of insults at Letterman, branding him a "LOSER" and critiquing his appearance and career. Trump's retort, lacking in substance and steeped in personal vendetta, underscores a terrifying willingness to attack and belittle any voice of dissent. It's a tactic pulled straight from the authoritarian playbook: diminish, discredit, and disempower any opposition.

The Bigger Picture

At the heart of this saga lies a much more profound issue than a mere war of words between public figures. It's a glaring example of how the machinations of power are used to control the narrative. Kimmel's suspension over his comments following the death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk speaks volumes about the current state of media freedom. His accusations against MAGA supporters and Trump's public mourning were met with an indefinite suspension, a move that reeks of political pressure and censorship.

Authoritarian Shadows

Letterman's warnings about the authoritarian tendencies of the Trump administration aren't to be taken lightly. The notion that comedians—and, by extension, any dissenting voices—could be targets for suppression is a chilling reminder of the fragility of free speech. The move to silence Kimmel, and Trump's subsequent attack on Letterman, are not isolated incidents but part of a broader, more disturbing trend towards media manipulation and authoritarian control.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

As we dissect the layers of this incident, it's crucial to recognize the underlying threats it reveals about our society's current trajectory. This isn't just about Trump lashing out at critics or the suspension of a late-night host; it's a stark illustration of how easily media can be managed, voices can be silenced, and authoritarian tendencies can seep into the fabric of our democracy.

In the grand tapestry of political discourse, the Letterman-Trump-Kimmel triangle serves as a poignant reminder of the need for vigilance, the importance of protecting free speech, and the relentless resistance against the encroachment of authoritarianism. Let's not be distracted by the spectacle but instead focus on the systemic issues at play.
