Trump admin thumbs nose at MLK's family with massive document dump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20254 min read

Portland, OR, USA - July 4, 2021: A printed sign that promotes social justice by quoting a motto from Martin Luther King Jr. is seen on a storefront window in Portland, Oregon. Photo credit: (Tada Images / Shutterstock)

President Donald Trump is releasing more files about the late civil rights leader, Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., despite opposition from his family.

The FBI has a section of its website called "The Vault," where many MLK files can be found. These new files deal specifically with the assassination in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1968.

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard claimed in April that the files would be released "this week." Three months later, she's making good on the claim, CBS News reported in April.

“Today, our family has learned that President Trump has ordered the declassification of the remaining records pertaining to the assassinations of President John F. Kennedy, his brother Robert F. Kennedy, and our father, Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr.," the April statement from the King family said.

"For us, the assassination of our father is a deeply personal family loss that we have endured over the last 56 years. We hope to be provided the opportunity to review the files as a family prior to its public release.”

Our Analysis:

Declassification Drama: Trump, MLK, and the Vault of Controversy

In a move that has stirred as much intrigue as it has controversy, President Donald Trump's decision to release more files about the late civil rights leader, Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., slices through the fabric of political and historical propriety with the subtlety of a sledgehammer. Despite the clear opposition from King's family, Trump has proceeded to unveil these documents, which pertain to one of the most somber chapters in American history: the assassination of Dr. King in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1968.

The Setting: The FBI's ominously named "The Vault," where many MLK files have been gathering digital dust. It's here that the new files have been added, an action underscored by the Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard's promise—a promise that took three months to fulfill, according to CBS News. The timing and the method of this release beg a question of intent and sensitivity, or a glaring lack thereof.

The King Family's Plea: The statement from the King family in April was both a testament to their enduring grief and a dignified request for respect. "For us, the assassination of our father is a deeply personal family loss that we have endured over the last 56 years," they stated, expressing a hope that seems to have been brushed aside: the opportunity to review the files as a family prior to their public release. This move by Trump not only undermines the family's wishes but also raises ethical questions about the balance between public interest and private grief.

The Trump Angle: Always a figure of polarizing actions and rhetoric, Trump's decision aligns with his history of controversial decisions regarding sensitive information. One cannot help but wonder at the motivation behind this release. Is it a genuine effort to shed light on a dark part of history, or a calculated move to distract, divide, or claim a bizarre form of transparency credit? The timing and defiance of the King family's wishes lean toward the latter.

The Bigger Picture: Beyond the immediate drama and potential disrespect shown to the King family, this act brings to the fore the ongoing struggle over control of narratives surrounding historical figures and events. The declassification and release of such files should, in an ideal world, serve the purpose of enlightening the public and enriching historical understanding. Yet, when done against the wishes of those personally connected to the events, it muddies the waters between public right to know and respect for individual grief and privacy.

In Conclusion: Trump's decision to release more files on Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., especially in the face of opposition from King's family, is a complex cocktail of controversy, politics, and ethics. It serves as a stark reminder of the delicate balance between uncovering truth and respecting the sanctity of personal loss. As these files make their way into the public domain, one hopes that the focus remains on honoring Dr. King's legacy and contribution to civil rights, rather than the spectacle of their release. However, given the current political climate and Trump's track record, one must prepare for a range of outcomes—some potentially distasteful.
