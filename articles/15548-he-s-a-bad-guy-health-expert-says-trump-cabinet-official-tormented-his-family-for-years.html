Baylor College of Medicine Prof. Peter Hotez, who made headlines when he experienced online harassment, told his story on social media Saturday.

It started with a supporter of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. saying, "Does anyone else notice how RFK NEVER attacks other people; even when thrown a softball?"

Hotez responded, "Well no, for years he, his orgs attacked me/family in his books/public outreach, making up a false narrative."

"He portrayed me as public enemy for his personal/political gain, we endured years of death threats/stalkings, when all I did: make low cost vaccines for the world’s poor," Hotez added.

He continued:

"He’s a bad guy, and to his family’s credit they have apologized to me on several occasions, for which I’m grateful."

The expert then concluded, saying, "Despite it all, if he wants rapprochement, I’m open to it, but so far he continues to push his phony vaccine-autism links and allegiance to the corrupt wellness, supplements, influencer $multibillion industry."

Professor Dorit Reiss chimed in, "He sued multiple people for defamation to try and silence them, too. And he told FDA members they're industry puppets."

"Totally," Hotez added on Saturday.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Shadows of Power: The Hotez Ordeal

In a revelation that cuts deep into the fabric of political power and its misuse, Prof. Peter Hotez of Baylor College of Medicine has brought to light a harrowing account of torment orchestrated by a member of Donald Trump's White House Cabinet, Robert F. Kennedy Jr.. This isn't just a tale of personal vendetta; it's a glaring example of how positions of power can be weaponized against science and public health.

The Irony of RFK Jr.'s Silence

The narrative begins with an ironic observation by a supporter of Kennedy, claiming the Health Secretary "NEVER attacks other people". This statement is not just incorrect; it's laughably naive or intentionally deceptive. Hotez's response sheds light on a darker truth: for years, Kennedy, his organizations, and his followers have not only attacked Hotez but have also woven a web of falsehoods around him and his family. This wasn't a mere disagreement over policy or science; it was a systematic campaign to vilify a man dedicated to creating low-cost vaccines for the world's poor.

The Weaponization of Influence

Kennedy's portrayal of Hotez as a "public enemy" for personal and political gain is a textbook example of how influence can be weaponized. The consequences were dire: death threats and stalkings. This goes beyond personal animosity; it's an assault on the very principles of public health and scientific endeavor. Kennedy's tactics, as described by Hotez, are not just abhorrent; they're dangerous.

A Family's Apology and the Quest for Rapprochement

In a surprising turn, Kennedy's family has apologized to Hotez on several occasions. This apology, while commendable, highlights a rift between Kennedy's personal crusade and his family's recognition of its destructive nature. Hotez's openness to rapprochement speaks volumes about his character but also underscores a sad truth: the perpetrator of his years of torment continues to peddle phony vaccine-autism links and align himself with the corrupt wellness and supplements industry.

The Silence of the FDA and Legal Intimidation

Professor Dorit Reiss's input that Kennedy "sued multiple people for defamation" to silence them and accused FDA members of being "industry puppets" adds another layer to this sordid tale. It's a classic tactic of intimidation and legal bullying, designed to stifle opposition and critique. This isn't just about Kennedy; it's a reflection of a broader trend where legal mechanisms are abused to silence scientific discourse and public health advocacy.

Conclusion: The Bitter Aftertaste of Power Misused

This story is a stark reminder of the perils facing those who dare to stand in opposition to powerful figures with personal agendas. Hotez's ordeal at the hands of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. isn't just an isolated incident; it's a symptom of a more significant systemic ailment where political power, personal vendettas, and anti-science rhetoric combine to form a toxic brew.

In the end, this isn't just about Hotez or Kennedy; it's a cautionary tale about the fragility of truth in the age of influence. As we navigate these troubled waters, let us be guided not by the whims of power but by the steadfast light of science, integrity, and compassion.
