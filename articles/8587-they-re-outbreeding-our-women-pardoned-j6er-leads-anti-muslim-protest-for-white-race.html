A pardoned Jan. 6 rioter led an anti-Muslim protest in Dearborn, Michigan, that had been planned and then denounced by Republican gubernatorial candidate Anthony Hudson.

On Tuesday, Hudson told the Michigan chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR-MI) that he had abandoned the anti-Muslim protest after visiting area mosques.

However, Jake Lang showed up in Dearborn on Tuesday afternoon in Hudson’s campaign bus, which he said he had legally acquired from one of the candidate’s former staffers.

“Sharia law is not going to ever stand in America!” Lang shouted after painting the word “cuck” on the bus. “And this traitor here, who bowed down in the mosque last week, Anthony Hudson, we got no room for cowards! We got no room for traitors!”

“You’re worse than the Islamic b------s! White men who turn against their own!” he continued. “Islamification of America ends today! Our country is worth fighting for. Europe is worth fighting for. White Western civilization is worth dying for.”

Lang pointed to the election of Zohran Mamdani as New York City mayor as proof that Muslims were “taking over every major city.”

“They’re outbreeding our women, four to one,” he complained. “In one generation, the white race completely wiped out. This is a stand for our country, for our posterity.”

“The Bible tells us very clearly, what fellowship does light have with darkness? How can a sinner and a righteous person sit at the same table and eat? They cannot!” Lang added. “Look at Australia. Look at London. Look at Rome. These look like third world s—tholes. These are the bastions of white Western civilization that have been taken over in just a half a generation. We will stand strong.”

Our Analysis:

The Disturbing Theater of the Absurd: A Pardoned Rioter’s Crusade

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of dystopian fiction, Jake Lang, a pardoned Jan. 6 rioter, commandeered an anti-Muslim protest in Dearborn, Michigan. This event, initially the brainchild of Republican gubernatorial candidate Anthony Hudson, morphed into a spectacle of hate speech and bigotry, despite Hudson’s late-hour attempts to distance himself from it.

The Absurdity of a Pardoned Rioter Leading the Charge

Lang’s leading role in this protest is not just alarming; it’s a grotesque caricature of justice gone awry. Pardoned for his involvement in the Jan. 6 riot, his actions in Dearborn underscore a dangerous precedent: that participation in an insurrectionist act can somehow translate into a twisted form of political capital among extremist circles.

A Bus, A Message, and A Frightening Ideology

Lang’s use of Hudson’s campaign bus as a vehicle—literally and figuratively—for his message, is a stark illustration of the tangled webs within political extremism. Painting the word “cuck” on the bus, Lang not only defaced property but also symbolically denounced Hudson for his perceived betrayal. His shouts that “Sharia law is not going to ever stand in America!” and his tirade against Hudson for visiting mosques, reveal a deep-seated xenophobia and Islamophobia that is both baseless and dangerous.

The Dangerous Rhetoric of “White Western Civilization”

Lang’s rhetoric dangerously echoes the most extreme forms of white supremacist ideology. His fear-mongering about the “Islamification of America” and the baseless claim that Muslims are “outbreeding our women, four to one,” is not just factually incorrect; it’s a deliberate attempt to invoke fear and division. His invocation of “white Western civilization” as something worth dying for, and his derision of cities with significant Muslim populations as “third world s---holes,” strips away any veneer of legitimate political discourse, leaving only the raw, ugly face of hate.

The Misuse of Religious Imagery

Lang’s attempt to cloak his bigotry in religious rhetoric is perhaps one of the most insidious aspects of his tirade. His question, “What fellowship does light have with darkness?” is a perverse co-opting of religious language to justify xenophobia and racism. This manipulation of religious texts to serve hate-filled ends is a tactic as old as it is cowardly.

Conclusion

The incident in Dearborn is not an isolated event but a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend in American political life. The fact that a pardoned Jan. 6 rioter can lead such a protest without immediate and widespread condemnation speaks volumes about the current state of political extremism. It’s high time to recognize these theatrics for what they are: a dangerous diversion from the principles of democracy and a threat to the very fabric of a diverse society.

In a world that increasingly feels like it’s teetering on the edge of reason, the events in Dearborn serve as a grim reminder of the work that remains to be done. The fight against extremism, hate speech, and bigotry is far from over. It’s a battle for the soul of America, and it’s one that must be waged with both urgency and steadfastness.
