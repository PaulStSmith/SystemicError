An attorney has criticized the rollout and redactions of Jeffrey Epstein’s files, as well as the reaction of United States Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche.

Glenn Kirschner, a former assistant U.S. Attorney in the office of the United States Attorney for the District of Columbia, accused Blanche of carrying out a cover up of the files and suggested Donald Trump’s administration was not coming clean with the files as fast as they should.

In an appearance on Brian Tyler Cohen’s podcast, Kirschner said, “What Todd Blanche is saying is absurd, it is obscene, it is a cover up, it is revictimizing these young victims.

“They were young at the time, and to even suggest that in the millions and millions of documents in the investigation that was conducted previously of these crimes, where reportedly more than 1,000 girls were sex trafficked - to suggest the only two people that have criminal culpability in this was Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell, is f------ laughable.”

Kirschner went on to say the victims are currently being “mistreated” by the Department of Justice and the Deputy Attorney General.

He added, “One way or another, justice has to come for the abuse these then young girls now women suffered as part of the sex trafficking conspiracy.

“There’s a little bit of light on the horizon because what we’re seeing the Department of Justice doing now in some of the recent reporting is they recklessly, if not intentionally, allowed thousands and thousands and thousands of references to victims names to be spewed into the public square when they released those three million documents.

“They had one job to do, redact victim information. They, at a minimum, recklessly, and it even feels more nefarious and sinister than that, but at least recklessly endangered and victimized hundreds of these victims.

“If there is any good news in all of this, if we can mine through all the bad news, it is that the DoJ’s recklessness has now given the victims absolute standing to file a lawsuit and to demand that all of the Epstein files, not being released in the first instance, but be put in the hands of a special master [an extension of the court].”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files Fiasco: A Masterclass in Governmental Failure

The saga surrounding Jeffrey Epstein’s files has transformed from a scandal into a full-blown showcase of governmental incompetence and, according to former assistant U.S. Attorney Glenn Kirschner, a deliberate cover-up. The recent criticisms aimed at the United States Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, and by extension the Trump administration, underscore a disturbing narrative of justice denied and mishandled.

“Absurd” and “Obscene” are the words Kirschner uses to describe Blanche’s handling of the Epstein case, painting a picture of a justice system that not only fails to protect but actively revictimizes those it’s supposed to shield. The assertion that Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell are the sole figures of criminal responsibility in a sprawling network that allegedly trafficked over a thousand girls is, as Kirschner rightly points out, “f------ laughable.”

Mismanagement and Victim Revictimization

The Department of Justice’s blunder in releasing three million documents, filled with thousands of references to victims’ names, is not just an error; it is a catastrophic failure. Their primary duty was to redact victim information, yet they failed spectacularly, leading to further victimization of the survivors. This negligence borders on the sinister, suggesting a lack of concern for the wellbeing and privacy of the individuals most harmed by Epstein’s network.

A Sliver of Hope?

Despite the grim scenario painted thus far, Kirschner highlights a potentially positive outcome. The Department of Justice’s reckless handling of the Epstein files could grant the victims standing to file a lawsuit and demand the appointment of a special master to oversee the release of the Epstein files. This could be a crucial step towards achieving some form of justice for the survivors, albeit one that comes after much unnecessary suffering.

Conclusion: A System in Dire Need of Reform

The Epstein case is a glaring example of systemic failure at multiple levels of the justice system. From the alleged cover-up to the mishandling of sensitive documents, it’s evident that there are deep-seated issues that need addressing. The victims of Epstein’s network deserved better from the start, and the ongoing missteps only add insult to injury.

It’s high time for a thorough investigation into how the Department of Justice has handled this case, with accountability and transparency at the forefront. The survivors of Epstein’s trafficking ring have waited long enough for justice. It’s incumbent upon the justice system to finally deliver it, free from obfuscation and delay.
