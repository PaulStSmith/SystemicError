'She doesn’t care': Trump biographer lays bare Melania’s latest 'sham'

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 27, 20263 min read

The Flattery Machine Is the Story, Not Melania Trump’s Friendships

A Spectacle Disguised as Scrutiny

The Daily Beast podcast described Fox Business hosts greeting first lady Melania Trump with extravagant praise, despite what Joanna Coles characterized as her unusually limited public schedule. That contradiction merits scrutiny. Instead, Michael Wolff and Coles wandered into speculation about Trump’s marriage, friendships, clothing and personal interest in her work.

The result is political coverage that notices a propaganda performance and then abandons it for gossip.

Fox Business Made the Relevant Decision

Melania Trump did not control the interview alone. Fox Business chose the setting, the questions and the reverential introduction. Its hosts reportedly told her they were “beaming” in her presence and praised how much she does. If, as Coles said, the Daily Beast’s audit found Trump to be the least publicly visible first lady of the past century, that discrepancy should have been the interview’s central subject.

Institutional power here belongs principally to the presidency that supplies the prestige and to the broadcaster that converts access into flattering television. The hosts were not merely “stuck,” as Wolff suggested. They were media professionals participating in an editorial production. Describing them as bewildered workers obscures their agency.

Gossip Lets the Institution Escape

Whether Melania Trump has female friends is politically irrelevant. Mocking her clothing does not explain how presidential image management works. Calling her marriage a “sham” is an unsupported assertion in the material provided, not analysis. Predicting that she “doesn’t care” assigns a motive the reporting does not establish.

This framing also weakens legitimate questions about her public role. It invites readers to judge a woman through her wardrobe, marriage and social circle while the network manufacturing deference receives comparatively little examination. The powerful institution becomes scenery; the woman’s personality becomes the indictment.

“Imperia” Needs Evidence, Not Sneering

Trump’s announced women-focused initiative, “Imperia,” may warrant skepticism, particularly when its substance remains unclear in the account. But the podcast offered ridicule where reporting was needed. What is the initiative’s legal structure? Who funds it? Who governs it? What commitments has it made? What access does it gain from association with the White House?

The supplied article answers none of those questions. It also ends by saying Trump has profited from her husband’s presidency without providing figures, transactions or supporting details. That may be an allegation worth investigating, but it cannot carry an argument until the evidence is supplied.

Access Journalism Rewards Submission

The sharper political story is the exchange of prestige between government and media. A first lady with limited public visibility grants an appearance; a television network treats access itself as an achievement; praise substitutes for questioning. The audience receives staged admiration instead of information about the initiative being promoted.

That arrangement does not require a secret conspiracy. The incentives are visible: officeholders and their families gain favorable exposure, while broadcasters gain proximity to power. Each side benefits from an interview that asks little and signifies much.

Contempt Is Not Accountability

Wolff and Coles recognized the emptiness of the Fox performance but reproduced its central failure: neither treated the public as entitled to concrete answers. Fox supplied adulation. The podcast supplied derision. Both personalized what should have been institutional scrutiny.

The larger pattern is a media system that oscillates between courtier behavior and celebrity mockery while leaving power insufficiently examined. Serious accountability would track money, access, governance and official leverage. Speculation about friendships and outfits merely fills the space where that reporting should be.
