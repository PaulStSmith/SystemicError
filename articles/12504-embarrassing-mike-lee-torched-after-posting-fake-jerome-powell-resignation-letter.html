'Embarrassing': Mike Lee torched after posting fake Jerome Powell resignation letter

Paulo SantosJuly 23, 20254 min read

U.S. Senator Mike Lee speaking with attendees at the 2022 Student Action Summit at the Tampa Convention Center in Tampa, Florida. (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT) was caught posting (and then deleting) what turned out to be a fake resignation letter from Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell. It was then promptly shared by others on the right as a bombshell. The problem, however, is the letter was clearly a phony.

Some mocked Lee for being clueless, but others pointed to obvious tells, such as typos and fake seals.

Journalist Aaron Rupar quipped, "The only conclusion I can draw is that Mike Lee is actually an idiot."

Politico congressional reporters Jordain Carney posted on X, "Sen. Mike Lee tweeted (and appears to have deleted) what he appears to believe is a resignation letter (??) from Powell."

Semafor political reporter David Weigel and Politico's Ben Jacobs pointed out other errors in the fake letter.

"The typo ridden letter has now been deleted by both Benny Johnson and Senator Mike Lee," Jacobs said.

Weigel replied, "A GOP senator not being able to spot a fake, loaded with typos... good stuff here. If you really think you're going to scoop the Bloomberg terminal on a Powell move, get psychological help."

Veteran and lawyer John Jackson similarly pointed out Lee's ignorance. "Mike Lee is an idiot. He just fell for this fake letter of Jerome Powell resigning."

Democratic Party staffer Matt Rein zeroed in on the "official" seal in the letter, showing the absurdity.

Influencer and commentator Boston Smalls, jeered on X, "For a senator. This is extremely embarrassing. I mean, Mike Lee is a complete toilet fish, but this is just another level."

Washington Post opinions editor Benjy Sarlin posted a screen capture of the Dow Jones after one person pointed out it wasn't smart to post about "market moving news without verifing it."

"It's funny that day traders, who frequently panic sell off viral rumors, were like 'Oh that's just Mike Lee, he does this all the time, ignore it,'" said Sarlin on X.

President Donald Trump has been attacking Powell for years for refusing to reduce interest rates.

Our Analysis:

The Sordid Tale of Senator Mike Lee's Blunder: A Case Study in Political Farce

In an episode that could only be described as part farce, part cautionary tale, Senator Mike Lee (R-UT) found himself embroiled in a debacle that underscores the perils of political echo chambers and the erosion of due diligence in the digital age. Lee, in what can only be characterized as a spectacular lapse in judgment, posted—and then hastily deleted—a fake resignation letter purportedly from Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell. This act of political theater quickly transformed into a spectacle of embarrassment, not only for Lee but for those on the right who prematurely celebrated this faux bombshell.

The Anatomy of a Blunder

At the heart of this debacle lies a document so obviously counterfeit that it would barely pass muster in a high school prank, let alone the scrutinizing gaze of national politics. Typos, fake seals, and a general aura of inauthenticity surrounded the letter—a fact that was quickly seized upon by journalists and commentators across the political spectrum. Aaron Rupar's quip that the only conclusion to be drawn is that "Mike Lee is actually an idiot" may seem harsh, but it underscores the gravity of Lee's error in judgment.

Politico's Jordain Carney and Ben Jacobs, along with Semafor's David Weigel, were quick to point out the myriad errors within the fake letter, highlighting not just its lack of veracity but the eagerness with which it was embraced by some quarters of the GOP. This enthusiasm for what was clearly a fabrication speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse and the dangerous game of echo chamber politics.

The Fallout: Ridicule, Embarrassment, and Lessons Unlearned

The fallout from Lee's misadventure was swift and merciless. Matt Rein and Boston Smalls highlighted the absurdity of the situation, with Smalls aptly dubbing Lee a "complete toilet fish"—a colorful epithet that, while humorous, underscores the seriousness of the senator's blunder. The criticism wasn't confined to partisan observers; Benjy Sarlin of the Washington Post pointed out the potential market implications of spreading unverified "market moving news," noting the bemusement of day traders who dismissed the incident as typical of Lee's antics.

This incident is emblematic of a broader issue plaguing our political landscape: a flagrant disregard for the truth and a willingness to embrace and propagate falsehoods if they serve one's political ends. The eagerness with which the fake resignation was shared speaks to a troubling trend of valuing sensationalism over accuracy, a trend that threatens the very foundations of our democratic discourse.

In Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance and Responsibility

Senator Mike Lee's foray into the world of fake news is more than just an embarrassing episode; it is a stark reminder of the responsibilities that come with political power. The readiness to share unverified information, especially when it concerns figures and institutions as critical as the Federal Reserve, is not just reckless—it's dangerous.

As we navigate these turbulent political waters, let this incident serve as a cautionary tale. The need for vigilance, skepticism, and a commitment to truth is more pressing than ever. In an era where falsehoods can spread with the click of a button, the consequences of failing to uphold these principles are not just embarrassing—they could be catastrophic.
