Trump voter bashes president's 'tone deaf' new move as a 'public relations spectacle'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 14, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Affordability Tour: Trump’s Misguided Spectacle

President Donald Trump delivers remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania on Dec. 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

A conservative Texas mom who voted for President Donald Trump slammed his effort to address the affordability crisis as an insulting “public relations spectacle.”

The president’s economic approval rating dropped to his worst ever, at 31 percent, as he hit the road this week for the first stop in his affordability tour to reassure anxious Americans about higher costs of living, but USA Today columnist Nicole Russell gave his performance a big thumbs-down.

“I voted for President Donald Trump, and I don’t regret it,” Russell wrote. “But some days, I get tired of his banter and smears. They’re prosaic and stupid and they usually don’t solve any problems or showcase progress, even if there has been.”

She said the concept of the tour was “tone deaf,” saying that Americans could decide for themselves whether prices were affordable or not.

“I have four kids; I need life to be as affordable as possible, just like most American families do,” Russell wrote. “But either America is affordable right now, or Trump is instituting policies that are helping make America affordable. There is no need for a public relations spectacle – if Trump is confident in his policies, and I think he should be, or he should be willing to make changes where necessary.”

Instead, she wrote, the president mocked the concept of affordability and complained that Democrats had made up the idea to make him look bad, although he also conceded that “prices are too high,” which he blamed on former President Joe Biden, and Russell said the messaging was so bad she wished for a different messenger.

“If I could have a Republican president who’s as articulate as Barack Obama, as effective as Ronald Reagan and as charismatic as John F. Kennedy, I’d manufacture this, but I cannot,” she wrote. “At the end of the day, I’m just a voter − just like you.”

Russell pointed out that Trump campaigned on a promise to fix affordability, which she said had lingered since the Covid-19 pandemic, and she praised his accomplishments – including tax cuts in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and a record-high stock market – but said the president must address high costs of living or watch his Republican majorities evaporate.

“If Trump is confident in these changes and feels he must go on a tour, then he should use that chance to articulate them better, not make fun of the idea that Americans want greater affordability,” Russell wrote. “How did he expect an in-person ‘tour’ criticizing Democrats for using the term ‘affordability’ to be effective messaging? It’s not.”

“There’s nothing wrong with wanting to live in an affordable America. That’s the dream,” the columnist added. “Trump can make it so, but not this way.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Affordability Tour: Trump’s Misguided Spectacle

In a recent display of political theatre, President Donald Trump embarked on what has been dubbed an “affordability tour”, a move that has sparked considerable controversy and criticism from various quarters, including from those within his own voting base. Nicole Russell, a self-proclaimed conservative Texas mom and Trump supporter, has voiced her disdain for what she perceives as a hollow and tone-deaf public relations stunt by the president, aimed at addressing the mounting affordability crisis gripping the nation.

Trump’s Approaching Economic Quagmire

Firstly, it’s crucial to highlight the backdrop against which this tour unfolds: Trump’s plummeting economic approval rating, now languishing at a dismal 31 percent. This figure is not just a number; it’s a stark indicator of the growing disillusionment among American voters, including those who once threw their unwavering support behind Trump’s economic policies. Russell’s critique underscores a critical misstep by the Trump administration: the failure to genuinely address the roots of the affordability crisis, choosing instead to embark on a superficial tour that seems to mock the very real struggles faced by countless Americans.

A Tone-Deaf Spectacle

Russell’s characterization of the tour as “tone deaf” hits the nail on the head. The notion that a sitting president would prioritize a glorified public relations campaign over substantive policy action is not only insulting but deeply concerning. It reflects a fundamental disconnect from the everyday realities of the American populace. Russell, with her candid admission of needing life to be “as affordable as possible” for her family, represents the voice of millions who are directly impacted by the policies enacted—or not enacted—by those in power.

Misguided Messaging

The president’s messaging strategy, as Russell points out, is baffling at best. Trump’s attempt to deflect blame onto Democrats and his predecessor, while simultaneously acknowledging the issue of high prices, is a study in contradiction. This muddled message does little to reassure Americans who are looking for clear, decisive action on affordability. Russell’s lament for a Republican president who combines Obama’s articulation, Reagan’s effectiveness, and Kennedy’s charisma speaks volumes about the leadership vacuum she perceives in the current administration.

The Illusion of Progress

While Russell acknowledges Trump’s accomplishments, such as the tax cuts in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and a record-high stock market, she rightly argues that these achievements pale in comparison to the urgent need to address the high costs of living. The fact that Trump’s economic policies have not sufficiently mitigated the affordability crisis is a glaring indictment of his administration’s priorities. The suggestion that Trump’s Republican majorities might evaporate if he fails to tackle this issue head-on is a stark warning that the political cost of inaction could be substantial.

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity

Russell’s critique of Trump’s affordability tour is a scathing indictment of a presidency that, in her view, has missed the mark on one of the most pressing issues facing the nation today. The tour, rather than being a platform for meaningful dialogue and policy promotion, has been reduced to a spectacle of missed opportunities and misguided messaging. In the final analysis, Russell’s commentary is a call to action for leadership that is as concerned with the lived experiences of its citizens as it is with the optics of governance. Trump’s affordability tour, rather than assuaging concerns, has only served to highlight the administration’s apparent disconnect from the economic realities of its constituents.
