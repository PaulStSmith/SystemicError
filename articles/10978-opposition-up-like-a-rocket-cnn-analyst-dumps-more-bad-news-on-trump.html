‘Opposition up like a rocket!’ CNN analyst dumps more bad news on Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 2, 20255 min read

Americans have resignedly rejected President Donald Trump’s so-called reciprocal tariffs, according to new polling that left CNN’s chief data analyst Harry Enten stunned on Monday.

“They have turned against tariffs the same way college football pundits have turned against Arch Manning after his performance in Columbus on Saturday,” Enten said, speaking to new polling that showed a souring on tariffs among Americans since Trump’s election victory last November.

Trump’s tariffs, which have been touted by the administration as a tool to increase the United States’ domestic manufacturing capabilities, have been a focal point of the president’s second term, having been placed on hundreds of countries. However, a federal court ruled Sunday that the tariffs were unlawful, though they will remain in place until at least Oct. 14 as the Trump administration likely pursues an appeal.

While the permanency of the tariffs will likely be debated over the coming month, Americans’ opinion on them has collapsed, according to the new polling revealed Monday, which showed their 52% favorability in November plummet to 40% over ten months.

“You go back to November of 2024, the popular position was 'favor,' they favored new tariffs on imported goods, 52%!” Enten said.

“You go to [now], look at that, the opposition is up like a rocket! We're talking about 60% opposing new tariffs compared to just 40% in favor. So it was majority favor back when Trump was re-elected in November of 2024, now it's a clear three in five Americans oppose it. The Americans have gotten a taste of the tariffs and they spit it right out.”

The root cause of Americans souring on tariffs, Enten explained, was the impact on the costs of goods.

According to the polling, 77% of respondents said they believe tariffs would increase costs in the short term, versus just 4% who said they believed tariffs would decrease costs. In the long term, 50% of Americans said they believed tariffs would increase costs, versus just 25% who said tariffs would decrease costs in the long term.

“It all comes down to money, out of the pocket book, impact of tariffs on prices,” Enten said. “In the short term, 77% of Americans now agree they raise prices in the short term.”

The plunge in support for tariffs among Americans could also pose a significant threat to Trump’s own favorability, even among his supporters, Enten went on to argue.

“This is why the political implications are so important, because Fox News literally asked what's the top reason that Trump's doing a bad job; what ranks as number one?” he said. “The economy and tariffs rank as number one, the number-one reason why those who disapprove of Trump think he's doing a bad job.”

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Turmoil: Unpacking America's Growing Discontent

In a recent revelation that seems to have left CNN's chief data analyst, Harry Enten, as stunned as the rest of us, polling data has surfaced showing a significant shift in American opinion against President Donald Trump's so-called reciprocal tariffs. Once a cornerstone of Trump's economic policy, aimed at bolstering domestic manufacturing, these tariffs have now become a source of national disillusionment.

The Numbers Don't Lie

The polling, which starkly showcases a plummet from a 52% favorability in November to a mere 40% over ten months, speaks volumes. This dramatic turn of events mirrors the kind of surprise and disbelief one might reserve for an underwhelming performance by a touted college football star—Enten's comparison to Arch Manning's performance not withstanding. It's not just a shift; it's a wholesale rejection of a policy once seen as favorable by a majority.

The Judgment of the Courts

Adding insult to injury, a federal court has recently ruled these tariffs unlawful, though they remain in effect until at least October 14 as the Trump administration scrambles for an appeal. This legal debacle only serves to underscore the growing perception of these tariffs as not only economically detrimental but potentially outside the bounds of legality.

A Deep Dive into the Discontent

Enten points to the impact on the cost of goods as the primary root of Americans' souring on tariffs. A staggering 77% of respondents believe tariffs would increase costs in the short term, a sentiment that hardly fades in the long view, with 50% still expecting increased costs in the long term. This overwhelming consensus paints a clear picture: the American public feels the pinch in their wallets and is unequivocally unhappy about it.

The Political Fallout

Perhaps most damning of all are the potential political repercussions for Trump himself. Enten highlights that, according to Fox News, the economy and tariffs rank as the number one area of dissatisfaction among those who disapprove of Trump's performance. This isn't just a minor hiccup in public opinion; it's a full-blown crisis of confidence in his economic stewardship.

Conclusion: A Bitter Pill to Swallow

The shift in public opinion on tariffs is more than just a statistical curiosity; it's a testament to the tangible impact of economic policy on everyday life. Americans, it seems, have tasted the bitter fruits of the tariff policy and have spat them out with decisive rejection. This isn't merely about numbers on a page or abstract economic principles; it's about the real-world implications of a policy that has reached into the pocketbooks of ordinary Americans and left them worse off.

As the Trump administration likely gears up to challenge the court's ruling, one can't help but wonder about the wisdom of doubling down on a policy that has lost the faith of the American public. With the economy and tariffs now a glaring Achilles' heel for Trump, the political landscape ahead promises to be fraught with challenges. The message from the American people is clear: it's time for a reevaluation of priorities, with a focus on policies that genuinely serve the public's best interest.
