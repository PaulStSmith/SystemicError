'Only race I care about': MAGA outcast vows to stop Trump-endorsed Gov candidate in Ohio

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 26, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Vivek Ramaswamy (Photo by Marco Bello for Reuters)

Neo-Nazi activist and “Groyper” leader Nick Fuentes has a new target to defeat in the political arena — and it’s a MAGA Republican.

According to Fox News, “White nationalist Nick Fuentes vowed to campaign against Vivek Ramaswamy in a slur-laced rant denouncing the Republican’s Ohio governor bid,” during a stream. Specifically, Fuentes said, “I think I’m going to go to Ohio, and the word that we are looking for is denial. We have to deny Vivek Ramaswamy the governorship. This is the only race I care about in ‘26. It’s the only one I care about.”

Fuentes then “used a slur to describe Ramaswamy and said he does not care if a Democrat defeats him in the governor’s race.”

It’s the latest salvo after Ramaswamy, a biotech entrepreneur born in Cincinnati who ran for president himself on a hard-right platform last year and helped advise the second Trump administration in the early days, penned a plea in The New York Times for the Republican Party to do a better job policing the racism inside it.

“Older Republicans who may doubt the rising prevalence of the blood-and-soil view should think again. My social media feeds are littered with hundreds of slurs, most from accounts that I don’t recognize, about ‘pajeets’ and ‘street s------s’ and calls to deport me ‘back to India,’” he wrote.

But many of Trump’s supporters were enraged by Ramaswamy’s warnings and called for his deportation.

On the other side of the governor race, Democrats are likely to nominate Dr. Amy Acton, who helped manage COVID-19 policy for the administration of incumbent Republican Gov. Mike DeWine.

Our Analysis:

Fuentes vs. Ramaswamy: A Disturbing Glimpse into Internal GOP Warfare

In the latest episode of political theater that could only be described as both grotesque and illuminating, Nick Fuentes, a neo-Nazi activist and leader of the so-called “Groyper” movement, has announced his latest crusade. This time, his target isn’t the usual scapegoats of his vitriol. Instead, he’s aiming at Vivek Ramaswamy, a MAGA Republican with aspirations for the Ohio governorship. The sheer absurdity of this situation reveals much about the current state of the Republican Party and the dangerous undercurrents within American politics.

Fuentes, in what can only be deemed a slur-laced rant, has vowed to prevent Ramaswamy from securing the governorship. His declaration, “I think I’m going to go to Ohio, and the word that we are looking for is denial,” is not just an assault on Ramaswamy but a blatant display of the toxic racism that festers within certain factions of the party. Fuentes’s readiness to use derogatory slurs against Ramaswamy, coupled with his indifference to the prospect of a Democrat winning, underscores a perverse ideology where racial purity trumps (no pun intended) political allegiance.

Ramaswamy, a biotech entrepreneur and a conservative figure who sought to advise the Trump administration, represents what one might consider a paradox. Here is a man who embodies the American Dream, yet he’s vilified by the very people who claim to champion such aspirations. His plea in The New York Times for the GOP to address and ameliorate the racism within its ranks is met not with introspection but with hostility and calls for his deportation. The irony is as thick as it is tragic.

The backlash against Ramaswamy’s call to action, filled with “hundreds of slurs” and vitriolic social media attacks, is indicative of a larger, more systemic issue. It’s a glaring beacon, highlighting the GOP’s struggle with a faction that is not just resistant to change but actively seeks to regress to an era marked by division and hatred.

On the other side of the upcoming governor race, Democrats seem poised to nominate Dr. Amy Acton, a figure known for her handling of COVID-19 policy. The contrast between the potential candidates and what they represent couldn’t be starker. On one hand, there’s the possibility of a governance rooted in science, empathy, and inclusivity. On the other, a battleground marred by extremist rhetoric and racial animosity.

The Bottom Line: The conflict between Fuentes and Ramaswamy is more than just a political skirmish; it’s a microcosm of the ideological battle waging within the Republican Party and, by extension, the nation. It’s a stark reminder of the dangers posed by allowing extremist elements to flourish unchallenged. As the GOP grapples with its identity in the wake of Trump’s presidency, the outcome of this particular confrontation may well signal the party’s direction in the coming years. For the sake of the nation’s collective moral and political health, one can only hope the GOP heeds Ramaswamy’s warning before it’s too late.
