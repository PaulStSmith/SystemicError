President Donald Trump‘s approval rating has plummeted to its lowest point yet, according to new polling results released Thursday.

Results from a new AP-NORC poll showed that Americans have grown further dissatisfied with Trump’s handling of the economy and immigration, a clear sign that these could make his party vulnerable ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, The Associated Press reported. Only 31% of U.S. adults said they approve of how Trump is handling the economy, which is down 40% from March and “marks the lowest economic approval he’s registered in an AP-NORC poll in his first or second term.”

And while Republicans haven’t completely abandoned their support of the president, it does show his influence has slipped.

“The new poll found that 36% of Americans approve of the way he’s handling his job as president, which is down slightly from 42% in March,” AP reported. “That signals that even if some people aren’t happy with elements of his approach, they might not be ready to say he’s doing a bad job as president. Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Plummet: Trump’s Approval Ratings Take a Dive

The latest AP-NORC poll results provide a stark picture: President Donald Trump’s approval rating has nosedived to its lowest point yet. This isn’t just a blip on the political radar; it’s a screaming siren signaling deep dissatisfaction among the American populace with Trump’s handling of critical issues like the economy and immigration. These aren’t mere numbers; they’re a clarion call to the GOP, highlighting vulnerabilities that could significantly impact the 2026 midterm elections.

The Economy: A Sinking Ship?

Let’s dissect the figures: A mere 31% of U.S. adults now approve of Trump’s economic leadership, a drastic fall from 40% in March. This isn’t just a decline; it’s a freefall. It represents the lowest economic approval rating Trump has ever received in an AP-NORC poll during his tenure. In a country where the economy often dictates political fortunes, these numbers spell potential disaster for Trump and his party. The economy, traditionally a strong suit for Trump, has now become his Achilles’ heel.

Immigration: The Wall of Discontent

Turning to immigration, Trump’s approval rating stands at a dismal 38%. This issue, once a cornerstone of Trump’s political identity, has become another battleground of discontent. It’s evident that the American people are not just disillusioned with Trump’s policies but are actively disapproving of his approach to an issue that remains at the forefront of national debate.

The Republican Conundrum

Despite these alarming numbers, the Republican base hasn’t completely abandoned Trump. Sure, his influence may have waned slightly, with his overall job approval dipping to 36% from 42% in March, but it’s not the mass exodus some might expect. This highlights a perplexing scenario: even as discontent grows within their ranks on key issues, Republicans largely remain loyal to Trump. This loyalty, however, could come at a cost, especially if the broader electorate’s dissatisfaction translates into electoral losses in the midterms.

The Social Media Echo Chamber

The reactions on social media range from mocking derision to bewildered concern. From Mike Murphy’s succinct “Dumpster fire…” to Bekah Freitas’s observation on Trump’s obliviousness to his falling ratings, the sentiment is overwhelmingly negative. Yet, Larry Schweikart offers a contrarian view, suggesting that despite the poor polling, Republican voter registration is on the rise. This discrepancy between public opinion and voter registration trends underscores the complexity of political dynamics in the U.S.

Final Thoughts

The AP-NORC poll’s findings are a wake-up call for Trump and the GOP. With the 2026 midterm elections on the horizon, these numbers should not be dismissed lightly. They reflect a growing dissatisfaction that, if not addressed, could have significant implications for the political landscape. The economy and immigration, once Trump’s winning tickets, are now his most significant liabilities. As the GOP rallies behind him, one must wonder: Is loyalty to Trump worth the potential cost?

In a political climate as volatile as ours, approval ratings are more than just numbers; they’re indicators of a broader, more profound discontent. As we move closer to the midterms, these indicators could very well dictate the future not just for Trump, but for the entire Republican Party.
