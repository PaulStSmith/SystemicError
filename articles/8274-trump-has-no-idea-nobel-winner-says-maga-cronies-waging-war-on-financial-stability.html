'Trump has no idea': Nobel winner says MAGA 'cronies' waging 'war on financial stability'

Donald Trump has “no idea” his MAGA group is fighting a “war on financial stability,” a Nobel Prize-winning economist has claimed.

The president seems to be blind to issues of the economy as expert Paul Krugman explains how “allies and cronies” are having more of an effect than Trump on the cost-of-living crisis. Krugman has since predicted a 2008-like crash in his latest Substack post, calling for Americans to brace themselves for turbulent times as the Trump administration picks apart economic safeguarding measures.

Krugman wrote, “The MAGA war on financial stability is being waged largely on two fronts. First, there’s an ongoing effort within some parts of the Federal Reserve to drastically weaken bank supervision — oversight of banks to prevent them from taking risks that could threaten the financial system.”

“The second front of MAGA’s war on financial stability is on behalf of the crypto industry. The Trump administration and its allies in Congress — including, I’m sorry to say, a number of Democrats in this case — are moving to promote wider use of crypto.”

“In particular, the GENIUS Act (gag me with an acronym), passed in July, aims to promote stablecoins. And the fact is that stablecoins are effectively an alternative, weakly regulated and poorly supervised form of banking.”

How much of the blame Trump can shoulder is unknown so far, as Krugman believes there are problems at play the president is simply yet to notice. He continued, “But here we are in 2025, and 2008 wasn’t that long ago. Many of us still have vivid memories of the gut-wrenching panic that gripped the world when Lehman fell.”

“Yet Donald Trump’s allies and cronies are now moving rapidly to dismantle the precautionary regulations introduced after 2008 to reduce the risk of future financial crises. I say ‘allies and cronies’ advisedly.”

“There’s no indication that Trump himself has any idea what’s happening on his watch. But key players in Congress, within the administration, and, alas, at the Federal Reserve, are apparently determined to make a 2008 rerun possible.”

Krugman has since urged the Democratic party to be well aware of the financial shortcomings of the Trump administration and its tariff policy, warning that they could be set to manage a 2008-like crisis should they be successful at the 2028 elections.

The economist warned, “Along with its many other sins, the Trump administration is doing its best to make a future financial crisis more likely. I hope the Democrats are paying attention and won’t let themselves be seduced by Wall Street and, worse, the blandishments of the crypto bros.

“Because if they don’t, they could set themselves for a 2008-type crash during a Democratic administration. And we can guess who will get the blame.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s MAGA and the War on Financial Stability: A Closer Examination

Donald Trump’s administration, often characterized by its fervent MAGA rallying cry, appears to be embroiled in what can only be described as a “war on financial stability.” This isn’t just another hyperbolic statement thrown into the political arena for effect; it’s a serious accusation backed by none other than Nobel Prize-winning economist, Paul Krugman. Let’s dissect Krugman’s claims, and perhaps more importantly, the looming implications they herald.

The Two Fronts of Financial Destabilization

Krugman articulates the MAGA movement’s assault on financial stability as twofold. Firstly, there’s the weakening of bank supervision within the Federal Reserve. This isn’t just a minor policy tweak; it’s a fundamental dismantling of the safeguards designed to prevent the kind of risk-taking that precipitated the 2008 financial crisis. The audacity to even consider such a regression is baffling, to say the least.

The second front, and equally concerning, is the promotion of the crypto industry — notably through legislation like the GENIUS Act. The irony of the acronym aside, the push for wider use of crypto, particularly stablecoins, is a clear move towards a less regulated, more volatile financial landscape. Stablecoins, despite their name, are anything but stable, representing a form of banking that’s “weakly regulated and poorly supervised.”

Trump’s Unwitting Role?

Krugman posits that Trump might be somewhat oblivious to the economic turmoil his administration is fostering. This isn’t particularly reassuring. The notion that the president could be unaware of such consequential policies under his watch doesn’t absolve him of responsibility; it magnifies the recklessness of his administration.

A 2008 Redux?

The specter of 2008 looms large over Krugman’s analysis. The dismantling of post-2008 financial regulations is a recipe for disaster, setting the stage for a potential repeat of the financial crisis. This isn’t mere speculation; it’s a forecast based on observable actions and policies being pursued by Trump’s “allies and cronies.” The term “cronies” here is particularly apt, suggesting a cabal of self-serving operatives indifferent to the broader societal fallout of their actions.

A Warning to Democrats

Krugman’s warning to the Democratic Party is stark: do not be seduced by Wall Street or the “crypto bros.” The allure of financial innovation or deregulation can be tempting, but the consequences, as history has shown, can be dire. The Democrats, should they find themselves in power come 2028, must heed the lessons of the past to avoid managing a crisis of their own making — a crisis for which, Krugman warns, they will undoubtedly be blamed.

In Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Paul Krugman’s analysis serves as a clarion call for vigilance. The “war on financial stability” being waged by the MAGA movement under Trump’s seemingly oblivious watch poses a clear and present danger to the economic well-being of the United States. It’s a scenario fraught with irony and loaded with peril. As we navigate these turbulent times, one thing becomes painfully clear: the cost of ignorance, whether willful or unwitting, is far too high. The stakes? Nothing less than the financial stability of the nation.
