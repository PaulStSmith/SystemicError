Ex-GOP congressman baffled Trumpers believe they can win 'fleeing' Latino voters

During the 2024 presidential election, President Donald Trump broke precedents when he achieved 42 percent of the Latino vote, a record for a Republican candidate. Yet according to former Trump supporter and ex-GOP lawmaker Joe Walsh, the result of Trump’s immigration policies is that these same voters are now “fleeing” the party.

“It’s clearly an issue that is profoundly moving Latino and Hispanic voters in this country — Latino and Hispanic voters who, I want to remind everybody, I want to remind every Democrat listening to me, voted for Trump in enormous numbers in 2024,” Walsh said on his Wednesday Substack episode. “Don’t forget that, Democrats. We — I’m a Democrat — we’ve got our own problems when it comes to Hispanic and Latino voters.”

He added, “They’ve turned on Trump now, within a year, on a dime. They’re fleeing Trump and the Republican Party.” Yet he added that this does not mean Democrats will automatically benefit from their disillusionment with the MAGA-fied GOP. The same polls which show Hispanic voters are upset with Trump also reveal that majorities want to enforce border security. All it means, according to Walsh, is that these same voters recognize the Republican Party is not a welcoming home for them.

“Trump has taken it to such a dark, ugly, cruel, hateful, un-American, lawless, and fascist place, Latino and Hispanic voters — those who voted for Trump, who voted Republican in 2024 — they’re bailing,” Walsh said.

Walsh is not the only pundit to note that Trump’s immigration policies are turning off voters, Hispanic and non-Hispanic alike. A February survey of 2,300 people taken by The Washington Post found that 39 percent of respondents supported Trump while 60 percent opposed him. Among those who disapproved, immigration was most commonly cited as the policy they objected to. Respondents blasted Trump for “detaining and deporting immigrants with no criminal background,” allowing “ICE all the power they have,” encouraging “Gestapo style of deporting illegal immigrants... even killing at least two U.S. citizens” and permitting “everything with ICE, deporting immigrants, etc.”

Speaking to Politico earlier in March, Mike Madrid, a Republican strategist, told Politico that the GOP had a brilliant way to address the situation, then threw it all away.

“With the border secure and Latinos responding to ICE raids and government overreach, the districts that Republicans thought were their future a year ago are likely to be their undoing. Hard to find another situation in the past 50 years where a political party has squandered a generational opportunity like this,” he said.

Daniel Guerrero, CEO of the South Texas Builders Association, told Politico in the same interview that anti-Trump sentiment is rampant in his industry due to the federal raids and attacks on random construction sites.

“The sentiment is pretty clear across the table, that nobody really expected this magnitude of enforcement,” Guerrero explained, admitting that he himself voted for Trump in 2024.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Paradox: Trump’s Latino Voter Conundrum

In an era marked by political polarization and ideological extremism, former President Donald Trump’s relationship with Latino voters during the 2024 presidential election unfolds like a paradox wrapped in a contradiction. As reported, Trump managed to shatter previous records by securing 42 percent of the Latino vote—a feat that would usually indicate a strong bond between the electorate and the candidate. Yet, the narrative swiftly transitions from triumph to tragedy, as these same voters are now reportedly abandoning the GOP ship in droves. The architect of their disillusionment? None other than Trump himself, aided and abetted by his own immigration policies.

The Swift Reversal

According to Joe Walsh, a former Trump supporter turned critic, the exodus of Latino and Hispanic voters from the Republican fold is not just significant—it’s seismic. Walsh’s observations, delivered through his Substack, underline a stark warning to Democrats not to take these voters for granted, even as they flee Trump’s MAGA-fied GOP. The reasons for this departure are as clear as they are damning. Trump’s policies have not only been described as “dark, ugly, cruel, hateful, un-American, lawless, and fascist” by Walsh but have turned a demographic that showed him considerable support into vocal critics.

The Immigration Policy Backlash

The backlash against Trump’s immigration stance is not a quiet undercurrent but a loud and clear message from the Latino and Hispanic community. A survey highlighted in the article points to a significant portion of the population—39 percent supporting Trump, with 60 percent in opposition. Among the detractors, immigration policies were a major point of contention, with criticisms ranging from the empowerment of ICE to the inhumane treatment and deportation of immigrants, including those with no criminal background.

A Squandered Opportunity

What’s particularly striking is the analysis from Republican strategist Mike Madrid and Daniel Guerrero, CEO of the South Texas Builders Association, both of whom pinpoint the GOP’s mishandling of the situation as not just a missed opportunity but a generational blunder. Madrid’s comments to Politico reflect a sentiment of wasted potential, suggesting that the party had the means to solidify and expand its Latino voter base but chose instead to alienate them through aggressive immigration policies and ICE raids.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

The story of Trump’s rise and fall with Latino voters serves as a cautionary tale for both major political parties in the United States. For Republicans, it’s a stark reminder that hardline policies and rhetoric can backfire, even among demographics that once showed them favor. For Democrats, it underscores the danger of complacency and the importance of actively engaging with and addressing the needs of Latino and Hispanic voters.

In the end, the political landscape is ever-changing, and the allegiance of its constituents is never guaranteed. The GOP’s handling of its relationship with Latino voters post-2024 election is not just a lesson in political strategy but a reflection of the broader challenges facing a divided America. As this drama unfolds, one thing is clear: the Latino vote cannot and should not be taken for granted by any party.
