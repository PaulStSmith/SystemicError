Trump's 'increasing disconnect from reality' is back with a vengeance: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 20, 20254 min read

Donald Trump gestures as he speaks to the press. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

A long rambling story Donald Trump told about his uncle this week, none of which was true, has raised fresh alarms that his advanced age and the pressures of the job appear to have him trapped in a downward spiral.

Speaking at a technology summit in Pittsburgh this week, the president told that crowd that his uncle John, a former professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), taught Ted Kaczynski –– better known as the Unabomber.

That would have been difficult since, as MSNBC's Michael A. Cohen wrote, "John Trump passed away in 1985. Kaczynski committed bombings that killed three people and wounded nearly two dozen others from 1978 to 1995. He was finally arrested in 1996 and sentenced to life in prison in 1998. If both the president and his uncle knew that Kaczynski was the Unabomber more than a decade before his arrest ... now that would be a story!"

Combined with President Trump pinning blame on ex-President Joe Biden for the appointment of Jerome Powell to the Fed, when Trump was responsible, the MSNBC analyst expressed alarm that Trump is still repeating the debunked stories with gusto as he becomes even more detached from facts.

"Trump’s Unabomber tale is yet another example of the what can feel like an increasing disconnect from reality. Yet, as with so many of Trump’s recent and unsettling statements, too many in the mainstream media seem uninterested in covering the story, " Cohen wrote.

He then cautioned, "Right now, as we speak, the president of the United States is showing substantial, arguably greater public evidence of possible cognitive decline. Trump at times is unaware of what is happening inside his administration, can seem clueless about major policy events, and doesn’t always appear to understand the very legislation that he is promoting."

He added that press is ignoring what is right in front of their faces, after a steady diet of stories about ex-President Joe Biden's decline.

"One can certainly debate the extent to which Biden was truly experiencing cognitive decline. But if reporters are going to argue that the media dropped the ball in not giving that story greater coverage, then how does one explain not even talking about what is happening right now?" he asked.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tall Tales: A Symptom of a Larger Problem

In a recent spectacle that should surprise no one yet alarm everyone, Donald Trump has once again demonstrated an astonishing detachment from reality. Speaking at a technology summit in Pittsburgh, Trump spun a yarn about his uncle, John Trump, and an alleged connection to Ted Kaczynski, the Unabomber. This story, as pointed out by MSNBC's Michael A. Cohen, is not only false but implausible given the timelines of the individuals involved.

The Unbelievable Unabomber Anecdote

Trump claimed his uncle, a former professor at MIT, taught Kaczynski, a statement that crumbles under the slightest scrutiny. John Trump passed away in 1985, while Kaczynski's reign of terror spanned from 1978 to 1995, culminating in his arrest in 1996. The logistics alone render Trump's story a fictional tale. Yet, the readiness with which the former president fabricates and disseminates these falsehoods is more than a mere gaffe; it's a troubling indicator of his grasp on reality—or lack thereof.

Blaming Biden for His Own Appointments

Adding to the absurdity, Trump has attempted to pin the appointment of Jerome Powell to the Fed on former President Joe Biden, despite it being Trump's own decision. This glaring distortion of easily verifiable facts underscores a pattern of deflection and distortion that Trump has perfected over the years. However, the stakes are higher when such falsehoods come from someone who once held, and evidently seeks to reclaim, the highest office in the land.

The Media's Muted Response

Cohen's critique extends beyond Trump's fabrications to the media's tepid response to these episodes of apparent cognitive dissonance. The press, once voracious in its coverage of Biden's supposed cognitive decline, seems uninterested in holding Trump to the same standard. This discrepancy not only exposes a double standard in political coverage but also suggests a dangerous complacency in addressing what Cohen describes as substantial public evidence of possible cognitive decline in Trump.

The question Cohen poses is both rhetorical and critical: If the media failed to adequately cover Biden's cognitive health, how can its silence on Trump's more blatant disconnections from reality be justified? The answer, though complex, hints at a broader media landscape that is selectively attentive at best and negligently complicit at worst.

Conclusion: A Call for Consistent Vigilance

Trump's latest foray into fictional storytelling is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger issue that encompasses both the individual in question and the ecosystem that reports on him. The media's role in democracy is to inform the public and hold power to account, irrespective of political affiliation. When stories such as Trump's Unabomber tale are met with silence or indifference, it not only undermines the media's credibility but also emboldens further departures from truth.

As we navigate these turbulent political waters, the need for consistent vigilance and unbiased scrutiny from our media institutions has never been more critical. Whether it's Trump's fantastical narratives or any politician's detachment from reality, the standard of truth must be upheld uniformly. Anything less is a disservice to the public and a threat to the very fabric of our democracy.
