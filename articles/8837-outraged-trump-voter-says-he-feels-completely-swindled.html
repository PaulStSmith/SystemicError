'Outraged' Trump voter says he feels 'completely swindled'

A Pennsylvania Trump voter says he regrets his vote and that the president’s conduct in office is a “complete betrayal” of his vote, reports The Daily Beast.

Morgen Morgus, a self-described Libertarian, writes in a letter to the editor of USA Today that “A year after the president election, I feel completely swindled. If the presidential election were held today—with the same candidates—I would sit it out,” he went on. “There was a hope that they would be willing to listen to us, but unfortunately that is not happening.”

Morgus, The Daily Beast notes, “joins a growing number of disillusioned voters who have publicly expressed regret backing MAGA at last year’s polls.”

One of those voters is Betty Szretter, a “MAGA fan” who deeply regrets her vote as well because she relies on Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits to keep her daughter’s diabetes in check.

“It all seems very selfish,” she told NBC . “Now he’s busy out of the country and demolishing the White House.”

“I believe the Trump administration, instead of focusing on presidential ballrooms, should be paying attention to individual Americans’ dining rooms,” Szretter told CNN.

The Daily Beast also points to a series of focus groups composed of Latino voters, among whom Trump otherwise secured a near-historic 48 percent of the vote last year, who “voiced similar fury over both the president’s handling of the economy and his sweeping mass deportation efforts.”

In his USA Today letter, Morgus says he’s “outraged by the MAGA administration’s “One Big Beautiful Bill” spending measures, given he’d enthusiastically backed the preceding cost-cutting drive under its Department of Government Efficiency initiative.”

“What DOGE found in terms of cuts was great, but right after that came the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and continuing resolutions that undid all of their progress by leaps and bounds,” he writes, describing the measures as “one of the largest spending packages in recent U.S. history.”

Morgus says he will vote in the midterms, but not for Republicans (or Democrats).

“I feel that I need to do what I can to ensure liberty advances, which includes voting for Libertarian Party candidates or candidates who have libertarian values,” he writes.

Our Analysis:

Voter Regret: A Harsh Reality Check for the MAGA Movement

In a striking turn of events, a Pennsylvania voter, Morgen Morgus, has come forward to express his deep regret over casting his ballot for Trump, a sentiment echoed by other voters, including those who rely on essential programs like the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). This narrative, spotlighted by The Daily Beast, not only underlines the profound disillusionment brewing among former MAGA supporters but also serves as a stark reminder of the consequences of political choices.

The Betrayal of Trust

Morgus, a self-proclaimed Libertarian, initially saw Trump’s presidency as a beacon of hope for those advocating for smaller government and efficient governance. His disillusionment stems from what he describes as a “complete betrayal” of his vote, particularly criticizing the administration’s “One Big Beautiful Bill” spending measures. These measures, according to Morgus, contradicted the administration’s earlier cost-cutting initiatives, highlighting a significant deviation from the fiscal conservatism that many of Trump’s voters expected.

The irony here is palpable. Morgus and others like him were promised a government that would wield the budgetary scalpel with precision. Instead, they witnessed an administration that, in their view, opted for the sledgehammer approach, ramping up spending and erasing prior efficiencies. This isn’t just a policy disagreement; it’s a fundamental breach of the fiscal trust that voters like Morgus placed in the administration.

The Ripple Effect of Disillusionment

It’s not just Libertarians like Morgus feeling the sting of regret. Betty Szretter, a self-described “MAGA fan,” now grapples with the administration’s actions that seemingly undermine her family’s wellbeing. Her reliance on SNAP benefits to manage her daughter’s diabetes clashes with her political allegiance, exposing the harsh reality of policy impacts on individual lives. Szretter’s disillusionment is a poignant example of how political rhetoric often crashes against the hard shore of personal circumstance.

Moreover, the article highlights the disenchantment among Latino voters, a demographic that significantly supported Trump in the previous election. The administration’s economic policies and aggressive deportation efforts have not only failed to win their allegiance but have actively fueled their fury. This is a demographic that could have been a stronghold for the MAGA movement, now potentially turned adversary due to perceived policy failures.

The Libertarian Dilemma and Beyond

Morgus’s declaration that he will vote for Libertarian candidates or those with libertarian values in the midterms underscores a broader issue within American politics—the search for representation that aligns with individual liberty and fiscal responsibility. His stance reflects a growing trend of voters disillusioned with the binary choice between Democrats and Republicans, seeking alternatives that better represent their core values.

However, let’s not mince words here. The article lays bare a critical lesson: Political support is not a blank check. It’s a contract, grounded in trust and expectations. When that trust is violated, the fallout can be severe and long-lasting. Morgus’s story is a cautionary tale of what happens when political leaders fail to deliver on their promises, leaving behind a trail of voter regret and a constituency in search of realignment.

In conclusion, this narrative isn’t just about disillusionment with the Trump administration. It’s a broader commentary on the state of American politics, where voters find themselves increasingly caught between their hopes and the harsh reality of unmet promises. As we move forward, one thing is clear: Politicians would do well to remember that their actions have consequences, and voters, as demonstrated by Morgus and others, will not forget.
