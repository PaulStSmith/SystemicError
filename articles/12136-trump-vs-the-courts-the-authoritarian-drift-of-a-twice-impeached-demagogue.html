Trump vs. the Courts: The Authoritarian Drift of a Twice-Impeached Demagogue

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

From Whining to WarpathThe Authoritarian PlaybookA Direct Threat to the ConstitutionContempt for Rule of LawDangerously Close to Dismantling Judicial PowerConclusion: Heed the Warning

When Donald J. Trump launched his campaign in 2015, his rhetoric was brash, crude, and lawless. Many dismissed it as political theater. Few took seriously his early promises to "lock up" opponents, to enact sweeping executive actions without oversight, or to "take the oil" from sovereign nations. Nearly a decade later, those promises have metastasized into something far more dangerous: a calculated assault on the foundations of the American republic.

His latest target? The federal judiciary.

From Whining to Warpath

Donald Trump has long viewed the legal system not as a check on power but as an obstacle to be bulldozed. During his presidency, he openly berated judges who ruled against him—most infamously U.S. District Judge Gonzalo Curiel, whom he smeared as biased due to his Mexican heritage. When rulings didn’t go his way, he labeled them the work of “Obama judges,” prompting Chief Justice John Roberts to make a rare public rebuke, stating: "We do not have Obama judges or Trump judges, Bush judges or Clinton judges."

But this pattern has only escalated. According to reporting from The Ring of Fire and backed up by transcripts and tweets, Trump is now claiming that federal courts are threatening to “ruin” the country if they dare interfere with his economic policies—especially tariffs.

Let’s be precise: tariffs are taxes on imported goods. Trump’s implementation of sweeping tariffs during his first term led to retaliatory tariffs, disrupted global supply chains, and contributed to rising prices for American consumers and businesses alike. The Peterson Institute for International Economics estimated in 2020 that Trump’s tariffs amounted to a $57 billion annual tax increase on Americans. [1]

When courts begin reviewing these tariff policies—many of which were issued through dubious emergency declarations—Trump does what he always does: cries foul, undermines legitimacy, and escalates his narrative of persecution.

The Authoritarian Playbook

Trump’s disdain for judicial independence isn’t a quirk. It’s a core component of his political strategy, and it mirrors the trajectory of other authoritarian strongmen.

  • Hungary: Viktor Orbán systematically packed courts and stripped judicial independence under the guise of "anti-elitism."

  • Turkey: Recep Tayyip Erdoğan used failed coups and national emergencies to replace judges and purge dissent.

  • Brazil: Jair Bolsonaro, Trump’s ideological twin, repeatedly attacked the Supreme Federal Court when it ruled against his COVID-19 policies and anti-democratic efforts.

Trump is not inventing new tactics. He’s importing them.

A Direct Threat to the Constitution

The U.S. Constitution outlines three co-equal branches of government—executive, legislative, and judicial. Trump, however, treats this structure as negotiable. Every time a court checks his abuse of power—whether on immigration bans, environmental deregulation, or political targeting—he paints it not as judicial review, but as sabotage.

Take his attacks on Judge Tanya Chutkan, who is overseeing one of his Jan. 6-related cases. Trump has repeatedly accused her of bias, echoing threats that border on witness intimidation. Or consider his posts on Truth Social in which he claims that if judges don’t rule in his favor, America is doomed to collapse.

This is the language of dictators.

Contempt for Rule of Law

Let’s be clear: Trump's contempt for the judiciary isn't hypothetical. It's an ongoing campaign. Here are just a few highlights:

  • Travel Ban: When federal judges blocked his Muslim ban in 2017, Trump raged: "The opinion of this so-called judge...is ridiculous."

  • Election Lawsuits: After dozens of courts—many with Trump-appointed judges—rejected his baseless 2020 election fraud claims, Trump accused the courts of being "corrupt" and "afraid."

  • Gag Orders: In multiple criminal and civil trials, Trump has flouted gag orders, threatened prosecutors and judges, and defied court decorum.

These are not isolated outbursts. They are deliberate attempts to condition his base to believe that the judiciary is illegitimate unless it serves Trump’s personal interests.

Dangerously Close to Dismantling Judicial Power

So when Trump now muses—however implicitly—about abolishing or nullifying court rulings, we must take it seriously. In his own words:

“If the courts rule against me, it will be the ruination of America.”

That is not policy discourse. That is blackmail. It’s a declaration that he is above the law—and if he is not allowed to rule unimpeded, the nation must fall with him.

This rhetoric isn’t just dangerous; it’s criminally seditious in spirit. It implies that the function of the judiciary is subordinate to the whims of one man. It threatens every legal precedent that safeguards democracy, civil rights, and constitutional order.

Conclusion: Heed the Warning

Trump's campaign against the courts isn’t just about tariffs. It’s about immunity. It’s about insulating himself from consequences, whether they be financial fraud, election interference, or incitement to insurrection. His war on judges is the logical next step of a man who sees laws as inconveniences and power as divine right.

We must not normalize this. We must not shrug it off as more Trumpian bluster. Because the day a nation stops believing in the impartiality and legitimacy of its courts is the day it ceases to be a republic.

The courts are not perfect. But they are a hell of a lot better than rule by tantrum, threats, and Twitter.

Trump’s endgame is clear: a nation where only one man decides what’s legal. That’s not a democracy. That’s a dictatorship in drag.
