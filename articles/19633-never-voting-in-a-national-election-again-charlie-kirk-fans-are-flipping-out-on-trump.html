'Never voting in a national election again': Charlie Kirk fans are flipping out on Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 2, 20265 min read

Charlie Kirk (Photo by Cheney Orr for Reuters)

Donald Trump’s decision to launch an attack on Iran without congressional approval, and then follow up with a continuing bombardment of the country, is alienating an important portion of his base that helped him regain the Oval Office after his loss in 2020 to President Joe Biden.

The attack, after promises that he would not engage in military adventurism, led to a resurfacing of an old post by Charlie Kirk, where the late founder of TPUSA broke with the president after an attack on Iran last year.

“This is insane. Regime change will result in a bloody civil war, killing hundreds of thousands and creating another massive Muslim refugee crisis. Topping a leader is NEVER as easy you think. It almost always results in further involvement, a civil war, and chaos. Resist this!” he wrote, and now, with the president taking the country to war, some of Kirk’s fans are doing just that.

Posting on X, longtime friend and the producer of Kirk’s podcast, Blake Neff, added his voice to the chorus of conservatives who are breaking with Trump after the attack –– and added he is not the only one.

“Charlie was opposed to a regime-change war with Iran, as was I. Wars by their nature are expensive and unpredictable. They endanger American lives and can last far, far longer than anyone anticipates. Nevertheless, President Trump has elected for regime change in Iran,” he began.

“As an American patriot I must hope for the best. Trump’s instinct is to avoid prolonged fighting and boots on the ground. We must simply trust that he has a strategy that will prevent both. Ideally, this will be our last MidEast War: Toppling the Islamic Republic would remove the last large hostile power in the region and let America finally disengage and focus its efforts elsewhere. If the President can deliver that, this will be a success for America,” he added hopefully.

Adding a cautionary note, he warned the president, “... he must be prepared to make that case forcefully. Trump/Vance ran on a peace platform, and it was popular. Right now some of my right-leaning friends are messaging me: ‘F*** this.’ ‘This is extremely depressing.’ ‘Never voting in a national election again’.”

“If this war is a swift, easy, and decisive victory, most of them will get over it. But if the war is anything else, there will be a lot of anger. The American people were not given a strong explanation of why this was necessary. But success can override bad explanations. So we must pray for success,” he concluded.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Iranian Gambit: A Masterclass in Political Betrayal

In what can only be described as a stunning departure from a platform that promised to eschew the hawkish foreign policy tendencies of his predecessors, Donald Trump’s unilateral decision to launch an attack on Iran, and the subsequent sustained bombardment, represents a breathtaking betrayal of his own base. The irony is palpable: the very constituency that propelled Trump back into the Oval Office, after his 2020 defeat to Joe Biden, is now reeling from the shock of his latest military adventurism.

The Echoes of Broken Promises

Trump’s actions resuscitate the ghost of a past critique from an unexpected quarter: Charlie Kirk, the late founder of TPUSA, whose posthumous voice now serves as an eerie premonition to the chaos unfolding. Kirk’s admonition against regime change in Iran — predicting a bloody civil war, a refugee crisis, and unending chaos — stands as a testament to the foresight ignored by the Trump administration. This is insane. The simplicity and clarity of Kirk’s warning underscore the recklessness of Trump’s decision. It’s as if the lessons of past foreign entanglements, which have cost the United States dearly in blood, treasure, and international standing, have been utterly disregarded.

The Conservative Conundrum

Blake Neff’s addition to the chorus of conservative dissent is particularly noteworthy. As a longtime friend of Kirk and a producer of his podcast, Neff embodies the internal conflict plaguing many conservatives who find themselves at odds with Trump’s warpath. His reluctant hope that Trump possesses a strategy to avoid prolonged conflict and “boots on the ground” is tinged with desperation. But hope is not a strategy. The notion that this could be “our last MidEast War” and that toppling the Islamic Republic would allow the United States to “finally disengage” is a fantasy, belied by decades of American foreign policy misadventures.

A Peace Platform Abandoned

The crux of the betrayal lies in the abandonment of a peace platform that was not only popular but also a significant factor in Trump/Vance’s electoral success. Neff’s recounting of right-leaning friends’ disillusionment — from expletive-laden messages to vows of abstaining from national elections — underscores the depth of the political miscalculation at play. The American people, it seems, were sold a bill of goods on Trump’s non-interventionist promises, only to be served a full course of military aggression without a coherent justification.

The Precarious Gamble on Success

Neff’s conclusion — a hope for success to justify the means — is a precarious gamble. It hinges on the war being “swift, easy, and decisive,” a trifecta that has eluded the United States in virtually every military engagement since World War II. The absence of a “strong explanation” for the war’s necessity, as Neff notes, is a glaring omission that cannot be papered over by even the most favorable outcomes. The American public’s tolerance for foreign entanglements is at an all-time low, and the threshold for what constitutes “success” in such ventures has shifted dramatically.

In Conclusion: A Systemic Error

Trump’s decision to attack Iran without congressional approval and the ensuing fallout among his base is not merely a political misstep; it’s a systemic error that reflects a profound misunderstanding of the American electorate’s appetite for war. It’s a miscalculation that could have lasting repercussions, not just for Trump’s political future, but for the conservative movement as a whole. The alienation of a significant portion of his base over a gamble in Iran may well be remembered as a pivotal moment when the promise of a non-interventionist presidency was irrevocably broken.
