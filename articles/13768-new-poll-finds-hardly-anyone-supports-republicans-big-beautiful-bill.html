New poll finds hardly anyone supports Republicans' 'big, beautiful bill'

A new Quinnipiac Poll shows that President Donald Trump and his 2026 budget, which he refers to as the "big, beautiful bill," are underwater with the American public.

Huffington Post senior politics editor Kevin Robillard posted screen captures of the crosstabs on X, showing that just 27% of Americans support the bill. He also noted that polling specifically looking at attitudes on the GOP tax bill has "been relatively rare."

Democrats are also "far more likely to have an opinion on the bill than Republicans and Independents," Robillard added.

"Among independents, it's 37 points underwater," said NBC News' Sahil Kapur.

Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) noted that the poll also found only 10% of Americans support the cuts to Medicaid included in the budget bill passed by the House.

"Last month, nearly every House Republican voted for the largest Medicaid cut in American history," Nadler wrote on X. "Today, they have a choice when they vote for technical changes to reconciliation: Double down on stripping health care from millions, or finally listen to the overwhelming majority of Americans and vote no on $800 billion in devastating Medicaid cuts."

"The poll also has a very bad approval rating for Trump, with 38% approval and 54% disapproval, so I would expect it to also be more negative on the tax bill than some other surveys might be," he also cited.

That is a dramatic drop for Trump. It also shows that he's falling behind in the economy, once his best issue. Polling from Marquette University Law School three weeks ago showed Trump slipping by 16% in his handling of the economy, with just 42% approving of his handling and 58% against.

In the Quinnipiac Poll released Wednesday, 51% of Americans believe that the economy is "worse" under Trump. Only 28% think it's improved.

Pennsylvania Capital-Star correspondent Nick Field noticed that Americans also want to continue granting birthright citizenship, with 64% in support and 31% opposed.

Data journalist G. Elliott Morris cautioned it's "possible this isn’t the dominant issue for Trump that some people have assumed! Trump at -11 net approval on immigration and -16 on deportations in new Quinnipiac poll."

Our Analysis:

The Dismal Depths of Trump's Budget Approval and the Undercurrent of Public Opinion

In a recent Quinnipiac Poll, the approval ratings for President Donald Trump and his 2026 budget, which he has affectionately dubbed the "big, beautiful bill," are anything but beautiful in the eyes of the American public. Only 27% of Americans support the bill, a figure so low it practically requires a submarine to find it. This data, brought to light by Huffington Post senior politics editor Kevin Robillard, who shared the crosstabs on X, paints a grim picture not just for the bill itself but for the broader GOP fiscal agenda.

The Rarity of Opposition and the GOP's Tax Bill Mirage

It's fascinating, yet unsurprising, that polling specifically looking at attitudes on the GOP tax bill has been "relatively rare." This scarcity of data perhaps hints at a strategic oversight or, more cynically, an avoidance tactic by supporters of the bill. Despite the polling paucity, Democrats are "far more likely to have an opinion on the bill than Republicans and Independents," according to Robillard. This discrepancy in engagement levels underscores a stark partisan divide, with Democrats seemingly more attuned to the ramifications of the bill.

Independents and the Sinking Ship

NBC News' Sahil Kapur highlighted that among independents, the bill is 37 points underwater. This staggering figure should serve as a distress flare to the GOP, indicating that the bill is not just unpalatable to a large segment of the American electorate but is actively alienating the crucial middle ground of the political spectrum.

The Medicaid Maelstrom

Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) illuminated another dark corner of this legislative debacle: the cuts to Medicaid. Only 10% of Americans support these cuts included in the budget bill passed by the House. Nadler's statement, "Last month, nearly every House Republican voted for the largest Medicaid cut in American history," is a chilling reminder of the party's priorities. The choice House Republicans face, as Nadler puts it, is between doubling down on stripping healthcare from millions or listening to the "overwhelming majority of Americans" and rejecting the $800 billion Medicaid cuts.

Trump's Tumbling Approval Ratings

The poll also revealed a dire approval rating for Trump, with 38% approval and 54% disapproval. This is a significant decline and signals a broader dissatisfaction with Trump's presidency, particularly his handling of the economy. Once considered his stronghold, the economy is now a weak link, with a Marquette University Law School poll showing a 16% slip in his handling of the economy. Furthermore, 51% of Americans believe the economy is "worse" under Trump, with only 28% seeing improvement.

Birthright Citizenship and Immigration: The Unmoved Mountains

Interestingly, despite Trump's hardline stance on immigration and birthright citizenship, 64% of Americans support continuing granting birthright citizenship. This shows a clear disjuncture between Trump's policies and public opinion. Trump's net approval on immigration and deportations is also in the negatives, further indicating that his policies may not be the political panacea he assumes them to be.

Conclusion

The Quinnipiac Poll serves as a stark reminder of the chasm between the Trump administration's policies and the will of the American people. The "big, beautiful bill" is anything but, in the public's eyes, and Trump's approval ratings are a testament to a presidency increasingly out of touch with the electorate's desires and needs. This poll should not just be a wake-up call but an alarm bell for the GOP, signaling the urgent need for reevaluation and recalibration of their legislative priorities.
