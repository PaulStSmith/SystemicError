“Remember, as you deport people, that’s gonna suppress private job numbers of small businesses,” he added. “But they’ll rebalance and they’ll regrow, so I think this is just a near-term event, and you’ll see as the numbers come through over the next couple of months, you’ll see that all pass.”

Reacting to Lutnick’s claims, a report from the New Republic dismissed them as “damage control” and cited numerous other factors to back up the claim that tariffs are harming the U.S. economy in ways Trump said they would not.

“Domestic manufacturing, as measured by the Institute for Supply Management Manufacturing index, fell for the ninth month in a row, showing that the tariffs are hurting an area that Trump boasts they will improve,” the piece explained. “And private companies, including wholesale retailer Costco, are suing the government to get a refund of the tariffs they’ve paid.

The report from ADP found that, for the month of November, private sector businesses employing at most 50 people saw job losses of nearly 32,000. This was notably off from the initial prediction that these businesses would add 10,000 new jobs. Small businesses, those that employ at most 50 people, were hit the hardest, losing 120,000 jobs. Medium-sized businesses, meanwhile, added 51,000 jobs, and largest businesses added 39,000, all of which was still not enough to create net positive job growth.

Reporting on these new numbers, CNBC host Steve Liesman said that Trump’s tariffs may be to blame for some of the pain felt by small businesses.

“That’s the fourth negative number in the past six months,” Liesman said. “The estimate was for plus 40,000, so the street was off on this one... Small business getting hammered and there is some information that some of this may be coming from being hammered by the tariffs.”

During an appearance on CNBC later in the day, Trump’s Commerce Secretary denied the theory that tariffs were to blame, instead putting the onus on the administration’s preferred target: Democrats.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump’s Economic Advisors Blame Everything but Tariffs

In a recent spectacle of political theater, one of President Donald Trump’s top economic advisors has embarked on a frantic quest to deflect blame from the administration’s tariffs policy, following a new jobs report that paints a grim picture for small businesses. This advisor, in a desperate bid to sidestep responsibility, pointed fingers at everything but the elephant in the room: the tariffs. The New Republic, however, was quick to call out this maneuver as nothing more than “damage control.”

The Blame Game: Shutdowns and Deportations

Lutnik, in a statement to CNBC, attempted to shift the blame towards the Democratic shutdown and the consequences of deporting people, suggesting these factors were responsible for the downturn in small business job numbers. His argument that these are temporary setbacks and that a rebound is on the horizon is a classic example of political spin. It’s a convenient narrative, one that ignores the broader, more systemic issues at play. Remember, it’s always easier to point fingers elsewhere than to admit a policy might not be working as intended.

The Real Culprit: Tariffs

Despite the administration’s attempts to muddy the waters, the New Republic cuts through the noise, highlighting how tariffs are inflicting damage on the U.S. economy—a reality President Trump assured would not happen. The decline in domestic manufacturing for the ninth consecutive month, as indicated by the Institute for Supply Management Manufacturing index, stands as a testament to the detrimental impact of tariffs. Furthermore, private companies, including giants like Costco, are taking legal action against the government to reclaim tariffs paid, underscoring the widespread discontent and financial strain caused by these policies.

The Stark Reality: Job Losses

The report from ADP delivers a sobering blow, revealing that private sector businesses, particularly small businesses with a workforce of at most 50 people, suffered nearly 32,000 job losses in November alone. This figure starkly contrasts the initial optimistic projection of 10,000 new jobs. Small businesses, the backbone of the American economy, bore the brunt of this downturn, losing 120,000 jobs. While medium and large-sized businesses did see job additions, the net effect remained a negative job growth. CNBC host Steve Liesman’s commentary that Trump’s tariffs might be a significant factor in the struggles faced by small businesses further reinforces the damaging consequences of these policies.

Deflection, Not Reflection

In an almost expected turn of events, Trump’s Commerce Secretary later appeared on CNBC, denying the notion that tariffs were at fault and instead, predictably, laying the blame on Democrats. This deflection strategy, favored by the administration, does little to address the underlying issues or provide solace to the thousands of small businesses reeling under the pressure of tariffs.

Conclusion

The administration’s efforts to deflect blame and avoid accountability for the adverse effects of tariffs on small businesses reveal a troubling disconnect from reality. As the data and expert analysis suggest, tariffs are not the boon to the economy President Trump claimed they would be. Instead, they’ve become a significant burden, particularly for small businesses that are now facing the brunt of these policies. The political spin, aimed at shifting blame, cannot and should not obscure the tangible damage being inflicted upon the American economy. It’s high time for a reevaluation of these tariffs, with a focus on policies that genuinely support and nurture the growth of small businesses, rather than hinder them.
