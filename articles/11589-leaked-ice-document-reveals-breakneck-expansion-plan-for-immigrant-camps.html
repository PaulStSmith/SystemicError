Leaked ICE document reveals 'breakneck expansion' plan for immigrant camps

Donald Trump visits “Alligator Alcatraz” in Ochopee, Florida. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

According to documents obtained by the Washington Post, Donald Trump’s administration has plans in hand to spend billions on multiple new internment camps that will be capable of holding more than 100,000 immigrants.

Those new Department of Homeland Security facilities will also include ones at which ICE will detain entire families until they can be shipped out of the U.S.

According to the Post, concentration camps currently in use can accommodate more than 50,00O detainees and DHS plans on “opening or expanding 125 facilities this year. By January, ICE will have the capacity to hold more than 107,000 people, internal agency documents show.”

The Post reported, “The road map, last updated July 30, shows that ICE intends to expand immigrant detention to new parts of the country, nearly doubling its number of large-scale, mega-detention centers and relying increasingly on makeshift ‘soft-sided’ structures that can be built in a few weeks and taken down just as easily. The government is also planning to dramatically expand its capacity for detaining parents and children in what could amount to the nation’s largest family detention program in decades.”

RELATED: 'Lies!' DHS melts down over explosive report of sex abuse and miscarriages at its centers

Responding to questions from the Post, DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin confirmed via email that “the planning document was created by ICE but said the list was outdated and had not been approved. She said the listed contracts ‘are not accurate’ but declined to elaborate.”

The documents reveal that Texas will be the biggest recipient of contracts for new facilities, with the top four, in terms of capacity, capable of holding a combined 12,000 detainees.

The WaPo report added, “Geo Group, ICE’s largest contractor and a company with close ties to the Trump administration, is in line to receive at least nine new or modified detention contracts with a total estimated value of over $500 million a year, the documents show. When combined with four new contracts that ICE had already awarded Geo this year, the company is on track to double its total annual revenue from ICE detention.”

Our Analysis:

Trump Administration's Blueprint for Mass Detention: A Closer Look

The recent revelations by the Washington Post about the Trump administration's grandiose plans to massively expand the internment of immigrants are not just disturbing; they are a glaring testament to a deeply flawed immigration system that prioritizes incarceration over humanity. According to documents obtained by the Post, there is a blueprint in hand that envisages spending billions on creating internment camps with the capacity to hold more than 100,000 immigrants. This is a move that not only raises serious human rights concerns but also questions the moral compass guiding U.S. immigration policy.

The Expansion of a Controversial System

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) seemingly intends to more than double its capacity to detain immigrants, with plans to "open or expand 125 facilities this year." This ambitious expansion would increase the number of detainees to more than 107,000 people. The strategy includes the establishment of "mega-detention centers" and an increased reliance on "soft-sided" structures, which can be rapidly deployed and dismantled. It's a chilling indication of a system gearing up for operation on an industrial scale, treating human beings as mere commodities to be processed.

The Advent of Family Detention

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of this plan is the significant expansion of family detention capacities, potentially marking the "nation's largest family detention program in decades." This policy direction not only subjects adults to incarceration but also ensnares innocent children in a traumatic and damaging environment. The psychological and emotional impact on detained families is profound and well-documented, casting a long shadow over the future well-being of thousands.

Financial Windfalls and Ethical Quandaries

It's impossible to ignore the financial undertones driving these expansion efforts. The documents highlight Geo Group, ICE's largest contractor, poised to secure contracts worth over $500 million a year. This financial windfall for a company with close ties to the Trump administration underscores a disturbing nexus between political agendas and corporate profits. At what point does the pursuit of security become indistinguishable from the pursuit of profit?

DHS's Murky Response

The response from DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin, confirming the document's authenticity but dismissing it as "outdated" and "not accurate," does little to dispel concerns. The lack of transparency and clarity only adds to the suspicion that there's more to this plan than meets the eye. When confronted with plans of such significant ethical and human rights implications, vague responses are inadequate.

Conclusion: A System in Dire Need of Reform

This report is a wake-up call for those who value human rights and dignity. The Trump administration's plans to expand the internment of immigrants on such a massive scale is a policy marred by moral bankruptcy and a blatant disregard for the well-being of vulnerable populations. It's a stark reminder of the urgent need for comprehensive immigration reform that puts humanity at the forefront, rather than treating it as an afterthought.

The expansion of detention facilities, particularly those that detain families, is not just an immigration policy issue—it's a matter of basic human decency. As the U.S. continues to grapple with the complexities of immigration, it must strive for solutions that reflect the country’s core values of freedom, justice, and respect for human rights. Anything less is a systemic error that history will not kindly forgive.
