'Looks bored': Onlookers crack up as Trump appears to be asleep at event for dead soldiers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 26, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The President Who Can’t Stay Awake, and the Machine That Explains Him Away

The Scene

At a Memorial Day ceremony honoring fallen soldiers, Donald Trump was caught on video with his eyes closed while Pete Hegseth spoke. The clip spread fast, and so did the familiar split reaction: mockery from critics, and a scramble from supporters to insist he was merely squinting in the sun.

Power Is Not The Argument

The point is not whether Trump blinked, dozed, or squinted. The point is that the holder of the most powerful office in the country was visibly disengaged during a public ritual of state memory, and his allies immediately tried to turn that into a harmless optical trick. That is not clarification. It is damage control for a man whose behavior routinely forces the public to argue with its own eyes.

The Usual Cover Story

Trump has previously denied falling asleep at Cabinet meetings and Oval Office appearances, claiming he closes his eyes while listening. That explanation matters because it is exactly how this political machine works: an obvious display of contempt, fatigue, or indifference gets rebranded as something dignified, ambient, or even patriotic. The defense is not meant to convince the skeptical. It is meant to give loyalists a script.

Who Benefits From The Blur

The weaker actors in this story are the people left to litigate whether the president was napping in public. The stronger actor is Trump, whose supporters and media defenders keep shifting attention away from conduct and toward interpretation. That is misdirection by design. It turns an accountability question into a trivial argument over posture, weather, and facial muscle tone.

Memorial As Stagecraft

A ceremony for the war dead is supposed to demand restraint and attention. Instead, it became another setting in which Trump’s detachment was absorbed into the permanent spectacle around him. The event mattered less as commemoration than as another opportunity for his movement to demonstrate that no setting is serious enough to interrupt the performance of impunity.

The Larger Pattern

This is the governing style in miniature: public disrespect, then instant rationalization; visible dysfunction, then loyalist narration; institutional power held by a man treated by his defenders as if he were a weather system rather than a decision-maker. The deeper scandal is not that Trump looked bored. It is that a political class has spent years building a culture where his obvious contempt can be laundered into innocence, one excuse at a time.
