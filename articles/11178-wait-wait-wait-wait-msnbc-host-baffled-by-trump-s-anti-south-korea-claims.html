'Wait, wait, wait, wait': MSNBC host baffled by Trump's anti-South Korea claims

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Topsy-Turvy Diplomacy: A Critical Analysis

U.S. President Donald Trump meets with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung at the Oval Office, at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 25, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Comments made by Donald Trump before his meeting with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung in the Oval Office both stunned and baffled a panel on MSNBC on Tuesday morning

The day after the meeting, MSNBC’s Jonathan Lemire summarized for his co-host on “Morning Joe” the criticisms the president has made about the lack of freedom –– but they were not aimed at North Korea’s Kim Jong-un, but instead at his guest.

The led co-host Joe Scarborough to interrupt after he had asked why Trump is so enamored of the North Korean strongman, who the host called “the world’s worst dictator.”

“This shows you just how topsy-turvy the Trump foreign policy is,” Lemire began. “He spent the last couple of days, including yesterday morning before the meeting really bashing South Korea, suggesting that they were sort of that, that there was undue political and religious persecution there, suggesting that––.”

“Wait, wait, wait, wait, wait ,wait, wait,” host Scarborough interrupted. “In Korea?”

“In South Korea,” Lemire responded.

“You get fed to the dogs just for the hell of it just because they don’t like you?” Scarborough asked incredulously.

“Yes. Where there are mass executions. of political foes and the like,” Lemire said of Trump's claims.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Topsy-Turvy Diplomacy: A Critical Analysis

In an era where political discourse often seems disconnected from the tangible realities of governance, former President Donald Trump's comments prior to his meeting with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung stand out as a glaring illustration of this phenomenon. The bewildering critique aimed not at the notorious North Korean regime under Kim Jong-un, but rather at South Korea, a longstanding ally of the United States, is as perplexing as it is concerning.

Trump's Misdirected Critique

Trump's assertions of "political and religious persecution" in South Korea are not only unfounded but are a stark misrepresentation of the democratic values that South Korea upholds. To juxtapose South Korea with the draconian and oppressive regime of North Korea is not only a gross mischaracterization but demonstrates a profound misunderstanding of the geopolitical dynamics at play. It is a critical error to equate the democratic governance of South Korea, with its vibrant civil society and robust protections for freedom of expression, with the totalitarian grip of Kim Jong-un over North Korea.

The Enigma of Trump's Admiration for Authoritarian Figures

The MSNBC panel's astonishment is emblematic of the broader confusion and concern that have characterized much of Trump's foreign policy positions. His peculiar affinity for authoritarian leaders, with Kim Jong-un being a prime example, raises serious questions about his understanding and commitment to democratic principles and international norms. Calling Kim Jong-un, a leader who has presided over egregious human rights abuses, "the world's worst dictator," while simultaneously attacking South Korea, a beacon of democracy in the region, is emblematic of a foreign policy that is not only "topsy-turvy" but deeply troubling.

The Consequences of Misguided Statements

Trump's comments have ramifications that extend beyond the confines of a news segment. They risk undermining the United States' relationship with South Korea, a key strategic partner in a region fraught with security challenges. Furthermore, they embolden authoritarian figures like Kim Jong-un by seemingly legitimizing their oppressive regimes at the expense of democratic allies. This approach to diplomacy is not only counterproductive but also dangerous, as it sends mixed signals about the United States' commitment to human rights and democratic governance.

Conclusion

In summary, Trump's remarks before his meeting with President Lee Jae Myung are symptomatic of a broader disregard for the principles that should guide the conduct of international relations. The confusion and consternation they have sparked are a testament to their incongruity with the realities of the geopolitical landscape. As we navigate an increasingly complex world, it is imperative that the United States champion democratic values and stand firmly with its allies. Misguided critiques and misplaced admiration for authoritarian leaders do nothing but undermine these imperatives, to the detriment of global stability and the principles of freedom and democracy.
