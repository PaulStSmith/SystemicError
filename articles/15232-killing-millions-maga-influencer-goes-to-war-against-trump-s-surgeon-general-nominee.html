'Killing millions!' MAGA influencer goes to war against Trump's surgeon general nominee

Loomer, a 9/11 Truther and self-described "Proud Islamophobe" who ran multiple failed bids for Congress in Florida and has frequently gained the president's ear, has taken to X to repeatedly attack Dr. Janette Nesheiwat, the sister-in-law of Trump's recently-dismissed National Security Adviser Mike Waltz.

Part of the controversy stems from the fact that Nesheiwat appears to have falsified parts of her credentials, with a new report revealing that she was never certified in emergency medicine as she claimed, and that she earned her degree from the American University of the Caribbean rather than American University in Washington, D.C. Loomer's greater objection, however, appears to be her role in treating COVID patients in New York and promoting vaccination during the pandemic.

"In a screenshot below, @DoctorJanette said 'Vaccine hesitancy is a Global health threat,'" Loomer wrote on X. "She used her access to Fox News to promote the dangerous Covid vaccine, which is now killing millions of people. She tried to shame people who didn’t take the vaccine by calling them global health threats. Vaccines are a matter of PERSONAL HEALTH FREEDOM. 'Vaccine hesitancy' is a matter of PERSONAL FREEDOM AND LIBERTY! It is not a Global Health Threat. MY BODY MY CHOICE!"

Loomer went on to falsely claim that vaccines are linked to autism, and lamented, "I genuinely wish we had a different nominee that was more aligned with personal liberties."

On Monday, Loomer followed up with a post hopeful that she might have managed to sink the nomination.

"Interesting. As of this morning, @DoctorJanette Dr. Janette Nesheiwat no longer has 'nominee for US Surgeon General' in her X bio," wrote Loomer. "It was there before. Perhaps she’s out! Her Senate confirmation hearing was supposed to take place this Thursday."Thanks to Raw Story

Our Analysis:

The Unhinged Fury of Laura Loomer: A Dive into MAGA Meltdown

In what reads like a script for the latest political drama, the nomination of Dr. Janette Nesheiwat as surgeon general has sparked a firestorm within MAGA circles, particularly igniting the wrath of conspiracy theorist and perennial candidate Laura Loomer. The depths of Loomer's fury, rooted in a blend of misinformation and personal vendetta, exemplifies the chaotic nature of fringe political discourse today.

The Controversy Unpacked

At the heart of the controversy is a mix of alleged professional misrepresentation and ideological clash. Reports suggest that Nesheiwat might have embellished her credentials, a serious charge that certainly warrants scrutiny. The distinction between the American University of the Caribbean and American University in Washington, D.C., while seemingly minor, speaks volumes about transparency and honesty in public service. This aspect of the controversy deserves a thorough investigation, as the integrity of public officials is non-negotiable.

However, the waters are muddied by Loomer's bizarre and unfounded attacks on Nesheiwat's efforts to combat COVID-19. Loomer's tirade against vaccines, dubbing them as "dangerous" and falsely linking them to autism, is not just scientifically baseless; it's dangerously irresponsible. Her rhetoric, soaked in the language of "personal health freedom," ignores the communal nature of pandemic response and vaccine efficacy. It's a stark reminder of how anti-vaccine narratives can infiltrate the highest levels of political discourse, potentially undermining public health efforts.

MAGA's Internal Strife

Loomer's clash with Trump over his surgeon general pick is a fascinating subplot in the MAGA saga, revealing cracks within the movement. Once a staunch ally of Trump, Loomer's dissent underscores the volatile nature of political alliances, especially when they are built on the shaky ground of conspiracy theories and extreme ideology. The notion that Loomer, a self-described "Proud Islamophobe," is concerned about "personal liberties" is a paradox that would be laughable if it weren't so troubling.

Misinformation as a Political Tool

Loomer's use of misinformation, particularly her false claims about vaccines and autism, is a dangerous game. It's a vivid illustration of how misinformation can be weaponized in political battles, regardless of the public health consequences. This tactic, while effective in rallying a base, erodes trust in public health institutions and experts, potentially endangering lives.

The Irony of "My Body, My Choice"

The invocation of "MY BODY MY CHOICE" by Loomer, in defense of vaccine refusal, is an ironic twist. This phrase, historically associated with the pro-choice movement, is co-opted here in a context that fundamentally misunderstands the concept of public health. Vaccines, unlike the personal decision of abortion, have widespread implications for community health and safety. The appropriation of this phrase reveals a deep misunderstanding of both public health and the original context of bodily autonomy.

Conclusion

The saga of Nesheiwat's nomination is more than a political skirmish; it's a microcosm of the larger battles raging within the United States over truth, expertise, and the common good. While the allegations of credential falsification are serious and demand attention, the ideological war waged by Loomer is a destructive sideshow. It's a stark reminder of the dangerous power of misinformation and the fragility of political allegiances in a movement fueled by conspiracy theories and extremism.
