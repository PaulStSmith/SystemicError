Trump faulted for giving GOP nothing to run on: 'Still looking for that agenda'

President Donald Trump did not provide Republicans with a message to rally around, according to an analyst on “CNN This Morning.”

The 79-year-old president spoke Tuesday for a record one hour and 47 minutes, where he boasted of an economic “turnaround for the ages” and claimed to be making deals to benefit all Americans, but CNN’s Edward-Isaac Dovere said he did not lay out much of an agenda for GOP candidates.

“I think that the question here for the Republicans in the audience is what the president gave them to run on this year, in this midterm year,” Dovere said. “It was a speech that was very much about him and his accomplishments and what he’s done, and when you have Democrats who are both attacking what he’s done and trying to say that the Republicans in Congress are just a rubber stamp on him, they are still looking for what that agenda would be, to say this is what we’re going to keep doing or what we’re going to do now, rather than what we’re going.”

Trump lambasted Democrats for opposing his mass deportation campaign but conspicuously did not address the controversial specifics of that agenda, including the Immigration and Customs Enforcement occupations of Democratic-led states and cities, host Audie Cornish pointed out.

“He did not address ICE, did not address DHS, did not address these things that are actually like lots of people, like, hey, give me an answer on this,” she said. “Like, where do you stand on this conceptually? Do you think it’s something that is going to, again, Republicans on the trail are going to have to answer for?”

Republican strategist Bryan Lanza, who was a senior adviser to Trump’s 2024 campaign, said those issues were just a matter of framing, but Cornish challenged him to explain how GOP candidates would do that.

“That’s why I’m asking what’s the framing?” Cornish said. “I think the framing so far is we’ve done a lot to help with the border, and they’ve done nothing. But he’s not talking about what happened on the way to accomplishing that.”

Lanza argued that voters didn’t care about the process.

“Well, I don’t think he has to,” he said. “I mean, at the end of the day the voters are going to look at the end result voters. What we’ve learned in politics, at least over my 20 years, is voters don’t care about process, they care about results, and so the process that you’re seeing take place is great for TV clicks and great for for newspaper articles. But voters ultimately decide was he was he successful.”

Dovere said the polling so far shows that voters do not believe Trump’s second presidency has been successful.

“At least so far, that’s not what we’re seeing from the reactions and the polls and other things over the last couple of months where people are saying, that a majority of them saying that they agree that there is a problem with immigration, the border, they they are still support for president trump doing things about it,” Dovere said. “But they have consistently said what happened in Minneapolis, not like this, essentially, right, and the president didn’t give it like he was saying. There was no acknowledgment, no acknowledgment, there was nothing to address that, to say, okay this is how that happened, and this is what’s going to happen differently going forward.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Trump’s Rhetoric and the GOP’s Directionless Drift

In an era where political narratives are as carefully curated as social media feeds, President Donald Trump’s latest oration, as dissected by a CNN analyst on “CNN This Morning,” epitomizes a recurring theme in his leadership style: all spectacle, scant substance. The 79-year-old’s marathon speech of one hour and 47 minutes was, according to Edward-Isaac Dovere, a self-congratulatory monologue devoid of a coherent agenda for the Republican party. This analysis isn’t just an isolated opinion but a glaring spotlight on the strategic void at the heart of the GOP’s midterm game plan.

Trump’s Egocentric Rallying Cry

Trump’s speech, as described, was a laundry list of self-proclaimed achievements, particularly an economic “turnaround for the ages.” However, the critical failure lies not in trumpeting these accomplishments but in the utter lack of a forward-looking agenda for the GOP. In a political landscape where the opposition is keenly dissecting Trump’s policies, the absence of a tangible, future-oriented plan leaves Republican candidates adrift, lacking a solid platform to campaign on. This scenario is particularly perilous in a midterm year, where clarity and vision could be the difference between victory and defeat.

The Elephant in the Room: Immigration

Trump’s evasion of the immigration issue, particularly his administration’s controversial policies and the role of ICE, is telling. Audie Cornish’s observation underscores a significant dilemma for the GOP: How do they frame and defend policies that are widely criticized and seen as morally and ethically contentious? Republican strategist Bryan Lanza’s assertion that “voters don’t care about the process, they care about results” is a simplistic reduction of voter intelligence and concerns. It’s an evasion of accountability that fails to recognize the complexity of voter sentiment, especially in light of polling data suggesting dissatisfaction with Trump’s handling of immigration and other policies.

Polls and Public Sentiment: A Reality Check

Dovere’s reference to polling data is a stark reminder that despite the bluster, the public is not wholly on board with Trump’s vision or execution of policies. This disconnect between Trump’s narrative of success and the electorate’s perception is a critical weakness for the GOP. It highlights a fundamental misalignment between the party’s messaging and the concerns of the electorate.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The article paints a picture of a party caught in the shadow of one man’s ego, struggling to articulate a cohesive and compelling vision for the future. Trump’s speech, while certainly a display of confidence, is emblematic of a broader issue within the GOP: a directionless drift. As Republicans look towards the midterms, the challenge will not just be to distance themselves from controversial policies but to present a unified, positive agenda that resonates with a broad spectrum of voters. In its current state, the party seems to be floundering in the wake of Trump’s larger-than-life persona, with no clear strategy for navigating the political landscape of 2024 and beyond. The GOP’s success hinges not on reliving past glories but on articulating a vision that addresses the present and future needs of the American people. Until then, they remain at a strategic impasse, with Trump’s rhetoric serving more as a millstone than a milestone.
