Newsmax host visibly cringes as Boebert gets smacked around in debate

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosNovember 1, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedThe incumbent holds the powerAccusation replaced accountabilityNewsmax supplied the escape routeThe spectacle conceals the decision

What happened

AlterNet reports that Democratic challenger Eileen Laubacher confronted Republican Representative Lauren Boebert during a recent debate over the reported Iran war, arguing that Boebert should vote against it if she believed war was never justified. Laubacher, a retired Navy rear admiral, delivered the remark after Boebert interrupted her and accused her of previously agreeing that war was good for business.

Newsmax host Greg Kelly later played the exchange while interviewing Boebert. His tightened expression and hesitant response are the basis for AlterNet’s claim that he “cringed,” though he did not say Boebert had performed badly. When invited to explain Laubacher, Boebert called her a “Deep State candidate” and alleged that she held millions of dollars in defense stocks, owned multiple homes, and had misrepresented her views on war. The article provides no independent substantiation or response from Laubacher to those claims.

Kelly then suggested that some veterans had been “weaponized by the deep state” and were using military service “as a weapon.” He ended the interview shortly afterward by wishing Boebert luck.

The incumbent holds the power

The consequential fact is not that Boebert endured an awkward television segment. It is that she is a sitting member of Congress confronting a challenger who asked how she would use the power she already possesses.

Laubacher’s argument was direct: if Boebert opposes the Iran war under discussion, she should vote against it. Boebert did not answer that challenge in the exchange AlterNet describes. She redirected the debate toward disputed remarks by her opponent and, later, toward her wealth, investments, property and supposed association with the “deep state.”

That distinction matters. Laubacher can criticize military policy. Boebert can cast congressional votes. Treating their disagreement primarily as a campaign spectacle obscures who currently has institutional agency.

Accusation replaced accountability

Boebert’s response followed a familiar political maneuver: when pressed about an exercise of public power, put the questioner on trial instead. The resulting pile of accusations was expansive enough to crowd out the original issue but, in AlterNet’s account, insufficiently documented to resolve any of it.

Potential conflicts involving defense investments would be legitimate subjects for scrutiny if established with records and presented with Laubacher’s response. “Deep state,” however, supplies no comparable evidence. It converts an opponent’s government and military background into proof of hidden disloyalty without identifying an institution, decision or act for which she can be held accountable.

The purpose of that rhetoric, whatever Boebert’s private motive, is clear in practice: it replaces a testable question about an incumbent’s vote with an insinuation that the challenger belongs to a shadowy enemy network.

Newsmax supplied the escape route

Kelly briefly appeared uncertain after showing the debate clip, but his next move was not to press Boebert on the vote Laubacher had raised. He asked what constituents “need to know” about her opponent. That invitation allowed Boebert to leave her own record behind and resume attacking Laubacher.

Kelly then strengthened the diversion by suggesting that veterans can be “weaponized by the deep state.” This was not neutral moderation. It gave conspiratorial language a television host’s endorsement and recast Laubacher’s military experience as presumptively suspect precisely when that experience supported her challenge on war.

The segment therefore exposed more than a strained reaction shot. It showed an allied media figure recognizing an uncomfortable exchange, then helping the incumbent change the subject.

The spectacle conceals the decision

AlterNet’s framing is weakest where it fixates on who “smacked around” whom. Debate humiliation is campaign entertainment. War policy is the exercise of state power, with consequences far beyond either candidate’s performance.

The article also appends allegations and scandals involving Boebert and her family that do not establish what she would do about the conflict. That material may intensify the portrait of a vulnerable incumbent, but it repeats the same structural error as the interview: personality and scandal displace the decision that only the officeholder can make.

The larger pattern is political evasion reinforced by partisan media. An incumbent is asked to account for her authority; she attacks the challenger’s identity and finances; the host validates the attack; and coverage packages the episode as embarrassment. The supposed cringe is incidental. The real story is how quickly responsibility for government action disappeared from view.
