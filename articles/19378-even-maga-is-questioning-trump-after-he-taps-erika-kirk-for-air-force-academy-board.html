Even MAGA is questioning Trump after he taps Erika Kirk for Air Force Academy board

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 11, 20265 min read

Erika Kirk, Charlie Kirk’s widow, gestures next to U.S. President Donald Trump at State Farm Stadium, in Glendale, Arizona, U.S., September 21, 2025. REUTERS/Daniel Cole

President Donald Trump quietly appointed Erika Kirk to the Air Force Academy board on Monday, prompting swift outrage and mockery from both sides of the political aisle.

Kirk replaces her husband, activist Charlie Kirk, who spent a short time on the board before his shooting death at a rally in Utah. Among his accomplishments on the board, Kirk held up construction of a chapel and urged academy cadets to “know that we are the greatest nation ever.”

Neither Erika nor her far-right husband have any experience in any branch of military service. The closest the latter got was the claim that he received a congressional appointment to the U.S. Military Academy at West Point but was replaced by a person of “a different ethnicity and gender,” reported Politico in an extensive 2018 profile. In a 2017 New Yorker profile, Kirk claimed he lost the spot to “a far less-qualified candidate.”

West Point told Politico that race is only considered for candidates who meet all of its other admission requirements. Of those who apply, only half meet such requirements, and only half of that half are accepted. As of 2024, the acceptance rate was 12 percent. Kirk ultimately attended a community college before he dropped out.

“She joins a high-profile board that includes several members of Congress, such as Representative Jeff Crank (CO-05), Senator Tommy Tuberville (AL), and Chairman August Pfluger (TX). If you don’t think that they have BIG plans for Erika Kirk, think again!!” commented investigative journalist Amy Leigh.

“What in the actual hell are we doing?” complained conservative Christian activist Zach Costello.

He later added, “This Erika Kirk appointment to this Air Force Academy Board happened quietly over the weekend, with no formal announcement. I’m sorry, but being the widow of an influential husband does not qualify you to advise the Defense Department about Air Force standards.”

“I am still team Erika Kirk but in all honestly, this doesn’t help her and it doesn’t look good for Trump. She has zero qualifications for this role and maybe she should read the room,” posted one woman on X.

“Just when you think the timeline couldn’t get any dumber. ... Trump goes and appoints Erika Kirk to the Air Force Academy board that oversees morale, curriculum, and academic methods at the academy smh,” lamented Wu Tang for the Children, a large X account.

One man called it “shady” and “greasy.”

“We’re in hell,” another account complained.

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Erika Kirk’s Appointment: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In a move that could only be described as both baffling and insultingly nepotistic, President Donald Trump has appointed Erika Kirk to the Air Force Academy board. This decision has managed to unite both ends of the political spectrum in a rare moment of shared disbelief and disapproval. The appointment of Kirk, who replaces her late husband, Charlie Kirk, a figure known more for his ideological activism than any tangible military experience, sends a jarring message about the current state of political appointments in the United States.

Qualifications? We Don’t Need No Stinking Qualifications!

Erika Kirk’s appointment is a stark emblem of a system that increasingly prioritizes political loyalty over competency and relevant experience. Neither Erika nor Charlie Kirk possess any military experience, with Charlie’s closest brush with military service being a disputed claim of almost attending West Point—a claim that crumbles under the slightest scrutiny. This lack of experience is not just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring hole in their resumes, especially for a position that influences the future of the nation’s military leadership. The fact that this appointment was made “quietly over the weekend,” as conservative Christian activist Zach Costello pointed out, only adds a layer of sneakiness to the whole affair.

A Board of High Profiles and Higher Questions

The Air Force Academy board is not a token position. It’s a high-profile board that includes members of Congress and oversees critical aspects of the Academy, such as morale, curriculum, and academic methods. The inclusion of Erika Kirk in this esteemed company raises an uncomfortable question: What exactly does she bring to the table? Investigative journalist Amy Leigh’s remark that “they have BIG plans for Erika Kirk” hints at the possibility of a hidden agenda, a suggestion that should alarm anyone concerned about the integrity of military education and leadership training.

The Echo Chamber of Outrage and Disbelief

The reaction to Kirk’s appointment has been swift and severe. From conservative activists to social media commentators, the consensus is clear: this appointment does not sit well with anyone looking for a semblance of meritocracy in government positions. The lamentations on social media, ranging from calling the situation “shady and greasy” to declarations that “we’re in hell,” reflect a broader discontent with the way appointments are being handled.

Wu Tang for the Children’s comment that “Just when you think the timeline couldn’t get any dumber... Trump goes and appoints Erika Kirk” captures the surreal nature of this appointment, highlighting a growing concern over the erosion of standards and qualifications in favor of political gamesmanship.

The Bottom Line

Erika Kirk’s appointment to the Air Force Academy board is not just a questionable decision—it’s a symptomatic example of a deeper rot within the political appointment process. It underscores a disturbing trend of valuing loyalty over expertise, connections over competence. In a world increasingly complex and fraught with challenges, these qualities are essential for leadership roles, especially those shaping the future leaders of the military.

One must wonder, what message does this send to the cadets of the Air Force Academy? That merit and experience are secondary to who you know—or, more accurately, to whom you are married? It’s a dangerous precedent, one that undermines the very foundations of meritocracy and competent governance.

In the end, the Kirk appointment should not just be a moment of bipartisan outrage but a wake-up call for a return to sanity and standards in political appointments. The security and future of the nation deserve nothing less.
