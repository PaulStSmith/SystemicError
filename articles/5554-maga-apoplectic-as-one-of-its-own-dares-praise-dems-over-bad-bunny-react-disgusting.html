MAGA apoplectic as one of its own dares praise Dems over Bad Bunny react: 'Disgusting'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 10, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Cultural Clash at the Super Bowl Halftime Show: A Reflection of America’s Identity Crisis

Super Bowl LX - Half-Time Show - New England Patriots v Seattle Seahawks - Levi’s Stadium, Santa Clara, California, United States - February 8, 2026 Bad Bunny performs during the halftime show REUTERS/Mike Blake

FBI Director Kash Patel’s country singer girlfriend was swamped with anger late Sunday as she reacted to a Democratic Party post lauding Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime performance.

Responding to the social media post featuring a stars-and-stripes rendering of the Puerto Rican rapper with the caption “All-American Halftime with Bad Bunny,” Alexis Wilkins, 27, wrote, “Unpopular: Republicans need to unite and get on better messaging because this branding is fantastic and allows all dems to get behind it. Also - super aesthetic.”

Bad Bunny, whose real name is Benito Antonio Martínez Ocasio, has been a vocal Trump critic and deportation opponent. At last week’s Grammy Awards, he stated, “Before I say ‘Thank God’ I want to say, ‘ICE out!’”

His performance Sunday consisted primarily of Spanish-language material, provoking significant anger from the Trump administration and its supporters.

MAGA supporters attacked Wilkins’ post, characterizing her comments as a “bad take,” “disgusting,” and “phony bulls--t.” One user wrote: “Nothing American about the halftime. Spanish is not our national language. Flying other countries [sic] flags is ridiculous and is treasonous! Bad Bunny does not represent America!”

Another accused Wilkins of being a “leftist” destroying the country and urged her departure, the Daily Beast reported.

Wilkins responded by clarifying that she had not watched the performance. She stated, “I’m not saying it’s good at all, I’m not watching it. This branding targets youth and is cosplaying as if it’s celebrating America. And basically as a whole the right needs to unite.” In a separate response, she added: “I offer 0 commentary on the show itself. The dems were smart with public branding and cosplaying as America fans, which means they’re trying to gain moderate ground again.”

Wilkins is a U.S. citizen of Armenian descent born in America. She has been dating Patel, 45, since 2023.

Trump had previously attacked Bad Bunny’s selection. He wrote on Truth Social: “The Super Bowl Halftime Show is absolutely terrible, one of the worst, EVER! It makes no sense, is an affront to the Greatness of America, and doesn’t represent our standards of Success, Creativity, or Excellence. Nobody understands a word this guy is saying, and the dancing is disgusting, especially for young children that are watching from throughout the U.S.A., and all over the World.”

Trump characterized the performance as a “slap in the face” to his administration’s stated achievements: “the Best Stock Market and 401(k)s in History!”

Despite Trump’s criticism—delivered despite his stated boycott of the event—Bad Bunny’s performance drew an estimated 135 million viewers. An alternative halftime show produced by Charlie Kirk’s Turning Point USA organization attracted approximately 5 million viewers. The Bad Bunny viewership may constitute the most-watched NFL halftime show in history.

Our Analysis:

The Cultural Clash at the Super Bowl Halftime Show: A Reflection of America’s Identity Crisis

The recent uproar over Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime performance, as depicted in the reactions from FBI Director Kash Patel’s country singer girlfriend, Alexis Wilkins, and the subsequent backlash from MAGA supporters, encapsulates the ongoing cultural and political divide in the United States. This incident, seemingly trivial on the surface, is a microcosm of the larger battle over American identity, linguistic homogeneity, and the politicization of entertainment.

The Faux Patriotism of MAGA Outrage

The vehement criticism aimed at Bad Bunny’s performance, primarily for its use of Spanish and the representation of Puerto Rican culture, is steeped in a narrow and exclusionary view of American identity. The argument that “Spanish is not our national language” and that flying other countries’ flags is “ridiculous and is treasonous” blatantly ignores the rich tapestry of cultures that constitute the United States. Moreover, it disregards the fact that Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory, and its residents are U.S. citizens. The assertion that Bad Bunny “does not represent America” underscores a xenophobic and monolingual perspective that seeks to homogenize the American experience—a stance that is both regressive and divisive.

The Strategic Blindness of Alexis Wilkins’ Comments

Alexis Wilkins’ remarks reveal a concerning trend among some political commentators: the prioritization of “branding” and “messaging” over substantive policy discussions and cultural inclusivity. While she commends the Democratic Party’s “fantastic” branding for rallying support, her comments about the performance “cosplaying as if it’s celebrating America” and attempting to “gain moderate ground” betray a superficial engagement with the issues at hand. Wilkins’ focus on the aesthetic and strategic aspects of the Democrats’ approach, rather than the genuine celebration of America’s cultural diversity that Bad Bunny’s performance embodies, is indicative of a broader political environment where image often trumps authenticity.

Donald Trump’s Predictable Vitriol

Donald Trump’s reaction to Bad Bunny’s halftime show is unsurprisingly hyperbolic and disconnected from reality. His characterization of the performance as a “slap in the face” to his administration’s achievements and an “affront to the Greatness of America” is emblematic of his tenure’s divisive rhetoric. Trump’s comments, ripe with xenophobia and cultural insensitivity, further polarize the nation. His focus on the alleged indecency of the performance and its supposed incomprehensibility to English speakers not only illustrates his narrow vision of American culture but also his disregard for the global nature of entertainment and the diverse fabric of the American populace.

The Broader Implications

This incident, while centered around a halftime show, reflects the broader societal debates over language, culture, and what it means to be American. The backlash against Bad Bunny’s performance and the support it garnered from figures like Alexis Wilkins and Donald Trump underscore a resistance to the evolving demographics and cultural expressions within the United States. The fact that Bad Bunny’s performance—celebrating Puerto Rican heritage and performed mainly in Spanish—drew an estimated 135 million viewers suggests a public appetite for diverse representations of American identity, contrary to the MAGA movement’s monolingual and monocultural vision.

In conclusion, the controversy surrounding Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime show is not just about entertainment; it’s about the soul of America. It’s a clash between an inclusive, multicultural vision of the country and a regressive, exclusionary nationalism. It’s high time for the MAGA crowd and their sympathizers to realize that America’s strength lies in its diversity, not in a homogenized, sanitized version of “patriotism” that excludes and diminishes the rich mosaic of cultures that truly make America great.
