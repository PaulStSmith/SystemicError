'Brutal disregard': How Trump resembles entertainer caught on hot mic deriding fans

President Donald Trump sparked his most recent mess like a famed entertainer caught on a hot mic disparaging his adoring fans, New York Times’ columnist Maureen Dowd wrote Saturday.

Trump’s devoted MAGA base thought he’d walk into his second term in White House shedding light on the darkness of conspiracy theories they are convinced the Deep State is keeping quiet.

Instead, wrote Dowd, the Jeffrey Epstein scandal he’s now buried in has turned that belief on its head.

“So the guy who spent his lifetime saying the deep state hides things from you and represses you is now saying ‘We’ve got nothing to hide, trust me,” wrote Dowd, quoting Trump biographer Tim O’Brien.

“And the people who follow him don’t. They think he’s just as bad as the people he criticized before he became president.”

The furor that has engulfed Trump for more than a week involves a client list MAGA has long believed incriminates powerful people associated with convicted sex trafficker Epstein.

Trump has fanned that theory for years — until his attorney general suddenly declared there was no list, and sparked uproar among his base.

He then took to Truth Social to disparage the “weaklings” and his “PAST supporters” who believed something was being hidden.

“It’s mythic, being devoured by the forces you unleashed,” wrote Dowd.

“Trump has trafficked in conspiracy theories since the despicable “birther” one about Barack Obama. Now that whirlpool of dark innuendo has sucked him down. He can no longer control the Epstein conspiracy madness inflamed by his top officials.”

In the pinnacle of her column, Dowd remembers Lonesome Rhodes, an entertainer in the 1957 movie “A Face in the Crowd” whose own actions destroy his standing with his loving audience.

“Engorged by flattery and riches, [Rhodes] has a narcissistic explosion,” she wrote.

“Not realizing the woman he betrayed flipped on his microphone, he calls his loyal fans ‘morons,’ ‘miserable slobs’ and ‘trained seals.’

“I can take chicken fertilizer and sell it to ’em for caviar,” he crows, grinning.

“Trump’s Truth Social posts backing up Pam Bondi’s claim that the Epstein files were much ado about nothingshowed that same brutal disregardfor his devout fans. They had taken himseriously? What fools!”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Trump's Latest Quagmire: A Scathing Analysis

In an era where the surreal often blurs with reality, President Donald Trump's latest debacle unfurls with a plot twist worthy of a daytime drama, yet Maureen Dowd's column captures this moment with a precision that slices through the farce like a scalpel. The man who once held the MAGA base in rapturous awe, promising to illuminate the shadowy machinations of the so-called Deep State, finds himself ensnared in the very web of conspiracy theories he helped weave. The Jeffrey Epstein scandal, a sordid saga Trump once exploited to fan the flames of intrigue and division, has now become his Achilles' heel.

Trump's Betrayal: A Tale of Hypocrisy and Irony

The delicious irony of Trump's predicament cannot be overstated. For years, Trump positioned himself as the harbinger of truth, the only man brave enough to peel back the curtain on the Deep State's darkest secrets. Yet, as Dowd articulates with biting clarity, the moment of reckoning arrived not with a triumphant unveiling of hidden truths, but with a stark declaration from his own attorney general: there is no list, no bombshell to be dropped. The emperor, it seems, has no clothes.

The MAGA Base's Disillusionment: A Bitter Pill to Swallow

Trump's subsequent tirades on Truth Social, where he lambasts his former devotees as "weaklings" and "PAST supporters," reek of desperation and betrayal. The man who once claimed to champion the silent majority now mocks them with a vitriol that echoes Lonesome Rhodes's downfall in "A Face in the Crowd." Trump's disdain for his base, once veiled by populist rhetoric and promises of salvation, is laid bare for all to see. His followers, once fervent believers in his crusade, find themselves cast aside, their loyalty repaid with scorn.

A Whirlpool of Dark Innuendo: Trump's Own Making

Dowd's comparison of Trump to the fictional Rhodes is apt, highlighting not just the betrayal but the narcissistic self-destruction that accompanies it. Trump's journey from conspiracy theorist-in-chief to the victim of his own machinations is a spectacle of Shakespearean proportions. The "birther" controversy, the Epstein connections—these were the seeds he sowed, fertilized with lies and paranoia. Now, as the whirlpool of dark innuendo he helped create threatens to consume him, one cannot help but ponder the poetic justice of it all.

Conclusion: The Emperor's New Clothes

In the final analysis, Dowd's column serves as a scathing indictment not only of Trump's character but of the volatile, conspiracy-laden landscape he cultivated. His fall from grace, if such a term can be applied, is not just the result of external forces or the machinations of his enemies. Rather, it is the inevitable outcome of his own actions, a self-inflicted wound born from hubris, deceit, and a profound underestimation of the very people he claimed to represent.

As this saga unfolds, one thing becomes crystal clear: Trump's legacy will not be defined by his battles against the perceived Deep State or his promises of American resurgence. Instead, he will be remembered as the man who, engorged by flattery and riches, betrayed his base, his principles, and ultimately, himself. In the end, the man who sold "chicken fertilizer" as caviar finds the market has collapsed, leaving him with nothing but the stench of his own making.
