Suspicious Judge imposes threat of perjury on Todd Blanche

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

The Trump Fund Was Never About Money. It Was About Control.

The Real Object Here

A federal judge blocked Donald Trump’s proposed $1.8 billion “Anti-Weaponization Fund,” and the order went further than a simple injunction: it forced named officials at DOJ and Treasury to swear under penalty of perjury that they would not create or operate it under any name. That detail matters because it shows the scheme was not treated as a misunderstood proposal. It was treated as something the administration might try to smuggle forward anyway.

That is the actual story: not a budgeting dispute, but an executive branch testing how far it can go in building a retaliation apparatus and then denying it is doing so.

Who Held the Power

The power sat with Trump’s team, not with the judge. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche, Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward Jr., and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent are the officials the court ordered to file sworn assurances. Those are the people who could make the fund move or stop. They are the ones with the machinery, the signatures, the institutional levers.

Andrew Block, the Justice Department lawyer, was not the decision-maker. He was the messenger standing in front of a judge who plainly did not believe the administration’s line. That distinction is the whole point. Legal defense lawyers absorb the heat; political appointees hold the power.

The Decision Was Already Made

The source makes clear that Blanche had already testified to Congress that the fund was not moving forward, while The Atlantic reported the administration was privately assuring allies that payout plans remained on track. Those two facts are not a contradiction by accident. They are the operating model: say one thing in public, preserve the option in private.

Blanche’s own reported comment that he is trying to build “roadblocks” against Democrats successfully prosecuting Trump and his administration officials if they win the House and Senate strips away the varnish. This is not neutral law enforcement. It is preemptive political defense by administrative power. The fund is an extension of that impulse, a discretionary weapon dressed up as policy.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The weak frame here is any suggestion that this is primarily about standing, procedure, or whether a judge has explained every doctrinal step. Those are the kind of lawyerly distractions that let a political abuse look like an academic dispute. The real issue is simpler: the administration appears to have been pursuing a mechanism for rewarding allies, insulating itself, or financing retaliation, then using legal ambiguity as cover.

The article’s hand-wringing over legal commentary misses the broader structure. When officials quietly keep payout plans “on track” after publicly denying them, the problem is not confusion. It is institutional dishonesty. When the Justice Department is used to create “roadblocks” against future prosecutions, the problem is not legal creativity. It is the conversion of public office into personal defense infrastructure.

The Judge Saw the Game

Judge Brinkema’s language was blunt because the situation was blunt. Her insistence on a sworn declaration under penalty of perjury signals a court that expects evasions, not good-faith compliance. That is what happens when a government’s promises have become too cheap to trust.

The mockery from legal analysts about standing or timing is secondary. Courts can debate doctrine. Power does not wait for doctrine to finish sorting itself out. The need for an order that bars the fund “in any manner, or under any name” tells you exactly how elastic the administration’s behavior is expected to be.

The Pattern Is Familiar

This is the modern governing pattern: create an institutional weapon, deny it publicly, defend it privately, and force opponents to fight through procedural mud while the machinery keeps moving. The cruelty is bureaucratic. The propaganda is legalistic. The target is accountability itself.

What this episode reveals is not merely one blocked fund. It reveals an executive class that treats government agencies as a protective shield for itself and a potential bludgeon against its enemies. The names change. The structure does not.
