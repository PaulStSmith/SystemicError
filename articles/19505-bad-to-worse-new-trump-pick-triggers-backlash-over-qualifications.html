'Bad to worse': New Trump pick triggers backlash over qualifications

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 8, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump holds a Cabinet meeting, Wednesday, April 30, 2025, in the Cabinet Room. (Official White House Photo by Molly Riley)

President Donald Trump’s announcement that Republican U.S. Senator Markwayne Mullin will become the new Secretary of Homeland Security — replacing embattled Kristi Noem — is drawing mockery.

CNN’s Kaitlan Collins reported that “Trump loves watching Mullin on TV and often praises him, which was a factor in this decision.”

Calling him “erratic” and “unstable,” California Democratic Governor Gavin Newsom slammed Mullin’s nomination.

“Markwayne Mullin could not remember if we were at war THIS WEEK,” he said. “His state has one of the highest crime rates in the country — with a murder rate 40% higher than California’s. He literally tried to fight union workers during a hearing and told them to ‘shut your mouth.’ And said ‘I don’t want reality’ at a Senate hearing about race.”

The president may have another challenge ahead of him.

After dodging increasing calls for Noem’s impeachment over her controversial congressional testimony on Wednesday, he wrote that Mullin will be the new DHS Secretary as of March 31. Politico’s Kyle Cheney notes there are other factors at work.

According to Cheney, “it’s not clear how Trump can simply announce this is effective on March 31. Mullin is not Senate-confirmed and not eligible to become acting secretary under laws governing cabinet-level vacancies.”

If it’s a matter of getting enough Democrats to support Mullin, Trump can already count on the Senator from Pennsylvania.

“As a member of the Homeland Security Committee + Ranking Member of Subcommittee on Border Security: I’m not sure how many fellow Democrats will vote to support our colleague @SenMullin as the next DHS Secretary, but I am AYE,” Democratic Senator John Fetterman wrote.

Meanwhile, critics continued to express opposition to the decision to hand the reins of the more than $100 billion federal agency to Mullin.

“Firing Noem to hire Markwayne Mullin is the definition of going from bad to worse,” declared Democratic strategist Max Burns.

Some pointed out that Mullin is the only current U.S. Senator to not hold a bachelor’s degree.

Others noted that he is “the same guy who was hiding from MAGA rioters during the January 6th insurrection.”

And some pointed to reports “showing him in violation of the STOCK Act.”

The progressive social media account The Tennessee Holler called Mullin “one of the biggest Trump sycophants in Congress.”

The Atlantic’s Norman Ornstein added, “That Markwayne Mullin is the dumbest member of the Senate was a qualification for Trump to choose him to head DHS.”

Our Analysis:

The Absurd Theater of Trump’s Latest Appointment

In a move that could only be conjured up in the most farcical realms of political theater, President Donald Trump has announced Republican U.S. Senator Markwayne Mullin as the new Secretary of Homeland Security, replacing the “embattled” Kristi Noem. This decision, as reported by CNN’s Kaitlan Collins, appears to be based less on qualifications and more on Trump’s penchant for rewarding television performances he enjoys. This is not governance; it’s a reality TV casting decision.

Markwayne Mullin: a name that now symbolizes the nadir of political appointments. Described as “erratic” and “unstable” by California Democratic Governor Gavin Newsom, Mullin’s nomination reads like a parody. Newsom’s critique paints a picture of a man out of touch with reality, citing Mullin’s forgetfulness about whether the country was at war, his state’s alarming crime rates, his aggression towards union workers, and a blatant disregard for reality during discussions on race. This is not the resume of a Homeland Security Secretary; it’s a cautionary tale.

The legality of Mullin’s appointment itself is under scrutiny. Politico’s Kyle Cheney points out the elephant in the room: Mullin is not Senate-confirmed nor eligible to become acting secretary under existing laws governing cabinet-level vacancies. This raises the question: Is the rule of law now an optional guideline in Trump’s administration?

Democratic Senator John Fetterman’s lone voice of support within his party for Mullin as DHS Secretary feels like a drop of sanity in an ocean of madness. Yet, it’s unlikely to quell the storm of criticism surrounding Mullin’s nomination, which ranges from his lack of a bachelor’s degree to hiding from MAGA rioters during the January 6th insurrection, and even allegations of violating the STOCK Act.

Critics, like Democratic strategist Max Burns, succinctly describe the transition from Noem to Mullin as “going from bad to worse.” This is not merely a change of personnel; it’s an alarming degradation of standards for public office. The Tennessee Holler’s branding of Mullin as “one of the biggest Trump sycophants in Congress” and The Atlantic’s Norman Ornstein dubbing him as “the dumbest member of the Senate” are not mere insults but a reflection of a broader concern about the qualifications of those chosen to lead critical federal agencies.

In a rational world, the head of the Department of Homeland Security should be someone of unimpeachable qualifications, given the agency’s pivotal role in protecting the United States from a myriad of threats. However, in Trump’s America, it seems the main qualification is loyalty to Trump himself, not to the country or its laws.

As this farce unfolds, one can’t help but wonder: Where does the line between governance and gross negligence lie? Trump’s decision to appoint Mullin disregards the safety, security, and sanity of the nation he’s supposed to protect. It’s a stark reminder of the systemic errors plaguing American politics, where qualifications are overshadowed by sycophancy, and reality TV stars are mistaken for statesmen. The appointment of Markwayne Mullin as Secretary of Homeland Security isn’t just a bad decision; it’s a dangerous precedent.
