'Path of autocracy': Ex-Trump officials condemn exec orders targeting national security experts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 21, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Escalation of Presidential Power: Trump's Latest Executive Orders

President Donald Trump on March 14, 2025 (Official White House Photo by Joyce N. Boghosian/Flickr)

Although President Donald Trump often rails against "radical-left lunatics" — many of them center-left Democrats who are way to the right of leftist parties in Europe — he isn't shy about attacking right-wing conservatives and libertarians he regards as enemies. Trump, on April 9, issued executive orders asking the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) to investigate two of his foes on the right: former Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) head Chris Krebs and Miles Taylor, who served as chief of staff for the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) during Trump's first presidency.

In 2018, Taylor wrote an anonymous op-ed for the New York Times that was highly critical of Trump. The op-ed was famously headlined "I Am Part of the Resistance Inside the Trump Administration."

Now, a bipartisan group of Trump critics — including some conservative Republicans who were part of his first administration — is circulating an open letter that wholeheartedly condemns Trump's call for an investigation of Krebs and Taylor.

The New York Times' Karoun Demirjian notes, "The letter was spearheaded by the State Democracy Defenders Fund, a group run by Norm Eisen, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution who served on the staff of the first team of House Democrats that worked to impeach Mr. Trump in 2019. The Brookings Institution is not involved in the fund, nor was it involved in the letter."

Those who signed the letter include attorney Ty Cobb, former DHS General Counsel John Mitnick, The Lincoln Project's Rick Wilson (a former GOP strategist), Olivia Troye (who served as a national security adviser to former President Mike Pence), Harvard University law professor Laurence Tribe, former Rep. David Jolly (R-Florida), and political strategist Lucy Caldwell, among many others.

The letter reads, "We write with grave concern about the two presidential memoranda dated April 9, 2025, targeting Chris Krebs and Miles Taylor, respectively — two former national security officials who served the people of the United States. These executive actions represent a dangerous escalation in the abuse of presidential power: weaponizing federal agencies to carry out personalized retribution against named individuals."

Those who signed the letter don't mince words, attacking Trump's executive orders as dangerously "authoritarian."

"Indeed, the President's actions not only evoke some of the worst moments in our history; they go even further," the letter warns. "For a president to personally and publicly direct the levers of the federal government against publicly named citizens for political reasons sets a new and perilous precedent in our republic. It brings to mind the abuses of power that characterize authoritarian nations, not the United States. No matter one's party or politics, every American should reject the notion that the awesome power of the presidency can be used to pursue individual vendettas. Behavior of this kind is more to be expected from a royal despot than the elected leader of a constitutional republic. This is the path of autocracy, not democracy."

Analyzing the Escalation of Presidential Power: Trump's Latest Executive Orders

In an audacious move that blurs the already murky lines between personal vendettas and presidential prerogatives, President Donald Trump has issued executive orders directing the Department of Justice (DOJ) to investigate two notable conservatives, Chris Krebs and Miles Taylor. This maneuver not only showcases Trump's relentless pursuit of his critics but also illuminates a disturbing trend towards authoritarianism that should alarm even his staunchest supporters.

The Targets: Krebs, the former head of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), and Taylor, a former chief of staff at the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), have both been vocal in their criticism of Trump. Taylor, in particular, emerged from anonymity as the author of a scathing op-ed and subsequent book that revealed internal resistance against Trump within his own administration. Their critiques, grounded in concern for national security and democratic norms, have now made them the focus of Trump's ire.

The Reaction: A bipartisan coalition, including figures from Trump's own administration, has risen in opposition to these executive orders. The collective voice of this group, articulated through an open letter, condemns Trump's actions as a "dangerous escalation in the abuse of presidential power." This letter, spearheaded by the State Democracy Defenders Fund, unites a diverse array of individuals, from attorney Ty Cobb to Harvard University's Laurence Tribe, in a singular message of resistance against Trump's authoritarian impulses.

The Implications: Trump's decision to single out Krebs and Taylor for DOJ investigations is not merely an act of political retribution; it is a stark manifestation of the president's willingness to leverage the machinery of government against his adversaries. This move, unprecedented in its explicitness and audacity, sets a dangerous precedent. It signals a shift towards a governance model where personal vendettas can dictate the use of federal power, a hallmark of authoritarian regimes, not democratic republics.

The Concern: The bipartisan letter rightly emphasizes the perilous path Trump is treading. By using executive power to pursue personal grievances, Trump is not only undermining the foundational principles of American democracy but also emboldening future administrations to further erode the separation of powers. This behavior, more befitting a "royal despot" than an elected official, represents a significant departure from the norms and expectations that have governed presidential conduct in the United States.

In essence, Trump's executive orders targeting Krebs and Taylor epitomize the dangers of a presidency unbound by the constraints of ethics or the constitution. The bipartisan outcry against these actions serves as a crucial reminder of the need for vigilance and resistance in the face of authoritarian tendencies. As this situation unfolds, it will be imperative for all Americans, regardless of political affiliation, to stand in defense of democratic principles and reject the weaponization of federal agencies for personal vendettas. The path to autocracy is paved with such abuses of power, and it is a path the United States must endeavor to avoid at all costs.
