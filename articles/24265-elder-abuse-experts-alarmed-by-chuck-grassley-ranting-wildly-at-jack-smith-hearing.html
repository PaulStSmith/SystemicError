'Elder abuse': Experts alarmed by Chuck Grassley 'ranting wildly' at Jack Smith hearing

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosSeptember 30, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

At a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing held on a Tuesday, Chairman Chuck Grassley accused former special counsel Jack Smith—the panel’s only witness—of running a politically motivated and “corrupt” operation against Republicans. RawStory reports that Grassley cited subpoenas involving Republican groups, Trump White House phones and lawmakers’ telephone toll records, while claiming constitutional protections had been breached.

The headline’s “elder abuse” remark came from independent journalist Marcy Wheeler, who posted it sarcastically while attacking Grassley’s performance. Former CIA officer and federal prosecutor Bryan Cunningham likewise disputed Grassley’s legal claims and scored his questioning as “fact-free.” The article does not establish that medical or elder-care experts raised an alarm, or that actual elder abuse occurred.

The chairman held the power

Grassley was not a confused bystander caught in somebody else’s spectacle. He chaired the committee, controlled the opening frame and used that authority to declare Smith’s work illegitimate before questioning began. Smith’s two federal cases against Donald Trump had been dismissed by the time of the hearing, but Grassley still had an institutional platform from which to recast the investigations as misconduct against Republicans.

That imbalance matters. A committee chairman can set agendas, select timing, characterize evidence and turn congressional oversight into political prosecution by microphone. Whatever the merits of Grassley’s specific legal arguments, his office—not his age—gave those arguments consequence.

Mockery obscured the real dispute

Wheeler’s “elder abuse” line and Cunningham’s reference to Grassley as “approximately 900 years old” were insults, not analysis. They supplied RawStory with a clickable frame while diverting attention from the substantive question: whether Grassley accurately described what Smith obtained, what legal process authorized it and what the Speech or Debate Clause protects.

Cunningham argued that the clause does not categorically prohibit investigators from obtaining legally authorized toll records and said a grand jury had authorized the investigation. Grassley alleged something broader, including acquisition of lawmakers’ communications. RawStory does not resolve that factual and legal disagreement. It mostly stacks Grassley’s assertions beside hostile social-media reactions.

Oversight became narrative control

Grassley explicitly said the hearing would expose Smith’s “political work and weaponized conduct.” He also portrayed Democratic demands for an earlier hearing as a strategy to let Smith “bury the story.” That framing converts disagreement over timing and evidence into presumed partisan conspiracy.

The political advantage is obvious even without inferring Grassley’s private motive. By labeling an investigation corrupt at the outset, the chairman placed Smith in the position of disproving a conclusion already announced from the dais. The hearing’s authority then lends institutional weight to a partisan account, regardless of whether the article supplies enough evidence to validate it.

The article chose the weaker target

RawStory’s framing invites readers to focus on whether Grassley appeared old, incoherent or embarrassing. That is the least important dimension of the event. The stronger critique concerns his exercise of Senate power: what records supported his accusations, whether he accurately stated the law and whether the committee tested those claims fairly.

Age-based ridicule also gives Grassley an escape route. It reduces accountable political conduct to personal frailty and allows supporters to dismiss criticism as contempt for an elderly senator. A powerful chairman should be judged by what he did with public authority, not by strangers diagnosing him through a hearing broadcast.

Spectacle protects institutional abuse

The larger pattern is congressional oversight repurposed as partisan narrative production, followed by media coverage that packages the conflict as viral humiliation. One side wields subpoena power, committee procedure and an official platform; critics answer with derision; the publication sells the derision.

That arrangement leaves the central exercise of power insufficiently examined. Grassley chose the hearing’s frame and made serious allegations against Smith. RawStory answered mainly with posts mocking his age and competence. The result is heat without accountability: institutional power disappears behind personality, and readers are encouraged to laugh where they should be asking what the chairman proved.
