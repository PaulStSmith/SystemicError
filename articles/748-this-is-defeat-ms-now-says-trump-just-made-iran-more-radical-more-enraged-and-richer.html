'This is defeat': MS NOW says Trump just made Iran 'more radical, more enraged and richer'

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Trump Lit the Match, Then Claimed the Ceasefire

The Deal Is the Aftermath

The source is a cable-news argument over a reported agreement to end the war in Iran, with Iranian officials confirming the deal and Trump claiming the Strait of Hormuz will reopen once the signatures are on paper. Joe Scarborough and Jonathan Lemire then do something rare in political television: they point directly at the person who set the disaster in motion. Their core claim is simple, even if the machinery around it keeps trying to blur it. Trump started the war, and now his allies want credit for stopping the damage.

Power Was Never Ambiguous

The actual institutional power sat with the president and the war-making apparatus around him. Trump chose escalation, Trump sold the war, and Trump now gets to frame the retreat as leverage. That is the recurring fraud of executive violence: the same office that authorizes the rupture also gets to narrate the repair. Everyone else is left reacting to a crisis they did not create, while the man who created it talks like a broker.

The Cost Is the Message

Scarborough and Lemire both underline the same point: whatever this agreement produces, it comes after billions spent, civilians killed, and regional instability made worse. They specifically point to the losses in the first hours of the war, including a girls’ school, and to the likelihood that Iran will emerge richer, angrier, and more secure in its hardline posture. That is the part the triumphalists always erase. A ceasefire after reckless escalation is not a win. It is the invoice.

The Favorite Misdirection

The familiar trick in this kind of coverage is to treat the ending of a self-inflicted disaster as proof that the disaster was strategic. Scarborough rejects that spin, and he is right to do so. Trump’s people had spent years attacking Biden over sanctions relief and attacking Obama over cash transfers to Tehran, as if moral seriousness were measured by how loudly you condemn your own concessions. Now the same crowd is prepared to applaud a much larger transfer of leverage and money, dressed up as peace. That is not prudence. It is reputational laundering.

What This Actually Rewarded

The segment’s most useful line is also its bleakest: Iran is stronger now than before the war. That should be the headline, because it identifies the governing logic. The United States used force in a way that worsened its own position, handed its opponent a harder line, and made the region more dangerous for everyone around Iran. Trump can celebrate the Strait opening as if reopening what was already open counts as statesmanship. It does not. It is a cosmetic victory over a problem he created.

The Pattern Is the Point

This is how authoritarian politics and imperial blundering converge. Escalate first, then demand praise for stopping the bleed. Deny the original decision, then market the cleanup as strength. Turn civilian harm into a footnote, turn strategic failure into a deal, and turn the press into a stage for retrospective amnesia. The real story here is not that Trump found a way out. It is that American power still lets one man manufacture a war, externalize the wreckage, and then claim the ending as proof of competence.
