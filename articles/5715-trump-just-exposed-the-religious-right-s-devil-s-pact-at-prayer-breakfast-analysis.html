President Donald Trump just exposed the religious right’s “devil’s pact” with his performance at the National Prayer Breakfast on Thursday.

In an analysis from David Smith, The Guardian‘s Washington bureau chief, Smith pointed out how Trump, whose Christian faith and religious life has been questioned in the past, hurled insults at political enemies and took swipes at “transgender insanity” throughout his speech in Washington, D.C. And despite these moves, and previous acknowledgement that he might not be the most faithful follower among Republicans, the right-wing Evangelicals have continued to support him.

This week, Trump recalled how Baptist Robert Jeffress gave a frank reaction to him, Smith reported.

“He may not be as good with the Bible as some of them. He may not have read the Bible as much as some of them. In fact, he may not have ever read the Bible, but he will be a much stronger messenger for us and he will get things done that no other man has the ability to get done,” Jeffress said.

And that’s something Trump hadn’t forgotten, all while he is “the religious right’s chosen instrument to turn the tide against liberal, godless America,” Smith explained.

“You know, I didn’t want to admit anything, but that was very interesting and I think we’ve gotten more done than anybody could have ever gotten done,” Trump said.

“There, in a nutshell, was the devil’s pact that Republicans and rightwing evangelicals have made,” Smith wrote. “Trump is the Persian king Cyrus the Great, who might not embody Christian values yet is God’s chosen instrument to achieve a particular purpose – namely, turning the tide against liberal, godless America. Our Analysis:

The Devil’s Pact Exposed: Trump and the Religious Right

In a recent spectacle that could only be dubbed as surreal, President Donald Trump took to the stage at the National Prayer Breakfast, transforming what should have been a sanctified event into a political circus. David Smith of The Guardian brings this to light, revealing a deeply troubling alliance between Trump and the religious right, a bond that reeks of hypocrisy and opportunism.

Trump’s Brazen Performance

Trump’s speech was a cavalcade of insults hurled at political adversaries and derogatory comments on “transgender insanity.” Such behavior is antithetical to the Christian values of love, forgiveness, and humility. Yet, this man, whose acquaintance with the Bible appears tenuous at best, has been anointed by right-wing evangelicals as their champion. It’s a stark manifestation of their willingness to overlook his glaring moral deficits for political gain.

The Faustian Bargain

The religious right’s support for Trump, despite his questionable fidelity to Christian principles, is astoundingly hypocritical. Their endorsement seems less about spiritual leadership and more about securing a political operative who can further their agenda. This is epitomized by Baptist Robert Jeffress’s admission that while Trump may not be the most devout Christian, he is seen as a potent tool for accomplishing their goals. This candid acknowledgment lays bare the transactional nature of their support.

Trump as a Modern-Day Cyrus

Smith draws a parallel between Trump and Cyrus the Great, suggesting that just as Cyrus, a non-believer, was used as an instrument of God’s will according to biblical narratives, so too Trump is viewed by his evangelical supporters. They see him as a bulwark against a “liberal, godless America,” willing to forgive his transgressions as long as he advances their cause. It’s a dangerous game of theological gymnastics that prioritizes political objectives over moral integrity.

Conclusion: The Moral Quagmire

The alliance between Trump and the religious right, as Smith points out, is a “devil’s pact” that exposes a profound moral quagmire at the heart of American evangelical politics. It’s a partnership based not on shared faith or values but on a mutual desire for power. The willingness to overlook the ethical and spiritual failings of a leader in exchange for political favor is a betrayal of the core principles of Christianity.

In essence, this unholy alliance between Trump and the religious right not only undermines the ethical foundations of their purported beliefs but also erodes the political discourse in America. It’s a cautionary tale of what happens when political ambition supersedes moral conviction, leaving the principles of faith trampled underfoot in the quest for power.
