Wisconsin GOP rocked as right-wing justice won’t run again citing conservative 'failures'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analysis of Wisconsin's Supreme Court Shakeup

Republicans in Wisconsin received a blow on Friday, as arch-conservative state Supreme Court Justice Rebecca Bradley announced she will not seek re-election next year, according to the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel.

"The conservative movement needs to take stock of its failures, identify the problem, and fix it," said Bradley in a statement. "I will not seek reelection to the Wisconsin Supreme Court because I believe the best path for me to rebuild the conservative movement and fight for liberty is not as a minority member of the Court."

Rumors that Bradley, a right-wing firebrand who has launched public attacks on her liberal colleagues, would not seek another term on the bench have swirled for months, with speculation further fueled by her lack of a meaningful campaign fundraising haul.

She drew a liberal challenger in Appeals Court Judge Chris Taylor, who has the backing of state Democrats.

"The incredible support for Judge Taylor shows how important this race is," said a Taylor spokesperson. "No matter who Republicans and right-wing special interests recruit to run against her, Wisconsinites will once again have a clear choice between a candidate who believes in stripping rights away and Judge Taylor, who has always been on the side of the people."

Wisconsin Supreme Court judicial elections are formally nonpartisan, but in practice, candidates almost always court the backing of one party or the other.

Liberal candidates have won four of the last five Wisconsin Supreme Court elections, flipping control of the court to a 4-3 liberal majority in 2023 with the election of Justice Janet Protasiewicz.

Since then, the court has handed Democrats a number of legal victories, including a forced redraw of the state's heavily GOP-gerrymandered legislative districts and a ruling that struck down Wisconsin's 176-year-old ban on abortion.

Analysis of Wisconsin's Supreme Court Shakeup

The political landscape of Wisconsin underwent a seismic shift on Friday, as arch-conservative state Supreme Court Justice Rebecca Bradley announced her decision not to seek re-election. This move is more than a mere reshuffling of judicial seats; it's a symptom of a deeper malaise within the conservative movement in Wisconsin—a movement that Bradley herself insists must undergo introspection and reform.

"The conservative movement needs to take stock of its failures, identify the problem, and fix it," Bradley stated, a rare admission of vulnerability within the usually stalwart conservative ranks. Her decision not to seek re-election is framed as a strategic retreat, aimed at rebuilding conservative strength from beyond the judicial bench. Yet, one can't help but see it as an acknowledgment of the waning influence of her brand of conservatism within the state's highest court.

Bradley's tenure has been marked by her right-wing firebrand status, often launching public attacks against her liberal colleagues. Her withdrawal from the race, amid swirling rumors and a lack of significant campaign fundraising, signals not just personal defeat but a broader existential crisis for conservatives within the state. Her liberal challenger, Appeals Court Judge Chris Taylor, buoyed by the support of state Democrats, stands as a testament to the shifting political winds in Wisconsin—a shift that Bradley seems to tacitly acknowledge.

"The incredible support for Judge Taylor shows how important this race is," stated a Taylor spokesperson, framing the upcoming judicial election as a battleground between regressive conservatism and progressive liberalism. This dichotomy, though simplistically binary, captures the essence of the struggle for the soul of Wisconsin's judiciary.

Wisconsin's Supreme Court elections, though formally nonpartisan, have been anything but. Candidates have historically sought and received the backing of political parties, making the court a de facto political battleground. The recent liberal victories, culminating in a 4-3 liberal majority with the election of Justice Janet Protasiewicz, mark a significant shift in the court's ideological balance. The court's decisions to force a redraw of the state's gerrymandered legislative districts and to strike down a centuries-old abortion ban are indicative of the profound impact this shift has had on state politics.

Bradley's departure and the ascent of liberal justices signal a changing of the guard, reflective of broader societal shifts towards more progressive values. Conservatives, as Bradley herself notes, are at a crossroads, faced with the challenge of redefining their movement in a state that is increasingly rejecting their hardline stances.

In conclusion, Rebecca Bradley's announcement is not just the closing of a chapter in her own career but a stark illustration of the ideological battles reshaping Wisconsin's political and judicial landscapes. As conservatives regroup and liberals advance, the state's Supreme Court remains a critical arena where the future of Wisconsin's policies and principles are being fought and determined.
