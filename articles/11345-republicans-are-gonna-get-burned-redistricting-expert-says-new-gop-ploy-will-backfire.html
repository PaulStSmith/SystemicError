'Republicans are gonna get burned': Redistricting expert says new GOP ploy will backfire

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20255 min read

Republicans’ latest ploy to strengthen their hand heading into the 2026 midterm elections is poised to backfire tremendously, one redistricting expert argued Thursday on CNN.

At the behest of President Donald Trump, Texas Republicans are currently working to arbitrarily redraw the state’s congressional district map to give the GOP five more House seats in Congress. The Texas House voted to adopt a gerrymandered map on Wednesday, with the Texas Senate expected to vote on the plan later on Thursday.

But according to John Bisognano, the president of the National Democratic Redistricting Committee, Republicans’ plan will end up hurting the GOP in the long run as Democrats across the country mount a coordinated response.

“Nobody wants this, that's one thing that we're seeing consistently; in polling, with donors, nobody seems to want what's happening in Texas,” Bisognano said, speaking with CNN. “What you're seeing in California and other states beyond is going to continue to happen. If Texas sparks this flame, I assure you, Republicans are going to get burned as well.”

California’s response to Texas has been to move forward with a similar gerrymandering effort, a plan that was endorsed by former President Barack Obama, and scorned by GOP leadership. California was just the start, Bisognano warned, with a full-on redistricting war only playing into Democrats’ hands.

“You're going to start to see people understand that gerrymandering is bad at a much higher rate, and we need to focus on those places to elevate the issue and make sure that such a complicated issue isn't so complicated or wonky,” Bisognano said. “...We're looking at places where we can offset what Republicans are doing to try and steal votes.”

States like Illinois and New York are already weighing the idea of redrawing their own districts in response to Texas, with Democratic party leaders hopeful the issue will motivate voters amid the Democratic Party’s tumbling poll numbers.

That Trump had launched the redistricting effort now, Bisognano said, was “no mistake” either, and pointed to the growing unpopularity of the president’s signature domestic policy bill, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and its tax cuts mostly enjoyed by the wealthy and cuts to social safety net programs.

“It's no mistake that it's happening right now,” Bisognano said. “What we're seeing right now is Donald Trump passed the least-popular budget bill at least I've ever seen; he's facing all kinds of headwinds and he knows he's going into a midterm where he's likely to lose, so he's trying to have JD Vance fly across the country, talk to people and ask them to gerrymander their states as much as possible.”

Our Analysis:

The Gerrymandering Gambit: A Desperate Move or Strategic Folly?

The latest maneuver by the Republican Party to redraw Texas's congressional district map in their favor, reportedly at the urging of President Donald Trump, is a classic case of political chicanery that might just spell disaster for the GOP. This attempt to secure five additional House seats through blatant gerrymandering—a tactic as old as the republic itself—exposes a party scrambling to cling onto power by any means necessary, even if it means warping the democratic process to its breaking point.

John Bisognano, president of the National Democratic Redistricting Committee, has thrown a scathing critique at this strategy, arguing that it will backfire spectacularly. His observations, shared on CNN, paint a picture of a GOP isolated in its quest, facing opposition not just from political adversaries but from the general public and donors alike. But what's more telling is the coordinated response taking shape across the nation, with states like California leading the charge in a tit-for-tat redistricting battle that could engulf the country in a full-on redistricting war.

The Irony of Unintended Consequences

Bisognano's insights hint at a larger, more ironic outcome: the GOP's blatant gerrymandering efforts might inadvertently raise public consciousness about the dangers and injustices of gerrymandering itself. The idea that a party's overreach could catalyze a nationwide movement against such political manipulations is rich with poetic justice.

However, this unfolding scenario also illustrates a grim reality of American politics: the descent into retaliatory gerrymandering—what Bisognano suggests could play into Democrats' hands—points to a vicious cycle that undermines the very foundations of democratic representation. The escalation of this redistricting arms race, with states like Illinois and New York considering their own countermeasures, suggests a bleak future where electoral fairness is sacrificed at the altar of partisan advantage.

Trump's Shadow Looms Large

Central to this narrative is Donald Trump, whose fingerprints on the redistricting efforts are as unmistakable as they are troubling. Bisognano’s remarks underscore a strategic desperation within the GOP, as Trump propels the party into controversial territories to distract from his own policy failures—most notably, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, a policy move characterized by tax breaks for the wealthy at the expense of social safety nets.

This strategy, according to Bisognano, is no accident. It's a calculated distraction, a sleight of hand designed to shift focus from the administration's unpopular policies and safeguard its precarious political future. Yet, in doing so, Trump and his acolytes risk igniting a redistricting backlash that could reshape the political landscape in ways they hadn't anticipated.

The Bigger Picture

The unfolding redistricting saga is more than just a tale of political intrigue; it's a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic norms in the face of unabashed power grabs. As both parties prepare to dig in for a prolonged battle over the nation's electoral maps, the ultimate casualty might well be the public's faith in a fair and representative electoral process.

The Republican Party's latest ploy is a gambit fraught with risk, a move that could either secure their immediate political future or consign them to the annals of history as architects of their own downfall. Only time will tell which path this redistricting war will carve through the heart of American democracy.
