Judge slams Trump’s 'shocking abuse of power' in decision halting his executive order

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Sparky's Analysis:Trump's Retaliatory Directive: A Stark Violation of Constitutional Principles

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on in the Oval Office of the White House, as he signs executive orders, in Washington, U.S., January 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

Legal journalist Chris Geidner reports U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan agreed to temporarily block a Trump administration directive, while describing the directive as against the ideals of the nation’s founding fathers.

"Frankly, I think the framers of our Constitution would see this as a shocking abuse of power," AliKhan said, according to Geidner. "While I wish other firms were not capitulating ... I admire Susman [and others challenging Trump's acts]."

Law firm Susman Godfrey launched a suit against the administration after Trump retaliated against it for its role in litigating for plaintiff Dominion Voting Systems in a defamation case against Fox News. That case, challenging false claims that Democrats stole the 2020 election from Trump, ultimately ended in victory. Fox News settled for nearly $1 billion.

The White House accuses several firms of "weaponizing" the legal system, claiming they have a connection to Trump’s legal and political adversaries, and they work on voting rights and other cases Trump personally opposes. The administration also seeks to punish firms over employment policies that encourage diversity.

Since taking office, Trump has been issuing executive orders and memos punishing major law firms by suspending security clearances, canceling government contracts, and barring employees from federal buildings, among other retaliatory tactics.

Nine firms, including Paul Weiss, Skadden Arps and Simpson Thacher have already cut deals with the White House to avoid threats to their right to conduct business. Many have pledged hundreds of millions of dollars in free legal work to Trump’s personal political and legal causes.

Judge AliKhan became the 4th judge to grant a temporary restraining order against the president’s retaliatory executive orders, finding the EO against Susman “retaliatory and discriminates based on viewpoint,” which is a First Amendment violation.

"Nothing in the order shows a compelling interest," Geidner reported the judge saying. She also found the order likely violates the Fifth Amendment right to due process.

Sparky's Analysis:

Trump's Retaliatory Directive: A Stark Violation of Constitutional Principles

In a recent development that underscores the troubling trajectory of the Trump administration's disdain for legal norms, U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan's decision to temporarily block a Trump administration directive represents a critical assertion of judicial oversight over executive overreach. According to legal journalist Chris Geidner, Judge AliKhan didn't mince words, suggesting that the founders of the American Constitution would be aghast at such a “shocking abuse of power.”

The Heart of the Matter: At the center of this legal maelanage is law firm Susman Godfrey, which had the audacity (read: civic responsibility) to represent Dominion Voting Systems in a defamation case against Fox News. This case, which stemmed from spurious allegations that the 2020 election was stolen from Trump, concluded with Fox News coughing up nearly $1 billion in a settlement. In what can only be described as a vindictive response, the Trump administration targeted Susman Godfrey and several other firms for their involvement in cases that Trump personally finds unpalatable.

Weaponizing Legal Authority: The administration’s actions, ostensibly aimed at firms it accuses of “weaponizing” the legal system, are themselves a perverse form of weaponization. By suspending security clearances, canceling government contracts, and implementing other retaliatory measures, Trump’s efforts to punish these firms over employment policies promoting diversity and for their legal opposition reveal a chilling disposition towards authoritarianism.

Complicity Through Coercion: It’s particularly telling that nine major law firms, including legal behemoths like Paul Weiss and Skadden Arps, have capitulated to the White House's threats. The agreements they've reached, which involve pledging hundreds of millions of dollars in free legal work to Trump’s causes, are a testament to the coercive power of the presidency when wielded without regard for democratic norms and principles.

A Judicial Rebuke: Judge AliKhan’s decision to issue a temporary restraining order against Trump’s executive actions is a beacon of hope in this otherwise dark narrative. Describing the executive order as “retaliatory and discriminates based on viewpoint,” she not only highlighted its violation of the First Amendment but also underscored its likely infringement on the Fifth Amendment's due process rights. This judicial rebuke is a crucial reminder of the judiciary's role as a bulwark against the abuse of executive power.

Conclusion: What this saga unfurls is a narrative not merely of a dispute between a law firm and an administration but a broader battle for the soul of American democracy. The Trump administration's retaliatory directive against Susman Godfrey and others is not just a “shocking abuse of power,” as Judge AliKhan rightly pointed out, but a fundamental threat to the principles upon which the United States was founded. It is incumbent upon all who cherish these principles to stand in opposition to such tyranny, to ensure that the weaponization of power for petty vendettas does not become a hallmark of American governance.
