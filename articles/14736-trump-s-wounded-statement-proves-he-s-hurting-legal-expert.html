A former prosecutor said President Donald Trump's social media outburst shows the Supreme Court "wounded" him Friday.

The high court blocked Trump from moving forward with deporting Venezuelan migrants under a rarely used 1700s-era law known as the Alien Enemies Act. The court extended its previous order temporarily blocking Trump's administration from deporting dozens of Venezuelan men held in North Texas.

"The Supreme Court has just ruled that the worst murderers, drug dealers, gang members, and even those who are mentally insane, who came into our Country illegally, are not allowed to be forced out without going through a long, protracted, and expensive Legal Process, one that will take, possibly, many years for each person, and one that will allow these people to commit many crimes before they even see the inside of a Courthouse," Trump raged.

He continued, "The result of this decision will let more CRIMINALS pour into our Country, doing great harm to our cherished American public. It will also encourage other criminals to illegally enter our Country, wreaking havoc and bedlam wherever they go. The Supreme Court of the United States is not allowing me to do what I was elected to do. Sleepy Joe Biden allowed MILLIONS of Criminal Aliens to come into our Country without any 'PROCESS' but, in order to get them out of our Country, we have to go through a long and extended PROCESS. In any event, thank you to Justice Alito and Justice Thomas for attempting to protect our Country. This is a bad and dangerous day for America!"

CNN legal analyst Elie Honig told anchor Anderson Cooper the defeat represented a huge loss for Trump.

"This one hurts. This really hurts the Trump administration. And if you need any proof of that, look no further than the president's wounded statement after the fact," he said.

Honig called attention to sharp language deployed by the high court, usually made up of "ordinarily gentile justices."

"They are fed up," he added. "The justices are fed up with the way the Trump administration' been handling immigration," he said.

Additionally, he suggested seven of the justices appear to have lost trust in Trump's administration, as migrants haven't received sufficient notice of their looming removal, and officials have "played fast and loose" with the rules, shipping migrants out on planes in the middle of hearings.

"This was a really sharp setback," he concluded.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Legal Defeat: A Scathing Analysis

In the latest episode of what can only be described as the political equivalent of a Shakespearean tragedy, the Supreme Court delivered a stinging rebuke to President Donald Trump's immigration policies. Specifically, the court blocked Trump from deporting Venezuelan migrants under the antiquated Alien Enemies Act, a move that not only hampers his administration's aggressive stance on immigration but also publicly undermines his authority. Trump's reaction? A volcanic eruption of frustration on Truth Social, which, according to CNN legal analyst Elie Honig, underscores just how deeply this decision has "wounded" him.

The High Court's Uncommon Valor

The Supreme Court's decision to extend its previous order temporarily blocking the deportation of dozens of Venezuelan men is a rare moment of judicial pushback against what has been perceived as an increasingly authoritarian executive branch. Trump's appointees to the court—three justices who presumably would align with his views—were part of the body that delivered this blow, making the defeat even more bitter for him.

Trump's Reaction: A Study in Hyperbole

Trump's response to the Supreme Court's ruling is a masterclass in hyperbole and fear-mongering. He claims, without evidence, that the court's decision will allow "the worst murderers, drug dealers, gang members, and even those who are mentally insane" to inundate the United States, all because they cannot be deported "without going through a long, protracted, and expensive Legal Process." This narrative is not only misleading but dangerously inflames public sentiment against migrants, many of whom are fleeing dire conditions in their home countries.

The Reality of the "Process"

Trump's lament about a "long and extended PROCESS" for deporting migrants is rich with irony. His administration has been characterized by attempts to circumvent due process and the legal rights of migrants at every turn. The notion that Joe Biden has allowed "MILLIONS of Criminal Aliens" into the country "without any 'PROCESS'" is not only factually inaccurate but a transparent attempt to shift blame and distract from his administration's own failings.

The Supreme Court's "Fed Up" Stance

Elie Honig's commentary that the Supreme Court justices are "fed up" with the Trump administration's handling of immigration is telling. It suggests a judiciary that is increasingly concerned with the executive branch's disregard for legal norms and procedures. The claim that migrants haven't received sufficient notice of their removal and that officials have "played fast and loose" with the rules is a damning indictment of the Trump administration's approach to immigration policy.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

This episode is not just about Trump's policy defeat or his incendiary rhetoric. It's a reflection of a broader struggle within the United States over the rule of law, the rights of migrants, and the balance of power among the branches of government. The Supreme Court's decision—and Trump's reaction to it—highlights the tensions at the heart of these issues. As the political landscape continues to evolve, one thing remains clear: the need for a reasoned, humane approach to immigration that respects the law and the dignity of all individuals.

In sum, Trump's social media outburst and the Supreme Court's ruling are not just fleeting news items. They are symptomatic of deeper systemic issues that require thoughtful analysis and debate. It's time for the American public to look beyond the bluster and consider the real implications of these developments for the nation's future.
