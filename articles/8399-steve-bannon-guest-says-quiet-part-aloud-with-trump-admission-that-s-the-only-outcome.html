Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Surrender Doctrine: Rickards and Bannon’s Controversial Take on the Ukraine War

In a recent appearance that can only be described as a masterclass in defeatism masked as pragmatism, former CIA adviser and MAGA supporter Jim Rickards laid out a vision for ending the war in Ukraine that would make even the most ardent appeasers blush. His plan? Give Russian President Vladimir Putin “everything he wants.” This startling admission was made during a conversation with none other than Steve Bannon, a figure synonymous with the hard-right political fringes in America.

The Flaws in the “Give-In” Strategy

Rickards’ argument hinges on a deeply flawed premise: that the only way to end the war in Ukraine is by capitulating entirely to Russian demands. This is not a strategy; it’s surrender on an international scale. According to Rickards, “The Russians won,” a statement that sidesteps the immense complexities of the conflict, not to mention the Ukrainian resistance and the international support for Ukraine’s sovereignty.

Bannon’s agreement with Rickards, “The Russians won the war,” is equally problematic. It’s a narrative that ignores the ongoing battles, the resilience of the Ukrainian people, and the very real possibility of a future where Ukraine can resist Russian aggression successfully, given sufficient support. It’s a dangerous simplification of a conflict that has far-reaching implications for international law, national sovereignty, and democratic values.

Ignoring the Consequences

Rickards’ assertion that the U.S. has only two choices—“Keep fighting or keep supporting the war, or agree to Putin’s plan”—is a false dichotomy that serves only to undermine the efforts of those seeking a resolution that doesn’t involve handing a victory to an aggressor nation. The idea that agreeing to Putin’s plan is the only alternative to World War III is not only defeatist but also ignores other diplomatic and strategic avenues that have not been fully explored.

Moreover, Rickards’ claim that “Ukraine has lost” and his praise for the supposed “brilliance” of a 28-point plan that gives in to Putin’s demands is an insult to the Ukrainian struggle and to the principles of sovereignty and self-determination. It’s a narrative that, if adopted, would have dangerous implications for global security, effectively signaling that aggression is rewarded and might makes right.

The Bigger Picture

What Rickards and Bannon are advocating is a policy of appeasement that history has shown to be disastrously ineffective. The parallels to the lead-up to World War II are impossible to ignore, where appeasement did not prevent war but rather emboldened aggressors. This approach not only undermines Ukraine’s right to self-defense but also sets a dangerous precedent for future conflicts—where aggressors might feel emboldened to invade neighbors with impunity, expecting little resistance from a world that has shown it will capitulate rather than confront.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The commentary from Rickards and Bannon is not just a casual discussion; it’s a dangerous and misguided proposal that forgets the lessons of history. It conflates surrender with peace and ignores the moral and strategic imperatives of supporting a nation fighting for its sovereignty. The solution to the war in Ukraine will undoubtedly be complex and fraught with challenges, but the answer is not to simply give Putin “everything he wants.” To suggest so is not only irresponsible but also a betrayal of the principles upon which international order is based.

In essence, the war in Ukraine is not just Ukraine’s fight; it’s a test of the international community’s resolve to stand up to aggression and defend the principles of sovereignty and democratic self-determination. Giving in to Putin’s demands, as Rickards and Bannon suggest, is not a strategy for peace—it’s a blueprint for a future where might trumps right, and the world is all the poorer for it.
