Comer tried to head off 'sticky line' of inquiry for Melania over Epstein: MS NOW

U.S. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) attends a dinner with Republican members of congress hosted by U.S. President Donald Trump, in the East Room of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 22, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

As House Oversight Committee Chair James Comer (R-KY) spoke to reporters about the depositions of the Clintons on their potential connections to the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case, he made a curious statement that, according to MS NOW’s Ali Vitali, was an effort to shield President Donald Trump — but also first lady Melania Trump.

“I want to make a statement, because they’ll probably not mention this when they come out here,” Comer told reporters. “Ranking Member [Robert] Garcia asked President Clinton, quote, ‘Should President Trump be called to answer questions from this committee? And President Clinton said, that’s for you to decide. And the president went on to say that President Trump ‘has never said anything to me to make me think he was involved.’”

“Okay,” said anchor Katy Tur. “So, Ali, that was Comer’s characterization of what President Clinton said. Congressman Garcia just was speaking a moment ago, and he said that’s not quite accurate.”

“I think there’s two things that struck me,” said Vitali. “First is that Democrats keep trying to respect this idea that they are not supposed to talk about the substance of what’s happening in these depositions, like truly specifics about what’s happening in these depositions until the transcript and video are released. But we’ve seen now is Republicans and Comer doing a pretty good job of trying to set the narrative of what is said inside.”

More broadly, though, she said, Comer’s remark “gives us some insight into how he might be thinking about the future of this investigation, is that they are trying to use Bill Clinton as a way to rebut claims from Democrats and others who say, okay, if you got a former president, Bill Clinton, why are you not asking President Trump, who has mentioned hundreds of times in the files, potentially more than any other person in there, or one of the top mentioned people in the files, and Comer by trying to use Clinton to say, well, Clinton says he never had any reason to think that Trump was involved with Epstein. They’re basically trying to cut that line of inquiry off at the pass.”

But it’s not just the president who would be vulnerable to this issue, Vitali added — it would be the first lady as well.

“If the committee wants to ask spouses who don’t have insight into their husband’s actions, for example, as Hillary Clinton didn’t into Bill, then why are you not also asking Melania Trump, who, by the way, is actually pictured in some of the Epstein files and has at least one correspondence with Ghislaine Maxwell, right?” said Vitali, referring to Epstein’s primary sex trafficking accomplice. “So it opens up this, this sticky line of partisan questioning. And that’s what I saw in Comer, they’re trying to rebut.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Comer’s Curious Strategy

In a recent spectacle that can only be termed as political theater at its most transparent, House Oversight Committee Chair James Comer (R-KY) made statements that seemed to dance around the truth with the grace of a bull in a china shop. His comments regarding the deposition of the Clintons concerning their potential connections to the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case are a masterclass in deflection and partisan maneuvering.

Comer’s attempt to shield Trump—and curiously, Melania Trump— under the guise of quoting former President Bill Clinton is as subtle as a sledgehammer. According to Ali Vitali of MS NOW, Comer’s remarks were aimed at preempting any calls for Donald Trump to testify, based on Clinton’s alleged statement that Trump “has never said anything to me to make me think he was involved.” Here, Comer’s narrative serves less as a beacon of truth and more as a smokescreen to protect the Trumps.

But let’s not be fooled by the sleight of hand. The real magician’s trick isn’t in what Comer said; it’s in the broader implication of his words. By using Bill Clinton’s supposed dismissal of Trump’s involvement with Epstein as a shield, Comer is not just protecting Trump; he’s actively working to shape the narrative of the investigation before the facts can speak for themselves.

The Premature Narrative Shaping

Vitali’s observation that Republicans, with Comer at the helm, are “trying to set the narrative of what is said inside” the depositions, is a glaring red flag about the integrity of this investigation. It’s a tactic as old as politics itself: control the story before the story controls you. However, this isn’t just a story; it’s a serious investigation into deeply troubling allegations of sex trafficking at the highest levels of power. The premature shaping of the narrative undermines the investigation’s credibility and the public’s trust in its outcomes.

The Problem with Partisan Questioning

Furthermore, the suggestion that Melania Trump, like Hillary Clinton, might not have insight into her husband’s actions but should be questioned due to her appearances in some Epstein files and correspondences with Ghislaine Maxwell, opens a “sticky line of partisan questioning.” This isn’t just sticky; it’s a veritable quagmire of potential bias and misdirection. The focus on spouses, particularly in the manner suggested, seems less about uncovering the truth and more about political point-scoring.

The Art of the Question Unasked

The critical issue here isn’t just about whom Comer chooses to shield or expose; it’s about the questions that are being strategically avoided. If the Oversight Committee’s goal is to uncover the truth about the Epstein connections, then why the selective focus? Why preemptively cut off lines of inquiry? The answer, though Comer might not say it outright, seems rooted in a desire to protect certain political figures at the expense of a thorough and unbiased investigation.

In Conclusion:

James Comer’s comments and the strategy they reveal are a disservice to the gravity of the investigation into Jeffrey Epstein’s connections. The blatant attempt to shape the narrative and protect certain individuals from scrutiny underlines the pervasive issue of partisan politics contaminating what should be a nonpartisan search for the truth. As this investigation continues, one can only hope for a return to integrity and a focus on justice over political convenience. But if Comer’s recent actions are any indication, we might be waiting a long time.
