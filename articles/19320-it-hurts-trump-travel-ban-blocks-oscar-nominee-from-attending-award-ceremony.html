‘It hurts’: Trump travel ban blocks Oscar nominee from attending award ceremony

Oscar-nominated actor Motaz Malhees is barred from attending this Sunday’s Academy Awards due to President Donald Trump’s travel ban for certain foreign nationals, with the actor taking to social media Friday to express his disappointment.

Malhees played a prominent role in the 2025 film “The Voice of Hind Rajab,” a dramatization of the real-life killing of Hind Rajab, a five-year-old Palestinian girl who was killed in 2024 when Israeli troops fired hundreds of rounds into a vehicle carrying her and her family. An Israeli tank reportedly fired on first responders attempting to reach her.

The film was nominated for Best International Film by the Academy Awards last December, but Malhees – who is Palestinian – said on Friday he won’t be able to attend the ceremony.

“I will not be there. I am not allowed to enter the United States because of my Palestinian citizenship,” Malhees wrote in a social media post on Instagram.

“It hurts. But here is the truth: You can block a passport. You cannot block a voice. I am Palestinian, and I stand with pride and dignity. My spirit will be with the voice of Hind Rajab that night. Good luck to all of you. Our story is bigger than any barrier, and it will be heard.”

Trumpsigned aproclamationlast December that added “full restrictions and entry limitations” to those holding passports issued to them by the Palestinian Authority. In a shocking move, the Trump administration has alsoissued a directiveto subject Palestinian immigrants, regardless of their citizenship, to heightened scrutiny when applying for asylum, a move described by legal experts asunprecedentedgiven its focus on an entire ethnic group.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Oscar Nominee’s Visa Denial and Its Political Implications

The recent news surrounding Oscar-nominated actor Motaz Malhees’s inability to attend the Academy Awards due to President Donald Trump’s travel ban has sparked a wide range of reactions and discussions. This situation offers a complex interplay of institutional power, individual decisions, and the framing of narratives. Our analysis seeks to unravel these elements and assess the accuracy and fairness of the criticisms directed in this scenario.

Institutional Power and Decision Makers

Who holds the power?

In this case, the institutional power lies squarely with the executive branch of the United States government, specifically under the administration of President Donald Trump. The travel ban, enacted through a proclamation, is a direct exercise of executive power that affects immigration policy and entry into the U.S.

Who made the decision?

The decision to bar entry to individuals holding passports issued by the Palestinian Authority was made by President Donald Trump and his administration. The directive to subject Palestinian immigrants to heightened scrutiny further emphasizes the targeted nature of these measures.

Analyzing the Article’s Framing

The article frames the situation as a consequence of President Trump’s travel ban, focusing on its impact on a distinguished actor who played a significant role in highlighting a tragic event through cinema. This framing correctly places responsibility on the executive decision that led to Malhees’s inability to attend the Oscars. It does not misdirect blame toward actors with limited control over U.S. immigration policies.

Misdirection and Responsibility

There is no apparent misdirection of responsibility in the article. It clearly attributes the actor’s visa denial to the policies implemented by the Trump administration. In many narratives, there is a risk of oversimplifying complex political issues or unfairly attributing blame. However, in this instance, the article maintains a clear line of accountability.

Conclusion: Criticism Where It Is Due

The criticism of the Trump administration’s policy, as implicit in the actor’s public statement and the article’s narrative, is well-placed. The decision to enact a travel ban that indiscriminately affects individuals based on their nationality or the authority issuing their passport is a significant exercise of institutional power that warrants scrutiny.

Moreover, the specific targeting of Palestinian individuals for heightened scrutiny, described as unprecedented by legal experts, raises serious questions about discrimination and the equitable application of immigration laws. This policy decision reflects broader geopolitical stances and has tangible effects on individuals, as exemplified by Motaz Malhees’s situation.

In our analysis, the framing of the article aligns with the facts and rightly identifies the sources of institutional power and decision-making. It avoids the pitfalls of false equivalence or lazy narratives, instead providing a clear critique of a policy and its implications for freedom of movement and expression.

In Summary, the analysis confirms the article’s accuracy in attributing responsibility for Motaz Malhees’s visa denial to the Trump administration’s policies. It underscores the importance of scrutinizing the actions of those in power and the impact of those actions on individuals and broader societal values.
