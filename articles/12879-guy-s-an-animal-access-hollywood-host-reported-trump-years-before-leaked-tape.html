'Guy's an animal': Access Hollywood host reported Trump years before leaked tape

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Former Access Hollywood host Billy Bush revealed that he told NBC that President Donald Trump tried to sleep with his co-host nearly a decade before a leaked tape almost ended the then-candidate's 2016 campaign.

During a conversation on Rob Lowe's podcast this week, Bush recalled riding on the bus in 2005 when Trump made his infamous "grab 'em by the p—" remarks.

"And this is when, you know, whenever you're with Trump, and I was for 10 years, there's two major topics, golf and the P word," Bush told Lowe. "But I always remembered that tape as the Nancy O'Dell tape because he kept talking about my co-host, Nancy O'Dell."

Bush said Trump told him that he hit on O'Dell by offering to take her furniture shopping. Although the former host indicated that he didn't remember hearing Trump's profane remarks about women at the time, he did take notice of the comments about O'Dell.

"All I said was Nancy O'Dell because I didn't hear the other stuff," he insisted. "I said, he's trying to take Nancy furniture shopping to sleep with her."

"This is crazy. He's done it again. The guy's an animal. I reported basically to my superior."

Our Analysis:

Bush's Bombshell: A Tale of Trump's Tawdriness

In a revelation that should shock no one but still manages to disgust, former Access Hollywood host Billy Bush has come forward with yet another sordid detail about President Donald Trump's infamous 2005 hot mic moment. During a candid conversation on Rob Lowe's podcast, Bush peeled back yet another layer of the vulgar onion that is Trump's treatment of women.

Trump's Unabashed Misogyny

Let's get this straight: The man who sat in the Oval Office, who made decisions affecting the lives of millions, not only bragged about sexually assaulting women but also, apparently, tried to seduce his co-host by promising to buy her furniture. Yes, you read that correctly. Furniture. The detail is so absurd it would be laughable if it weren't so deeply repugnant.

Selective Amnesia or Complicit Silence?

Bush's recollection of events raises eyebrows, to say the least. He claims he only remembered Trump's comments about Nancy O'Dell, his co-host, because he somehow missed the "grab 'em by the p—" remark. This claim stretches the bounds of believability to its breaking point. It's hard to imagine how one could overlook such a blatantly offensive statement, especially considering its crass nature and the fact that it directly precedes Trump's comments about O'Dell. Is this a case of selective amnesia, or was it complicit silence? Either way, Bush's admission does little to absolve his failure to confront or adequately report Trump's behavior at the time.

A Culture of Complicity

What Bush's story highlights, more than anything, is the culture of complicity that surrounds powerful men like Trump. Bush admits to having reported Trump's comments about O'Dell to his superior, yet what did it amount to? Absolutely nothing. The tape remained under wraps, and Trump's behavior went unchecked for over a decade until it was leaked during the 2016 campaign.

The truly alarming aspect of this saga isn't just the vulgar, predatory behavior of Trump himself; it's the system that allows this type of conduct to go unchallenged. It's a system where men can make lewd, disrespectful comments about women, and their peers, aware of their behavior, do little more than shrug it off or bury it in the bureaucracy of corporate America.

The Unending Cycle

Bush's revelations serve as a reminder of the unending cycle of scandal, outrage, and, ultimately, societal amnesia that surrounds figures like Trump. Despite the bombshell nature of the 2016 tape, Trump went on to serve as President of the United States. And now, with Bush's latest comments, we are reminded once again of the depths of Trump's disrespect for women.

But what will come of it? If history is any indication, not much. The cycle will continue, with those in power finding new ways to excuse, overlook, and ultimately forget the inexcusable.

In a world that often feels post-truth, stories like Bush's are a stark reminder of the reality we must continue to confront: powerful men are still getting away with misogyny, and society's mechanisms for holding them accountable are woefully inadequate. Until we address the root of this systemic failure, we are doomed to repeat it, ad nauseam.
