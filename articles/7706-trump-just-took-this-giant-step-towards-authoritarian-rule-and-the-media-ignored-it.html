Trump just took this giant step towards authoritarian rule — and the media ignored it

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 14, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Administration’s Orwellian ‘Enemies List’

Donald Trump speaks to members of the media. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

The Trump FBI is drawing up an enemies list that could encompass well over half the US public: Do you “advance … opposition to law and immigration enforcement”? Do you have “extreme views in favor of mass migration and open borders”? Show an “adherence to radical gender ideology,” meaning you think trans people exist? Do you exhibit (what the Trump administration would interpret as) “anti-Americanism,” “anti-capitalism,” or “anti-Christianity”? Do you display “hostility towards traditional views on family, religion, and morality”?

Congratulations — you may be headed for Attorney General Pam Bondi’s “list of groups or entities engaging in acts that may constitute domestic terrorism.”

“Terrorism,” of course, is the magic word that strips you of all sorts of legal protections, especially in the post-9/11 era.

This is from a Justice Department memo obtained by independent journalist Ken Klippenstein (12/6/25) — which goes on to instruct the FBI to set up “a cash reward system” for people who turn in those promoting such thoughtcrime, and “establish cooperators to provide information and eventually testify against other members” of groups with these dangerous ideas.

This is the implementation of the Trump administration’s avowed policy of criminalizing dissent — in the words of the NSPM-7 decree, outlawing “organized campaigns of … radicalization … designed to … change or direct policy outcomes” (FAIR.org, 10/3/25; CounterSpin, 10/17/25) — and as such is another giant step towards authoritarianism. Establishment media didn’t see it that way, however.

As Klippenstein (12/9/25) pointed out, virtually no corporate media outlets covered this catastrophic memo, and those who did report on it did a generally poor job.

The Guardian headline (12/5/25) was “Pam Bondi Tells Law Enforcement Agencies to Investigate Antifa Groups for ‘Tax Crimes,’” and Bloomberg Law (12/5/25) had “Bondi Orders FBI Extremism Intelligence Review with Antifa Focus” — completely misleading framing that suggests that if you’re not “Antifa,” the memo isn’t about you.

Here’s Reuters’ entirely unhelpful “summary” (12/4/25):

  • Bondi orders FBI to prioritize domestic terrorism investigations

  • Memo targets antifa and similar groups

  • FBI to develop strategies to disrupt criminal networks

  • DOJ calls for prosecuting extremist groups for tax crimes

The DOJ is issuing marching orders for a witch hunt, and Reuters presents it with a straight face as an effort to go after “domestic terrorism,” “criminal networks,” and “extremist groups” who commit “tax crimes.” Who could object to that?

Among corporate media outlets, only The Hill (12/5/25), a specialty outlet aimed at congressional staffers and lobbyists, conveyed the enormity of the directive. Its second paragraph read:

Bondi’s memo could be the starting point for charges against a number of left-leaning advocacy groups and nonprofits the Trump administration has accused without evidence of having ties to extremists.

The Hill‘s Rebecca Beitsch quoted Andrew Bataj of the group Whistleblower Aid: “This memo expressly seeks to redefine political dissent against the president as domestic terrorism.”

But beyond that, to get actual coverage of the threat DOJ is posing to civil liberties and democracy itself, you had to go to independent outlets like Democracy Now! (12/8/25) and the Lever (12/8/25). The counterrevolution will not be televised.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration’s Orwellian ‘Enemies List’

In a chilling echo of the darkest chapters of 20th-century authoritarianism, the Trump administration appears to be compiling an enemies list—a concept so nefariously vague and broad that it could potentially include more than half of the United States population. Based on a Justice Department memo unearthed by the indefatigable independent journalist Ken Klippenstein, the criteria for inclusion on this list are dystopian in their breadth and ambiguity.

Let’s dissect the criteria that could land you on Attorney General Pam Bondi’s radar:

  1. Opposition to law and immigration enforcement. In other words, critique or challenge the status quo of current immigration policies, and you’re a suspect.

  2. Extreme views in favor of mass migration and open borders. ‘Extreme’ is the operative word here, left conveniently undefined to cast a wide net over anyone advocating for more humane immigration policies.

  3. Adherence to radical gender ideology. Translation: Acknowledge the existence and rights of trans people, and you’re on the list.

  4. Anti-Americanism, anti-capitalism, or anti-Christianity. Critique the government, question capitalism, or differ in religious beliefs, and you’re marked.

  5. Hostility towards traditional views on family, religion, and morality. Dare to think differently about the constructs of family, religion, or what’s considered moral, and you’re targeted.

The memo goes further, proposing a cash reward system for informants and the establishment of cooperators to infiltrate and testify against groups deemed to hold these dangerous ideologies. This isn’t just about monitoring or surveillance; it’s about incentivizing the betrayal of community trust, echoing some of the most repressive regimes in history.

Criminalizing Dissent: A Leap Towards Authoritarianism

This is not merely a policy shift; it’s a monumental leap towards authoritarianism under the guise of combating domestic terrorism. The Trump administration’s NSPM-7 decree, designed to outlaw “organized campaigns of radicalization,” is, in essence, a blunt instrument aimed at criminalizing dissent. It’s a policy that would make Orwell blush, where opposition to government policy is equated with terrorism.

The Media’s Muted Response

The corporate media’s tepid response to this revelation is as alarming as it is disappointing. Outlets like The Guardian and Bloomberg Law have offered misleading headlines that obscure the memo’s broader implications, focusing narrowly on Antifa and tax crimes. This framing dangerously narrows the public’s understanding of the memo’s reach, suggesting that only those involved in Antifa activities need be concerned.

Reuters, on the other hand, presented the DOJ’s directives without a hint of critical analysis, effectively normalizing a witch hunt against political dissent. It’s a stark reminder of the media’s capacity to either illuminate government overreach or, conversely, to sanitize it through omission and obfuscation.

Only The Hill and a handful of independent outlets have managed to capture the gravity of the situation, emphasizing the memo’s potential to target a broad swath of left-leaning advocacy groups, nonprofits, and essentially anyone daring to express dissent against the Trump administration.

Conclusion: The Counterrevolution Will Not Be Televised

In an era where independent journalism is more critical than ever, the lack of comprehensive coverage on this issue is a damning indictment of the mainstream media’s priorities. This memo, and the administration’s broader attempts to criminalize dissent, represent a fundamental threat to the democratic principles of free speech and political expression.

As this administration continues to blur the lines between legitimate security concerns and politically motivated persecution, it falls upon independent journalists and vigilant citizens to hold the line. The counterrevolution, it seems, will not be televised—but it must be opposed, with every tool at our disposal.
