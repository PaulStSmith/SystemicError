'I'm over it': Tornado-ravaged red state residents hit out at 'America first'

By Paulo Santos

Tylertown, Mississippi resident Bobby McGinnis on May 20, 2025 (Image: Screengrab via PBS NewsHour / YouTube)

Earlier this year, almost 20 tornadoes rampaged across several Mississippi towns, killing seven people and damaging hundreds of homes. Residents are still waiting for aid from President Donald Trump's administration months later.

PBS NewsHour recently reported that even in the deep-red Southern state where Trump handily won in all three of his presidential campaigns, the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) has yet to send any aid from Washington. According to PBS, Mississippi Governor Tate Reeves (R) applied for a federal disaster declaration from the Trump administration on April 1 for the storms that hit in mid-March, though he has yet to get a response. One particularly hard-hit area was Tylertown, which is the Walthall County seat in the southern part of the state.

"I know President Trump said that 'America First, we're gonna help our American folks first,' but we haven't seen the federal folks down here," Tylertown, Mississippi resident Bobby McGinnis told PBS.

“I don’t know what you got to do or what you got to have to be able to be declared for a federal disaster area because this is pretty bad,” Tylertown resident Brian Lowery said. “We can’t help you because, whatever, we’re waiting on a letter; we’re waiting on somebody to sign his name. You know, all that. I’m just over it.”

The pending disaster request from Mississippi comes at the same time FEMA has underwent significant changes in its leadership. Former acting FEMA administrator Cameron Hamilton was fired after he publicly disagreed with Trump on his desire to shut down the agency, and a recent internal presentation determined that FEMA was "not ready" to assist with the upcoming 2025 Atlantic Hurricane Season, which officially begins on June 1.

Mississippi isn't the only ruby-red state that has been left in the lurch by the Trump administration. Arkansas Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders (R) also applied for federal assistance after tornadoes tore through her state this spring, though FEMA denied both her request and her appeal of that rejection. Sen. Josh Hawley (R-Mo.) also used his allotted time for questioning during a recent Senate testimony by Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem to beg her to send aid to Missouri after the Show Me State experienced its own spate of deadly tornadoes.

Our Analysis:

A Storm of Neglect: Analyzing FEMA's Inaction Under Trump

The recent PBS NewsHour report casts a glaring spotlight on the Trump administration's failure to provide timely aid to Mississippi, a state ravaged by almost 20 tornadoes earlier this year. This situation is not just a bureaucratic hiccup; it's a stark manifestation of incompetence at the highest levels of government, particularly in an administration that has touted an "America First" philosophy. Yet, when it comes to aiding its own citizens in the aftermath of devastating natural disasters, the Trump administration's actions—or, more accurately, its inactions—speak louder than its words.

The Unfulfilled Promise of "America First"

It's bitterly ironic that residents of Tylertown, Mississippi, a town severely affected by the March tornadoes, are still awaiting federal assistance. The town's residents, like Bobby McGinnis, echo a sentiment of abandonment, directly contradicting President Trump's pledge to prioritize American citizens. This scenario is not just disappointing; it's a blatant failure of the administration to live up to its own standards and promises.

Bureaucratic Inertia or Strategic Neglect?

Mississippi Governor Tate Reeves's request for a federal disaster declaration has apparently disappeared into a black hole, with no response since April 1. This isn't mere bureaucratic delay; it's a dereliction of duty at the federal level. The question arises: Is this inertia a result of incompetence, or is it strategic neglect? Given the administration's track record, both seem plausible.

FEMA's Leadership Turmoil

The article highlights a worrying trend within FEMA—a leadership crisis. The firing of acting administrator Cameron Hamilton after his disagreement with Trump's desire to dismantle the agency is particularly troubling. This turmoil at the top inevitably trickles down, affecting the agency's readiness and capability to respond to disasters. An internal presentation revealing FEMA's unpreparedness for the 2025 Atlantic Hurricane Season is a red flag that cannot be ignored. It's a prelude to future failures, setting a dangerous precedent for handling natural disasters.

Not Just Mississippi's Plight

Mississippi is not alone in its plight. Arkansas and Missouri, both "ruby-red" states, have also been left in the lurch. The denial of federal assistance to Arkansas, even after Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders's appeal, and Senator Josh Hawley's plea for Missouri, underscore a systemic issue within the Trump administration's approach to disaster management. This is not a partisan issue; it's a national failure affecting citizens across the political spectrum.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Reform

The PBS NewsHour report is more than just a news piece; it's a clarion call for immediate reform within FEMA and a reevaluation of the Trump administration's commitment to its citizens. The federal government's primary role is to protect and aid its citizens, especially in times of crisis. The administration's failure to do so not only undermines its credibility but also endangers lives.

The situation demands not just criticism but action. It is imperative that there be a thorough investigation into FEMA's failures and the administration's neglect. Accountability is key, and those responsible for the delays and denials of aid must be held to account.

In the end, the measure of a country's greatness is not found in its rhetoric but in its actions—especially in how it treats its most vulnerable in times of need. The Trump administration has a long way to go to prove that it can live up to the true meaning of "America First."
