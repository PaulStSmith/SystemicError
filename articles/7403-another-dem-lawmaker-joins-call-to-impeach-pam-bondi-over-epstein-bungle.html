Another Dem lawmaker joins call to impeach Pam Bondi over Epstein bungle

Rep. James Clyburn appears on CNN, Dec. 20, 2025. (Screengrab / CNN)

Rep. James Clyburn (D-SC) joined his House colleague Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) Saturday in calling for the impeachment of Attorney General Pam Bondi over her agency’s failure to release all of its files on Jeffrey Epstein, which was mandated by law to be released in full on Friday.

Following the release of thousands of files related to Jeffrey Epstein on Friday, Khanna said he was drafting articles of impeachment against Bondi for her failure to adhere to the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which required all of the Justice Department’s files on Epstein to be released Friday.

And on Saturday, Clyburn said he agreed with Khanna.

“I think Ro Khanna is on to something here, the only way I see us getting to this is to [do] the impeachment, that’s one thing the Supreme Court has got nothing to do with,” Clyburn said during an appearance Saturday on CNN.

Clyburn expressed concern that the Supreme Court would allow the Trump administration to continue to violate the Epstein Files Transparency Act by not releasing the entirety of the DOJ’s files on Epstein. Therefore, Clyburn argued, impeachment was the best course of action to force the Trump administration’s hand.

“I am absolutely not satisfied, and I agree with Ro Khanna, but I’m not surprised that we have all of this delay and officiation, this is what you get from this White House!” Clyburn said.

“I don’t expect them to ever release these documents because I don’t think the Supreme Court will ever make them do it, it’s pretty much determined that the president is above all else and all others, and therefore, I expect for them to ride this out, and I expect the Supreme Court to allow them to do it.”

Our Analysis:

The Impeachment Imperative: A Dissection of Dysfunction

In an audacious move that slices through the cacophony of Capitol Hill’s usual humdrum, Rep. James Clyburn (D-SC), alongside Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA), has called for the impeachment of Attorney General Pam Bondi. Why, you ask? The heart of the matter lies in the Epstein Files Transparency Act—a mandate seemingly as clear as day, requiring the full release of the Justice Department’s files on Jeffrey Epstein, a figure synonymous with scandal and systemic failure.

Here we stand, witnessing yet another chapter in the seemingly endless saga of governmental opacity and obfuscation. The failure to release these documents in full, as explicitly required by law, is not just a procedural oversight; it’s a brazen display of contempt for legal mandates and public accountability.

The Allegations: A Matter of Law and Transparency

Let’s cut through the legalese. The Epstein Files Transparency Act wasn’t a suggestion; it was a mandate. A mandate that Attorney General Pam Bondi, by the allegations put forth, decided she could sidestep or ignore. This isn’t just about Epstein; it’s about whether the law applies equally to all, or if those in corridors of power can, with impunity, decide which laws to follow and which to flout.

Khanna’s drafting of articles of impeachment isn’t a knee-jerk reaction; it’s a measured response to a flagrant disregard for legal obligations. Clyburn’s support underscores the gravity of the situation. His assertion that impeachment is the sole recourse left, given the Supreme Court’s perceived unwillingness to act, speaks volumes about the perceived erosion of checks and balances within the U.S. political framework.

The Broader Implications: A Question of Accountability

Clyburn’s scathing indictment of the White House and his bleak outlook on the Supreme Court’s role—or lack thereof—in enforcing compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act raises an alarming question: Are we witnessing an era where certain branches of government are considered above the law?

The implications of such a scenario are chilling. It suggests a systemic bias, a shield of invincibility around those who should be the most accountable, given their positions of power and influence. This isn’t just about the delayed release of documents; it’s a litmus test for the health of American democracy and the rule of law.

The Path Forward: Impeachment as a Tool for Justice

Impeachment is a drastic measure, but it’s enshrined in the Constitution for moments exactly like this—when the mechanisms of governance are so corroded that only the most severe of remedies will suffice. If the allegations against Bondi hold true, then impeachment isn’t just warranted; it’s necessary.

Clyburn and Khanna’s call to action is more than political theater; it’s a clarion call for transparency, accountability, and adherence to the rule of law. In a time when public trust in governmental institutions is perilously low, their stand serves as a reminder of what public service should be about: not the protection of the powerful, but the safeguarding of public trust and the enforcement of the law—without fear or favor.

In sum, the unfolding drama is a stark reminder of the systemic errors that plague our political system. We must demand better, and if necessary, use the tools at our disposal—like impeachment—to ensure that the principles of transparency and accountability are not just lofty ideals, but lived realities.
