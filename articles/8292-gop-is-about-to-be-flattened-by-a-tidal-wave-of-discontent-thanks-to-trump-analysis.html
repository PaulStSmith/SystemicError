GOP is about to be 'flattened by a tidal wave of discontent' thanks to Trump: analysis

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he speaks during the opening of a temporary migrant detention center informally known as “Alligator Alcatraz” in Ochopee, Florida, U.S., July 1, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Bloomberg opinion columnist Ronald Brownstein argued recent elections show Republicans’ treacherous path to winning in 2026 is more narrow and dangerous than ever.

“For Republicans, November was bookended by two ominous developments: Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation and the party’s resounding defeats in the New Jersey and Virginia gubernatorial races,” said Brownstein. “The Republican candidates in those races — Jack Ciattarelli in New Jersey and Winsome Earle-Sears in Virginia — tried one strategy for dealing with President Donald Trump: appeasing him at all costs. Greene tried the other: showing some independence from the president. That both roads ended in disaster underscores how narrow a path is available next year to Republicans in competitive states and congressional districts.”

Ciattarelli and Earle-Sears followed the course almost all elected Republicans have adopted in Trump’s shadow after he remade the party in his image. Throughout 2024, both resolutely refused to criticize him, even when Trump took actions that demonstrably hurt their states. This included refusing to criticize Trump laying off thousands of Virginians who work for the federal government, despite the massive layoffs raising the state’s unemploymenr rate.

“Ciattarelli and Earle-Sears fatally refused to change course even as polls for months made clear that Trump’s tumultuous second term was bleeding his support. In the end, Ciattarelli and Earle-Sears were both flattened by a tidal wave of discontent over Trump,” said Brownstein.

It did not seem to register with either of the GOP candidates that a solid majority of voters in both New Jersey and Virginia disapproved of Trump, and that more than 90 percent of those disapproving backed the Democratic candidates, according to polls.

“The landslide Democratic wins in both states sent a clear message to any GOP candidates facing competitive races: aligning too closely with Trump in this environment can be dangerous to your political future,” said Brownstein. “But just weeks later, Greene’s resignation, under pressure from the president, offered a cautionary addendum: distancing too much from Trump also can be dangerous to your political future.”

Brownstein notes that the Greene flew back to Washington to vote against Trump’s second impeachment over his Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol — the same night her father had brain surgery for tumors. And yet, Trump labeled her a “traitor” as soon as she parted ways with him on the release of the Justice Department’s files on convicted sex-trafficker and Trump friend Jeffrey Epstein.

Green’s district was reliably red, but Brownstein said the choices “are even more excruciating for Republicans running in less reliably red areas.”

“Among Republican strategists it’s become conventional wisdom that their candidates can’t win without Trump’s favor because that’s the key to turning out his base of irregular working-class voters. But even if turning out Trump’s base is necessary for Republicans in 2026, there’s virtually no chance it will be sufficient in competitive races,” Brownstein said.

Trump has occasionally toned down his criticism of Republicans in difficult swing districts, like Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine). But with Trump’s popularity bombing now in so many districts cross the nation, it would be best for him to go hands-off on many more GOP lawmakers. But that’s not likely, said Brownstein.

“The level of self-awareness and self-discipline such a strategy would require … has not exactly been a hallmark of Trump’s volatile political career,” Brownstein said.

Our Analysis:

The Republican Dilemma: Trump’s Shadow and the Narrow Path Ahead

Ronald Brownstein’s recent column in Bloomberg, as summarized, provides a compelling analysis of the treacherous terrain the Republican Party finds itself navigating as it looks toward 2026. The twin failures of Jack Ciattarelli in New Jersey and Winsome Earle-Sears in Virginia, alongside Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation, serve as the latest episodes in the ongoing saga of the GOP’s Trump conundrum. Let’s dissect the key points and the underlying implications of these developments with the unflinching scrutiny they deserve.

The GOP’s Trump Bind: A Lose-Lose Situation

Brownstein astutely captures the essence of the Republican Party’s quandary. On one hand, candidates who have aligned themselves too closely with Donald Trump, such as Ciattarelli and Earle-Sears, have faced electoral backlash in areas where Trump’s approval ratings are underwater. On the other hand, showing even a modicum of independence from Trump, as Greene attempted, can lead to ostracization and political peril within the party. This dichotomy underscores a fundamental flaw in the GOP’s current strategy: the party’s overreliance on Trump’s base to the detriment of broader electoral viability.

The Tidal Wave of Discontent

The “tidal wave of discontent” over Trump that Brownstein mentions is not just a metaphor but a palpable force that has evidently swept away any Republican candidate who has failed to distance themselves from Trump’s most divisive and damaging actions. The refusal of Ciattarelli and Earle-Sears to criticize Trump’s decisions that directly harmed their constituents reveals a shocking level of political cowardice or, at best, a misguided calculation about the value of Trump’s endorsement. This miscalculation was laid bare as both states handed resounding victories to Democratic candidates, signaling a clear rejection of Trump’s brand of politics.

The Polls and the People

The detail that a solid majority of voters in both New Jersey and Virginia disapproved of Trump, with more than 90 percent of those disapproving backing the Democratic candidates, is a statistical siren call to the GOP. It starkly highlights the widening chasm between the party’s base and the broader electorate. This disapproval rating is not just a number; it’s a loud, clear voice of the electorate that the GOP seems hell-bent on ignoring.

Greene’s Cautionary Tale

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation under pressure from Trump, after a brief flirtation with independence, serves as a grim cautionary tale for Republicans. It illustrates the perils of straying from Trump’s orbit, no matter how toxic it becomes. Greene’s political demise should have been a wake-up call to the GOP; instead, it appears to have only reinforced the fear of Trump’s wrath among Republican candidates.

The Conventional Wisdom Trap

Brownstein’s observation that turning out Trump’s base is seen as necessary but not sufficient for Republicans in competitive races is a critical insight. This “conventional wisdom” has become a trap, ensnaring the GOP in a cycle of short-term tactical moves that undermine its long-term strategic viability. The party’s inability to expand its appeal beyond the core Trump base is a recipe for electoral disaster, particularly in swing states and districts.

Trump’s Unlikely Change of Course

Finally, Brownstein’s skepticism about Trump toning down his criticism of Republicans in difficult swing districts is well-founded. Expecting “self-awareness and self-discipline” from Trump, given his track record, is akin to expecting a leopard to change its spots. The GOP’s hope that Trump will voluntarily reduce his influence for the greater good of the party is not only naïve but dangerously delusional.

In Conclusion

The Republican Party’s path to success in 2026 and beyond is indeed narrow and fraught with peril, as Brownstein suggests. However, the root of this treacherous path lies not just in the divisive figure of Trump, but in the GOP’s willful blindness to the shifting sands of the American electorate. As long as the party remains shackled to Trump’s whims and base, it risks alienating a generation of voters. The GOP faces a choice: continue down this destructive path, or forge a new, more inclusive direction. The clock is ticking, and the world is watching.
