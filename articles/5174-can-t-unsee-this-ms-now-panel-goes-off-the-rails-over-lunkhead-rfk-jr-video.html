A “workout” video posted by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. to X late Tuesday was greeted by gales of laughter and no small measure of disgust and bafflement on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe” on Wednesday morning.

With no introduction, the video of the controversial RFK Jr. and aging rocker Kid Rock taking off their shirts and exercising, with Rock’s “Bawitdaba” from 1999 as a soundtrack, was shown, before the “Morning Joe“ producers cut to the panel, most of whom were laughing.

Our Analysis:

A Dissected Spectacle: RFK Jr.’s “Workout” Video

In an era where the lines between political gravitas and pop culture blur with increasing regularity, a recent episode on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe” pushes this trend into the realm of the absurd. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., alongside aging rocker Kid Rock, presented a “workout” video that has ignited a firestorm of reactions ranging from bewildered amusement to outright disdain.

The Performance

With no preamble, viewers were treated (or subjected, depending on one’s perspective) to the sight of RFK Jr. and Kid Rock shedding their shirts and engaging in physical exercise, all set to the 1999 anthem “Bawitdaba” by Kid Rock himself. This spectacle was enough to induce co-host Mika Brzezinski to enact a gag reflex live on air, a reaction that was both telling and, frankly, understandable.

The attempt at humor by co-host Joe Scarborough, likening RFK Jr. to Vladimir Putin, only underscores the bizarre juxtaposition of high office and lowbrow entertainment. The spectacle of a shirtless government official doing sit-ups in denim jeans is, to put it mildly, an odd choice of messaging and attire.

The Message

Beyond the immediate visual absurdity, the stated aim of this video—to encourage Americans to “get active and eat real food”—is a message that, while fundamentally sound, is lost in the execution. The choice of medium and messengers detracts significantly from the message itself. It’s hard to take health and wellness advice seriously when it’s delivered by two shirtless celebrities in jeans, one of whom is plunging into a hot tub on camera.

The Critique

The panel’s reaction, a mix of laughter, disgust, and incredulity, is indicative of the broader public sentiment. The critique isn’t merely about the choice of workout attire—though the focus on RFK Jr.’s denim jeans does highlight the absurdity—but about the total misjudgment in how this message was delivered.

The term “lunkheads” used by Scarborough to describe the administration, in light of this spectacle, might seem harsh, but it’s an understandable sentiment. It speaks to a larger issue of tone-deafness and a disconnect from the dignity traditionally associated with public service.

The Larger Implication

This incident is emblematic of a deeper systemic problem: the erosion of the seriousness with which public office is held. When public officials engage in antics better suited to reality TV than governmental leadership, it undermines not only their own credibility but also the public’s trust in the institutions they represent.

The intent to promote health and wellness is commendable. However, the execution here is a textbook example of how not to communicate important messages to the public. It trivializes the office, the message, and, by extension, the very issues of health and wellness it seeks to address.

In conclusion, while the RFK Jr. and Kid Rock “workout” video may have been intended as a light-hearted encouragement for a healthier lifestyle, it serves as a stark reminder of the importance of appropriateness and decorum in public communication. The laughter it induced on “Morning Joe” is indicative of a broader issue of credibility and respect that is no laughing matter.
