'Harvard is a joke!' Trump melts down on university for defying him

President Donald Trump ratcheted up his attacks on Harvard University after the school defied his demands for control.

The Trump administration announced it was freezing $2.2 billion in grants to Harvard after the nation's oldest university refused to comply with the president's demanded changes to its hiring, admissions and curriculum, and he kept up his rhetorical attacks on the private educational institution.

"Everyone knows that Harvard has 'lost its way,'" Trump posted at 7:05 a.m. Wednesday morning on Truth Social. "They hired, from New York (Bill D) and Chicago (Lori L), at ridiculously high salaries/fees, two of the WORST and MOST INCOMPETENT mayors in the history of our Country, to 'teach' municipal management and government. These two Radical Left fools left behind two cities that will take years to recover from their incompetence and evil."

Trump then attacked Claudine Gay, who resigned early last year as Harvard's first black woman president over plagiarism allegations dating back to 1993.

"Harvard has been hiring almost all woke, Radical Left, idiots and 'birdbrains' who are only capable of teaching FAILURE to students and so-called 'future leaders,'" Trump posted. "Look just to the recent past at their plagiarizing President, who so greatly embarrassed Harvard before the United States States Congress. When it got so bad that they just couldn’t take it anymore, they moved this grossly inept woman into another position, teaching, rather than firing her ON THE SPOT. Since then much else has been found out about her, but she remains in place."

Gay, who is now a Harvard professor of government and African and African-American studies, was grilled by a congressional committee with other university presidents in December of 2023 over their handling of antisemitism on campus amid the Israel-Hamas war.

"Many others, like these Leftist dopes, are teaching at Harvard, and because of that, Harvard can no longer be considered even a decent place of learning, and should not be considered on any list of the World’s Great Universities or Colleges," Trump posted. "Harvard is a JOKE, teaches Hate and Stupidity, and should no longer receive Federal Funds. Thank you for your attention to this matter!"

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Assault on Harvard University

In a move as brazen as it is bewildering, President Donald Trump has escalated his vendetta against Harvard University, an institution as synonymous with academic prestige as it is with controversy. Trump's latest onslaught against Harvard unfolds via a series of inflammatory and accusatory statements, culminating in the freezing of a staggering $2.2 billion in grants. This punitive action stems from Harvard's refusal to bend to Trump's will regarding its hiring, admissions, and curriculum policies.

The Essence of Trump's Grievance

Trump's grievances, aired publicly in a morning tirade on Truth Social, target several aspects of Harvard's operations and ethos. He lambasts the university for its hiring decisions, specifically calling out the employment of former mayors Bill D from New York and Lori L from Chicago. Trump disparages these figures as "the WORST and MOST INCOMPETENT mayors," criticizing their roles in teaching municipal management and government. This attack not only questions the competence of these individuals but also implies a nefarious influence on the students under their tutelage.

A Deeper Dive into Trump's Attacks

Trump's tirade doesn't stop with former mayors. He sets his sights on Claudine Gay, Harvard's first black woman president, who resigned amid plagiarism allegations. Trump's narrative doesn't just critique Gay's alleged academic misconduct; it paints her as a symbol of what he perceives as Harvard's broader decline into "woke," "Radical Left" ideologies. He accuses the institution of harboring "idiots and 'birdbrains'" who propagate "FAILURE."

The Underlying Motivations

Trump's attack on Harvard seems less about genuine concern for academic integrity or educational quality and more about a calculated political maneuver. By framing Harvard as a bastion of "woke" and "Radical Left" ideologies, Trump is tapping into the cultural and political divides that polarize the nation. His use of charged language like "idiots," "birdbrains," and "evil" is not an appeal to rational critique but a rallying cry to his base.

The Broader Implications

The freezing of $2.2 billion in grants to Harvard is no small matter. It's a significant escalation that moves beyond rhetoric into actions that could have tangible effects on research, education, and the lives of students and faculty. This move raises questions about the use of federal power to coerce educational institutions and potentially curtail academic freedom.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent?

In the grand scheme, Trump's actions and words against Harvard do not exist in a vacuum. They reflect a broader trend of political figures attempting to influence or control educational institutions to fit a particular ideological mold. Such a trend is not only dangerous for the independence and integrity of academic institutions but also for the democratic principles that underpin the freedom of thought and expression.

Harvard, with all its flaws and controversies, should be critiqued, debated, and held accountable. However, the manner in which Trump has chosen to engage with the university—through punitive financial measures and incendiary language—sets a dangerous precedent. It suggests a willingness to leverage federal power to suppress dissenting views and control the narrative within one of the nation's most prestigious educational institutions.

In this saga of power, politics, and education, one thing is clear: the battle is not just about Harvard. It's about the soul of academic freedom in the United States.
