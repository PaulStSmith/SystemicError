Former prosecutor Andrew McCarthy has no love for the “craven video” Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) and five Democrats released to the public advising military members to ignore illegal orders. But he said President Donald Trump’s executive power abuses in reacting to it represent a whole “new level” of threat.

McCarthy tells the National Review that he partially blames Democrats for Trump ordering the Pentagon to demote Kelly and cut his benefits because “Kelly knows, when Democrats poke another hole in another norm, the president’s MO is to drive a truck through it.”

The author and National Review Institute senior fellow also notes Trump is howling “sedition” like he knows what it means.

“What is truly bizarre is to find the president, who likes to remind us that he is the nation’s chief law enforcement official, grossly misstating the law while claiming that the ‘Seditionist Six’ are dangerously misstating the law (when in fact they’ve accurately stated the law),” McCarthy said. “As one of the few current or former prosecutors in the United States to have actually charged and convicted people for seditious conspiracy, I’m here to tell you that the heart of any sedition offense is the use of force against the nation or its government.”

Section 2384 of federal criminal law defines the crime as conspiring to levy war against the United States or to forcibly (1) destroy the government, (2) prevent execution of the laws, or (3) seize government property. The military law definition, said McCarthy, is even more narrow: One must join in the creation of a “revolt, violence, or other disturbance against” government authority, “with intent to cause the overthrow or destruction of” that authority.

“Nothing on the Democrats’ video comes close to urging or promoting violence. Indeed, in comparison to Trump’s fiery Ellipse speech prior to the Capitol riot … the lawmakers’ video is vanilla,” said McCarthy. “If Kelly had been urging his fellow military members to disobey lawful orders, that would be insubordination, not sedition. But he wasn’t.”

“As the president watches his poll numbers plummet, it either doesn’t dawn on him or he just doesn’t care that he is in office, in part, because the voting public was unnerved by Democratic lawfare.” Said McCarthy. “Clearly, Trump’s statist mismanagement of the economy is his biggest problem, as it was Biden’s. But lawfare … is a bigger problem for Trump.”

“Trump and his minions revel in lawfare,” said McCarthy, which “further normalizes the noxious practice, potentially entrenching it.”

“Trump is also handing Democrats the articles of impeachment they will swiftly enact if, as seems increasingly likely, his erratic governance hands them back the House next year — and maybe even the Senate the way things are going,” McCarthy warned. “… Incorporating the Pentagon into the lawfare campaign against political enemies raises the abuse of executive power to a new level.”

“For the president to begin pulling the military he commands into his ongoing, punitive use of government processes against his partisan opponents is a red line,” said McCarthy. “Justice Department lawfare is bad, but the courts are equipped to handle it. Politicization of the military is a different, more threatening beast.”

Our Analysis:

The Perilous Politicization of the Military: A Critical Analysis

In a recent article, Andrew McCarthy, a former prosecutor and National Review Institute senior fellow, casts a glaring spotlight on a concerning escalation in the abuse of executive power by President Donald Trump. McCarthy’s critique, grounded in his seasoned legal expertise, underscores a chilling venture into dangerous territory: the politicization of the military. This analysis will dissect the key points raised by McCarthy, highlighting the grave implications of Trump’s actions.

Democrat Actions vs. Trump’s Reactions: A Dangerous Cycle

McCarthy points out a troubling dynamic between Democratic actions and Trump’s reactions. The “craven video” released by Sen. Mark Kelly and five Democrats, advising military members to ignore illegal orders, is criticized by McCarthy not for its content but for provoking an expectedly disproportionate response from Trump. Trump’s decision to order the Pentagon to demote Kelly and slash his benefits is a stark manifestation of this reactionary politics, where a breach of norms by one side leads to an escalated breach by the other. McCarthy’s observation that “Kelly knows, when Democrats poke another hole in another norm, the president’s MO is to drive a truck through it,” highlights a vicious cycle of norm-breaking that is spiraling out of control.

Misinterpretation of Sedition: Ignorance or Willful Misdirection?

Trump’s accusation of “sedition” against the so-called ‘Seditionist Six’ is another focal point of McCarthy’s critique. McCarthy, with his prosecutorial background in charging seditious conspiracy, clarifies that sedition involves the use of force against the nation or its government—a criterion starkly absent from the Democrats’ video. Trump’s misrepresentation of the law, therefore, either stems from a misunderstanding or a deliberate attempt to weaponize legal terminology against political opponents. McCarthy’s comparison of the Democrats’ video to Trump’s incendiary speech prior to the Capitol riot underscores a hypocrisy in Trump’s accusations, making it clear that the real danger to lawful governance and public safety comes not from the actions criticized by Trump but from his own rhetoric and behavior.

The Larger Threat: Politicization of the Military

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of McCarthy’s analysis is the warning against the politicization of the military. By dragging the military into his “ongoing, punitive use of government processes against his partisan opponents,” Trump is crossing a “red line.” McCarthy rightly points out that while the Justice Department has been subjected to politicization, it remains within the capacity of the courts to manage. The military, however, represents a “different, more threatening beast.” This move not only undermines the apolitical nature of the military but also sets a dangerous precedent for the use of armed forces as a tool in partisan warfare.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

McCarthy’s insights offer a sobering reminder of the thin ice upon which American democracy currently treads. The escalation of executive power abuses, particularly the politicization of the military, poses a threat not just to the norms and practices of governance but to the very foundations of the republic. It is a clarion call for vigilance and resistance against the normalization of such practices.

Trump’s actions, as outlined by McCarthy, are not merely political maneuvers; they are betrayals of the trust placed in the office of the presidency. The implications of these actions reach far beyond the immediate political landscape, threatening the integrity of the nation’s democratic institutions and the rule of law.

As we reflect on McCarthy’s analysis, it becomes clear that the issue at hand transcends partisan politics. It is a question of preserving the sanctity of our democratic principles and the institutions that support them. The politicization of the military, as a tool for partisan advantage, is a step too far and a danger that must be addressed with unyielding resolve.
